# Craps

Craps (花旗骰) as Macau deals it: `craps.html`, `craps.js` and `craps.css`, its rules in `craps-engine.js`, the layout drawn by `craps-layout.js` (the page bets on it and the course shows it), the course in `craps-course.js` on the stage `craps-stage.js`, and the rules on one page in `craps-rules.js`. The two dice and the way they tumble (`dieSvg`, `tumble`) are Sic Bo's, in `casino-dice.js` and `casino-dice.css`; `tumble` moved there from `sicbo.js` so that both games roll the same way. Craps' tile and its Modern and Kontor logos are in `apps.js`.

The game, by the gaming regulator's rules for it (the DICJ's Despacho 53/2010): the shooter (主客) throws two dice to the far wall of the table and they must roll, or the throw is void (無效, Article 3) and thrown again; here you are always the shooter. A round starts with the come-out roll (出場擲骰): a 7 or 11 wins the pass line (派司拉) and a 2, 3 or 12 loses it; any other total is the point, and the shooter throws on until the point comes again (the pass line wins) or a 7 does (a seven out: it loses). Don't pass (東派司) is the other side, and a 12 on the come-out roll is a push (和局, "bar 12"). Come (襟) and don't come (東襟) are the same bets begun on any later roll: the bet goes to the number thrown and waits there. Behind a line or come bet there is the additional bet (附加投注), the odds, which pays what the number is truly worth against a 7: 2 to 1 on a 4 or 10, 3 to 2 on a 5 or 9, 6 to 5 on a 6 or 8, and behind don't pass the lay at 1 to 2, 2 to 3 and 5 to 6. The rules list the rest by Article 6: the field (1 to 1; the rules let a table pay 3 to 1 on a 12, and this one pays 2 to 1, as it does on a 2); Big (8 to 12) and Small (2 to 6) at 1 to 1; the hard ways (a pair before a 7 or the number made another way) at 9 to 1 on 6 and 8 and 7 to 1 on 4 and 10; the Big 6 and 8 at 7 to 6, 5 and 9 at 7 to 5, 4 and 10 at 9 to 5; and the one-roll bets: Any seven 4 to 1, Any craps 7 to 1, Eleven and Ace-deuce 15 to 1, Aces and Boxcars 30 to 1, the Horn (a 2, 3, 11 or 12 in one bet) 27 to 4 on a 2 or 12 and 3 to 1 on a 3 or 11. Nothing is rounded: the house's edge on every bet is worked out exactly, over the 36 ways the dice fall and, for a bet that waits, every way it can end, and a play-out through the rules agrees with each. Pass keeps 1.41% (7 in 495), don't pass 1.36% (3 in 220), the odds and lays nothing (the only bets that keep nothing: the number's ways against the 7's six are exactly what they pay), the Big 6 and 8 1.52%, the Big 5 and 9 4%, the field 5.56% (2.78% were a 12 to pay 3 to 1), the Big 4 and 10 6.67%, a hard 6 or 8 9.09%, a hard 4 or 10 11.11%, Any craps, Eleven and Ace-deuce 11.11%, the Horn 12.5%, Aces and Boxcars 13.89%, Any seven and Big or Small 16.67%. That is the lesson of the table: the pass line and the odds behind it are the cheap bets, and the bets that pay big and look generous are the expensive ones. The odds don't change what the line keeps, but they put money on a bet that keeps nothing, so what the house keeps of all you stake falls with them: 1.41% with none, 0.85% with 1 times the line behind it, 0.61% with 2, 0.47% with 3, 0.33% with 5 and 0.18% with 10 (behind don't pass, 0.68% and 0.23% with 1 and 5 times).

Where the rules are silent this table chooses, and the rules page says so. Every bet works on every roll, the come-out roll too. A bet that is decided comes down, win or lose, and Rebet puts back what came down; a bet that isn't (a line bet at its point, a come bet on its number, a hard way, a Big bet, the odds) stays up for the next roll, so a round of several rolls is played on the same table. The odds, the hard ways and the Big bets may be taken down at any time (Clear becomes Take down when nothing has been put down since the roll); a line or come bet may not once the dice are thrown. Pass and don't pass go down on the come-out roll, come and don't come and the odds once there is a point (Article 5). The odds go up to 5 times the bet behind them (a lay, to win 5 times it); the rules name no limit. Stakes go in HK$50s, at least HK$100, and in the steps that keep every payout in whole dollars: the Horn in HK$100s, a Big 6 or 8 in HK$300s, a lay in HK$100s, HK$150s or HK$300s on 4 or 10, 5 or 9, 6 or 8. The most is HK$100,000 on a line or come bet, HK$50,000 on the field, Small or Big, HK$30,000 on a Big number, HK$5,000 on a hard way, Any seven or Any craps, HK$4,000 on the Horn, HK$2,000 on Eleven or Ace-deuce and HK$1,000 on Aces or Boxcars. The rules set no limits and no table's were found, so these are the game's own. The rules list no place, buy or lay bets (apart from the lay behind don't pass) and no C and E, so none is played. You start with HK$20,000.

The page: the strip has what you hold, "Come-out roll" or "Point 6", and the roll. Under it the screen over the table: the last twenty totals as balls, newest first and the latest ringed (a 7 in red, which ends a point), and what the last hundred came to (sevens as a share, the hottest and the coldest totals). Under that the tray with the two dice and the stickman's call: the total with Natural, Craps, Point 6, Point made, Seven out or Hard way, and what the roll paid you. The layout, as big as the room left allows, in the order a table lays it out: the pass line and don't pass with the odds and the lay under them; come and don't come; the six numbers, each a box with the come and don't come halves where a come bet travels (a tap on a half puts odds behind it) and the puck, ON, on the point; the field; Small and Big; the Big 4 to 10; the hard ways; the one-roll bets at the foot. A cell the table won't take a chip on now (the pass line once there is a point, the odds before one) is dimmed, and a tap says why. A tap puts the chip on a cell, its amount at the cell's corner and a toast saying what it bet and what it pays. The layout is longer than a phone has room for, so it scrolls inside its own box (the page doesn't) on a short screen; on a 390×844 phone it fits. Held sideways the layout takes the left of the screen and the screen, the tray, the chips and the buttons the right. The rail: the chips (HK$50 to HK$5,000), Clear or Take down, Undo (the last chip; Rebet, the bets of the last roll that can go down again, when none is put down) and Roll. Roll: the dice tumble in the tray and land, the cells they win are ringed, and every bet decided shows what it won or lost at its corner; a come bet that travels says to which number. Menu: a new table (the screen starts again, the puck comes off), Top up, the course, Craps on one page, quick rolls. The game is kept as it stands (`craps:run`: your bankroll, the point, the bets on the table, what was put down since the last roll, the rolls so far), the chip and quick rolls in `craps:opts`, the course in `craps:course`. The screen is dark by day and by night, as an electronic one is.

The course: the dice (two dice and the 36 ways they fall, with the totals as a chart; the throw and the void throw); the line (the come-out roll and the point; don't pass and the bar 12; the odds, at what they pay, why they keep nothing, and what they do to what the house keeps of all you stake, with the 5 times this table takes; come and don't come); the traps (the one-roll bets and the Horn; the field and its 20 losing throws of 36; the hard ways and the Big bets; every bet ranked from least to most, and what 100 rolls of HK$100 on Any seven cost against 100 rounds of the pass line); on the floor (the words, Cantonese with Jyutping). Where it speaks of Macau it rests on the DICJ's rules. Not found, and so not claimed: any Macau table's limits, whether a table works its bets on the come-out roll (the rules are silent, so this one does), the printed Chinese on a Macau layout beyond the rules' own words, and the most odds a Macau table lets you take.
