# Banquet
The hand games of a Chinese banquet table, for two (October 2026), against the computer or at a table with a friend each on their own phone (Invite players: seats.js runs it, as it runs Liar's dice). Two games, chosen in the menu, played for lives: each starts with three (or five, or seven), and whoever loses them all first loses. No drinking in the games (4 November 2026, Korbi: "remove the drinking references across the games (PG), just say 'loses' or 'loses a life'"): a loss is a loss or a life; `tests/no-drinking.mjs` keeps the words out.

划拳 (huáquán, "drawing fists"): both throw a hand at once, none to five fingers, and call the total of both hands. Fingers go up from the thumb, as the game is played (one is the thumb, two the thumb and the forefinger: pointing a finger at someone is rude). Whoever calls it right while the other doesn't makes the other lose a life; both right, or neither, and you go again. Each total has its verse, said with the throw, and every region has its own; Banquet uses among the commonest: 宝拳 (bǎo quán) for none, 一心敬, 哥俩好 (gē liǎ hǎo, "two good brothers"), 三星照, 四喜财, 五魁首 (wǔ kuí shǒu, "five top scholars"), 六六顺, 七个巧, 八匹马, 九连环, 十全美, each shown with its pinyin and the number, and read out in Mandarin when the hands are shown. A call your own hand rules out, below your own fingers or past them and five more, is a 臭拳 (chòu quán, "stinking fist"), and the buttons don't offer it.

十五二十 (sap6 ng5 ji6 sap6, "fifteen, twenty"), Hong Kong's: each shows both hands, open (five) or a fist (nothing), so the four hands make 0, 5, 10, 15 or 20. One player calls, the other just shows; call it right twice in a row and the other loses a life (and calls next); call it wrong and the call passes. The calls are Cantonese, with their jyutping, and read out in Cantonese where the phone has the voice: 冇 (mou5), 五, 十, 十五, 二十.

A tap on a hand and a tap on a call throws. The runner takes moves one seat at a time, so the throws come in turn (the first thrower alternates in 划拳; the caller throws first in 十五二十), but neither sees the other's until both are in, and you can throw before your turn: your throw waits and goes in the moment it's yours, so both throw at once as at the table. Then both hands are shown, drawn in ink on the page's paper (no skin), the calls in large characters, struck through when wrong, the total, and who loses a life, for two and a half seconds. The computer (Lao Wang) on Easy throws and calls at random; on Sharp it watches what you've thrown before, never this round's, and calls on your habits, the last few throws counting more, with a surprise one time in four. The game's tile counts a run of wins.

The games, in eight lessons: a hand (fingers from the thumb), the total of both, the verses (酒令: the eleven, and three to name), your call (tap every call three fingers may make: below your own or past it and five more is a 臭拳, refused with why), who loses a life (one right, both, neither), 十五二十 (both hands, open or a fist; its Cantonese calls), the caller (twice running and the other loses a life); then a first game against Easy. Playing well, in six: the odds (against a hand thrown at random each reachable call is one in six, so the only thing to read is the thrower), reading Lao Wang's last throws (call your hand and his favourite; the last few count most, as Sharp counts them), not being read (seven twos in ten is what Sharp feeds on), 十五二十's odds against hands opened at random (call five more than you show: half the time; the others a quarter), against a mixer (a third each, whatever you call); then a game against Sharp. A step is words over hands, a hand to tap the calls of, a call to make (tap it, then Call), or a choice.
