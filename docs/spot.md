# Spot
Sums fly across a dark field: thrown up from below in an arc, flung in from a side and falling away, or dropping faster and faster with a sway, each on its own path. Three lives; every 5 right in a row raises the score multiplier by half, up to ×3.

The pace is a budget of difficulty: every sum has a cost (1 for a small addition, 2 for a times, 3 for a division, +1 for bigger numbers, +1 under a modifier), and the run has a budget of difficulty a second that rises slowly (0.45 at the start, 1.3 at four minutes, then gently on). A cheap sum is followed quickly by the next; a dear one buys a few seconds, so you might get four easy sums in a row or one division with time to think. Lifetimes scale with cost (5.3 s for a small addition, 12 s for a big division) and shorten by a fifth over four minutes. Times tables fade in from 12 s, division from 35 s, bigger numbers from 60 s; the level shown goes up one every 30 s.

Modifiers: from 45 s, a badge in the header shows an operation to apply to every result before anything else (+1, +2, +3, −1, −2, +10 or ×2), changing every 20 s; a bit over a third of the time there is none. Each sum is tagged with the modifier it came under, so a change mid-flight is never ambiguous. Alone, the shelf shows modified answers, and the unmodified answer is always planted among the fakes. Versus, everyone has the alone game's modifiers, the same for all. Co-op, each player has their own modifier, and a pair is two sums that make the same number after each player's own modifier, so the numbers you call are the modified ones.

Alone: the answers wait on a shelf of eight round tokens below, among fakes. Tap an answer before its sum runs out; a wrong tap or a sum that runs out costs a life. Fakes are the slips you'd make: off by one or ten, swapped digits, a neighbouring times-table answer, adding instead of multiplying, forgetting the modifier. No two sums on screen show the same value, no fake matches one, and no number shows twice on a shelf. Play a random run or today's run (the same for everyone that day); bests are kept.

Each number comes onto the shelf at a moment of its own, 0.3 to 3.5 seconds before its sum flies in, into a free slot picked at random (October 2026). The answer used to arrive with its sum, its fakes beside it, in the first free slot, which was the first slot nine times in ten: the newest number in the first place was the answer, so it could be tapped without working the sum out. Now the shelf holds answers to sums still to come among the fakes, every number arriving on its own, so neither when nor where one appears says which sum it answers (`tests/spot.mjs` measures it: the answer as its sum appears is spread evenly over the eight slots, and the newest number on the shelf about one time in four, as likely as any of the few that came in just before). An answer tapped before its sum has come answers nothing on screen: a wrong tap, costing a life once however fast the taps, and the number stays for its sum. The moments come from a stream of each sum's own, apart from the sums'. Numbers that can share the shelf are kept apart over the longer stretch the early ones make (11 seconds and a tenth, plus the 3.5), which changes a seed's sums from the first time it rules a number out, today's run included.

At a table (October 2026): a pair's match from the partner sheet, or a party of up to six, everyone on their own phone, in one of two modes, chosen in the lobby before a run starts (or in the menu, once the last run is over for everyone). The lobby shows who's at the table in their colours, and Start deals a run to everyone there, starting four seconds later on the database's clock. Opened in a room, the page shows the rete (Joining your partner, or Joining the table at a party) until the room's lobby is drawn, never the solo start meanwhile, where a tap on Play started a run of your own (October 2026).

Versus, a party's to start with: everyone plays the alone game on the same seed from the same start, so the same sums are thrown at the same moments at everyone, each answering on their own shelf. The others' scores run live in the line under the header; once your lives are gone the standings show who's still playing, and once everyone's are, who won (the most points; equal scores share a place, and a draw wins nobody the pair's record), with Play again and Back to solo. Nothing but taps travels: each phone replays every player from their own taps on the one map (`versusStandings` in `spot-table.js`), so the scores agree everywhere, and a phone that leaves runs out of lives on its own as its sums go unanswered, holding nobody up.

Co-op, a pair's as it always was: there's no shelf, only sums, and each of you sees only your own. Most come in pairs: a sum on one screen and a sum making the same number on another, the second arriving 0.6–4 s after the first. You call out what yours make; whoever has the other taps the one of theirs that makes a number someone called, now or when it arrives (a tap clears the pair, even a partner still to appear). Some sums have no partner: tapping one costs a life. A pair that leaves both screens untapped costs a life. Lives and score are the table's, and no number means two things at once. For more than two the pairs are dealt across everyone at the table, each pair on two different screens and every screen its share, and a little faster than for two (√(n/2) times: 1.2 for three, 1.7 for six), since each call has more ears but must stay something a table can follow; two are dealt exactly as before. A run is dealt to those at the table when it starts: one who sits down after watches it out and plays the next.

How it's built: everything comes from a seed and the taps. `spot-engine.js` makes the timeline from the seed alone (the budget, the modifiers per seat, the pairs) and replays the taps over it to get the state at any moment (`play()`), so two phones with the same seed and taps see the same game, and only taps travel through the room (each with its time on the database clock, which `sync.js` provides as an offset). The page replays the run every frame; flight paths come from each sum's id. Solo runs pause while the app is in the background (a table's clock is everyone's and never stops), and the screen stays awake while you play. `spot-table.js` is the table's part, pure: the room's state, the seats a run is dealt to, the standings and the result. `tests/spot.mjs` checks the timelines, the pace (over twenty runs: one run's thirty seconds hold only a dozen sums), the modifiers, the pairs (for two to six), the replays, that the shelf gives nothing away, and Versus: the same map for every seat, the standings, the end and the draw.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the field takes the left, all the height under the score, and the shelf of answers stands beside it, two across and low; Co-op, which has no shelf, gives the field the whole width (it used to leave the shelf's column empty).
