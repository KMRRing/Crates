# Coming soon

Games on the list that aren't built (31 October 2026, Korbi: "Bridges, masyu, nurikabe, slitherlink, rack, go, pegs, meld, French tarot if not already there… I want all of these but have used 84% of weekly limit… the rest just get a tile placeholder with todo for now"). Each has a tile on the games screen, in the section it will live in (Bridges, Masyu, Nurikabe and Slitherlink in Classics; Rack, Stone, Pegs, Meld and Tarot in Board & card games; Rack, Meld, Pegs, Slitherlink, Bridges, Masyu and Nurikabe have since been built, see their sections), with a logo in each house's drawing (`soon-logos.js`: Codex's navy and brass, Modern's flat colour, Kontor's and Office's line drawing). The tile opens `soon.html?game=<id>` (one page for all of them, `soon.js` and `soon.css`), which names the game, says in a sentence what it will be, and lists what's left to do, with where the risk is: those notes are `soon-data.js`, the plan in one place, so it can be read there and a reader doesn't have to ask what was meant. Nothing is kept, nothing plays, and nothing syncs.

A placeholder is nothing else in the app: Random leaves it out ("not built yet", `LEFT_OUT`), Start a game over doesn't list it, and its best isn't shared with your partner. When a game is built, its entry leaves `soon-data.js`, its logos move into `apps.js` with the rest, its tile points at its own page, and it goes in Random's `ROUNDS` or `LEFT_OUT` with a reason; `tests/soon.mjs` fails while a tile and its entry disagree, and `tests/random.mjs` while a tile is in neither list.

The order they're meant to be built in is the order of how much of the existing machinery each reuses: Meld and Pegs (both built) on seats.js (a pure rules module and a bot, as Big Two and Landlord are), then Slitherlink and Bridges (both built) on the puzzle pipeline Hitori and Sudoku use (a generator that proves a board has one solution, a solver that grades it by the hardest step a person would need), then Masyu (built) on Slitherlink's loop engine (its edge solver and loop rule, now `loop-engine.js`), then Nurikabe (built) on a generator that never needs a guess (see its section for how it comes by one). Rack (built, on the suite's SCOWL list) went last of the three that waited on a choice; Stone waits on the choice of a computer opponent and Tarot on a bot for a game with bidding.

Three more tiles were added on 2 November 2026 (Korbi, after the library review: "solitaire, 2048, connect four: ok, add placeholders"): **Solitaire** (Klondike, FreeCell and Spider with a daily deal) and **Silo** (four in a row, under that name because Connect Four is Hasbro's) in Board & card games, and **Ingot** (2048's game, tiles as bars of metal) in Idle. The names are working names; a tile's name is a string in `apps.js` and `soon-data.js`, its id is what saves and Random's lists use, so a rename later costs nothing. Silo and Solitaire are the cheapest of the board and card games (no opponent for Solitaire; for Silo a few plies of search and a count of open threats). Ingot is a few lines of rules and the choice of one swipe in four.

Three more placeholders followed on 2 November 2026 (Korbi: "othello, draughts, falling blocks... sounds ok"): **Flank** (Reversi; Othello is a trademark) and **Crown** (draughts, with English and International rules as data) in Board & card games, and **Cargo** (falling blocks) in Idle, built last because the Tetris Company has won against a clone that copied the look and feel (Tetris Holding v. Xio, 2012), so Cargo has its own piece set, colours and a narrower well. Flank and Crown are our own searches on seats.js: the strong free engines for them (Edax, Scan) are GPL. On chess, Korbi's call: Gambit keeps its engine only if one under a permissive licence will do (its Stockfish and Fairy-Stockfish are GPL v3, in `vendor/`); the worst case is puzzles only, so nothing GPL has to be in the app. Which permissive engine is the open question.

Speed maths followed on 2 November 2026 (Korbi: "add a speed maths placeholder in puzzle"): a tile in Puzzles beside Spot, typed answers one sum at a time against the clock (Zetamac's genre), with the unit chains below as its second mode. Its name is a working name; its id is `speedmaths`.

Fuel maths followed on 3 November 2026 (Korbi, 2 November: two versions of Speed maths, a generic mercantile one in Puzzles and a fuel-pricing one for his refining work, with typed estimates accepted to one or two significant figures depending on the calculation): a tile in the Appendix. It is not built: `speedfuels` in `soon-data.js` says what it needs, and `LEFT_OUT` in `random-core.js` lists it as not built yet.

**Not a tile yet: unit chains** (Korbi, 2 November 2026: "one more fast arithmetic game that essentially is just unit conversions", to be recorded and not built). Beside Spot in Puzzles: the computer picks a start figure and a chain of operations, each unit followed by the next step, and you type the answer as fast as you can. Korbi's examples are all commodities, in the order a trader does them: a flow in thousand barrels a day; how many tonnes a month; 5% of that; a saving of 80% against a baseline of 94 gCO2e/MJ and how many tonnes of CO2e a month that saves; at 120 GBP a tonne, how many pounds a month; the same in dollars a year. Long chains of mental arithmetic, not necessarily logical ones, each step the next unit's operation. What it needs: a pool of figures and conversions with their factors (barrels to tonnes by product, energy content, emission factors, exchange rates fixed at the start so the answer is exact), chains of three to six steps that end on a figure a person can type (rounded to a stated place, a tolerance, or integers by construction), a timer and a streak, and a plain-language pool (litres to gallons, miles to kilometres, recipes) so the public app has a way in that is not oil. The data lives in `kb/units.js`, which already holds the units.
