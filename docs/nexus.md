# Nexus

A thing from the knowledge base in the middle (the nexus), a ring of candidates round it, and you pick the ones the Codex links to it directly (Korbi, 1 November 2026: "a game where a central (nexus) item and a bunch of adjacent items are shown from the knowledge graph and the user identifies all the ones that are linked to the nexus item"; he preferred it to a Spot-style game for the knowledge base, and Arb stayed beside it until he had played it; it is gone, 3 November 2026). "Linked" is what somebody wrote down with its "because" (`kb/links.js`), either way round: Norway has Equinor as a clue, and Equinor has Norway as its answer. The words are always "in the Codex", since a decoy may be linked in life; the reveal says where it goes instead.

Three kinds of round, five to a run, random or today's, in one mix or one kind (menu, Rounds):
- **Orbit**: everything linked to the nexus among 8, 10 or 12 candidates (menu, Candidates): three to five of them.
- **Aspect**: only what is linked under one aspect of it. A clue link carries the aspects Crates weights (Norway: Troll under Energy, Fjords under Nature), so the round asks for Norway as Energy, and the nexus's other links, which are linked but under another aspect, are half the decoys, the rest being other things' links under that aspect. 178 nexuses have an aspect with three links or more.
- **Intruder**: four linked and one that isn't, with one pick. 672 nexuses have four links.

There are 1,201 things to start from (three links or more, a name short enough for a chip). Orbit and Intruder are dealt from all of them, Aspect from the 178.

Decoys are near misses, never random things, because a decoy a player can rule out by its kind isn't testing the link. Three sources: the links of the nexus's own sort (another country's clues beside a country's), the links of its true links (what Equinor goes with that isn't Norway), and the links of the other things its true links are linked to (Brazil, Equinor's other country). A decoy is of the kind of the true links (a building among buildings) whenever the pools hold enough, and the others only fill what is short; no decoy has more than 60 links (a hub is linked to half the world in life, if not in the Codex). What a clue only loosely fits (`alt`, over half the clue links) is neither a true link nor a decoy: it could fairly be called either, so it never appears for the other answer. Decoys keep their home (the thing they are linked to), which the reveal names. The pieces are `nexus-engine.js`'s, pure; `tests/nexus.mjs` checks that no decoy is linked or loosely fitting, that every link is, that Aspect's links all carry its aspect and its wrong-aspect decoys don't, that decoys are of the links' kind, and that a seed deals the same run.

Scoring: a round scores what you found less what you picked wrongly, over the links there are, out of 100 (found 3 of 4 with one wrong: 50); an Intruder is 100 or 0. Check turns the ring into the same list with a verdict for each (found, missed, wrong pick, not linked, or linked under another aspect) and the link as it was written ("Troll → Norway: One of the largest offshore gas fields ever found"); a decoy says "Not linked to Norway" and the link it has instead. A run is out of 500, counts for your best on the standard run (Rounds Mixed, 10 candidates, topics on a preset) and today's is always that; your own mix, kind of round or ring size keeps its best out of the games screen.

Learning. What a round showed you didn't know goes to the pile (Deck): a true link you left out becomes "which of these is linked to X in the Codex?" with your wrong picks among the options; a wrong pick, when nothing was left out, "which of these is not linked to X?" (an Aspect round asks for a link again, since its wrong pick may be linked under another aspect); an Intruder you missed asks for the intruder. The cards are about the two things, so other games' learning modes ask about them, and Deck keeps the options as written (`fixed`) rather than swapping in others of the kind, which could be linked in life. In learning mode a random run puts the nexuses of what is due in rounds 1 and 3, as Orbits holding the link to find and the wrong pick to meet again, and the nexuses other games found you weak on (Crates' Norway is Nexus's Norway); playing a round settles the cards it was dealt for, up a pile if you got it, back to the first if not. Today's run and a match take nothing of yours. Nothing again until the rest has had its turn (the nexuses dealt lately, 600 of them, are left out of a new run).

**Asking what a thing is** (1 November 2026, Korbi: "In arb and nexus, the user should be able get clues for the description of the central items", then "User should be able to request description for any item in nexus/arb but when done; it'll be marked as not known for the purpose of deck"). A Clue button on the nexus and a "?" on every candidate (in the ring, and in the verdicts after Check) give that thing's description a clue at a time, from vague to specific (`thing-clues.js`, `clueLines`): what it is (a country says its part of the world, a commodity its kind), where or when (born and died, built, founded, country, population, capital), then, for the nexus only, up to two of the hints written for its links, and its note. A line that names the nexus or any candidate is left out, so a clue never gives a link away; a candidate gets no hints at all, since those were written for its links and one of them could be the one asked. The clues cost no points. They cost the thing: asking about it files it in the pile as not known (once a round, a Today's run included, never in a match), as a card Deck can ask (`whatCard`): usually "which of these describes X?", its description among three of things of its kind said the same way (what it is, then where or when) and none sharing a particular with it, so none gives itself away; a thing too thin for that is asked the other way round, its fuller description and "which thing is this?" among the names of three of its kind. About nine things in ten have a card; the others (an architecture term with a single line to say for itself) are described but can't be asked, and the dialog says so by leaving out its note that asking marks it. The key is the thing's (`what~id`), so asking again is one item that drops back to the first pile each time.

The nexus's picture, if it has one (234 of the 1,201 do, a Wikipedia thumbnail, `pic` in `kb/`), shows beside its name in little; a tap opens it in full, with its credit (Korbi, 1 November 2026: "important for architecture for instance"). It is fetched as the other games fetch theirs (`pics.js`, so offline it comes from what was kept) and a picture that won't load leaves no empty frame.

Topics (set once for every game, 2 November 2026): a random run's nexuses are drawn from the topics that are on in Settings, the one set every knowledge game shares (README, The knowledge base, "Topics, set once for every game"), drawing a nexus by the weight of its likeliest topic (`drawEnd` in `kb-things.js`); Nexus has no control of its own. The menu shows where they stand ("Topics: STEM, 8 of 23 on") with a Change topics link that opens the same sheet over the game, shown whether today's run or a random one is picked (today's says it deals every topic), and a change made with today's picked turns the next run to a random one. The topics are the knowledge base's (a thing is about the topics of its links, each topic belonging to a subject: `topicsOf` in `kb-things.js` makes the mix, a topic in when its subject is on and out when it isn't), and they are read when a run is dealt, so a change reaches the next run and a run already dealt keeps what it was dealt. There is no more or less any longer (Korbi: on or off is all that's needed), so Balanced, Trader and Culture night are gone from the menu: every subject on deals what Balanced dealt, seed for seed, in every kind of round, and the engine still draws by a weight of 0, 1 or 2, so a link carrying an older mix still deals. Nothing on deals from every topic, and the line says so. A random run counts for your best on the standard run (Rounds Mixed, 10 candidates) when the topics are a preset (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade or All off); a mix of your own doesn't, and the menu says so. The old `nexus:topics` key is no longer read or written, and is left alone.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the ring or the verdicts take the board's column, as many across as fit, and the nexus with its question, the verdict and the button stand beside it, the button low. The game's enamel is plum; the logo is a hub with three linked satellites and two that aren't.

Together: the same five rounds for everyone at the table (README, Head to head), from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six, from the dealer's kind of round, ring size and mix; the highest total wins, then the quicker. Nothing of yours changes.

Arb is gone (3 November 2026) and the pieces Nexus drew from its engine (`ENT`, `ABOUT`, the topic lists, `drawEnd`) are `kb-things.js`, tested in `tests/kb-things.mjs`.
