# Rooms
Two players link once, on the games screen, and stay linked: the partner chip there makes a four-letter code to give your partner, or takes theirs. The link is a room that is yours for good (crates/rooms/CODE), and it belongs to the player, not the device: the code and your player id sync with your solo code, so your phone and laptop both know your partner. Nothing about playing together is set up inside a game.

Every page you open tells the room what you're doing (live/PLAYER/DEVICE: the game, solo or together, online while the page is), so your partner always sees it: on the games screen's partner chip ("Sarah · Slate, solo"), and in a small line in every game's title (green while they're online). That line gives way to the header's buttons: where a game's title leaves little room (Cartel, with Rules beside the menu), it shortens to "…" rather than push the menu off the screen, and the games screen has it in full. From the partner sheet either of you can ask the other to play a game together, theirs or yours, among the games that have a duo match (Crates, Slate, Chart, Delta, Punt, Quote, Spot, Stow, Origin, Order, Manifest, Hong, Tribute, River, Gambit, and the head-to-heads on one deal: Gambit's puzzle rush, Blend, Survey, Pipes, Refinery; and Crossword and the Classics, each as a team or a race: README, Classics): a banner appears over whatever the other is doing, "Korbi wants to play Slate together", Play or Not now, for the two minutes a request stands. It sits in the topmost open dialog when there is one, since a modal dialog (the games screen itself, a menu) leaves everything outside it out of sight and out of reach: on the page behind, the banner never showed to a partner on the games screen. Every answer is a transaction on the request (Play, Not now, or the asking device at two minutes), so only the first lands and the two of you can't part on it: a Play after the asker was told "No answer" says the request lapsed. Play takes both of you into the duo match, the game opened in the room at a new table (?room=CODE&new=, the request's time): whichever of you arrives first deals it over whatever was left there, and the other sits down at it (every game on `together.js`, and Slate, which keeps a room of its own; Crates, which keeps one too, carries on with the match in the room). The device that asked goes as soon as the yes comes in, from whatever page it's on by then (the page that asked may have closed, or the phone slept through the answer), once, and within ten minutes. Each solo game stays exactly where it was (Hong, Tribute and Stow don't deal or play on yours while you sit down); Back to solo (in the game's menu or the partner sheet) returns to it, and Join on the partner sheet sits you down again at the table your partner is at. The games' menus no longer start, join or share rooms: they point to the games screen. Tiles on the games screen always open a game solo. On `together.js` a seat belongs to a player, not a device: it keeps the player id of whoever sits in it, so a player who comes in on another device (phone to iPad, or the app on the home screen and the same site in Safari, which keep separate sign-ins) gets their own seat back, moves and all, without being asked, and the device they left goes back to solo; before, that device took the free seat, the one kept for the partner, or was asked which of the two players to be. Only when both seats belong to others (a device without your solo code has a player id of its own) do you choose which player to carry on as. Your partner's line follows you as you sit down at a table or leave one, not only when a page opens, and a page of yours left open elsewhere is never taken for your partner. A new partner code is a room from its first request: until October 2026 a room opened only if a game had made it (`createRoom`), so a pair linked with a new code got "No game with that code" on every match. `tests/rooms.mjs` checks the seating (`sitDown` in `rooms.js`, the joining transaction of every game on `together.js`: the first deals, the second sits across, your own seat on any device, a new table for each request and none without one, a third player changing nothing unless they chose a seat) and that a pair's room opens. A run with two players and a second device of one of them, separate browsers on one stand-in database, went through Tribute (the same table, the same side, the iPad taking its player's seat, Join, a new table for the next request), Hong (the asking device following from another game), Stow and Delta, and lapsed requests. You can watch your partner's solo game: the partner sheet offers Watch while they play solo. The game you have open is mirrored to the pair's room as you play (every key of its own: its run, its settings), at most every 1.5 seconds while you keep moving. Your partner opens the same game with ?watch: that page doesn't run the game itself (it holds it) but shows it in a frame, the same page with &frame, where every read of that game's keys returns your copy and every write goes nowhere, so their own solo state is never touched. When you move, a new frame loads behind the one on show and takes its place once drawn, at most every 1.2 seconds, so the view changes in place instead of reloading in front of them (the first version reloaded the whole page on every save, which in a game that saves every second meant a page forever reloading). The frames take no taps and connect to nothing; a bar says who you're watching, with Stop. It needs no code in a game, only that the game keeps what's on its screen in storage under its own prefixes (`PREFIXES` in `suite.js`: the game's id, but Slate's glyph:, its old name, and Blend's blend: and blend2:); Crates' run link and the suite's keys never go across. Where a game kept part of its screen only in memory, the frame drew a screen of its own: a scan of every game in October 2026 found Calibre's design, the chess rush's position, Parley's card and Pipes' board kept that way, and Blend's save under a prefix that wasn't copied. Each now keeps what it shows as it goes (see their sections), which also brings your own work back after a reload. Still not shown: Spot's targets, a Deck review under way, and a Harbour run (the plan shows, not the hours it has run). A frame never speaks (Parley says each card aloud, and every swap would say it again), and never ends or restarts a run because it's hidden. The swap was tested on Pipes with ten saves in five seconds: four swaps, the page never reloaded, the view ended on the latest save, and the watcher's own state stayed as it was.

What the database refuses (October 2026). Firebase refuses a whole write that has undefined anywhere in it (or NaN, or a key with . # $ / [ ]), and Tribute's every first move together failed so while the two-tab tests passed: their stand-in for the database (`tests/fake-sync.js`) quietly dropped undefined, and closed up lists where the database leaves gaps. It now refuses what Firebase refuses and keeps lists as Firebase does, and `tests/rtdb.js` does the same for the node tests (its rules are `memdb.js`'s, which an offline party's host keeps its database by), through which Tribute and Hong play whole matches move by move. `together.js` drops anything undefined from a state before saving it, with a warning in the console, so a slip of that kind costs a field, not every move. Joining is quicker on a slow connection (from Shanghai to the database in Belgium, each round trip is a large part of a second): a game reads what it needs of the room, its own branch, the request and the room's mark (`openBranch` in `rooms.js`), not the whole room, which holds every game's branch and what each of you has open mirrored for watching, and which it opened as a transaction, two round trips at least and run again for every write anywhere in the room meanwhile (each page saying where it is); the joining transaction runs with the branch already held by the page, one round trip instead of two; and the database's address, found once, is kept on the device (`suite:db`) rather than asked for on every connection. A two-page run on the strict stand-in went from Korbi's request on the games screen, through Pookie's banner ("Korbi wants to play Tribute together") and Play, to one table, with either of them arriving first: each named right on both screens, two whole hands of Tribute (tribute and its return included) and forty moves of Hong played, the computer players moved by one device only.

Joining fast, and on the same question from the first moment (October 2026). Korbi found joining a party or a duo match slow, often several seconds and never the same twice, and partners in Chart and Punt on different questions for the first ten seconds or so. From Asia each round trip to the database in Belgium is about a quarter of a second, and a page makes several before it's seated: the connection (its socket, Firebase's handshake, the sign-in's token), reading the room, sitting down. What made it slow and uneven was Firebase's fallback. It marks every socket it opens as failed until the server has answered twice, and a connection that starts while the mark is there begins on long polling (each message a plain request, seconds at a time from Asia), moving to a socket only once that's up; a page left before its socket had proved itself (the phone moving on to the game the party chose, a glance at another app, the camera to scan a code) left the mark for the next page. Now `sync.js` clears the mark as it connects (a network where sockets really fail costs one try before Firebase falls back by itself) and opens the connection at once, while the sign-in is read back, rather than when the first read asks for it; and a page opened to play with others (`?room=`, `?table=` or `?party=` in its address) starts connecting in its first moment, from `theme.js`, the first script every page runs, while the rest of the page loads. A page back from less than twenty seconds away (another app, the camera) keeps a connection that's still up, where it used to drop it and open a new one every time (several round trips, and the others seeing it away meanwhile); after longer, or offline, it reconnects as before, since Safari may have let the socket die while the page slept. Every game with a duo match now shows the rete, Joining your partner (Joining the table when a party sent you there: `joiningWords` in `rooms.js`), until the room's state is drawn, never your own game meanwhile, and goes to your own game only if the join doesn't come off: Chart, Punt, Quote, Spot, Delta, Slate and Crates drew your own game first, so each partner was on a question of their own until the room's arrived, and a tap meanwhile went to the solo game (Hong, Tribute, Stow, River and Gambit already held it back). Crates and Slate, which keep rooms of their own, now read only their part of the room to join, as the games on `together.js` do, with it held while joining so the transaction takes one round trip. A run of every duo game on a stand-in database answering each request after 700 ms showed the rete with no solo game under it, both partners on the same question once in, the name asked for over the rete, and your own game after a room that isn't there.

Sign-in without the pop-up (23 October 2026; Korbi: "Connecting to the database even to watch a partner game like dominos now takes ages. There's something fundamentally broken with the multiplayer engines"). Not measured: the container this was written in can't reach Firebase, so the cause is read from Firebase's code, not timed. `getAuth()` also sets up the pop-up sign-in, and on a phone (iOS, Safari, Android) it does so at once, before it will say who is signed in: a hidden frame from the project's `firebaseapp.com` and Google's `apis.google.com` script, both awaited, though this suite only ever signs in anonymously. From Asia those hosts are slow or unreachable, so every phone's connection, a join's, a watcher's and a table's, waited for them to fail, which is the "ages", while a desktop browser (no proactive set-up) didn't. `sync.js` now calls `initializeAuth` with the same storage in the same order (IndexedDB, then local, then session) and no pop-up resolver, so a device keeps the user it has and asks Google for nothing but the token. If the connection is still slow from Asia after this, what's left is the round trips (above) and `pull()` in `suite.js`, which reads a solo code's whole store, every game's saved state, on each page that opens (a stamp saying whether anything changed would let it skip that).

Joining says where it has got to (October 2026; Korbi: “add a progress bar and status descriptions so it's clear where things are at”). A join is several round trips to a server far away, and the rete alone never said which it was waiting for. Now a bar under its words fills as each stage begins, and the stage is written under the bar: the connection's (Connecting to the game server, Finding the game server, Signing in: `net.js`, `sync.js`; Reaching the host's phone in an offline party), then a table's (Finding the table, or the party; Taking your seat, passed by when the seat is yours already; Setting out the table: `table-ui.js`, told by `table.js`'s `onStep`) or a duo match's (Finding the game; Dealing a new game, for whoever arrives first; Taking your seat; Setting out the game: `together.js`), until the game draws the table and the rete goes. A page opened to join others says Joining the party, Joining the table or Joining your partner, over a bar, from its first moment, while its own files are still loading (`theme.js`, the first script every page runs, sets the words), and the page's own take over when it runs with the rete never going between (a wait put up then shows at once, not after its usual moment: it used to go as the page became ready and come back). The host's phone says how a party opens (Opening a party: Reserving a room for it) and how a game it starts goes out (Starting Bones: Setting up Bones for everyone, then Opening Bones); a phone the party sends elsewhere says where while the next page loads (Opening Bones, the host having started it; Following the party; Back to the party; Leaving the party), where it used to sit as it was, its taps going nowhere. A head-to-head says Joining your partner, as every duo match does, where it said Joining the match. Tried on the stand-in database slowed to a far server's pace, the game's own files slowed too: a guest scanning into a party (Finding the party, Taking your seat), the host starting Boast for two (both phones through the first words to Finding the table), and Chart, Hong and Pipes opened from a request, the rete up without a break from the first moment to the table; in all four themes, day and night, on a phone and a tablet both ways.

Three scores show on each tile: your solo best, your partner's (by initial), and your duo record: for a team game the pair's best result together (Punt's pot, Delta's fastest board, as a time), for a head-to-head one the wins each way ("Duo 2–1" on yours, "1–2" on theirs; a draw counts for neither). Each player's device reports a finished duo match from its own side (duo/GAME/MATCH/PLAYER: score, won, team or not), so a match counts once and both sides agree on the winner; games on `together.js` say what a match came to through its `result` option (Chart and Quote head to head; Delta, Stow, Order and Origin as a team; Punt and Manifest either, by the table's mode); Crates, Slate and Spot report from their own match code: Crates a board cleared together or not ("Duo 5/7", cleared of played), Slate a board solved together and its time (the fastest shows), Spot the pair's score in co-op and the winner in Versus. A game played both ways shows both on its tile, the wins and then the team's best ("Duo 2–1 · team 1,240"; `duo-records.js`, checked by `tests/duo-records.mjs`): until October 2026 a tile showed one or the other, and a game's first team match would have hidden every head-to-head. A team's best is in the game's own terms: a time for Slate and Delta, pieces for Stow (it showed as a time, 0:11 for eleven), and the pot for Punt (whose solo best is a return a question, so a pot of 1,240 showed as +124000%). `suite.js` holds the pair, the presence, the requests and the records; `rooms.js` and `together.js` the rooms themselves. A party of two plays a duo match in the party's room the same way (README, Party): its host writes the request, already said yes to, and both phones go; that match isn't the pair's, so it isn't recorded for them (`reportDuo` records only a match in the pair's own room). Tribute and Hong seat a party of three or four the same way: the request carries how many (`people`), the table keeps that many seats for people (`sitDown`'s `slots`, a function of the table), the first two across from each other and the others between them (`seatOfSlot` in `rooms.js`), and the computer players move on the device of the first to sit down who's here (`runsElsewhere`); `tests/rooms.mjs` seats four, turns a fifth away, and keeps a pair's table at two. A table of more than two (October 2026): `together.js` gives a game `others()`, everyone else seated with whether they're here, and `slot`, this device's seat; seats beyond the pair's cobalt and copper take verdigris, plum, ochre and olive (`--p2`…`--p5` in style.css); and `standings.js` draws a table's standings the same way in every game (each player's rank, a dot in their colour, what they did this round under the name, the round's change and the total, in duel.css's rows; `tests/standings.mjs`). A phone that sleeps is away. Each page holds a session in the room's here while its connection is up, and the database drops the list once the last one goes, which `enterRoom` read as online until October 2026, so a head-to-head's lobby could wait for the Ready of a phone that had gone to sleep; the seat's own mark (`presence`, which `together.js` keeps) now counts too.
