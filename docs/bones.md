# Dominoes
Dominoes (October 2026), for two to four, against the computer or with friends each on their own phone (Invite players: seats.js runs it, the computer taking the seats nobody has). Three games of the double-six set (28 tiles, every pair of numbers from blank to six once), as pagat.com gives them, chosen in the menu. A tile is laid against an end of the line with the same number, a double across it; whoever can't lay one knocks.

**Partnership**, the block game of Latin America and the Caribbean: four, partners across the table, seven tiles each, play passing to the right. The first hand is led with the double six by whoever holds it; after that the lead passes on and any tile may lead. A hand ends when someone lays their last tile, and their side scores the pips left in the other side's hands, or when nobody can lay one (all four knock in a row), and the side holding fewer pips scores the other side's; level, no score. To 100, 150 or 200; the menu can count every hand's pips instead (as in Puerto Rico).

**Gaple**, Indonesia's: four, each for themselves, seven each. The first hand opens with the double blank; after it the last hand's winner leads a double, or the next player round who has one. Whoever goes out, or holds the fewest pips when nobody can lay a tile, wins the hand (a tie to the holder of the lowest tile), and every player pays every other the difference between their counts, as chips: at 12, 5, 11 and 7, the 5 collects 15. After 4, 8 or 12 deals the most chips win. In the menu: lay a tile face down when stuck, dead and counting against you, instead of passing; and a lone double blank counting 25.

**Fives and Threes**, the English pub game: two to four, four as two partnerships; five tiles each, seven for two (or the championship's nine for two and six for four), the rest out of play, never drawn. The first lead goes by lot, then passes to the left; any tile leads and play goes to the left. Every tile scores what the two ends then add up to, a double at an end counting both halves and the first tile all its pips: a point for every five and every three in it (5 or 3 make 1, 6 and 10 make 2, 9 makes 3, 12 makes 4, 15 makes 8, 18 makes 6, 20 makes 4). Going out scores 1; in partnerships play goes on until both of a side are out, and that side gets the point. Points that would take a side past the target don't count, and making it exactly (31, 61 or 121) wins at once.

The line is laid out as dominoes snake across a table (`bones-layout.js`): the first tile in the middle, each arm along the middle row toward its edge, then turning under its last tile's end (the right arm down, the left up) and running back along the next row; a double lies across the line, except in a turn. The row's length is the shortest that leaves each arm room for a long hand on the board's shape, so the tiles are as large as they can be from the first tile (never more than 2.4rem across) and don't shrink as the line grows; an arm that runs past its room widens the view. The others sit along the top in their places round the table (the next to play on your right in Partnership and Gaple, on your left in Fives and Threes), each with their tiles face down, their score in the individual games, and a bubble for what they just did (a knock, the points they made, out). Your tiles are along the foot, those that fit lifted on your turn: a tap lays one. Where a tile would go on either end, both places show on the line as dashed outlines, to tap (or the two buttons, by the number each end shows); where both ends are alike it goes on the shorter arm. The last tile laid is ringed, and the line under your tiles says what was last done. A hand's end shows over the line, with every hand's tiles left and their pips (in Fives and Threes, what each made that hand).

The computer players (`bones-bot.js`) all play a rule of thumb: in the block games shed the heaviest tiles and the doubles (which fit only their own number) early and keep tiles that fit the ends left; in Fives and Threes score what the ends allow, the most first. Medium and Hard look further: they deal the tiles they can't see among the others as they could lie (each hand's size, and nothing a player has knocked on: a knock on a five and a two means no five or two in that hand, nor in what it laid face down; the tiles out of play anywhere), play every move they may to the end of the hand in each deal by the rule of thumb, and take the move that did best for their side on average: the hand's points won or lost, the chips, or the points scored either way (nothing past the target, a point for going out). Medium deals 12 times, Hard 24, a few milliseconds a move. Between the computer's own players, Hard partners beat Easy ones about three games in four, Medium about two in three.

The course (Learn, in the header, as Dou Dizhu's and Liar's dice's; October 2026): dominoes taught from nothing in 18 lessons in two chapters, on the course sheet the games off a board share (`course.js`; README, Dou Dizhu) with a stage of its own (`bones-course.js`): examples in rows of tiles, the others round the table (their tiles face down, what they've knocked on, your partner marked; in Fives and Threes the score), the line on its felt as the table lays it (a long one laid out again to the board's shape), your tiles along the foot, larger where the stage has room. The game: the 28 tiles (every number on seven), laying a tile against an end, doubles across the line, knocking when nothing fits and what a knock tells the table, then the three games: Partnership (going out, the blocked hand or tranque, the side with fewer pips scoring the other side's), Gaple (each paying every other the difference) and Fives and Threes (the ends counted, a double at an end both halves, 61 exactly); then a first game against Easy. Playing well: counting the numbers (how many of a number are out, and who can't hold them), making the next player knock (pasar: both ends on numbers they've knocked on), doubles that hang (ahorcado: a double whose number is gone can never be laid), owning a number (la data: both ends on the number you hold most of), closing the hand when your side holds fewer (el tranque, with the pips counted), keeping a tile that fits, keeping it light in Gaple (a pip left in your hand costs three chips), looking a play ahead in Fives and Threes (a point now that hands the next player four), and 61 exactly; then a game against Hard. A step is words over tiles, a hand to find the fits in (tap every tile that fits an end: a wrong one is dimmed, with why; Hint marks the rest), a tile to lay (tap it, and where it would go on either end tap the place on the line, then Lay; once right, the line shows it laid and the hand loses it, and a wrong one is answered with what it leaves: "That leaves a 4 and a 5: Rosa may hold fives"), a tile that scores most in Fives and Threes (the ends' total shown; what each play would make isn't, until it's laid), or a choice. Lines are written left to right as they lie, the first tile laid in brackets ("1-6 [6-3] 3-5"), and checked as they're read: a tile that doesn't touch its neighbour is refused. Every position in Playing well comes from a game the computer players played, and its tile to lay is the one that does best when the tiles you can't see are dealt every way they could lie and each deal is played to the end of the hand exactly (`bones-solve.js`, below), by a clear margin over every other. Where you are is kept (`bones:course`). `tests/bones-course.mjs` checks every line, that no step shows a tile twice, that every hand to search holds as many fits as its words say, that of every play a hand allows only the meant ones are judged right (in Fives and Threes, exactly those that score most, and as much as the words say), every total the words quote, the scores the lessons work out against the rules' own scoring (a side going out, the blocked hand with all seven fours down, Gaple's chips); weighs every Playing well position again, 300 deals each, the lesson's tile best by at least half the rush's margin and three standard errors of the difference clear of every other play, every position a whole deal (the line, the hands and the tiles out of play make 28) whose knocks some deal honours; checks the figures the lessons quote (the fours out, the pips at the block, Gaple's 22 and 27, what Alf could make after each play, 61 exactly); and taps every exercise through the stage to its end on a page of plain objects. The Rules sheet opens from the course's map and the menu.

The solver (`bones-solve.js`) plays a hand's rest with every tile seen (double dummy), each player doing what's best for their side: Partnership and two-sided Fives and Threes as one number, searched with alpha-beta (a search that skips the moves that can't change the result) and a table of positions already solved; three or four each for themselves (Gaple, Fives and Threes for three) by each maximising their own, every position solved once. Worth is what the computer players count (the hand's points either way, the chips, the points scored to the end of the hand, the target made exactly counted as 100). A position is weighed as its player sees it: the tiles they can't see dealt as the computer players deal them (`bones-bot.js`: each hand's size, nothing a player has knocked on), the same deals for every play, each solved. Hands of four tiles solve in a few milliseconds a deal; it's for the course's checks and the rush's puzzles, not a turn at the table.

Puzzle rush (Menu, October 2026): the best tile in positions from the computer players' own games, as many as you can find, with every game's rush settings (README, Puzzle rush: Random or Today's; 3 min, 5 min or Survival; three misses end the run; until the settings were made one, five minutes and no choice). It has a sheet of its own over the game, laid out as the course's, the table drawn by the course's stage: the others with their tiles and their knocks (and the score, in Fives and Threes), the line, your tiles; tap a tile, its place if it could go on either end, and Lay. The verdict comes at once: Best (or As good), or Not the best, with what the best and yours were worth on average over the puzzle's deals (points a hand in Partnership, chips in Gaple, points either way in Fives and Threes, a game won counted as 100), the best laid on the line, and what it does in a sentence: the knock it forces ("It leaves the ends 1 and 5, numbers Rosa and Marisol have knocked on: both must knock again"), the hand it closes, the end only you can play on, the number you hold most of, the double while its number is about, the heavy tile, the most points now or fewer now and less for the next player, 61 exactly. The clock stands while you read it, while the sheet is closed and while the app is in the background; a run starts easy and rises, four puzzles at each rating, those seen lately last. The bank (`bones-puzzles.js`, 380 puzzles: 45 at each rating from 1 to 8 and 20 at 9; Partnership 123, Gaple 115, Fives and Threes for two, three and four 142) is found by `tools/bones-puzzles.mjs <first deal> <deals> <out.jsonl>`, then `merge`: hands the computer players (Medium) play among themselves, every decision late in a hand with two plays or more (every hand at five tiles or fewer, four in Gaple) weighed by the solver in batches of 40 deals until one play beats every other by a clear margin (4 points in Partnership, 6 chips in Gaple, 1.5 points in Fives and Threes), four standard errors clear, at most 400 deals; 3,118 puzzles from 2,400 deals in seven minutes on two cores, the bank taking the games in turn at each rating and lines of 18 tiles at most. A puzzle is a position written as the course writes its steps (`bones-course.js`: `lineText`, `playName`, `motifsOf`), so the stage draws it and the rules judge it; it's rated by whether the computer's rule of thumb and Medium would have found it, how close the call, how many plays and tiles. The sheet is `rush-sheet.js` (shared with Schafkopf's rush: the run, its order, the clock, the intro and the score, any game's course stage drawing the position), `bones-rush-ui.js` Dominoes' part of it (its stage, its bank, its verdict), `bones-rush.js` the verdict and the words without a page. `tests/bones-rush.mjs` checks that every puzzle is a position a deal could reach (the line, the hands and the tiles out of play make 28, some deal honours every knock) with a choice, that the plays weighed are exactly the hand's, the best the one worth most and clear of the rest by the margin, its motifs found again, the ratings and games stocked, a sample weighed again in deals of its own with the same best, a run's rising order, and the verdicts and their words. The run is kept as it goes (`bones:rush`, so it waits where it was), your bests by clock in `bones:rushBest` and today's runs in `bones:rushDaily`, as the sheet keeps them for every game on it (README, Puzzle rush). It's solo only.

`tests/bones.mjs` checks the set and the count of the ends (a double led, a double left at an end, the first tile); Partnership (the double six leading and nothing else, a tile that doesn't fit refused, a pass only when nothing fits and what it was on remembered, going out and a block scored both ways, level with no score, the next hand led by the next player, the target); Gaple (the double blank opening, pagat's example of the chips, the winner leading a double next, the lone double blank, a tile face down counting); Fives and Threes (the deals, the tiles out of play, each play scored, nothing past the target, exactly it winning at once, the point for going out, partners playing on until both are out); the state through the database at every move; the layout of sixty random lines at five row lengths (no tile on another, each touching the one before, each with its own numbers); and whole games of every game at every level, every move legal, with Hard partners beating Easy ones.

The 3D view (Menu, View; 3 November 2026, Korbi: "add an aesthetic 3d mode whenever you have time"): the table tipped away from you by CSS alone (`bones-3d.css`), the flat table the default, the choice the device's own (`suite:bones-view`).
