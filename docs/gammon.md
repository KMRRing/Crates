# Gammon
Backgammon (October 2026), against the computer or at a table with a friend each on their own phone (Invite players: seats.js runs it). A match to 1, 3, 5 or 7 points, with the doubling cube if you like; a gammon counts two, a backgammon three, times the cube.

The board is drawn as the one playing it sees it: your home board at the bottom right, your checkers ivory, the other side's in ink, the points in terracotta and navy on parchment in a walnut frame, the borne-off checkers as slabs in the tray on the right, the dice on your side of the bar on your turn and theirs on theirs. A tap on one of your checkers moves it if it can go only one way; otherwise the points it may go to are ringed, and a tap on one moves it there (the tray, to bear off). Only moves that are part of a legal play are offered: the most dice that can be used must be, and when only one of two can be, the larger where it can (gammon-board.js works out every play of a roll, each order of the same moves kept, so a play can be made a move at a time in any order). Undo takes moves back; Done sends the play. Hint lays out the computer's play on Hard, for Done or Undo. A turn with no move is shown and passed. The other side's play is shown a move at a time, its points faded where they moved from. Before rolling, a side that may double gets Double or Roll; a double is taken (the cube to the taker, at twice) or dropped (the game to the doubler at the cube's value). The game after someone first comes within a point of the match, the Crawford game, has no cube; nor does a double that couldn't matter (the doubler already winning the match on the cube as it is).

The computer (Dimitri) judges a position by hand-made rules, not a trained network: in a race, the pip counts, the side to roll worth about four pips; in contact, the pips and what a player weighs: points made (the home board's 4 and 5 most, the bar point, an anchor on the other side's 5 or 4 point), the longest prime and the checkers trapped behind it, every blot with the shots at it counted over all 36 rolls and what a hit costs there (more far from home and against a strong board), checkers on the bar, stacks. Easy picks with a wobble; Medium plays the best by that judgement; Hard looks a roll further for its four best plays, the other side's best answer to each of the 21 rolls, in about a tenth of a second. It doubles at a seventy per cent chance (not past ninety, where it plays on for a gammon) and takes at a quarter.

The course (Learn, in the header; October 2026): backgammon taught from nothing, on the course sheet the games share (`course.js`; README, Landlord) with a board of its own: the table's, drawn by `gammon-draw.js` (moved out of the page so both draw it) with each point numbered by its tip, as large as the stage allows. A play is made as at the table: a tap on a checker moves it if it can go only one way, else the points it may go to are ringed; Undo takes a move back; Play sends it. Ten lessons: board and checkers (fifteen each, the pip count, 167 at the start), a move (3-1 as 8/5 6/5, 6-6 as 24/18(2) 13/7(2)), hitting a blot and where it goes, entering from the bar (and a closed board), bearing off (a bigger die from the highest point), using both dice (the larger if only one), gammon and backgammon, the cube (take, drop), a match and the Crawford game; then a first match against Easy. Positions are written side by side, each in its own numbering ("6:5 8:3 13:5 24:2 bar:1 off:3"), plays as players write them ("24/18(2)", "bar/22", "13/11*", "6/off"). Every play a lesson asks for is GNU Backgammon's own first choice (asked offline, its answer kept: gnubg is GPL and isn't shipped), the hit lesson's 13/11* 13/9 among them, every hit accepted there. `tests/gammon-course.mjs` checks every position (fifteen a side, no point held by both), that every play asked for or accepted can be made with its roll and is judged right, every wrong play with words judged wrong with them, and of every play the roll allows only the meant ones right; the figures quoted (the pips, 167, the gammon, the closed board); and taps every play through the stage on a page of plain objects. The Rules sheet opens from the course's map and the menu.

Playing well, the course's second chapter: the opening rolls (four that make a point at once, 3-1 the 5 point, 4-2 the 4, 6-1 the bar, 5-3 the 3, best first, and 6-5's run to safety; 3-1, 6-1 and 6-5 to play), pointing on a blot (13/7* 8/7 on a checker left on your bar point, 8/5* 6/5 on one split to your 5 point), an anchor (4-4 after they ran one back checker: the golden point, 24/20(2) 13/9(2)), a prime (8/4 6/4 for five in a row in front of two trapped checkers), the race and the cube (the 8-9-12 rule: three races on roll, 4, 8 and 19 pips ahead of 87, no double, double and take, double and pass; the pips shown), bearing off safely against an anchor (5/off 6/2, not 5/off 4/off and a blot in front of it); then a match against Hard. Every answer is GNU Backgammon's, money play at its 2-ply: a play its first choice, and clear of the rest, and a cube question its verdict. Its equities are kept with each step, best first, and quoted in the words ("+0.22 of a game; the next best, 13/10 24/23, −0.01"). `tools/gnubg.mjs` asks gnubg (the @nodots-llc/gnubg-hints add-on: now money play, where before it asked as if at 0-0 in a match to 7, which changed none of the first lesson's answers), and its cube verdicts read the package's take hint the way that agrees with the 8-9-12 rule (asked the other way it called takes 37% behind). `tools/gammon-check.mjs` asks again about every play and cube question the course has, and fails where gnubg's choice, its verdict or an equity has moved. `tests/gammon-course.mjs` checks, without gnubg, that the plays kept for each step can be made, each to its own position, best first; that the first is the answer, every play accepted within 0.02 of it and the rest 0.03 below; that each cube answer is the verdict kept, and that the verdict follows from its equities; and the race's leads as the words give them.

Puzzle rush (the menu; October 2026): as Bones' and Schafkopf's, on the same sheet (`rush-sheet.js`), with every game's rush settings (README, Puzzle rush), with 400 positions (`gammon-puzzles.js`, 40 at each rating) from the computer players' own games. `tools/gammon-puzzles.mjs` plays Medium against Medium and now and then, on a roll with four plays or more, asks GNU Backgammon about every play the roll allows (money play, 2-ply): a position is kept when its best play is ahead of the next by 0.06 of a game or more, pure races more rarely than positions with contact. Every play is kept with gnubg's equity (only its numbers: gnubg is GPL), so any play you make has its value. A puzzle is drawn on the course's board, the play made as at the table (tap a checker, its point if it may go more than one way, Undo, Play); the verdict gives gnubg's equity for the best and for yours (or the next best), and how much of a game yours gave away, and what the best play does: comes in from the bar, hits (or hits two), makes a point in your board or your bar point, an anchor in theirs, a longer prime, brings a back checker out, bears off, or leaves nothing to hit where the next best leaves a blot. Ratings are by rank: Medium and Hard (a roll further) missing it, a closer call, more plays to choose from make one harder. Plays are written as players write them, the course's too: a checker moved on is written from where it started to where it ended ("24/13", "bar/15"), with a star at a point it hit on the way. `tests/gammon-rush.mjs` checks every puzzle: a board the game allows and a roll, every play the roll allows listed once (by the board it leaves) and best first, the best clear of the next, those as good within half the margin, what the best does found again, the best tapped on the board named back as the bank names it; and every rating stocked, every kind of play among the bests, a run's order and the verdicts' words.

`tests/gammon.mjs` checks the board (the start, pips, a made point blocking, a blot hit, entering from the bar first, bearing off exactly and from the highest point with a bigger die, never with a checker outside), the plays of a roll (both dice, the larger when only one, four of a double, each position once), single, gammon and backgammon, a match on seats.js (the opening roll, an illegal play refused, a play made, the cube offered, taken and dropped, the Crawford game, a turn with no move passed), the state through the database, and the computer playing whole games legally, Hard beating Easy.
