# Baccarat
Punto banco as Macau deals it, the Venetian's tables in mind (October 2026): `baccarat.html`, `baccarat.js` and `baccarat.css`, its rules in `baccarat-engine.js`, the scoreboard drawn by `baccarat-roads.js`, the squeeze by `baccarat-squeeze.js`, the course in `baccarat-course.js` on the stage `baccarat-stage.js` (which also draws the betting layout, the table's and the course's alike), and the rules on one page in `baccarat-rules.js`.

The game, by the gaming regulator's rules for baccarat (the DICJ's: Despacho 55/2004 and its amendments): eight decks in a shoe (the rules allow six to twelve), shuffled from a seed; the first card turned and as many burned face down as it counts, one of the three ways the rules let a casino burn (the others: as many cards as there are decks, or a set number), with a ten or a court card burning ten here; a cut card fourteen cards from the back (the rules say at least about twelve), after which one more hand is dealt and the shoe is changed (the rules allow that hand or one more). Each hand two cards to the Player (閒) and two to the Banker (莊), Player first, a third card by the tableau (the Player draws on 0–5; the Banker on 0–2 always, on 3–6 by the Player's third card, on 7 never; a natural, 例牌, 8 or 9 on either side, ends it), the total's last digit nearer nine winning, equal totals a tie. After a tie a bet on Player or Banker stays on the table for the next hand, to be left, taken back or moved (the rules' Article 12), and the page says so. New games sit at a no-commission table (免佣), the usual kind on Macau's main floors now, Cotai's above all (Ryan Ho in Inside Asian Gaming, March 2025; Maven of Macau, July 2026); the menu switches to commission (佣金), the high-limit rooms' kind.

The bets: Player 1 to 1; Banker 0.95 to 1 at a commission table, or 1 to 1 at a no-commission one but half when it wins with six; Tie 8 to 1; and the side bets Sands China's tables carry: Player pair and Banker pair (a hand's first two cards the same rank) 11 to 1; Pairs+ (a pair in either hand 3 to 1, one in each 30 to 1, the same pair in each 300 to 1); Lucky 6 (幸運六點, the Banker winning with six: 12 to 1 on two cards, 20 to 1 on three), Small 6 (two cards only, 22 to 1) and Big 6 (three only, 50 to 1); Lucky 7 (the Player winning with seven: 6 to 1 on two cards, 15 to 1 on three), Small 7 (15 to 1) and Big 7 (30 to 1); Super Lucky 7 (the Player's seven beating the Banker's six: 30 to 1, 40 to 1 with one third card between them, 100 to 1 with two); and Monkey no Monkey (the first four cards: even money with no court card among them, 50 to 1 if all four are). What the house keeps of each, with eight decks: Banker 1.06% (1.46% without commission), Player 1.24%, Tie 14.36%, a pair 10.36%, Small 7 13.05%, Small 6 14.33%, Super Lucky 7 14.83%, Big 6 15.25%, Big 7 15.41%, Monkey 16.50%, Lucky 6 16.68%, Pairs+ 17.14%, Lucky 7 18.30% (`EDGES` in the engine; the figures Wizard of Odds and Ryan Ho publish, which the test works out again exactly). GGRAsia's site checks found Small and Big 6 on every Venetian table in October 2024, and Pairs+ (in place of the two pairs on many tables) and Monkey no Monkey at Sands China's casinos from April 2026. The first version had a Super 6 at 12 to 1, a bet found in no source on Macau's tables; a game saved by it loses any Super 6 bet on the table (`upgrade`). Bets go down in HK$100s, Player or Banker never both, from HK$500 to HK$100,000 on either, up to HK$10,000 on each side bet; you start with HK$50,000.

The page: the strip has what you hold, the table's kind and limits, and the shoe and hand. Under it the scoreboard, as the screen over a Macau table shows it: the big road across the top (a column for each run, a ring for each win, red for the Banker and blue for the Player, a tie a green slash across the ring before it with how many, a pair a dot, a run past six rows turning along the foot as the dragon's tail), the bead plate below on the left (every hand in order, 莊, 閒 or 和 in its colour), and beside it the three derived roads at half size: the big eye boy's rings, the small road's dots and the cockroach road's slashes. Under the screen the shoe's counts, and the two ask buttons (莊問, 閒問) with the mark each derived road would make if the next hand went that way; tap one and the next marks blink on the screen where they'd go, as the table's own buttons show them. The felt: the Player's hand and the Banker's, each with its total in its colour, the third card laid sideways as the dealer lays it; the dealer's call (莊贏, 閒贏, 和) with the pairs and any side bet of yours that came in, and what the hand paid you; and the layout, the side bets above (the Tie first, three to a row) and Player and Banker below, nearest you, as a seat's boxes lie on the felt. Which side bets it carries is the menu's Side bets, so it can match the table in front of you: up to five spots beside the Tie (the pairs and the small and big six or seven come two at a time), one chosen past that pushing out the earliest; by default the Venetian's main floor of 2026, Pairs+, Lucky 6, Small and Big 6 and Monkey no Monkey. Each spot is lettered in Chinese only where the rules or the floor letter it so (閒, 莊, 和, 閒對, 莊對, 幸運6): the Chinese the Venetian prints for the newer bets wasn't found, so they're in English rather than in a guess. The chips on a spot stay there while the hand is dealt (the first version cleared them as Deal was pressed), and after it each spot shows what it won or lost, on a side bet before what it pays. The rail: the chips (HK$100 to HK$10,000), Clear, Rebet (the last hand's bets) and Deal. Deal deals as the dealer does: Player, Banker, Player, Banker, the Player's cards turned then the Banker's, the third cards if the tableau calls for them. On a short screen (under 760 points high, as a phone in Safari) the side bets are tighter, Player and Banker each on one line, the chips and buttons a little smaller and the scoreboard's squares too, so that two rows of side bets leave the cards their room. With Squeeze on (Menu), the cards of the side you're on are yours to squeeze (咪牌), as the biggest bet on a side squeezes them on the floor: each comes up large over the felt, face down, drawn with its pips laid out as a real card's (`baccarat-squeeze.js`); drag in from any edge and the back peels away from that edge as far as your finger goes, the bent strip showing; the two corners with the index stay down, as a squeezer's thumbs keep them, or the first peel would give the card away; let go and it lies flat again, unless it's mostly peeled, when it turns over; Turn it turns it at once. A long side peeled first shows whether there's a column of pips there at all, and how many. Menu: a new shoe, Top up, the course, Baccarat on one page, the table's kind, the side bets, Squeeze. The game is kept as it stands (`baccarat:run`: the shoe, where it's got to, the roads, your bankroll and the bets on the table), the chip, the side bets and Squeeze in `baccarat:opts`, the course in `baccarat:course`. Held sideways the felt takes the left with all the height, and the strip, the scoreboard, the chips and the buttons stand beside it.

Baccarat on one page (the menu, and the course's map): the game, the chart, every bet with what it pays and keeps, the shoe and the squeeze, the screen, the house's rules, and the words of the floor with their Jyutping.

The roads, as `baccarat-engine.js` works them out: the bead plate in order, six to a column; the big road's columns (ties marked on the win before them, the shoe's first ties on its first win), laid out six high with a run that would leave the grid, or meet a mark already there, turning right along its row; each derived road (k = 1, 2, 3 columns back) marks each ring of the big road from where it starts (the second ring of column k, or the top of column k + 1): a ring starting a column red if the two columns before it (the last and the one k further back) are the same length, blue if not; a ring further down red if the column k back reaches its row, blue if it stopped just short of it, red if it stopped two or more short; then laid out in columns by colour, as the big road is. The ask road appends a Banker or a Player win and reports each road's new mark; a road's two answers are always opposite colours.

The course (Learn, in the header; the course sheet the card games share, `course.js`): everything the floor shows and says, from nothing, in four chapters. The game: two hands and nobody's choice, counting a hand (公, the court cards, nothing), naturals (例牌), the Player's third card (補牌), the Banker's (the chart, as a table of D and S, with questions on single cells of it). The bets: Player, Banker and the Tie (and what a tie does to your bet), commission or none, the pairs and Pairs+, the sixes, the sevens, Monkey no Monkey, the limits sign (限紅, with this year's minimums at Sands China's Cotai casinos from GGRAsia's site checks), and what the house keeps on each. The scoreboard: the bead plate, the big road, the dragon's tail, the big eye boy (worked through a big road mark by mark), the small road and the cockroach road, the ask buttons, a whole screen read, and the patterns the floor names (長龍, 單跳, 雙跳) and why they don't move the odds. On the floor: the shoe (牌靴; the cut card, 白咭; the burn, 銷牌, the burned cards drawn; the last hand; the smart tables Sands China has had on every mass-floor baccarat table since January 2025, whose shoe reads the cards and fills the screen); the squeeze (who holds the cards and what they may do, by the rules' Article 16; the pips down a long side, 二邊, 三邊, 四邊, and those in the middle, in stills drawn by the squeeze itself; what to hope for on a Player's third card; the table's shouts, 開, 吹, 頂); money and chips (Hong Kong dollars throughout; the cage); what you'll hear (買定離手, 例牌, 補牌, 莊贏, 閒贏, 和局, 開); and the house's rules (21 and over since 2012, no cameras on the floor and no phone at the table, smoking only in the lounges since 2019 and e-cigarettes not at all, who touches the cards and the chips, dress). Chinese terms come with Cantonese Jyutping the first time. The first version called a natural 天牌; the DICJ's rules say 例牌. Where the course speaks of Macau it rests on the DICJ's rules, GGRAsia's site checks (2023 to 2026), Inside Asian Gaming, Desmond Lam's 2007 study of Macau's baccarat players (the squeeze's words), Macau's Law 10/2012 and its smoking laws. Not found for the Venetian, and so not claimed: its table maximums, its chips' colours, whether its tables take cash and in which currency, its burn and last-hand rules, its screen's exact layout, whether its dealers call 買定離手, its tipping, and the Chinese on its layouts for the newer side bets.
