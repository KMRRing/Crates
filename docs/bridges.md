# Bridges
Join the islands with bridges until every island has as many bridges as its number and all the islands are one network (Hashiwokakero, "build bridges"; 31 October 2026, Korbi's list of genres to add). A bridge runs straight to the next island in its row or column, one or two between a pair, never across another bridge or over an island. Every puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning, never by a guess. Tap the dotted line between two islands to build a bridge, again to make it double, again to clear it; hold a link, or right-click it, to clear it at once. A bridge can't be built across another (the line says so). A finger means the nearest link within a third of a cell that isn't on an island; where a link no bridge could go on (another crosses it) meets one that can be built, the tap goes to the one that can. The puzzle is solved the moment exactly its answer is built. A rule you break shows at once and stays shown for as long as it is broken, from the rules alone and never from the answer: an island with more bridges than its number or bound (a 4+ or a ≤3 included) is filled oxblood with its numeral oxblood and ringed twice, and the bridges on it turn oxblood too; a group that has all its bridges while other islands are left out is ringed in dashes, its bridges oxblood. The line under the board says which in words, naming the island and how many too many ("The ≤3 has 4 bridges, two too many."; several are counted by their number, "Too many bridges on 4 islands: three 1s and the ≤3.", and a shut-off group is said once nothing is over), after any tap, undo or reload and not only after the tap that broke it (`whatBreaks` in `bridges-engine.js`, tested in `tests/bridges.mjs`); a bridge refused for crossing another is said first, once. The 3D view's plates do the same. An island turns blue once it has its number. Undo and Redo step through your marks. A hint (30 seconds) first clears a bridge that isn't in the answer, and otherwise makes the next step a person would take and says why ("the other links of this 4 can't carry enough, so this link has at least 1 bridge"), the link it built in gilt and the island that says so ringed. There is no mark for "no bridge here": an empty link says it, and a hint that only rules a link out is folded into the step that follows from it.

Wrong islands were hard to read, 1 November 2026 (Korbi: "highlight it more when the 1+ and ≤5 are wrong and stuff like that, not always super clear from the highlights what's off"). Three causes, all in what the board showed: the oxblood fill of an island with too many bridges lost to the warm fill of a range island (4+, ≤3) and its ring to a second realm's copper, because they were written later in the stylesheet at the same weight, so a wrong ≤5 showed only a slightly thicker ring; the ring itself was a single line, the same weight as an island that has its number; and the line under the board said only "An island has more bridges than its number", for the tapped link's islands, and went at the next tap. Now the wrong-state rules come after the twists' (and a hint's gilt ring still outranks them), the island has a second ring and oxblood numeral and bridges, and the line is worked out from the marks each time. Checked by screenshot in all three themes, day and night, at 390 wide, and in the 3D view.

Sizes 7×7, 9×9 and 11×11 (the menu); three difficulties (the header's dropdown, called Difficulty since November 2026 to leave "Level" for the ladder below, mirrored in the menu); three twists to the rules (below). Nothing comes from the knowledge base: Bridges is numbers, and has no content to choose.

Making a puzzle (`bridges-engine.js`). Unlike Slitherlink there is no loop to pare numbers from: the islands are the clues. A board is grown from one island, each new island placed two to five cells from an earlier one over water that nothing else uses, never beside another island, joined to it by one or two bridges (about a third double); then more bridges between islands side by side wherever water allows. Each island's number is its bridges. Such a board often has more than one answer, so it is kept only when a solver of the level asked for can finish it from the numbers alone: a solver that finishes by forced steps has found the only answer there is, which is the whole of the uniqueness argument, with no search in the generator. In 200 layouts at 9×9 roughly three in four are not solvable by reasoning at all (they have many answers), about one in ten is Easy, one in twenty to one in seven Medium, and a few in a hundred need a trial; the generator tries up to 600 layouts at about a millisecond each. `tests/bridges.mjs` checks the solver's rules against a plain search of every way to build bridges on 80 small boards (it counts exactly as many answers as that does, 30 or more of them with several), and 18 puzzles across every size and level against a counting search that guesses; 270 more were checked outside the suite the same way (none had two answers or none, every one landed on its level).

Difficulty is the hardest step a solver needs that reasons as a person does, never how many islands there are. Easy: each island's number against its links (a link has at least what the island's other links can't carry, and at most what they leave; so an island whose links can only just hold its number takes every bridge they can, and a 1 with one way out takes it) and that no bridge may cross another. Medium: that the islands must all end up joined (a group that has all its bridges while other islands are left out is shut off, so no bridge may complete it: two 1s can't join each other; a link no bridge could join across two parts of the board must be there). Hard: a trial as well, a link supposed to have at least or at most so many bridges and the rules followed until one breaks; if they do, the opposite holds. A puzzle's level is the lowest that solves it, and one no trial of one link finishes is never dealt. Puzzles are made in a worker (`bridges-worker.js`) with a spare of each kind lately played kept, so a new puzzle is instant; measured in Node an 11×11 takes about a tenth of a second at worst (a phone is slower), so the rete hardly shows. The same seed makes the same puzzle on every device.

Score: your time plus 30 seconds a hint, lower better. Bests by size and the level a puzzle came to, in `bridges:times` (not `:best`). Today's puzzle is a 9×9 at Medium made from the date (`today()`), the same for everyone; its first solve is today's time on the games screen (`noteDayTime`, `bridges:today`), counted the moment the bridges are the answer, and the tile opens today's while it's still to play, asking before leaving a puzzle you've started. A link (Copy a link to this puzzle) carries the seed, size and level. The run in play is `bridges:run` (the puzzle and its answer, your bridges, undo and redo, the clock, hints, the hint showing), saved at every change and every five seconds, so a reload or a partner watching sees exactly what's on the screen; `bridges:prefs` is the size and level, `bridges:spares` the spare puzzles. The clock stands while the menu, the rules or the games screen is open, and while the page is away. Random mode counts a solve as a round (`roundOver`). Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height, the time beside it at the top, the line and the tools low.

Not done: the head-to-head and a split board on `duel.js`, the computer rival, boards past 11×11, and a mark for "no bridge here".

## Levels and twists

Added in November 2026, after Korbi's note that Bridges was good and wanted levels like Topple's, which is the ladder as Survey has it (see its Levels): the menu's Play choice is Levels or Free play (`bridges:track` keeps which; someone with a puzzle in hand from before carries on with it, anyone else begins on the ladder), a list of every level as its chart in miniature (`bridges-mini.js`: the islands, the bridges of the answer, the twists' marks), three to a row under their chapter with number, stars, name and size. Beating a level opens the next, any open level can be played at any time and is kept as it was left (`bridges:lruns`, one puzzle per level), and the header shows "Level N" in place of the difficulty dropdown (a level's size and difficulty are its own) with the par and the level's name in the line above the board. On a narrow phone the Rules button gives way in a level so the header still fits; Rules stay in the menu. The level being played and the stars won are `bridges:camp`; a link to a level is `#lv=LEVEL&s=SEED`, and a free puzzle's link gains `&t=reefs` (or `ranges`, `realms`, `hills`, `lanes`, joined by `+`) when it has a twist. Free play has the twists as a Twist choice in the menu, any of them with any size and difficulty; its bests are kept by size, difficulty and twist in `bridges:times`.

Three twists, each of which keeps the rule that a puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning (the three spins were my choice; I could not recover the proposal they came from, so Korbi's thumb sets which stay):

- **Reefs**: rock in the water, drawn as small grey crags. No bridge goes over one, so two islands are linked only where nothing, rock included, lies between them. They make a puzzle easier to start and harder to finish: fewer links, but a link that looked free may not be.
- **Ranges**: some numbers are only a bound, "4+" (at least four bridges) or "≤3" (at most three), drawn on a warm disc. An island "at most" may in principle be left with none by its number, but the network needs a bridge on every island, so it has at least one. A range is a row of answers rather than one, so the generator only relaxes a number where the puzzle still has one answer in all.
- **Two realms**: two colours of island, the second copper, its bridges too. A bridge joins two of one colour; an island of the other colour in the way stops a link as any island does, and each colour is a network of its own (every realm has at least three islands). Two networks on one board, crossing each other's water but never each other.

Two more, **Viaducts** and **Fairways** (1 November 2026, from Korbi's thoughts on a third dimension, sea routes that must stay open, and height; he took the first and the second, and the 3D picture below is for the atmosphere):

- **Viaducts**: some islands are hills, drawn with two contour rings in brass (the cartography's own mark for height). A bridge between two hills is a viaduct, ribbed with piers and cased in the paper's colour, and it goes over any low bridge it meets, which then seems to pass under it. Two viaducts, or two low bridges, still can't cross, and a hill joined to lowland is a low bridge, so height is a property of a bridge (both ends hills) and not of an island alone. A viaduct is the only way two crossing lines can both be built, which changes what is forced: where two links cross, the old rule "one or the other" becomes "one or the other unless one is a viaduct".
- **Fairways**: lanes of open water (a run of tinted cells with a small wave) that ships need, so nothing but a viaduct may span one. Two islands with a lane between are linked only if both are hills; a low bridge never runs over one. Lanes come with viaducts (a puzzle with lanes has hills), and the generator puts each lane under a viaduct of the answer, so a lane is never decoration: it is the reason a link is there or not.

On the page a finger on a viaduct's casing means the viaduct, not the low bridge under it (`CASE` in `bridges.js`), the lane is a translucent tint laid over the bridges so a viaduct's casing doesn't cut it in two, and the viaducts are drawn after the low bridges so the low ones really seem to pass beneath. The engine's `hills` and `lanes` strings join `kinds` and `realms` as a twist's own characters (`.` or `1` a cell, `.` or `~` a cell), saved with a run, carried through the worker and the database, and the layout marks each link `high` when both its islands are hills; two links cross only if both are of one height. Plain puzzles and the other twists consume the generator's random numbers exactly as before (the hash test still passes): the hill draws come after everything else and only when hills are on.

**3D** (1 November 2026, from Korbi: "Fun atmosphere, which is what the 3D model is really intended for", with merchantmen of the seventeenth century sailing slowly between the islands; the same afternoon: "a scenic mode, or just called 3d, which can be applied at all times, and switches the 2d puzzle representation to a beautiful 3d one"): a 3D button beside Hint (2D when it is on; Play in 3D / Play in 2D in the menu, for after a win, when the tools give way to the result) turns the chart into the same board as a relief map, drawn with three.js from the copy Survey already keeps on the device (`bridges-3d.js`), so it works offline; its code and three.js are fetched only the first time it is asked for, and where there is no WebGL the chart stays and says so. It is the puzzle itself, not a picture of it, at all times: the chart and the model are one run (`prefs.view` only says which is drawn), so you can build a bridge in one, turn to the other and carry on, undo and redo work in both, and the clock, hints and the win are the same. Every link without a bridge has a dashed guide on it (a viaduct's hangs in the air where it would stand); a tap on a link builds, a second tap doubles it, a third clears it, and a hold of 430 ms (or a right click) clears it at once, exactly as on the chart, with the link glowing gilt under the finger while it is down (under the pointer, for a mouse). A bridge that would cross another is refused with the chart's words. A drag turns the board (a finger that strays more than nine pixels is a drag and builds nothing), two taps on the water turn it back to where it began (and out to the whole board), and it can't go lower than 30° over the sea, from where the hills would hide the links behind them. It zooms (1 November 2026, Korbi: "Allow zooming in bridges"): two fingers pinch in and out and carry the board with them (the ground under the fingers stays under them), the wheel or a trackpad's pinch does it with a mouse, and a pair of + and − buttons in the corner (`.brg3-zoom`) for a hand with neither, up to 3.6 times the fit; shift and drag moves the board with a mouse. Zoomed out it is exactly the board that fits, and it can't be pushed off the screen: the pan stops where the board's edge (half a cell more) reaches the frame's. A second finger puts any tap or hold in progress aside, so a pinch never builds a bridge, and when one finger is left it turns the board again from where it is. Picking is the same projection as ever, so a tap lands on the right link at any zoom, and the plates (numbers) grow a little with it. Which link a finger means is worked out on the screen, not in the world: every link is projected by the camera as it is now (a line between its islands, set 0.45 cells off each island's middle, at its bridge's height), the nearest within half a cell's pixels (never under ten, nor over twenty-four) wins, a finger on an island (its disc, higher for a hill) is on no link, a link no bridge can go on (another crosses it) loses a close call, and a viaduct wins a call within three pixels over the low bridge that seems to pass under it, as the chart's casing does (`linkLine`, `pickLink` and `pickTolerance` in `bridges-model.js`, tested on 168 links of real puzzles seen from above; the ends are set in by each island's own size, `islandRadius`). A hint's link glows gilt in the model as it does in the chart, and its island's plate has a gilt ring; after a win every bridge glows gilt for three seconds as the board starts to turn. Each island's number is on a plate over it (met, over or shut off in the chart's colours). The camera now keeps the islands in view rather than the board's whole circle, so a puzzle whose islands keep off the corners is drawn larger (it never zooms in past four fifths of the circle's size). Islands are as big as their number (1 November 2026, Korbi: "islands of varying sizes depending on number": `islandRadius`, 0.28 cells for a one up to about half a cell for an eight, so a one is an islet and an eight about fills its cell; islands are never nearer than two cells, so two eights still leave water between them): sand with a beach ring, a lawn of grass and a few trees on the diagonals (more of them on a bigger island, never on the sides a bridge leaves by), and a pale ring of shallows in the water round it; hills are terraces, contour lines made solid, with a brass cap; a low bridge is a timber deck arched over its length (a hump at the middle of up to 0.15 cells, `deckProfile`) with a railing along each edge and trestles under it every three quarters of a cell, double where double, with a gilt strip that follows the arch when it is built; a viaduct is a stone arcade, its arches high enough that a ship passes under (the crown is checked against a ship's masts in the tests), a parapet along each edge of the road, its piers never in a lane or where a low bridge may be built (`piersOf`), crossing over the low bridges below it as the rules say; reefs are rocks; a second realm is copper. All the posts and rails of a bridge are one merged piece of geometry, so a board with thirty bridges is not thousands of things to draw. Ships are small three-masted merchantmen (a hull from a side profile, square sails, a lateen on the mizzen, a flag; a lantern at night) bobbing on a sea whose waves are a sum of three slow sines. A fairway has a ship on the longest straight run of each of its parts, sailing to and fro between the lane's buoys (red and white, along its edges, not across a bend) and coming about over six seconds at each end; a board with no lane has two ships going round it in opposite ways, and one more goes round a board that has. Ships go at 0.2 to 0.3 cells a second. A win turns the board slowly for a few seconds. It draws at most thirty frames a second, only while it is shown, and stands still while the menu is open or the tab is hidden; with reduced motion asked for it draws a frame when something changes and the ships stay put. Kept separate so Node can test it: `bridges-model.js` has the geometry with no picture in it (routes, buoys, piers, arch outlines, island sizes, a deck's hump, plates' text), tested in `tests/bridges-model.mjs`; the picture itself is looked at in a browser (390×664, 390×844, iPad both ways, day and night, Kontor, Modern; a fairways level on 9 and 11 played through to the win with taps, a touch, a hold, a drag, a double tap and a hint; the zoom by wheel, buttons, a two-finger pinch and pan and shift-drag, with every link on screen picked after it), not run in CI, as Survey's block is not. The choice of chart or model is `view` ("chart" or "model") in `bridges:prefs`.

The engine was generalised, not forked (`bridges-engine.js`): an island has a lowest and highest count (`mn`, `mx`) in place of one number, a grid cell may be a reef, the layout knows each island's realm and links only islands of one, the propagation's room for a group is min(highest, sum of the group's highest) − sum of its lowest, and "all one network" is asked of each realm separately. The solver's steps are the same forced ones, so uniqueness is proved as before. The generator grows the board as it always did and then adds the twists: reefs in water no bridge uses, realms by colouring whole connected parts, ranges by loosening numbers one at a time while the puzzle stays solvable at the level asked (`loosen`). Plain puzzles are made in the order they always were: the same seed, size and difficulty is the same puzzle (checked by hash for 18 of them in `tests/bridges-levels.mjs`, which is what keeps today's puzzle and old links valid).

The ladder (`bridges-levels.js`) is 39 levels in eight chapters, each bringing something to the one before: **Harbour charts** (six plain charts from 7×7 easy to 9×9 hard), **Reefs** (five), **Ranges** (five), **Two realms** (five), **The admiralty** (four, the twists in pairs and then all three on an 11×11 hard), **Viaducts** (five), **Fairways** (five) and **The grand charts** (four: reefs with viaducts, ranges with lanes, realms with lanes, and every twist but one, hills being part of lanes, on an 11×11 hard). The last three come after the admiralty, not before it, only so that a level's number, and with it the stars won, the puzzle left half done and the seed, is what it was for everyone who has played (they are kept by number; a test checks that the first 25 are where they were). A level is a recipe (a size, a difficulty, a twist), not one puzzle: the first time it is the same for everyone (its own seed), and after it is solved, or on New puzzle, a new one of the same recipe, so a level can be replayed for its stars. The page says what is new on a chapter's first level, and the rules sheet has a section on each twist.

Stars are for the score as everywhere in Bridges (seconds plus 30 a hint, lower is better): one for solving it, two within twice the par, three within the par. The par is 8 seconds plus 4 for each island, more for a harder puzzle (35% more for Medium, 70% for Hard). Those numbers are guesses, set so the pars range from 48 seconds to 4 minutes; whether three stars should be that easy or hard is for play to say, and `PAR_BASE` and `PAR_PER_ISLAND` are the knobs. The chapters' order is mine too.

Measured in Node: 117 puzzles (every level at three seeds) in about five seconds, the slowest 460 ms; the generator alone makes a viaduct puzzle in 10 to 70 ms at 7×7 and 9×9 and 80 to 270 ms at 11×11, and one with lanes in the same time. Of 360 viaduct and fairway puzzles across every size and difficulty 357 came out at the difficulty asked (the three that didn't are 7×7 Hard with lanes, where the nearest is dealt and the page says so). `tests/bridges-levels.mjs` checks the twists one at a time on small boards, the solver against a plain search of every way to build bridges on 274 small boards with reefs, bounds and realms, and on 340 more with hills thrown on at random and lanes across the water (the plain search knows nothing of the solver's tables: a cell holds one low bridge and one high one at most, a lane is spanned only by a link between two hills; 72 of those boards have another number of answers read as plain water, so the twist is doing something) (it counts exactly as many answers as that does, 65 of them with several), the ladder (names, chapters, each chapter's first level bringing its twist, the first 25 levels where they were), a puzzle of every level at three seeds (one answer by reasoning, its twist really in it (hills and lanes only where asked), a realm of three islands at least, the answer a true building: no bridge over a reef, none over a lane unless it is a viaduct, none crossing at one height, each island within its bound, each realm one network, the same twice), the stars and pars, the plain puzzles from before, and the miniature (hills as rings, a fairway as tinted water).
