# Lots
Crates' Classic (2 November 2026; see Crates, above): no tile, a page of Crates reached from its header, under Crates' name and logo, so Party and a partner's request call it Crates Classic. Connections' genre, by its rules (October 2026): sixteen words, four lots of four, each lot held together by a link you have to see. Pick four and Submit: four of one lot and it's found, a bar in its colour with the link named; three of one lot is "One away"; anything else "Not a lot". Four mistakes end the board; the same four can't be tried twice (it costs nothing); with three found the fourth falls into place. The lots are ranked plain to devious and coloured as the newspaper's are, in the suite's enamels: ochre, verdigris, cobalt, oxblood. Once the board is over every lot shows, and a tap on a lot's bar says why its words belong (FOOT·BALL, CARP inside CARPENTER, PERU rearranged, S·PAIN) and which were red herrings. Shuffle reorders the words left (a reload keeps the order), Deselect drops the picks, a tap on a pick takes it back, Enter submits from a keyboard. The rules, the bank and the generator are `lots-engine.js` and `lots-bank.js`, a board as it's played `lots-run.js`, the computer player `lots-bot.js`, the pair's table `lots-room.js`, all pure; the page is `lots.js`.

The bank (`lots-bank.js`), written for Lots: the knowledge base knows countries, commodities and paintings, and Lots' links are about words, so nothing comes from it. 354 categories in ten topics (Wordplay, Phrases, Meanings, Things with, Markets, Science & nature, Places, Culture, Sport & games, Food & drink), each with a difficulty: 1, a plain set anyone sees at once (Trees, Board games), 73 of them; 2, a set or meaning that takes a moment, or a common phrase, 147; 3, a specialist's set, a harder phrase, "things with", a homophone, 105; 4, wordplay you have to spot and the most devious, 29 (Hidden animals, Anagrams of countries, Bonds sold abroad by their nicknames). Each has more than four members (4,298 in all, 3,460 different words), so a lot is never the same four twice, and a list of words that fit it too but are never dealt in it (a drake is a bird, a mako a fish, a viola has a bridge): that list is how the generator sees a red herring, so it errs wide. The kinds: sets and meanings (Things that bark; Steal); things with (Things with keys: PIANO, MAP); 67 phrases before or after a word (___ JACK: FLAP, CRACKER, LUMBER, APPLE); homophones (They sound like letters: SEA, GEE, AYE, CUE); and wordplay the page checks by the letters, so any word on a board that fits counts, listed or not: a word hidden inside, a word it starts with, an anagram, a letter before a word (G + LOBE) or after one (ARM + Y), a word without its first letter (S·PAIN), palindromes, no vowel, letters in alphabetical order, another word backwards. Markets is Korbi's own topic, 32 categories: the Greeks, crude grades, Incoterms, ship sizes, biofuels, bonds' nicknames, rogue traders, curve shapes. The test holds the phrases to the suite's word list (`wordlist.js`): any word of the bank that makes a phrase's compound there (FOOT and BALL make FOOTBALL) must be in that phrase, and likewise any word that reads as another backwards; the few compounds the list has and nobody says are set aside by name.

Making a board (`deal`, from a seed). The level picks four difficulties (Easy 1-1-2-2 or near it, Medium 1-2-3-4 or near, Hard 2-3-4-4 or near); categories are drawn for them weighted by your topics (one dealt lately at a fifth), at most two of a topic, never two of one trick (two anagram lots are the same idea twice) or on one key word, and four members each, no tile holding another lot's key (FOOTBALL beside ___ BALL). Red herrings are planned, not stumbled on: a word of one lot that fits another lot on the board, drawn from an index of such bridges made once (by lot, then word, or the few categories half the bank fits would deal every herring). Crates' rules hold: a word fits at most one other lot, and no two herrings join the same two lots (they could swap). Easy has none; Medium sometimes one (planned on two boards in five, about half have one); Hard always one, two on about one board in five, and one must bite: its false home is the easier lot, which then shows five words, so a harder lot must be found first. Then the proof: the solver counts the ways the sixteen sort into the four links, every word in a lot whose link it fits, four to a lot, stopping at two, and only a board with exactly one is dealt. It counts beyond the board: any other category four of the words fit across lots (a decoy: four metals, one of them a golf club) is tried in place of each link, and the board must still sort one way; Easy and Medium allow no decoy, Hard one. In Chromium with the processor slowed four times, a phone's stand-in, a board takes well under a millisecond typically and a tenth of a second at worst; the first on a fresh page a third of a second, while the index of which words fit what is made.

Difficulty is what a solver has to do, not how many words there are. The grade is the links' difficulties, plus twice the times a person must look past (the solver takes the easiest link whose words in sight are exactly four; each time the easiest left shows five, a harder must come first), plus the herrings, plus twice the decoys. Measured: Easy grades 5 to 7, Medium 9 to 18 (median 10), Hard 15 to 24 (median 18). The computer players agree: on 150 boards a level, Club solves every Easy one without a mistake in 54 seconds on average, 96% of Medium (0.22 mistakes, 89 s), 87% of Hard (1.27, 161 s).

Today's board: Medium, classic, every topic, the same for everyone (the suite's day is the seed), one go. It counts the moment it's over: points (a point a lot, and once all four are found a point for each mistake to spare: 8 is perfect), then time, in Crates' form, `lots:daily` keeps each day's figure by the day, `lots:best` the best (no tile shows it since Lots joined Crates). The menu's Today's board opens it, asking before it leaves a board you've started. Share copies the guesses as the newspaper's coloured squares.

Play (Settings): Classic, as above, or The wall, Only Connect's connecting wall: two and a half minutes, no Submit (the fourth pick is the guess), a wrong guess says only Wrong, guesses free until two lots are found and three lives after; when the clock or the lives run out, or the wall is solved, it freezes and you name each lot's link from four (its own and three that fit some of its words, or play the same trick), those you didn't find too: a point a lot, a point a link, two more for all eight, ten in all. A classic board solved oxblood first, ochre last, is called a reverse rainbow, as players boast of it. Weighed and left out: five lots of five (25 words don't fit a phone's width legibly) and hints (they'd give away the genre's one decision, what the link is).

Content (the menu): the links choice (Countries leaves for Crates, asking first if a board is unfinished), Random or Today's, the level (Easy, Medium, Hard, also in the header), and the topics, which are Settings' (README, The knowledge base): the menu says where they stand and links to the control. Lots' ten topics answer to the subjects (Wordplay, Phrases, Meanings and Things with to Language; Markets to Finance; Science & nature to Science; Places to Geography; Culture; Sport & games to Sport; Food & drink to Food), each on or out, none dealt more. Subjects that leave no topic to deal from deal every topic and say so; today's board, a race and a pair's table always deal from every one. The Trader, Culture night and Wordsmith presets are gone with the more-or-less weights.

The rival (Settings, Against the computer: Off, Novice, Club, Expert) plays your board beside you as a person would. Each link comes to it after a while, or never, sooner the plainer the link and the better the player (a devious link: Novice two times in five, Expert seventeen in twenty); a link it sees shows it every word that fits, herrings included; it puts in the lot it's surest of first (one whose words in sight are exactly four), waits while its links show five, and when it can wait no longer guesses the likeliest four, keeping three after a one away. On Medium Novice solves 90% (0.47 mistakes, two and a half minutes), Club 96%, Expert 99% (0.07, 53 s); on Hard 69%, 87% and 92%. Its whole game is worked out at the deal, a timeline the board's clock plays, so a pause, a reload or a partner watching sees the same; four squares under the header fill in as it finds lots. The more points win, then the quicker; your record against each level is kept.

Together. The pair, from the partner sheet, plays one board (together.js) three ways, chosen in the menu, none of them leaving the less able player to watch. Open: both see all sixteen and each other's picks (ringed, with an initial), four mistakes between them. Crossed: both see all sixteen, but what a guess came to shows only on the other's phone (the guesser's says Wrong, the other's one away or not), so you have to say; and a lot one of you finds is the other's to name, from four links, a point more for each named (12 in all). Hidden, the default: each sees eight of the sixteen and the other's eight sealed, every lot holding one to three words from each side, so neither can make a lot alone; you pick among your own, a guess is both players' picks, either submits, and a one away tells the one who didn't guess whose pick was off. A board counts in the pair's record as a team board, solved or not ("Duo 5/7"). Or a race (duel.js): the same board for everyone, each on their own phone, the more points then the quicker winning; Race instead in the menu takes the pair from their board to one, and a Party plays the race, up to six. The computer doesn't sit at a table; the rival is for playing alone.

On the screen: the tiles size themselves to the board's box (container units: four across, as tall as the box allows), a long word shrinks to its tile rather than wrap, and nothing changes size during a board. Held sideways the board takes the left; the strip, the rival, your wrong guesses so far (a list a phone has no room for), the line and the buttons stand at the right, the buttons low. A wrong guess shakes its picks once. Checked at 390×600, 390×664, 390×844, 820×1180 and 1180×820 in Codex, Modern and Kontor (and Office) by day and night, played with clicks, and the three ways together and the race played through on two pages over the stand-in database.

The rules (October 2026) are a sheet of their own, from the menu's The rules (the header's Rules went when the links choice came, 2 November 2026), as the other games' are: in plain prose, how a guess is answered, the lots' colours and the kinds of link (Things with keys, ___ JACK, letters' sound-alikes, wordplay), red herrings, the taps, the score and the reverse rainbow, the levels, the wall and its points, the rival, today's board and the pair's three ways. Its numbers come from the engine as it opens and the pair's ways are told in the menu's own words, so the sheet can't drift from the game, and a board alone (the wall's clock too) and the rival stand still while it's open, as for the menu. Looked at on a phone (390×664, 390×844) and an iPad both ways, in Codex, Modern and Kontor by day and night.

What's kept, under `lots:`: `run` (the board as it stands: the words' order, picks, guesses, lots found, mistakes, the time, the rival's timeline, the wall's names), `prefs` (level, play, rival, the pair's way; an old save's topic mix is ignored), `stats` (played, solved, perfect, streaks, by level, against each rival, the wall's best), `seen` (the last 80 categories dealt, dealt less often), `daily` and `best`. A partner watching sees the board as it stands; a watching frame runs no clock. The clock stands while the menu or the games screen is open, or the page is away.

`tests/lots.mjs` checks the bank (sizes, no word twice, every rule's members fit it, the phrases and reversals against the word list); the rules; the solver on boards made to sort one way and two; 270 boards across the levels (one way to sort, by the solver and by brute force; the level's herrings and decoys; no tile holding a key; easiest first; each under 400 ms); the grades' separation, the herrings' shares, topics left out and lots seen lately; the rival (the stronger fewer mistakes and quicker, Hard harder for every level, its game fixed by its seed); a classic run and a wall through JSON, and the naming; the hidden split (the two halves make the board, neither holds a lot); the pair's table through the database's round trip (Crossed's split of what a guess came to, its naming, Hidden's odd one out); and today's board, the same everywhere for a day.

Not done: five lots of five; hints; the computer as a partner (a pair's table seats two people); the knowledge base's countries and commodities as sets (Lots' bank is its own); the pair's races are recorded under `lotsrace`, which no tile reads yet.
