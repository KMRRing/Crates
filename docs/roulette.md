# Roulette

The game, by the gaming regulator's rules for roulette (the DICJ's Despacho 60/2004, unamended): a wheel of 37 pockets, 1 to 36 red and black by turns and a green zero, the only wheel the rules allow (no 00 anywhere in Macau). The bets and what they pay: straight up (孤丁) 35 to 1, a split (兩門騎綫) 17, a street (三門一列) 11, a corner (四門騎綫) 8, a six line (六門) 5, a dozen (小, 中, 大) or a column (直綫十二門) 2, and the even-money bets (紅黑, 單雙, 小大 for 1–18 and 19–36) 1 to 1. Zero takes every bet but a straight one on itself, the even-money bets too (the rules' Article 8: no half back, no prison), and can only be bet straight up: the rules list no split, trio or first four with it, so the layout takes none. Every bet pays 36 over the numbers it covers less the stake, so every one keeps the house 1 in 37, 2.70%. (The rules also allow a nine-number bet at 3 to 1 and two set twelve-number bets at 2 to 1 that no layout seems to carry; they're named on the rules page, not played.) The table: at least HK$100 on the numbers a spin and HK$500 on each outside bet (a guide to the Venetian gives these; the Londoner next door had HK$200 and HK$500 tables in GGRAsia's February 2024 check), up to HK$5,000 a number (a bet on several takes as many times that), HK$50,000 on a dozen or column and HK$100,000 on an even-money bet (no table's maximums were found published), in HK$50s; you start with HK$20,000.

The page: the strip has what you hold, the table's minimums and the spin. Under it the screen over the table: the last twenty numbers as coloured balls, newest first and ringed, and what the last hundred came to (red, black, zero, odd, even, the three hottest numbers and the three longest away). The layout, as big as the room left allows: upright on a phone (zero at the head, the numbers three to a row, the dozens and the even-money bets down the side of the first column, the columns' 2 to 1 at the foot), lying as the table does when held sideways. A tap on the numbers bets as the mode under the layout says (Number, Split, Street, Corner, Line: the split with the neighbour on the side nearest the tap, the corner nearest, the line with the row on the side nearest); an outside bet or zero takes a tap in any mode. Each chip lands where the dealer would put it (on the line between two numbers, at a corner, at the end of a row) and a toast says what it bet and what it pays. The rail: the chips (HK$50 to HK$5,000), Clear, Undo (the last chip; Rebet, the last spin's bets, when the layout is empty) and Spin. Spin: the wheel comes over the layout (held sideways it stands beside it), the rotor turning one way and the ball the other, slowing until the ball drops into the pocket the engine spun and rides the last of the rotor's turn under the pointer; then the dolly stands on the number, the winning bets light up with their chips, and the call over the layout says the number, what it is and what the spin paid you. Menu: a new table (the screen starts again), Top up, the course, Roulette on one page, quick spins. The game is kept as it stands (`roulette:run`: your bankroll, the bets on the layout, the numbers so far), the chip, the mode and quick spins in `roulette:opts`, the course in `roulette:course`.

The course: the wheel (37 pockets and why the numbers are mixed round it; a spin, the croupier's call and the dolly; a void spin, spun again from the last winning pocket); the bets (on the numbers, outside, zero and what it takes, and why every bet keeps the house the same); at the table (how betting closes, by bell or timer before the spin or at the call 停止投注 during it, and 買定離手; the pay-out, losers swept first and straight up paid last, by the rules' order; the screen, and why a hot number is no likelier; the electronic seats and the automatic wheels).
