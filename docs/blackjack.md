# Blackjack
Blackjack as Sands China deals it in Macau, the Venetian's tables in mind (October 2026): `blackjack.html`, `blackjack.js` and `blackjack.css`, its rules in `blackjack-engine.js`, basic strategy worked out in `blackjack-strategy.js`, the course in `blackjack-course.js` on the stage `blackjack-stage.js`, and the rules on one page, with the felt's words and the chart, in `blackjack-rules.js`. The strip, the chips, the buttons and the toast are the casino games' (`casino.js`, `casino.css`).

Macau's rules for the game (the DICJ's Despacho 57/2009, which replaced 56/2004 and its 2007 amendment) are a menu each casino picks from with the regulator's approval: the dealer's second card dealt after the players (no hole card) or a hole card checked by hand or a viewer; against the dealer's blackjack only the original bet lost, splits (Article 16) and doubles on any two cards (Article 17) coming back; surrender for half against anything but an ace, before any card is drawn (Article 18, which a casino may drop); insurance against an ace from half the bet to all of it, paid 2 to 1; even money for a blackjack against an ace or a ten; half the bet paid for five cards under 22 against anything but an ace (Article 19); three times the bet for a suited 6, 7, 8 or three 7s (Article 15); and one side bet a box from a list (any pair 11 to 1, the 7s, Over/Under 13, Perfect Pairs, a streak). On the dealer's soft 17 the text and the tables disagree: since 2007 the rules list hitting it, but every account of play has the dealer standing, and the edge published for Sands China's tables only comes out with the dealer standing.

The game here is what the sources found at the Venetian and its Sands China neighbours: six decks through a continuous shuffler (each round's cards go back in, so every round is dealt from all six: Maven of Macau's five or six, the Wizard of Macau's six, and the six its published edge fits); the dealer standing on every 17; double any two cards and after a split; split to four hands, aces once with one card each, and a pair two cards of one rank, as the rules define one (a jack and a queen aren't a pair; tens are never split, so it changes nothing); surrender against a 2 to a 10; blackjack 3 to 2; and against the dealer's blackjack only the original bet lost but for a double on 11, which Sands China's tables, the Venetian, the Parisian, the Londoner and the Plaza, keep whole (Maven of Macau, July 2026: the city's 0.16% becomes 0.24% there). Over/Under 13, the Venetian's side bet: your first two cards with an ace counting one, over or under 13 even money, 13 losing either; one or the other, as the rules allow one side bet a box. Bets HK$300 (the Venetian's minimum in 2026, by Maven of Macau; HK$500 to HK$1,000 in Golden Week 2023 and at Chinese New Year 2024, by GGRAsia's checks) to HK$50,000 (no maximum was found published), in HK$100s, Over/Under 13 HK$100 to HK$5,000 (the game's own); you start with HK$50,000. Insurance is half the bet; even money is offered against an ace only; the five-card and 6-7-8 payouts aren't dealt, nothing showing that Sands China's tables pay them.

Basic strategy isn't copied: `blackjack-strategy.js` works out every play for every hand against every up card, the dealer's chances drawn from the shoe less your two cards and the up card, a dealer's blackjack still to come weighed in as the table's rules have it, and a chart cell the play best over the hands that make its total. For the Venetian's rules it is the six-deck chart that stands on soft 17 and doubles after a split, with 14, 15 and 16 surrendered against a ten, 16 against a 9, 7s against a ten and 8s against a ten (else split), and 11 against a ten hit, not doubled. Played by it the house keeps 0.244% (Maven of Macau: 0.24%), 0.163% where a double on 11 comes back (the Wizard of Macau and Maven of Macau: 0.16%), 0.38% with the dealer hitting soft 17; Over 13 6.55%, Under 13 10.07%, insurance 6.8% or more. An independent calculation for this game (three-card removal, the dealer's hands exact) came to the same figures, to two decimal places.

The page: the strip has what you hold, the table's limits and the shuffler. The felt has the dealer's cards at its head, the rules in two arcs as a felt prints them (squeezed where a theme's lettering runs longer than the arc), your hands below (a split's side by side, the one in play ringed), and while you bet the circle with Over 13 and Under 13 either side of it. A chip is picked on the rail and tapped onto the circle or a side bet; Clear, Rebet (the last round's bets) and Deal. The cards come out one by one as the dealer deals them; your plays replace the chips: Double, Split, Surrender, Hit and Stand, each with the signal the dealer reads, and an ace up asks insurance first (even money with a blackjack). Over/Under 13 is settled under your hands as soon as your second card shows. The round's end says what the dealer made and what you came away with, and the next round's bets wait under it, smaller, already placed, so Deal goes again. The cards are sized to what the felt holds, measured, so a split of four with a third card each still fits. The coach (Menu → Coach, 23 October 2026: Korbi, "off:hint:check:always, and explanations as to why each option is right") speaks in one of four ways, the same four as Chess's. Off: nothing. Hints: nothing until you ask; Hint, in the coach's panel at the head of the rail, gives the chart's play for the decision you are at (ringed on its button, and "Stand: 16 against a 5" in the panel) until you play. Check: the Hint button too, and after each play of yours a line saying whether it was the chart's ("✓ Stand: the chart's play" or "✗ Double: the chart says stand (15 against a 5), worth 0.61 of a bet more"), which stays until the next round is dealt. Always: the chart's play ringed and named before you play, a different one also noted in a toast, and, held sideways, the chart beside the felt with the hand's cell picked out, where there's room for it. A play within a hundredth of a bet of the chart's is "level", not a mistake: the chart's cell is an average over the hands making the total, so two plays that close are a tie.
Why is the second half of it: Why, in the panel, opens a sheet with the hand ("a pair of 8s against a 6"), a sentence or two on why the chart plays as it does, and every play open to you with what it is worth on average (in bets, "+0.58", "−0.39") and a note ("busts 54% at once", "wins 42%, loses 58%", "can't bust: the ace drops to 1", "one card only, for twice the bet"), the chart's marked and, after a check, yours. The sentences are made from the numbers, not written per hand: the dealer's chance of busting from its up card, the chance a hit busts, what standing wins, so they stay true on any table's rules; a few hands (aces, eights, tens, 11, soft totals) get a line of their own, and insurance is the arithmetic of a ten under the ace, from the shoe as it stands (it needs a third, and wins 31%; with a blackjack, playing on is worth 1.04 against even money's sure 1). What each play is worth is the strategy's own working (`strategy().ev`, a total's cell and a pair's own), so the coach can't say one thing and the chart another; `blackjack-coach.js` has no page and `tests/blackjack.mjs` runs it over 1,500 rounds, every decision's list led by the chart's play. The panel keeps its height whatever it says, so nothing shifts as it speaks, and the page never scrolls. The setting was once on or off (`blackjack:opts`): on reads as Always, off as Off, so nothing changes for a player who had it.
