# Quote
Make a market on a number you don't know: a bid and an ask. Every question carries documented tiers, the radius a market may have around the truth for each grade: SS (exact, where exactness can be expected), S, A, B; wider is a C, a miss. Marie Curie's birth (1867): the 1860s is an S, 1850–1880 an A, 1850–1900 a B, 1800–1900 a miss. A market's radius is the distance from the truth to its farther edge, plus the miss again when the truth lies outside, so a confident wrong market grades harder than a wide right one and a near miss (1870–1880) still earns an A. The payoff is stepwise by grade (300 at exact, 250 at the S edge, 180 at A, 100 at B, 0 at twice the B radius, then a loss growing towards −300) with a continuous run between the steps, so a touch tighter earns a touch more, all scaled by the question's difficulty (1 to 5: a 1 pays 0.7 of the curve, a 5 pays 1.3). The card shows the tiers; the shortcuts set a market of the S, A or B radius around what you've typed; the verdict names the grade. Only a B or below banks the question to the pile: an A is adequate knowledge. Tiers and difficulty are in `quote-bank.js` (`tiers`, `d`), derived from each question's range (years: S 2.5× the range, A 2.5× S, B 4.5× S; magnitudes: S 1.5×, A 3.5×, B 7×) and meant to be edited by hand. Know a figure exactly? Give it as both bid and ask: a market on one figure, drawn as a dot on the tape, and the only way to an SS on a question that can be known exactly.

Every question has its own range for "close" (`tol` in the bank): ±1% on Everest's height, ±15% on a city's population, ±40% on Picasso's output; 2 years on a modern date, 20 on an ancient one; whole units on a count. The card says it ("close means ±15%"). The payout is continuous: inside the market it is 300·e^(−v/2), with v the width in ranges, so a point market exactly right pays 300, one a range wide about 180 and one five ranges wide about 25; outside, that fades by e^(−u) with u the miss in ranges while a loss of 300·(1 − e^(−u/3)) grows in its place, so a miss by a third of a range still pays about 60% of exact, a miss by a whole range about breaks even, and far out costs up to 300. It's continuous across the market's edge. Widths and misses are in doublings for magnitudes (log2 of the ratio, the range converted the same way) and plain units otherwise. Shortcuts under the fields set a market of half, one, two or four ranges around what you've typed. After you quote, a tape shows your market and where the truth fell, with a note on the basis of the figure.

Together (October 2026): a pair's duo match, or a party of up to six, each on their own phone. Ranges, the default: everyone makes their own market on the same question, in private, scored exactly as a market alone is; a line under the Quote button shows who has quoted (ticked) and who's away. The reveal comes once everyone at the table has quoted: your verdict, then every market drawn on one scale in its player's colour with the truth through them all (one that missed it dashed) and what each earned, the best first; then the standings (standings.js: each book from the 1,000 everyone starts with, this question's change, and under each name their market and grade, a C said as wide or a miss); then the note. The table moves on once everyone at it has pressed Next, by Chart's rules for people away and the slow: nobody away holds it up, and once those who have quoted are at least as many as those still to and half a minute has passed since the last market, they may reveal without the rest (a missing market scores nothing); Next the same way. The last question's reveal ends the set, its result final there and then; the closing bell shows who won, every book with how many markets held the truth, your result on each question, Play again and Back to solo. Trade, the other way a pair can play (the menu's together part: Ranges or Trade, a fresh set either way; a table of three or more always plays Ranges): one makes the market without seeing anything else; the other then sees it and hits the bid (sells to the maker at it), lifts the offer (buys at it) or passes. A trade settles between the two by how far the traded price was off the truth, in the question's ranges, on the same smooth curve (up to 300 each way); a pass settles the maker against the house as when alone. Ten questions, five markets each, and either one's Next moves both on. Either way the higher book counts for the pair's duo record. The closing bell's list names a painting or building where the question said "this" ("The year The Kiss was painted"), as the list has no pictures to point at. Opened for a table, the page shows the rete (Joining your partner, or Joining the table at a party) until the table's set is drawn, never your own set meanwhile (October 2026; your own set used to be drawn first, so for the first seconds you and your partner were on different questions); Back to solo returns to your own set as you left it, and nothing at a table touches your own run, bests, today's, the Ledger or your pile. The table's rules, both ways, are pure functions in `quote-table.js` (everything kept by seat and by question, never in a list by number), tested in `tests/quote.mjs` through the database's round trip; three phones in one browser played a set of Ranges through (the reveal on each, Next from all three, one leaving mid-set, the grace for a slow quoter, the closing bell, Play again), and a pair from the partner sheet's address played Ranges, switched to Trade in the menu and traded a set through to its record. Topics (set in Settings, 2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed"; in October 2026 Quote had a control of its own, each category out, in or more, with the presets Balanced, Trader and Culture night; README, The knowledge base): a random set deals from the bank's categories (Trade, Cities, Wine, Art, People, Geography, Markets, Refining, Scale) whose subjects are on, in Settings or in the sheet this game's Change topics link opens over the game. Each category belongs to a subject (`TOPIC_OF.quote` in `topics.js`): Trade to Trade & shipping, Cities to Cities, Wine to Food & drink, Art to Art & architecture, People to People, Geography to Geography, Scale to Science (a tennis court's area, a jerrycan, a gold bar: sizes and energies), Markets to Finance, Refining to Energy. A category is in when its subject is on and out when it isn't, so the Culture preset deals Wine, Art and People, Trade deals Trade, Markets and Refining, and a subject Quote has no questions on (Sport, Religion, Maths) changes nothing here. The menu shows only where the topics stand ("Topics: Culture (10 of 23 on). Quote deals 3 of its 9 topics"), whether today's set or a random one is picked (22 October 2026: the old control showed only once Random was, and each game opens on today's, so Korbi never found it), and a change made with today's picked turns the next set to a random one. The subjects are read when a set is dealt, so a change reaches the next set and a set already dealt keeps what it was dealt. With every subject on a set is Balanced, as it always was: at most two from a category, the same set for the same seed as before there were topics, and today's set is always that, whatever you have on. Nothing on, or only subjects Quote has no category in, deals every category, and the menu says so. A random set on a preset (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade or All off) that deals more than one category counts for your best, with learning mode off; your own mix doesn't, and the menu says so. One category left in is that category's set, all ten from it, with a best and a link of its own (`&f=refining`) and none for the games screen's best, as refining sets always were: STEM leaves Refining and Scale (Energy and Science are the two of its subjects Quote has), and a single subject can leave one category (Finance alone: Markets), which is then that category's own set. The refining set is European fuel specs (EN 228, EN 590, EN 15940, EN 14214 limits), the octane, vapour pressure and density of each blendstock, cetane and cold flow by component, energy and oxygen contents, conversions, RED III and the THG-quota, fuel taxes and duty, buy-out prices and crack spreads: 111 questions, so a player who crushes them has the numbers of the European road-fuel trade; the menu's About line shows its best beside the book. Any other mix goes into the set's link category by category (`&t=trade.1,art.1`); a link from before the change may carry a 2, which still deals that category twice as often and up to four of it in a set, and a Trader or Culture night mix in a link still counts as a preset. The old `quote:topics` is left alone and no longer written. At a table, a fresh set comes from the topics of whoever deals it.

`quote-bank.js` holds the questions (1,345 across trade, cities, art, wine, people, geography, markets, refining and scale, 523 of them written from figures on things in `kb/measures.js`: README, The knowledge base), `quote-engine.js` settles markets and picks sets (seeded, at most two questions from a category and one of a family, or by your mix), and `tests/quote.mjs` checks both.

The page fits the screen: once a market is settled the bid and ask boxes and the width shortcuts make way for the result (the tape shows your market against the truth), and a long note scrolls inside. Widths are rounded where they're made (0.1 × 3 used to show as 0.30000000000000004).

A question shows its unit only: the grade widths ("S ±3 ppm · A ±7 · B ±15") and the width shortcuts gave away the scale of the answer, so you work out your own range. A range that holds the answer never loses money, however wide, and always earns something: past the curve's B, 30% of B's payoff out to twice the B radius, shrinking as the range widens (100 to 1,500 on Switzerland's GDP of 885 earns about +15). Its verdict is yellow, "Wide, but in range", never red; red is for a range that missed. Below A it still goes to the review pile. A range that misses never earns, by however little: it's a C and loses at least 10, more the further the answer is beyond it against the question's B radius (a year beyond on a ±15-year question loses about 14; five beyond about 34), up to the old losses for big misses.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the card opens like a ledger's facing pages: the question, its unit and its picture on the left, the market beside them, level with the question and above where a tablet's keyboard rises (the card's two halves are `.fit-half`, which on a phone aren't boxes at all).

The ruler (31 October 2026, Korbi: "do both: log scale dial, but also user can type"): typing a bid and an ask is exact and slow on a phone, so a ruler above the boxes sets both by hand, and the boxes show what it set (typing in a box moves the ruler too; the boxes stay the one place the market lives, so everything that reads them, the quote, the table's lock and the result, is as it was). Tap the bare ruler to make a market there, drag either handle (‹ is the bid, › the ask) or the bar between them to move both with the same width, and with a handle focused the arrow keys move it a hundredth of the ruler and the page keys a twentieth. A magnitude (an area, a population, a length: a question on a log scale) has a log ruler, five decades wide, where a doubling is the same distance anywhere on it, which is how the market's width is counted and scored; a year has a plain ruler from 1000 to 2050; a percentage from 0 to 100. A count or an age has none, because a window round the truth would say where it is: the room stays (nothing changes size between questions) and the ruler is hidden. The ruler must not tell the answer, so a log ruler's decades are placed by the question's id, never by the truth: the answer lies one, two or three decades from the left end, each as often as the others (`tests/quote-dial.mjs` counts), and its ends are round numbers. A place gives a value of two figures, as someone typing would; a figure typed beyond an end shows at the end, hollow. A settled market, or one that's not yours to change at a table, hides or locks it. `quote-dial.js` is the ruler, with no page in it, so it's tested in Node.
