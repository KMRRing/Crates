# Stow
A hold to fill. Since October 2026 Stow is a stowage plan, not Block Blast: each level is a manifest, a list of pieces that must all go into a hold partly filled with cargo already aboard, filling it exactly. There's no line clearing; the puzzle is the fit. Block Blast's game, which was Stow until then, is kept beside it as Loose cargo (below), its 72 levels and stars with it. The rework answered "too easy and too samey: little tension, no aha when pieces slot together": an exact packing (every cell filled, every piece used) has the click built in, and a counter that proves most manifests have one stow and no other makes every level a deduction rather than a fill.

**Playing a manifest.** The whole manifest is in the tray from the start, every piece on one scale, so you can plan before you place. A tap turns a piece a quarter clockwise; a drag lifts it (held above the finger) and it snaps to the nearest place it fits. Pieces turn but never flip: crates are marked this way up, so an L is never a J. A stowed piece in a flat hold is dragged back off to take it out (dropped over the hold where it doesn't fit, it stays put); Undo, the tray's last tile, takes out the last piece in. Every piece taken out or moved after it went in is a restow, the shipping word for a move that costs a crane cycle: three stars for a stow with none, two for a few (two, or a quarter of the pieces), one for any stow. Starting the manifest again starts the count again. The fit is felt: the sides a piece seats flush against (the hold's side, cargo, another piece) flash gilt with a tap of the phone (`navigator.vibrate`; an iPhone has none, so a hidden switch toggled under the finger gives iOS's own tap), a piece that fills a pocket exactly glows and says Tight with a double tap, and when the last piece seats with nothing broken the hatch covers slide shut over the hold, lettered Stowed, with the stars under it (a tap on the hatch lifts it to look at the stow, another shuts it). Space no piece left could fill is hatched in oxblood at once (a pocket whose size no set of the remaining pieces adds up to; in a hold in decks, any cell under a crate, which no crane can reach), so a mistake shows when it's made, not at the end. A line over the tray says what matters most now: a hint on a chapter's first levels, dead space, a rule broken.

**Chapters**, one stowage rule each, the rule's state always shown in the orders over the tray (verdigris when kept, oxblood when broken); every rule chapter ends with holds in decks (below), marked with a cube in the menu:
- The hold: flat, the pieces and nothing else (the first two dealt as they go, before turning's been met).
- Below decks: holds two and three decks deep, in 3D (below). The crane lowers each piece until it meets a crate, so a piece can rest on one stack and hang over a gap beside it, which then can't be filled: the order you lower pieces in matters.
- Discharge order: every piece is for Antwerp, Rotterdam or Amsterdam (ANR, RTM, AMS, the UN/LOCODE port codes less the country), called in that order. Cargo for an earlier port must never sit under cargo for a later one, or it can't come off without a restow at that port; each port wears its enamel (cobalt, oxblood, verdigris).
- Reefers: refrigerated pieces (a snowflake) must each sit on a power plug, as many plugs as reefers, so a plug under anything else is wasted and struck through (and the line says so). In decks the sockets are on the floor, so the reefers go low and the rest on them.
- Dangerous goods: pieces placarded IMDG class 3 (flammable liquids: ethanol, biodiesel) and 5.1 (oxidisers) must never touch, not even at a corner. The IMDG code's segregation table puts the pair at "separated from"; touching at a corner is the puzzle's line. In decks it holds in all three directions: not over or under each other either, nor at an edge or corner of a crate.
- Even keel: some pieces weigh 2 or 3 t a crate; when the hold's full, port and starboard (left and right, the bow up the page) must weigh the same, cargo aboard counting a tonne a crate. A clinometer over the tray shows the list as you go. The holds are the same either side of their centreline, so it's the heavy cargo that has to be split. In decks, P and S are lettered outside the walls at the rim and the bow marked with a brass arrow, so the sides read whichever way the hold is turned; stacking heavy on heavy is fine, it's only side against side.
- The voyage: rules together, flat and in decks (reefers with the discharge order; dangerous goods with an even keel).

The campaign only grows at the end. Progress is kept by a manifest's id, its place in the list, so a level added later goes last in `tools/stow-manifests.mjs`'s recipes, whatever its chapter, and is numbered on in its chapter (the decks levels of Reefers, Dangerous goods, Even keel and The voyage came this way: 11, after the first 58); the campaign is played chapter by chapter in each chapter's own order, a level opening once the one before it in its chapter is stowed. `tests/stow.mjs` checks the 58 first published are as they were.

**Making manifests** (`stow-pack.js`, pure). A manifest is made from a recipe (the hold, the crates aboard, the sizes of piece to cut, the rules, how many pieces, how hard): crates laid in clumps (in decks, stacks from the floor, nothing floating), leaving the space in one piece; the space cut into pieces, each started at the empty cell with fewest empty neighbours and grown mostly into the tightest corners, never stranding a pocket too small for a piece (flat pieces are polyominoes of two to five; in decks polycubes of two to four, never a screw: two cubes twisting, hard to read in a tray); the pieces tagged on that cut so it keeps every rule (a reefer's plug under one of its cells; 5.1s near the 3s but not touching, where the rule bites; ports mostly later going down a stack; heavy pieces whose extra weight sits as much to port as to starboard); each dealt a random turn. Then the counter, an exact cover search (every empty cell covered once, every piece used once): it fills the lowest-numbered empty cell next with every piece left in every turn and place whose lowest cell is that one, so no stow is found twice; pieces alike in shape and tags count once (swapping them isn't another stow); a branch leaving a pocket no piece left could fill is dropped; rules are checked as pieces go in; and a full hold counts only if the crane could have stowed it (no two pieces each under the other, so some order lowers them all) and, with Even keel, it balances. A cut with a second stow is mended where the two differ, those pieces cut again, a dozen times before a new hold is tried, which finds a manifest with one stow far sooner than cutting afresh. A level is kept when it has exactly one stow (every level after a chapter's first), when without any one of its rules it would have more (so every rule matters), and when the counter's effort lands in the recipe's band, which is how the chapters ramp. `tools/stow-manifests.mjs` writes the campaign, `stow-manifests.js` (69 manifests in 7 chapters), from its recipes, seeds running on from each level's own until one is kept, so the same recipes write the same campaign.

**Today's manifest** is made on the device from the date (`today()`), in the worker, the same everywhere (integers decide everything): a different order each day of the week (a plain hold, decks, the discharge order, reefers, dangerous goods, an even keel, reefers and dangerous goods), its recipes chosen to come in a few hundred milliseconds on a laptop (a phone takes a few times that, under the rete), with a plain hold if the day's finds nothing in its tries. Its stars are kept by the day (`stow:daily`), the moment the last piece seats.

**Keys.** `stow:mode` (manifests or Loose cargo), `stow:plan` (the manifest in play: its id and a signature of what it is, so a run kept for a manifest that has since changed starts afresh; what's stowed where by piece, restows, and the tray's turns, so a reload or a watching partner sees the tray as you left it), `stow:plans` (each manifest's best stars and fewest restows), `stow:dayplan` (today's manifest, made once), `stow:daily`, `stow:view` (a hold's turn); Loose cargo's `stow:run` and `stow:progress`. Stow's figure on the games screen is the manifests' stars, Loose cargo's its own. Until October 2026 `stow:day` kept Loose cargo's daily; it's removed on opening.

**Holds in decks, in 3D** (`stow-3d.js`): seen from above at a slant, orthographic (parallel lines stay parallel, so a deck's rows stay true), each crate the prism of its cell, drawn back to front by the depth of their centres (a true painter's order for cells of one lattice), only the faces the eye can see; the near walls left off so the eye sees in, the far walls ruled a crate apart and marked at each deck, the rim in brass. A drag across the hold turns it round and tilts it, the arrows in its lower corners (where the hold never reaches) turn it an eighth; the view is kept and the tray's pieces are drawn in it, so a piece in hand looks as it will in the hold. A piece held over the hold with a place to go is drawn there among the crates (what the cargo hides shows through, dashed), lowered from over the hatch when you let go, and edged in gilt as it seats; crates out of discharge order are edged in oxblood. Pieces next to each other in the stow wear different enamels (eight: the four and their lighter tones); in the discharge order each its port's, lighter beside one of the same port. The rules are painted on the crates, on every face they show, as on a container: a dangerous good's placard (a lozenge in its class's red or yellow), a reefer's snowflake; heavy crates are shaded darker; a reefer's floor socket shows through the crates in front, dashed, until something stands on it. A piece in the tray wears the same, with its tag in the corner. In decks a piece turns about the vertical only (the crane turns a load, it never tips one), and only Undo takes pieces out, the last in being the one with nothing on it.

**Together**: a pair's match (from the games screen) or a party's table of up to six: one hold and one manifest, stowed a move at a time round the table, a move being a piece in, a piece moved, or a piece taken out (the team's restow); each of you turns the tray's pieces on your own phone. Someone away (their phone asleep, or gone back to solo) is passed over until they're back, so a sleeping phone never holds up the table; the line over the tray says whose turn it is and who's been passed over. Another player's piece seats on your screen as it did on theirs (the table keeps the last move). The stow and its stars are the team's, recorded for the pair as restows (fewer better). A new table starts at your manifest (today's is yours alone: the first you haven't stowed, then); starting another asks first while one is under way. Until October 2026 a table played Loose cargo, each with a hand of three the others couldn't see; a table from then is dealt afresh. The table is `stow-room.js`, pure: what's stowed kept by piece (`p0`, `p1`…, as a list comes back from the database with holes and an empty one not at all), the turn passing over the away, a move through `stow-pack.js`'s own.

**Loose cargo** has a tile of its own in Idle (4 November 2026, the split review: a different game under Stow's name; `stow.html?mode=loose`, id `loose`, the page wearing its name and logo; the Stow tile opens the manifests; neither menu links to the other). 4 November 2026, Korbi: no setting duplicates a tile or a suite setting. Trap: the mode is read from the address and written to `stow:mode` only when the page is opened, never on a reload, or two devices in different modes would reload each other through the sync for ever; a watching frame has no address and follows the partner's `stow:mode`. Its saves stay `stow:run` and `stow:progress`; its figure is `loose:stars`, Stow's `stow:stars` is the manifests'. Never name it after Block Blast (a trademark). (`stow-loose.js`, `stow-engine.js`, the levels in `stow-levels.js`; solo since October 2026): drag the shapes into the hold; a line full from end to end clears. Three pieces at a time, each placed once and never turned (Block Blast's rule; on triangles, turning would change which way a cell points), a new three once all three are in. A level is won when every consignment (the red cargo stamped in brass) has gone out with a line, and lost when nothing in hand fits. Before you let go the hold shows what the piece would do: its cells, and the lines it would fill, lit. A piece is dragged from the tray or tapped and then put down with a tap in the hold. Eight chapters, 72 levels: The hold (8×8), Bays (9×9, where each 3×3 bay clears too), Hexagons (61 cells, lines in three directions), Triangles (54 and 96, a piece only fitting where its triangles point its way), Odd holds (a hull in section, a deck round a hatch, a cross, a well, a hub: a line is every cell on it), and three of holds in decks: Below decks, Hexagon decks and The ship. In decks the crane lowers each piece until a crate stops it; a line along a deck clears, then the hold settles, every crate dropping until it rests on another or the floor, which can fill another line (a chain, a wave at a time), and everything a move would clear, every wave of it, is lit before you let go in the piece's colour. Columns from floor to hatch clearing as lines were tried and left out (topping up a consignment's column freed it in a piece or two), and so were decks of triangles (prisms pointing both ways, which the eye can't sort out in 3D). Every level is a puzzle with a par: `tools/stow-levels.mjs` makes each from a recipe, cargo laid in clumps and consignments on different lines, then a beam search (keeping the best boards at every step, judged by consignments gone, lines with consignments nearly full, room, and no walled-in cells or hollows) finds the fewest pieces it can win in; a level is kept when its par lands in range and the same search keeping one board, a careless player, does worse. Stars: three at par, two within about a third over, one for any win. Its daily ended with the manifests'.

**Tests** (`tests/stow.mjs`): Loose cargo's pieces counted against the known numbers of polyominoes, polyhexes, polyiamonds, polycubes and prisms, its clearing and settling, deals, and every level replayed in its par; the manifests' turns (never a flip), the counter against known tilings (dominoes: 2×4 five ways, 3×4 eleven, 4×4 thirty-six) and the crane (two pieces each under the other refused; a piece over gaps stowed once they're filled), each rule, the moves and their restows and stars, a piece in decks only where the crane brings it to rest and none lifted from under another, every manifest stowed by its solution in an order the crane allows and proved to have that stow and no other, each of its rules mattering, the 58 first published unchanged and every rule with holds in decks (a reefer's sockets on their floor); today's made twice the same; and three stowing a manifest at a table through the database's round trip, one passed over while away, one restow.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the hold takes the left with all the height, the line, the orders and the tray beside it.

**3D** (Korbi, 4 November 2026: "the screen always resizes itself when the perspective changes in 3D mode ... a proper 3D mode with the hanseatic items instead of just boring boxes"): `stow-gl.js` (three.js, goods from `stow-goods.js`) stands in for `stow-3d.js`, which stays as Flat and shares its camera, so picking is unchanged; the frame never depends on the view (`frameOf`). View choice is `stow:look`, else Settings' 2D mode.
