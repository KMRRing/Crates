# Ledger
Is it working? Ledger is the record of how well you learn, kept from what the games already do. Recall: every item from the pile that comes back, in Deck or in a game, right or wrong, sorted by how long it was away (within the hour, two days, a week or two, a month, three months); away a week or more and still known is the headline, since that's what lasts, with a weekly line under it and the six piles' counts. Confidence: every bet in Punt, read backwards through Kelly's rule (staking a share f of the pot at decimal odds o, you act as if your chance were (1 + f(o − 1)) / o), plotted against how often those bets won, a dot a tenth, the diagonal the ideal; the average belief against the win rate says whether you stake surer than you are, the Brier score (the mean squared gap between that chance and what happened) sums it up (0 perfect, .250 always saying evens), and the win rate by stake size shows whether bigger stakes win more often. Markets: in Quote, how often the truth lay inside your bid and ask, and the grades. The games report as they settle, to `ledger-log.js` (the pile on every item that comes back, Punt on every bet, Quote on every market), which keeps weekly totals under `ledger:stats`, a few kilobytes however much you play, synced with the solo code like everything else; a game that feeds the pile is measured with no change of its own. As the games change the record can be started afresh (Menu, Settings) without touching the piles. Tested in `tests/ledger.mjs`: ISO weeks across year ends, the gaps, Kelly backwards, the totals, and the pile's once-only move of what was learned under the old four piles.

Knowledge, read two ways: each Punt bet also records the chance a pure guess had (one in the number of options; one in the ways to pick several right ones; none for a typed answer). A stake's Kelly chance less that, over what's left (the standard correction for guessing), is the share of the answer the stake claimed to know: a pass claims none, all in claims all, and at a fair price it's the stake itself. Your hit rate corrected the same way is what you knew (right half the time on four options is knowing a third). The Ledger sets the two side by side, overall and by stake, says whether your stakes rise with what you know, and counts only bets made since Punt began recording the guess's chance.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the cards stand side by side, as many across as fit.
