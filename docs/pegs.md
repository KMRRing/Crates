# Cribbage
Cribbage for two (31 October 2026, Korbi: "pegs"), against the computer or at a table with a friend on their own phone (README, Seats); second of the placeholder games to be built, on seats.js like Meld: `pegs-rules.js` the pure rules, `pegs-bot.js` the computer player, `pegs.js` the page.

The rules are the standard six-card game. Six cards each, two put in the crib (the dealer's), the starter turned (a jack, his heels, two for the dealer). The play: the one who didn't deal leads, each plays a card in turn and the count, which may not pass 31, goes up, scoring as it goes: 15 and 31 two each, a pair two, three alike six, four alike twelve, a run of three or more (the last cards played, in any order, with nothing foreign inside it) as many as its length, and a run and a fifteen together. A player who can't play without passing 31 says go: Cribbage says it for them, since there is nothing to decide in it, and the other plays on while they can; the last to play scores one for go (two for exactly 31) and the count starts again, led by the other. One for the last card. The show: the one who didn't deal counts their four cards and the starter first, then the dealer, then the dealer counts the crib (a flush in the crib needs all five cards): fifteens two each, pairs two each, runs by length with each way to make one counted (a double run counts twice), four of a suit four and five with the starter, the jack of the starter's suit one. Cribbage counts for you and itemises it; muggins (the other claiming what you missed) has no place when nobody can miss. First to 121 wins at once, in the middle of the play or of a count, so the one who counts first can win before the dealer counts; the game can be to 61. A loser left under 91 is skunked, under 61 double skunked (said at the end; it changes no score).

The count is `countHand`: every subset of the five cards for the fifteens, every pair for the pairs, and for the runs the longest length that exists, counted once for each set of cards that makes it. `tests/pegs.mjs` checks it against hands whose totals are known (the 29, the 24 of a double double run, the nineteen hand that scores nothing) and the play's scoring (pairs royal, runs in any order, a run and a fifteen at once).

The computer player has three strengths, as Meld's. Putting two cards in the crib, Easy keeps the four that look best against a handful of starters; Medium weighs all 46 starters that could turn up; Hard also reckons what the two sent are worth to the crib, which it adds when it deals and takes off when it doesn't (pairs, fifteens, fives, neighbours in rank, suit). In the play, Easy plays a card that scores if it has one, else any; Medium takes what scores and steers clear of counts that give points away (a count of 5 or 21 lets a ten make 15 or 31; so do the others by how many of those cards are still about); Hard counts the cards it has not seen to weigh both that and the chance the other holds a card to pair its own, leads low and never a five, and spends high cards while they are safe. Over 300 games Hard beats Medium 56 times in a hundred and Medium beats Easy 60; cribbage has too much luck in it for more.

The page: the other hand as backs, with a mark for whose crib it is; the board, a lane a side, each with a front peg (the score) and a back peg (where it was before the last scoring), a tick every five holes and the skunk lines dashed; the starter, the count, the crib as four backs, and this round's cards played in a row (yours ringed) with the latest thing said under them. Your cards along the foot: in the discard, tap two and send them; in the play, tap one and play it, the cards that would pass 31 greyed. At the show each count is a card of its own: the four cards and the starter, the fifteens, pairs, run, flush and nobs as lines with their points, the total, and a fuse for the time it stays up (25 seconds, or a tap on Next count by either player). Random's round is a deal; a finished game counts for the streak on the games screen.

The 3D view (Menu, View; 3 November 2026, Korbi: "add an aesthetic 3d mode whenever you have time"): the table tipped away from you by CSS alone (`pegs-3d.css`), the flat table the default, the choice the device's own (`suite:pegs-view`).

Lessons (Menu, Learn; 4 November 2026, Korbi: "a 'lessons' tab accessible there same as any other lesson for these games"): `pegs-course.js` on the suite's course sheet, progress under `pegs:course`. Every count a lesson states comes from `countHand` and `playScore`; `tests/pegs-course.mjs` checks them and that the computer players keep, throw, lead and play as the lessons teach, so a lesson's position needs its answer to agree with `pegs-bot.js`.
