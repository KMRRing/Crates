# Origin

A hidden country, found from clues. The clues come one at a time, the most obscure first (difficulty 3, then 2, then 1) and, within each, trade and commodities ahead of the rest; they are the knowledge base's own clue links (the ones Crates and Punt draw on), every one of a country's, dropping any that names the country or a place in it. Guess whenever you like, any country on Chart's map by any of its names, typed (the app's own suggestion list opens as you type: names that start with what you've typed first, arrows and Enter work) or tapped on the world map under the clues (Chart's outlines; drag to move it, with a mouse or a finger, and pinch, scroll the wheel or press + and − to zoom, around the pointer; the map moves by its own view rather than by scrolling, so there are no scrollbars; a country's name shows once it's big enough on screen; one tap names a country, a second guesses it; guessed countries take their warmth, the answer turns green): a wrong guess shows only how warm it is, as a colour on its chip and on the map, blue for the far side of the world through to red next door (a log scale of the distance between Chart's centroids: 300 km and nearer fully red, 12,000 km and further fully blue), never the distance or the direction, and turns up the next clue. A country scores 100, less 10 for each clue after the first and 5 for each wrong guess (at least 5); giving up scores nothing. Five countries a run, random or today's (the suite's day, the UTC date, the same five for everyone); the run counts the moment its last country is decided, and after a finished run, or from its tile, today's comes first while it's still to play. 135 of Chart's 168 countries qualify (at least six clues, Crates' clue links or plain links with a reason, once those that give the country away are dropped, and a place on the map); `tests/origin.mjs` checks every answer, and that no clue in a deal names its country. The countries, the clues and the scoring are `origin-engine.js`'s, pure; the page draws them and the map.

Topics (set once for every game, 2 November 2026): a random run's clues come from Crates' country topics (the aspects of the clue links) that are on in Settings, the one set every knowledge game shares (README, The knowledge base, "Topics, set once for every game"); Origin has no control of its own. The menu shows where they stand ("Topics: Culture, 10 of 23 on") with a Change topics link that opens the same sheet over the game, whether today's run or a random one is picked (22 October 2026: the old control showed only once Random was, and each game opens on today's, so Korbi never found it; today's says it deals every topic), and a change made with today's picked turns the next run to a random one. Each country topic belongs to a subject (`TOPIC_OF.kb` in `topics.js`) and `topicsOf` makes the mix: a topic in when its subject is on, out when it isn't, read when a run is dealt, so a change reaches the next run and a run already dealt keeps its clues. There is no more or less any longer (Korbi: on or off is all that's needed), so Balanced, Trader and Culture night are gone from the menu. A clue only of topics out never comes, unless its country would be left with fewer than six; which countries come up doesn't change. Every subject on deals exactly as Origin always did (Balanced, seed for seed), and so does today's run, always. The engine still puts a clue of weight 2 first within its difficulty, so a mix a table or link carried from before still deals. Nothing on is dealt as every topic, and the line says so. A random run counts for your best when the topics are a preset (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade or All off); a mix of your own doesn't, and the menu says so. At a table, a new game comes from the topics of whoever deals it, and is kept in the table's state as it was dealt. The old `origin:topics` key is no longer read or written, and is left alone.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the map takes the card's left side, all its height, and the clues, the guesses and the box to type in stand at the right, high, clear of a tablet's keyboard.

Together (October 2026): one run for the table, played as a team, from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six, each on their own phone (`together.js`). Every phone shows the same country, the same clues and the same guesses, and anyone guesses (typed, or tapped twice on their own map: the first tap names a country on that phone only), takes another clue, gives up (asked first) or moves on with Next; the score is the team's. Each guess carries its player's initial on their seat's colour (README, Rooms), the strip shows who's at the table the same way, yours ringed and anyone away greyed, and a decided country says who found it or gave up on it. The first tap counts: every change is a transaction made only if the table is still as the tapping phone showed it (the game, the country and, for a clue, the clues showing), so two Another clue taps at once turn up one clue, two Nexts move on one country, and a guess that lands after someone else's right one does nothing and says so ("Too late: Sarah found it"). Nobody waits for anybody, so a phone that sleeps or leaves holds nothing up. The end, on every phone, is the team's total, with Another game and Back to solo; the menu's Together part deals a new game for the table (asked first while one is under way) and says who's there. The pair's record gets a team result, the total, the moment the last country is decided (a win with all five found); a Party's game isn't the pair's. Your solo run, best and today's stay as they were, and a table in the address is joined under the rete, with no solo run dealt beneath it. It replaced, the same day, a head-to-head on `duel.js`, everyone racing the same five countries on their own phone: Korbi wanted it friendly, anyone guessing. `tests/origin.mjs` plays a table of three through the database's own round trip (guesses by several players, the races above, the record, a new game); three phones in one browser played it end to end, and a pair from the partner sheet's address.
