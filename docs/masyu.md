# Pearl

Draw one closed loop through the middles of the cells of a grid, from cell to neighbouring cell, passing every pearl (31 October 2026, Korbi's list of genres to add). A white pearl is passed straight through, and the loop turns in the cell just before it or just after it (or both); a black pearl is a turn, and the loop goes straight on for at least one more cell each way. The loop never crosses, branches or touches itself, and need not visit every cell. Every puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning, never by a guess.

Playing (`masyu.js`). Drag from cell to cell and the loop follows the finger: each cell it enters draws the line from the one before (a finger that goes back over the last line takes it back, and one that starts on a line rubs lines out instead). A stroke is one step to undo, so is a hint's and so is Clear the board (an undo entry is `[edge, from, to, stroke]`, the stroke a time, so a saved run's numbers never meet a new one). A tap on a line between two cells cycles it as in Circuit (line, crossed, clear), a hold or a right click crosses it, and only the lines count: the puzzle is solved the moment exactly its answer is drawn. A cell the finger is only just inside is ignored until it is well in, so a finger wavering on a boundary doesn't flick between two cells. A rule broken shows at once, from the rules alone (three lines in a cell, a dead end, a pearl no way is left to meet in oxblood, a loop closed past the wrong pearls), a pearl the lines meet turns green, and the solved loop turns gilt from the top left.

Sizes 6×6, 8×8 and 10×10 (the menu; always even, for the loops below); three levels (the header, mirrored in the menu). Nothing comes from the knowledge base. Today's puzzle is an 8×8 at Medium. Bests, the daily, spares made in the background, a link with the seed, the run in `masyu:run`, Random's round and the clock work as Circuit's do (`masyu:times`, `masyu:today`, `masyu:prefs`, `masyu:spares`).

Making a puzzle (`masyu-engine.js`, off the page in `masyu-worker.js`). The loop is the outline of a random tree of 2×2 blocks: each block is a cell of an inside region on the board one smaller each way, each branch of the tree a cell joining two, so the outline is one loop that never meets itself and passes through every cell of the Pearl board, unless leaves of the tree were cut off first (a share of 55 to 100% of the blocks is kept, so loops of every length come, and some skip cells). Circuit's way, a blob of cells grown at random, gave outlines of short steps with almost nowhere to put a pearl: none of the 27 puzzles tried that way was settled by reasoning even with every pearl its loop could have. Then every pearl the loop can have is put on (a white one on a straight cell with a turn beside it, a black one on a turn with a straight cell on each side), and pearls are taken away in random order, white ones first so the black ones, which say more, stay, each only for as long as a solver of the level asked for can still finish the puzzle from what is left. That proves the answer unique, as Circuit's does. A puzzle whose every pearl doesn't settle it at any level is thrown away and another loop made (up to ten).

The rules a solver follows are the loop kernel's (a cell has two lines or none; a line may join the two ends of one path only when that closes the whole answer) and the pearls': a white pearl lies along one axis, the one where both its sides are open, nothing is a line across it and the cells on either side don't both go straight on (so a white pearl against the board's edge runs along it, and a white pearl whose run goes straight on at one end must turn at the other); a black pearl turns off one way in each axis, a way being open when its edge and the next one on are not crossed, the cell beside it is not itself a black pearl (a black pearl can't be gone straight through), and the way back isn't already a line. Easy: those alone. Medium: a trial as well, an edge supposed a line or crossed and the rules followed for two rounds. Hard: a trial followed to the end. The hint says which: a white pearl with one axis left, a white pearl's neighbour that must turn, a black pearl with one way round left, then the kernel's rules, then a trial.

Score and bests as Circuit's. Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height. The rules are a sheet of their own, from Rules in the header or The rules in the menu. The colour is jade (`--msy-in`), the pearls paper and ink.

`tests/masyu.mjs` (about three seconds) checks the board's cells, edges and the way back along each, the solver against every loop of a 4×4 and 5×5 board for 150 sets of pearls (118 with several answers, so a solver that guessed would show), that every pearl a loop can have is met by it, 15 puzzles across every size and level (one answer each, a loop passing every pearl as the rules say, both colours of pearl, loops that skip cells, the answer the solver finds, the level asked for), a seed's puzzle the same every time and today's the same for a day, each level needing its hardest step and no harder, marks and the rules they break, hints (every step true of the answer and enough alone to solve a puzzle), a run through JSON and the database's round trip, and the page wired to the engine.

Not done: the head-to-head and a split board on `duel.js`, the computer rival, boards past 10×10 and odd sizes (the tree loop needs an even side), and Expert.

Co-op (3 November 2026, `docs/coop.md`, `masyu-coop.js`): a paired partner joins and draws on the same loop; a shared solve earns no best, today's time or Random round, and the host's `masyu:run` follows the shared board. A stroke is many edge edits, so undoing it puts back each edge only if it is still as you left it: a line your partner drew or rubbed out since stays theirs.
