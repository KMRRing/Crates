# Brut
Wine. The name is the driest common Champagne and the French for crude oil (*le brut*).

Blind: a flight of six classic wines (103 in all: the first 39 run from Chablis and Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc to Barolo, Amarone, Vintage Port and Dézaley Chasselas; on 31 October 2026, at Korbi's asking, 64 more went in: whites from the Loire (Pouilly-Fumé, Muscadet, Savennières), Alsace, Italy, Iberia and Greece; Germany's Pfalz and Bavaria's Franken, with its Silvaner; Austria and Württemberg (Blaufränkisch under two names); Switzerland's Geneva (Satigny's Chasselas, Aligoté, Gamay and Gamaret), the Valais (Petite Arvine, Cornalin) and Ticino's Merlot; Cava, Franciacorta, English sparkling, Moscato d'Asti and a dry Lambrusco; Tokaji, icewine, Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise, Madeira, Oloroso, Pedro Ximénez and Rutherglen Muscat; and more of the New World: Margaret River, Coonawarra, McLaren Vale, Hawke's Bay, Oregon, Washington, Uruguay and the Cape, Pinotage among them), each with what a taster finds at each stage. You see it first: the colour in a drawn glass (bubbles rising for sparkling wines) and the words for it; taste further for the nose, then the palate (sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body and finish as dots), then the conclusions (Old or New World, climate, oak). Name it from six candidates, drawn from the same colour by likeness: the same grape from elsewhere, then the wines nearest on the palate. A right call pays 100 on sight, 70 on the nose, 45 on the palate, 25 on the conclusions, 15 off for each wrong call; the reveal gives the tell ("Pale in the glass but huge in tannin and acid, tar and roses: Nebbiolo"). A new flight or today's flight (the same for everyone); best flights kept; grapes on the candidates can be hidden from the menu for a harder flight. A wine you couldn't name before the palate goes to the pile.

Study: the wine bank (`wine-bank.js`), 162 cards in twelve units: grapes; Bordeaux (the banks, 1855, the first growths, Saint-Émilion's 2022 reshuffle, Sauternes); Burgundy and Beaujolais; Champagne and sparkling (the method, the sweetness scale, the houses); the Rhône, Loire, Alsace and Provence; Italy; Spain and Portugal (Rioja's ageing, sherry, Port, Madeira); Germany, Austria and Switzerland (Prädikat, Franken, the Wachau, Chasselas, Lavaux, the Valais, Geneva's Satigny); the New World; making wine; service, pairing and faults; the trade (en primeur, the Place de Bordeaux, Liv-ex, in bond, the Lafite bubble, grape marc in Annex IX). A new card shows the fact and then asks a question on it; due cards and missed wines come first; up to 15 new a day. The same cards are Punt's Wine level. `brut-data.js` holds the wines, `brut-engine.js` the flight, candidates and points; `tests/brut.mjs` checks them.

The page fits the screen: in a tasting the glass and its note take the room left (a long note scrolls inside, so the options never move); the units list and a study card scroll inside themselves. With a hundred wines some names and regions run long, and on a phone three rows of tall candidate cards squeezed the glass until the note spilled over the heading below it (Madeira Bual, Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise): a candidate's grape and region is now at most two lines, its cards a little tighter (79 px at the tallest, three rows 250 px), and the glass shrinks into its frame when the room is short, as on a 390×600 screen, rather than standing over it. `tests/brut.mjs` keeps the data to what fits: no name over 32 characters, no grape-and-region line over 54, no more than three wines with the same palate dots (so the palate stage always tells something), every wine's nose and tell its own.

Every game's header dropdown (the level, mode, size or course) is the suite's own, drawn by `dropdown.js` in the game's logo colours rather than the phone's plain list: Crates, Punt, Slate, Delta, Survey, Parley, Brut, Blend and Refinery (its crude picker too). (Rush had one for its clock, until its clocks went into the menu as every rush's are.)

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the glass, larger, and its notes stand on the left and your guess beside them, the buttons low; in study, the course and its units, and a card and its answers, stand side by side.

Together (October 2026): a blind flight for both of you, the same six wines in the same order with the same candidates, grapes shown or not as the one who dealt it had them (the menu's setting deals the next match), each tasting and calling on their own phone (README, Head to head), from the partner sheet. The others' scores and which wine they're on show beside yours. The highest total wins, then the quicker; the result lists your flight with each wine's tell. A miss goes to the pile as in any flight; your own flight, bests and today's stay as they were. A party doesn't offer it (October 2026): a long, quiet game makes a poor round at a table of friends.
