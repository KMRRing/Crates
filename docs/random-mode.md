# Random mode

A game at random, and when its round is over, straight on to the next (27 October 2026, Korbi: "add a "random" option to almanac that just takes a game (user can select which ones can appear) and every time a round is over, goes straight to the next one"). It's a way to play, not a game, so it isn't a tile: in the banner under the games screen's head (one row of three buttons, a third each: Deck, Random and Choose), a plate, ⤮ Random, opens one of the games chosen, and Choose opens the sheet, Random's side of it: every game random mode can deal, by the games screen's sections, each a tick box, with All and None for each section (menu.js's `ticks`, its include kind), every one ticked at first. The choice is this device's own (`suite:random`, which never syncs: the phone and the iPad may want different games), kept as the games taken out, so a game added later comes in, as every game does at first. With nothing ticked, Random can't start and the choice says to tick a game. Deck's plate is beside it, and Choose's other side is Deck's choice (README, Deck, "Scope"); all three can be put away in Settings.

While random mode plays a game, the game's title wears RANDOM beside its name (a tag in the room the header has left, as your partner's pill takes it: whole, its ⤮ mark alone where there's little room, nothing where there's none, so it never moves the header's buttons; Dandan, full screen, has no title to wear it), and its menu opens on a Random part, Next game (skip this one now) and Stop (random mode ends, this game stays), as the games screen's banner does then (the plate says Next, and a Stop button joins the row between Random and Choose). The moment the round's result is final (a puzzle solved or given up, a hand or a deal settled, a run's last answer, an idle game's game over), a banner over the page names the next game, its logo and its name in the house's capitals, and counts three seconds, the figure and a brass fuse under it running down, then opens it; Stay ends random mode and leaves you where you are, the result in front of you. The countdown stands still while the page is out of sight or you've opened the games screen, a menu or the rules, and goes on when you're back; a result the game itself put up in such a dialog doesn't hold it. The next game is any of those chosen but the one just played, unless it's the only one, and it opens as its tile does: on today's daily while that's still to play.

A run of random mode is the session's (`suite:random-run`, the game it sent you to): closing the app ends it, and so does opening any other page yourself, a tile, a link or the games screen's own page; the same game reloading (an update, a sync) plays on. It's solo only: never in a partner's watching frame, a duo match, a table, a party or an offline party, and a page that sits down at a table without reloading drops it. A round only drawn again as it was left (a finished board reopened) isn't one played: random mode waits for a tap or a key on the page before it counts one. Where a game counted its result only when its summary was opened (Arb's run, Brut's flight, Manifest's run), it now counts it the moment it's final, as the dailies do (README, The games screen's figures), so leaving on the banner loses nothing.

What counts as a round is each game's own, the natural unit you'd stop at:
- **Knowledge:** Crates a board, solved or out of lives; Punt a run, its last bet settled (an endless one when you end it); Quote and Chart a set, its last quote settled or pin in; Origin a run of five countries, Arb one of five routes and Nexus one of five rounds, the last decided; Order a run, its last card placed or its lives gone; Crossword a grid, solved or revealed.
- **Courses:** Calibre a level, its movement running; Blend an order certified, or a desk's tanks closed; Refinery a level run to more stars than it had; Brut a flight of six wines named, or a study session; Parley a study session.
- **Puzzles:** Slate and Delta a board, solved or given up; Spot a run, its lives gone; Manifest a run, twelve rounds or its lives gone; Harbour a level's plan run to delivery, or a week of a region's season; Pipes a level, cleared or spilled, or a descent; Survey a block cleared; Stow a manifest stowed, or a level of loose cargo won or stuck.
- **Classics:** Sudoku, Calcudoku, Kakuro, Codeword, Hitori, Slitherlink, Masyu, Nurikabe, Bridges and Isomer a puzzle solved; Mines a field, cleared or blown up; Nonogram a picture finished, and named when naming is on (the round waits for the name); Bulls a code broken or its rows run out, a duel settled or a lock opened; Assay a word, found or its guesses gone; Yield, whose puzzles have no end of their own, Master refiner reached (Spelling Bee's Genius), or the puzzle over: every word, a sprint's clock, or the words shown; Ladder a ladder climbed; Lode a board finished; Acrostic a text solved.
- **Idle:** Heliograph, Shunt, Polder, Topple (in Levels, the level), Orrery, Avalanche (a lesson's day isn't one), Netting, Settle and Drift the game over, after its last move has played out; Slipway a part carved to a star, or an assembly that holds in the gale; Loom a cloth woven; Guilloché a plate, every zone cut true.
- **Board & card games:** a hand where the game is dealt hand by hand, else the game: Hong, Tribute and Meld a hand; Big Two, Landlord, Pegs and Contract a deal (one thrown in or passed out, dealt again, isn't one); Trumps and Bones a hand; Gammon a game of the match; Boast a round of bidding, its cups lifted; Banquet a match, its throws being seconds each; Cartel, River, Gambit, Factor and Dandan a game. A game left mid-match is there as it was when random mode comes back to it.
- **Casino:** Baccarat a coup, Blackjack a hand, Roulette a spin, Sic Bo a roll, Craps a pass line decided (a come-out's natural or craps, the point made or a seven out), Hold'em a hand.

Left out: Party and its games, Cue and Quest, made for people round a table; Lexicon and Ledger, the knowledge base and the record of how you learn, not games; and Timegrapher, an instrument. Deck, the review of every game's misses, has no tile and so isn't in the list: it deals only what's due, and often has nothing to play. A puzzle rush (Gambit's page of its own, or a rush in Hong, Tribute, River or on a sheet) is a mode of its game and ends no round.

The rules are `random-core.js`'s, pure: which games and what a round of each is (`ROUNDS`, with `LEFT_OUT` and why), the choice kept, the pick (`pickNext`), the solo-only address and a seats game's round. `random.js` is the page's side, loaded by every page through the games screen (`apps.js`' `bindRandom`): the plate and the choice's pane (the sheet itself is `banner.js`'s, shared with Deck's side), the tag, the menu's part (menu.js's `menuHead`, at the head of every game's menu as a party's part is), the banner and its countdown. A game says its round is over with `roundOver(id)`, its id on the games screen, the moment the result is final, from its own end-of-round code; a game on `seats.js` gives every view to `seatRounds(id, steps)`, which counts the game's end and, where it's dealt hand by hand, the steps that end a hand (shown, scored, a game of a match), only as they change. `tests/random.mjs` checks every game on the games screen is dealt or left out, every game dealt calls it from its own files and names itself, the pick never deals the game just played while another is chosen, a choice of nothing can't start, and the choice, the run, the address and a seats game's steps; a change to either module runs every test (`tools/pick-tests.mjs`), since every game loads them. Looked at in a browser at 390×664, 390×844, 820×1180 and 1180×820, in all four themes by day and night: the row, the choice, the tag, the menu's part, a real round (a Roulette spin, a Sic Bo roll, a hand of Blackjack, Crates, Lots and Assay boards) ending in the banner and the next game, Next game, Stop and Stay.
