# Gears
Wind a watch's train: every level is a plate and a hand of whole gears that must all fit one train from the mainspring to the dial, with stars. Every square holds a **single** gear (teeth to all four edges), a **duo** (two small gears meshed inside the square) or a **trio** (three); only the two end gears of a duo or trio reach out, one to each of two sides (straight or round a corner). Gears are always drawn whole, never clipped. Gears was Pipes until 4 November 2026, from Pipe Dream (1989) by way of a fitting puzzle.

- **Where his sentence and the physics differ (I followed the physics; say if you meant something else):** (1) Meshing gears turn *opposite* ways; that is normal, not a lock. A lock is two meshing gears forced to turn the *same* way (an odd loop of meshes), so that is what jams. (2) A duo, not a trio, is the inverter: the outside neighbours of a 2-gear square turn opposite ways, of a 1- or 3-gear square the same way (n gears: same if n is odd). A trio differs from a single in geometry and reach, not parity.

## Rules
- Power leaves the mainspring (clockwise); every gear's turn is the 2-colouring of the graph of meshes from it. The dial must turn the way its arrow shows; a **load** (ring with an arrow) takes a single gear only, which must turn that way.
- A **jam**: meshing gears forced the same way. Squares side by side mesh wherever both have a port facing, wanted or not, so a train that touches itself beside a duo jams. Jammed gears flash oxblood, stop, and the level is not solved while any jam stands.
- Solved: every piece laid, no jam, every gear on one train (a path through all pieces from the mainspring to the dial) driven by the mainspring, loads and dial turning their way.

## Traps
- A level's `id` is its identity: stars (`pipes:trainstars`) and runs (`pipes:train`) are kept by it. Levels are `w<chapter><n>`; new levels go at the end of a chapter with a new id; a run keeps a signature of its level and starts over if that changed. Levels are written by `node pipes-train-pick.mjs`, not by hand; the tests re-solve all of them and want the listed answers and no others. The 4 November levels replaced the old forty (`g<chapter><n>`, keys `pipes:puzzle`, `pipes:stars`): their content was different, so those stars are deleted at start-up rather than carried over.
- All pieces share one tooth size (a wheel of n teeth has pitch radius 2.5 n), so any two mesh; ports sit at the middle of an edge, tangent. `tests/pipes-train.mjs` checks the geometry of every piece. Small gears have 4 to 6 teeth by design (legible on a phone).
- Elbow trios are drawn so the end gears don't touch: three gears all touching would be an odd loop, a jam inside the piece.
- The solver counts answers by distinct pieces on distinct squares, and prunes with the same jam rule as play; a level may list several answers (early chapters), and `layRoute` writes a route as N E S W (single), n e s w (duo), ^ > v < (trio).
- Teeth phase: `phasesOf` turns each wheel from the one it was reached by so a tooth lies in a gap; a closed loop can leave a pair slightly off (a picture only). The solved train turns with the Web Animations API (each wheel's duration is set by its tooth count); the board draws the ground first and the wheels over all of it so a tooth reaching into the next square is not painted over. No SVG filters (Safari blurs them). Only Chromium has been looked at.
- The 3D map's old-model engine (`pipes-puzzle*.js`, `pipes-map-*.js`, `pipes-draw.js`'s old drawing) is kept whole and tested (`tests/pipes-puzzle.mjs`, `tests/pipes-map.mjs`); its saved keys `pipes:map`, `pipes:mapstars` are never touched. `pipes-pieces.js` and parts of `pipes-draw.js` are shared with the new engine.
- The sound is synthesised (`pipes-sound.js`, no files), the menu's Sound toggle (`pipes:prefs`), and silent in a watching frame; a new jam is two dull clacks.
