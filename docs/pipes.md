# Gears
Wind a watch's train: every level is a plate and a hand of gears that must all fit one train from the mainspring to the load, with stars. The menu's Board chooses where: **Flat (square)** or the **Cube (3D map)**, a stack of floors where gears also join up and down on arbors (shafts). Gears was Pipes until 4 November 2026, from Pipe Dream (1989) by way of a fitting puzzle.

- To confirm with Korbi: the display name is Gears, but the id stays `pipes` (files `pipes*.js`, saved keys `pipes:`) so stars, runs and synced devices survive. Renaming the id would orphan them.
- The special "movement" pieces are my choice, not Korbi's: the **reduction** (two small gears in one square that flip the turn), the **arbor** (map only: joins up and down without meshing), fixed gears, numbered loads with a way to turn, and the crossover.

## Rules
- Power leaves the mainspring (turns clockwise) and runs through gears edge to edge; each opening is a mesh. Meshed gears turn opposite ways, so a gear's way is its distance from the mainspring in meshes. The load (a watch dial) must turn the way its arrow shows, and every numbered load must turn its way, reached in order.
- A reduction passes the turn on flipped: the one piece that changes the count. An arbor (up/down) passes it on unflipped. This is the heart of the deduction: a plain grid is bipartite, so without them every way is decided by the position alone.
- A closed loop back to the mainspring jams if its last gear would turn it the other way (only possible through a reduction); the cross says so.

## Traps
- A level's `id` is its identity: stars (`pipes:stars`, `pipes:mapstars`) and runs (`pipes:puzzle`, `pipes:map`) are kept by it. Flat levels are `g<chapter><n>`, map levels `m<chapter><n>`; new levels go at the end of a chapter with a new id; a run also keeps a signature of its level and starts over if that changed. Levels are written by `node pipes-puzzle-pick.mjs` (flat) and `node pipes-map-pick.mjs` (map), not by hand; the tests re-solve all of them and want the listed routes and no others. Changing a target or recipe re-picks the chapter and the stars kept for it mean something else.
- Level text: a digit marks a load that turns clockwise, a letter `a`-`i` one that turns counter-clockwise; the level's `spin` is the dial's way. A route lists the side it leaves each square by; a lower-case letter marks a reduction. Old ids (`a1`...) from the Pipes levels no longer exist, so earlier stars show as unplayed.
- The puzzle wins when the power, run through what is laid, goes through every gear and fixed gear, passes the loads in order each turning its way, and enters the dial turning its way: it never compares with the listed answer, so another train that works is a solve. A hint caps the level at one star, and followed from any state it ends in an answer (tested).
- Squares are numbered `z * cols * rows + y * cols + x`; sides 0 N, 1 E, 2 S, 3 W, 4 U, 5 D. Pieces never turn: the map has 18 kinds plus the reductions, and a hand shows only the kinds in it. Piece ids (`h`, `ne`, `x`...) are the old pipe names; they are shapes, not words on screen.
- The map is drawn as one SVG plate per floor under an affine matrix from `pipes-map-view.js`'s orthographic camera, so each plate is the flat board's own picture and taps go back through the inverse. Pitch is clamped above the horizon: the painter's order is floor 0 first. The arbors between floors are drawn on the screen, not on a plate. No SVG filters (Safari blurs them).
- A gear's teeth are clipped to its openings, and neighbours alternate tooth phase on a checkerboard so they interleave; the solved train turns with the Web Animations API (each gear's duration is set by its place in the train, the dial's hands follow). Only Chromium has been looked at.
- The sound is synthesised (`pipes-sound.js`, no files), the menu's Sound toggle (`pipes:prefs`), and silent in a watching frame.
- The camera and the floor in front (`view`, `layer`) live in `pipes:map`, so a watching frame sees the same; its gestures and writes go nowhere.
- The buttons under the board belong to whichever board shows: `pipes.js` wires them; a board's `hide()` must give back what its `show()` changed.
