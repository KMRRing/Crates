# Pipes
Lay pipe for the oil: every level is a board and a hand of pieces that must all fit one route from the wellhead to the terminal, with stars. The menu's Board chooses where: **Flat** (a grid, pieces never turned), the **Cube** (the same puzzle on the faces of a cube you turn with a finger; pieces turn) or the **Dodecahedron** (twelve pentagons: tight and easy bends only). From Pipe Dream (1989), by way of a fitting puzzle.

## Rulings
- 3 November 2026, the plan: "One best-effort level-based fitting puzzle for you to test-play; axed if it still is not fun." The queue board (Plan, Rush) and the 3D shaft were cut the same day.
- 3 November 2026, Korbi: "pipes: the puzzle format is right. this same idea, for 3D. remove the old 2 attempts ... maybe also a dodecahedron or something mode. cube definitely, not sure if there's another shape that'd work". So the cube is the point; the dodecahedron is the "maybe": 12 tiles and levels that never need more than about a thousand tries of the solver make it a short, easy extra (cut it by taking it out of `MODES` in `pipes.js`).

## Traps
- A level's `id` is its identity: stars (`pipes:stars`, `pipes:cubestars`, `pipes:dodecastars`) and runs (`pipes:puzzle`, `pipes:cube`, `pipes:dodeca`) are kept by it. New levels go at the end of a chapter with a new id; a run also keeps a signature of its level and starts over if that changed. Levels are written by `node pipes-puzzle-pick.mjs` (flat) and `node pipes-solid-pick.mjs cube|dodeca`, not by hand; the tests re-solve all of them and want the listed routes and no others. Changing a target or recipe re-picks the chapter and the stars kept for it mean something else.
- The puzzle wins when the oil, run through what is laid, goes through every piece and old pipe, passes the stakes in order and enters the terminal: it never compares with the listed answer, so another route that works is a solve. A hint caps the level at one star, and followed from any state, even one with wrong pieces, it ends in an answer (tested).
- A solid's tile sides are numbered counter-clockwise seen from outside, and a tile's art square (60 units, as the flat puzzle's) has side 0 up. `artFrame` in `pipes-solid-3d.js` carries that square onto the solid, and `tests/pipes-solid.mjs` checks a pipe leaving one tile lands on the middle of the next tile's side: change the numbering and every pipe breaks at the edges.
- The solid is drawn on a 2D canvas with an orthographic camera, no WebGL: a convex solid's visible faces never overlap, so nothing is sorted. This only holds for convex solids. `pipes-solid-3d.js` must load in Node (the test imports it), so `Path2D` is made when first drawn.
- Everything the cube shows is in its keys (the camera is `view` in `pipes:cube`), so a watching frame sees the same board; its taps and writes go nowhere.
- A bend laid by a tap is turned to take the oil as it stands, and where it could go two ways, the way that leads nearer the terminal (`fitted` with `way`). Tapping a piece laid turns it a step; the Lift button, then a tap, takes it back (only that counts towards the third star).
- The buttons under the board belong to whichever board shows: `pipes.js` wires them to it; a board's `hide()` must give back what its `show()` changed (the flat puzzle's Clear button, the board's classes).
