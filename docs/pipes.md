# Pipes
Lay pipe for the oil. The menu's Game chooses: the **Puzzle** (levels: a hand of pieces that must all fit one route from the wellhead to the terminal, with stars), **Plan** (a board: pieces come off a queue and the oil moves a square for each one you lay), **Rush** (the same board on a clock) and **3D** (a descent down a shaft). From Pipe Dream (1989).

## Rulings
- October 2026: Pipes was a board of scrambled tiles to turn before the oil came, grown into a roguelike of jobs, boons and bosses; it wasn't fun, and drawing a route between ends is Delta's and Flow's ground. So the pieces come off a queue and you lay them.
- October 2026: Korbi found it too fast. The menu's Pace slows Rush and 3D (Plan has no clock), and changes the game under way at once. A slower game is an easier one, so each pace keeps bests of its own, and a run counts under the slowest pace it was played at.
- November 2026, Korbi on the board: "more of a minigame now, and not fun enough. too stressful, fiddly. I think idea direction good, needs work though". The cause was the clock: the game was how fast a finger finds a square. So **Plan**, the default of the board, has no clock, and two taps for a piece (the first shows what laying it does, the second lays it; a mis-tap costs nothing) and Undo (three pieces a level). Rush stays as it was.
- 3 November 2026, the plan: "One best-effort level-based fitting puzzle for you to test-play; axed if it still is not fun." Built; the verdict is below.

## Verdict on the Puzzle (3 November 2026, by the agent that built it; no person has test-played it)
Provably fair and clean, but only mildly fun, and I am not sure it earns a place. 30 levels, six chapters, each with one answer (a solver proves it). Chapter 1 is nearly trivial; chapters 2 to 5 are solved by counting the hand's bends and trying, which is a pleasant minute, not an aha; the last chapter needs real deduction and leans on trial and error. A player who tries pieces at random needs about 12 to 78 squares tried on chapter 1 and 500 to 2,100 on chapter 6. Missing: handmade levels with a single clever trick (these are generated, so none has one); a reason to come back (no daily, no stars on the games screen: `suite.js`'s `markOf` is shared); a human's difficulty curve. Keep it as a small mode if Korbi enjoys chapters 2 and 3 on his phone; if not, cut `pipes-puzzle*.js` and the puzzle branches in `pipes.js` and keep `pipes-draw.js`, which the board shares.

## Traps
- A Puzzle level's `id` is its identity: stars (`pipes:stars`) and the run (`pipes:puzzle`) are kept by it. New levels go at the end of a chapter with a new id; a run also keeps a signature of its map and starts over if the map was changed. Levels are written by `node pipes-puzzle-pick.mjs`, not by hand, and `tests/pipes-puzzle.mjs` re-solves all of them and wants the listed routes and no others. Changing a target or recipe there re-picks the chapter and the stars kept for it mean something else.
- The Puzzle wins when the oil, run through what is laid, goes through every piece and old pipe, passes the stakes in order and enters the terminal: it never compares with the listed answer, so another route that works is a solve. A hint (`hintFor`) caps the level at one star, and followed from any state, even one with wrong pieces, it ends in an answer (tested).
- The first time Pipes opens with the Puzzle in it the mode is set to the Puzzle once (`puzzleSeen` in `pipes:prefs`); after that the choice is the player's. `leavePuzzle()` must clear the board's `--cols`/`--rows` and its other leftovers, or the other modes draw on the Puzzle's shape.
- Runs are kept per mode: `pipes:plan`, `pipes:flat` (Rush's: a run with no `flow` is Rush's, so an old save plays on as it was), `pipes:deep`, `pipes:puzzle`. Changing the Game leaves the others where they were. Bests are `pipes:best` by mode (`plan`, `flat`, `deep`, plus `:slower`/`:slowest`); they are not comparable, and the games screen shows the highest. Everything a game shows is in these keys, so a partner's watching frame sees the same board; a watching frame runs a Plan "Run the oil" or a Rush clock on only by the time since it was kept (up to half a minute), and holds the oil where it is when the save says the game stands still. A mode just switched to is kept at once, or a frame runs it on from when it was last played.
- Plan's lead (the pieces laid before the oil leaves: `levelOf`) is the one number that sets its difficulty. It was set against a bot that plans a few squares ahead and cannot stop to think, so people with Undo should find it easier than the bot did; if they do, move the lead. `winsNow` (can the route as laid clear the quota) must agree with running the oil out, which the test checks.
- Rush's boards are checked as dealt (`pipes-engine.js`): the wellhead off the edge with two clear squares ahead, the terminal at least five squares from it with its inlet open, no open ground walled off. A new board feature must keep those true.
- A match deals one seed in the board's mode as dealt (a match dealt before Plan existed was Rush) at its dealer's pace; equal scores draw (the duel's usual tie-break, the quicker run, would favour a descent the oil caught first). The Puzzle has no match: the duel's menu keeps the 3D toggle.
- 3D's shaft is made from its seed twelve layers at a time (`pipes-deep.js`), each stretch round a clear way down, so there is always one; slabs need two gaps (with one the bot died at the first more often than not). `pipes-3d.js` draws on a canvas by hand, with the colours the page gives as `--pi-stone`, `--pi-wall`, `--pi-oil`, `--pi-label` (Modern, Kontor and Office have their own in `themes.css`).
- Old Pipes' saves and bests were another game's and were cleared the first time the new one opened.
