# Pipes
Pipe Dream's game (1989), on a board and down a shaft in 3D (October 2026). Pipes was a board of scrambled tiles to turn before the oil came, grown into a roguelike of jobs, boons and bosses; it wasn't fun, and drawing a route between ends is Delta's ground (and Flow's), so it was rethought: the pieces now come off a queue and you lay them, and the pressure is the oil coming after you.

**The board.** Seven squares across, nine down. The next piece is the large one at the left of the queue (five on show); tap a square and it goes there, at once, on the finger going down. Pieces are never turned: two straights, four bends and the crossing come as they are, dealt in shuffled bags of thirteen (two of each straight and bend, one crossing), so no shape stays away for long, as it can when each piece is drawn on its own (Tetris deals in bags for the same reason). Tap one of your pieces the oil hasn't reached and the next piece replaces it: a blast, costing 50 points and 0.7 s before you can lay again.

After a countdown the oil leaves the wellhead and runs from piece to piece; it must pass through the level's quota of pipe before it spills, off the edge, into rock, into an empty square, a piece that doesn't take it in, or pipe it has already filled. Short of the quota the spill ends the run (one life, as in Pipe Dream); past it, the level is cleared and the next waits behind the level's card. A crossing carries the oil straight across either way and can be passed twice, once each way: both passes count toward the quota and the second pays a loop's 200. Pumps (from level 3) are fixed straights paying 250 when the oil goes through; tanks (from level 5) are fixed straights that take four times as long to fill, time in hand; the terminal (from level 4) pays 1,000 and ends the level cleanly when the oil is led into it past the quota, and refuses it sooner (a spill). Points: 50 a piece the oil passes through; on a level cleared, 25 less for each piece of yours it never reached, as Pipe Dream charged (on the level that ends the run nothing more is taken: a run's total is never below what its levels made). Oil now starts the oil before the countdown is out, 10 points for each whole second left (paid on a level cleared: let go on an empty board, the oil spills at once, and that was once a score); once it flows, Hurry runs it eight times faster to the end of the level and everything it fills from then scores double. A dashed line runs through the pieces the oil will pass as the board stands, the square its route needs next is ringed, and a red cross marks where it would spill; the line under the board counts the route against the quota.

The levels: level 1 gives 25 s of countdown, 3 s a piece and a quota of 8 on an empty board; each level after is 10% faster, two pieces longer and has a rock more, the countdown 1.5 s shorter, until level 15 (0.75 s a piece, a quota of 32, 8 s of countdown, twelve rocks, two pumps and two tanks), and from there they stay as hard. Every board is checked as it's dealt (`pipes-engine.js`): the wellhead off the edge with two clear squares ahead of its outlet, the terminal at least five squares away with its inlet open, pumps and tanks open at both ends, and no open ground walled off. The curve was set against a bot that plays as a person does, laying a piece every 1.3 s: it keeps in mind the one of forty quick routes from the open end that the five pieces on show fill furthest before the oil gets there, lays each piece on the earliest square of it that fits, and dumps the rest far off. It clears level 1 always, level 3 96% of the time, level 6 about 80% and level 10 about 8%, so a run usually ends between levels 6 and 9; slower bots (1.8 s a piece) fail sooner, and seeing ten pieces instead of five made it markedly stronger, which is why five show.

**Plan (November 2026).** Korbi, on the board: "more of a minigame now, and not fun enough. too stressful, fiddly. I think idea direction good, needs work though". The cause was that the oil ran on a clock, so the game was how fast a finger can find a square, and a decision (which of five pieces goes where, so that the route stays open) had no time to be made. The board now plays two ways, chosen by the menu's Board choice: **Plan**, the default, and **Rush**, everything described above, kept as it was. In Plan nothing runs on a clock. The oil waits at the wellhead until the level's first pieces are down (15 on level 1, `lead` in `levelOf`: `max(5, round(15 − n/2))`, so 5 from level 20), then moves one square for every piece you lay: into the next piece if there is one, else it spills. A tank holds it for four pieces, a blast (laying over a piece of yours the oil hasn't reached, still 50 points) is a piece like any, and the points are Rush's less what only a clock has (Oil now's seconds, Hurry). What you have to beat is the pieces, not the seconds, so you may stop and look as long as you like, and the five pieces on show are the information a decision is made on: the road you've laid ahead of the oil is slack, each piece laid elsewhere spends one square of it, and a bend you needed and didn't get is a gap to plan round.

*Two taps, a preview and a way back.* The first tap on a square picks it: the piece shows there, faint, in a dashed frame, with the route the oil would take drawn as it would run with the piece laid, and the line under the board says what laying it does (the route's length against the quota; a red frame and "the oil spills…" where laying it there would end the level at once; "Lay again to blast it" over a piece of yours). The second tap on the same square lays it; a tap elsewhere picks that square instead. It is two taps for a piece because a stressful, fiddly game was the complaint, and a mis-tap now costs nothing. **Undo** takes back the last piece, and the oil's step with it and the queue with it, three times a level (a level's state before each of the last three pieces is kept with the run, as JSON of the engine's state: `tests/pipes-plan.mjs` checks that a level saved and read back at any piece plays on exactly as it would have). **Run the oil** lights once the route as laid clears the quota (and isn't led into the terminal short of it: `winsNow`, which the test checks against actually running it out in 446 positions); the oil then walks the route at a square every 40 to 140 ms, the whole run taking about three seconds, and the level ends as it would have (all at once if the phone is set to reduce motion). Unused pieces cost their 25 each, so there's a reason not to lay more than you need, and a reason to when the next piece wouldn't fit anywhere useful.

*How hard.* The lead is the one number that sets the difficulty: more pieces before the oil leaves is more road to lay before anything is at stake, but also more pieces that must be spent somewhere useful. It was set by a bot (`tests/pipes-plan-bot.js`) that plans a route several squares ahead, keeping only the stretch of it that the five pieces on show can fill in time, and lays the next piece where the plan can use it, a few tries a move. At the lead above it clears level 1 every time (30 of 30), level 6 in 22 of 30 and level 12 in 3 of 30, the same thirty seeds each time. A person who can stop and think, and has Undo, should do better than that, so the curve probably runs easier for people than for the bot; if it does, the lead is the number to move (`levelOf`), and the test's bands are wide enough to take a change of a piece or two. Four leads were measured over the same thirty seeds (levels 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, the bot's wins): `max(4, 14 − n)` 63, 10, 10, 0, 0%, a cliff between levels 6 and 8; `max(5, 16 − n)` 67, 30, 13, 3, 0%; `max(6, 18 − n)` 73, 43, 27, 7, 0%; and the one kept, `max(5, 15 − n/2)`, 73, 47, 30, 10, 0%, which falls most gently, so each level asks a little more than the one before instead of a few asking much more. Level 1 is cleared every time by all four.

*Keeping it.* A Plan run is kept as `pipes:plan`, Rush's as `pipes:flat` as before (a run with no `flow` is Rush's, so a saved Rush run plays on as it was), 3D's as `pipes:deep`; each keeps its own run, so changing the Board choice, like the 3D switch, leaves the other where it was. The picked square, the undo snapshots and a Run under way are part of the run, so a reload, or a partner watching, sees the same ghost and the same board (a watching frame runs a Run the oil on, nothing else moves). Plan's bests are their own (`plan` in `pipes:best`, beside `flat` for Rush and `deep`; they aren't comparable, since a run with no clock to beat scores differently); the menu's About lines give each, and the games screen shows the highest. A Plan board has no pace: the menu's Pace is for Rush and 3D only.

**3D: the descent.** The menu's 3D switch takes the oil down a shaft four squares across, as deep as you can keep ahead of it. It waits 6 s at the wellhead, then runs down the pipe a square at a time, 3.2 s a square at the surface (very slowly, to learn on), 2% faster with every layer, to 0.45 s. You lay the pipe at its open end, one piece at a time: a straight, a long straight (two squares), an elbow, or a jog (a square sideways, then on the way it was going), dealt in bags of ten (three straights, four elbows, two jogs, one long). The piece in hand shows at the open end in copper, the square it leads into outlined (green if it's open, red if it's shut), and turns a quarter at a time about the way the pipe is going (Turn, or a tap on the shaft): an elbow can go any of four ways, sideways or back up, and a jog step four ways. Lay puts it in. Hold sets the piece in hand aside for the one held before (or the next), once a piece; Scrap throws it away for the next, but the oil surges on a square meanwhile. You can be at most six squares ahead of the oil (the dots under the shaft, red when it's about to catch up), so the oil is always in view; a piece of two squares waits for room for both. Hurry runs it five times faster until it's two squares behind you, for when you're waiting on it, and lets it go from the wellhead if it's still waiting there. It's over when the oil reaches the open end.

On the way: rock to steer round, scattered thicker with depth (5% of the squares near the top, a quarter by 80 layers down), pillars a few layers tall, and slabs that fill a whole layer but for a gap or two, in about half of every twelve layers. The shaft is made from its seed twelve layers at a time as the oil goes down (`pipes-deep.js`), each stretch round a vein, a way down kept clear of rock, so there is always one; nothing within three layers of the surface. And the boons, taken by laying pipe through their square: a barrel (most layers have one) worth 50; a charge, which blasts the rock the open end faces (Lay becomes Blast while it does); and a tank, laid into the pipe, which holds the oil four times as long. A point for every metre the oil gets down (a layer is ten metres), and 50 a barrel. A bot laying a piece every 1.4 s (it weighs each way of the piece in hand and the one held, a piece further on, by depth, the open ground below and the ways on) gets down about 470 m before the oil catches it, two or three minutes; much the same at 2 s a piece, since what stops it is mostly being boxed in, and 380 m at 2.6 s. A person turning pieces in 3D is slower than that, so the oil's pace matters more for them. The slabs had a single gap at first, and the bot died at the first one more often than not; a second gap made them a thing to steer for.

`pipes-3d.js` draws the shaft on a canvas in perspective, by hand (no library, so it works offline), as Mines' solids are drawn: the camera looks down into it from 35° above and follows the oil down, so the view scrolls as it goes; a drag turns it round the shaft and tips it. The two walls behind are banded with strata and lined in squares, the depth marked every fifty metres at the left, the walls in front left open. Rock is blocks of stone, the boons small things that glow, the pipe brass tube with the oil dark inside it; everything is drawn back to front, square by square, and rock that stands between the camera and the oil, the last pipe laid or the piece in hand is drawn faint, so it never hides the work. Its colours are the theme's, read from the page (`--pi-stone`, `--pi-wall`, `--pi-oil`, `--pi-label` are the shaft's own, with Modern, Kontor and Office versions).

Pace (October 2026, when Korbi found it too fast): the menu's Pace slows Rush and 3D (Plan has no clock to slow). Slower takes half as long again over everything timed, the countdown, the oil's every pipe or square and the wait at the wellhead, and Slowest twice as long; the boards, the queues and the shaft are the same at every pace (`deal(seed, n, pace)` stretches a level's times, a descent carries its `pace`). It changes the game under way at once rather than from the next, since the moment it feels too fast is mid-game. A slower game is an easier one, so each pace keeps bests and today's of its own (`pipes:best` holds `flat`, `deep`, `flat:slower`, `deep:slowest`…), and a run counts under the slowest pace it was played at, so slowing down for the hard part can't lift a Normal best. A match is dealt at its dealer's pace, the same for everyone; in a match the menu's Pace is for the next one.

Today's run and today's shaft are dealt from the day, the same for everyone. Each mode (Plan, Rush, 3D) keeps its own run, so switching to another and back leaves it where it was; a run is kept as it's played (every piece laid, and every second while the oil moves), so a reload, or a partner watching, picks it up where it stands, the clock waiting rather than running on (a watching frame runs it on by the time since it was kept, up to half a minute). While your game stands still (its menu or rules open, the app out of sight) its save says so, and a partner's watching frame holds the oil where it is: it knows only what was last kept, and used to run the oil on into spills that never happened. A mode just switched to is kept at once, so a frame doesn't run it on from when it was last played. Bests are by mode (`pipes:best`: the board's score and level, 3D's score and depth; the games screen shows the higher). The old Pipes' saves and bests were another game's and are cleared the first time the new one opens. Pipes stands still while its menu or rules are open, or the app is out of sight.

Together: a match deals one seed, and everyone at the table plays it on their own phone (README, Head to head): on the board, in the way the board was set to play when it was dealt (Plan or Rush; a match dealt before Plan existed was Rush, and one in Plan has no pace), the same levels and the same queues, for the menu's Match length (5 or 10 levels, or till the spill; in your own menu's Together part too, so the first match deals what you chose), the highest score once everyone's run is over winning; in 3D, the same shaft and the same pieces, the highest score winning. Equal scores draw (the duel's usual tie-break, the quicker run, would favour the descent the oil caught first). The others' scores show under yours. A match's clock doesn't stop for the menu, or while the app is out of sight (it catches up in full when you're back); your own runs, bests and today's stay as they were, and Back to solo returns to the mode you were in. A Party plays it for up to six. Two tabs on the stand-in database (each its own player) went through the lobby, Ready, the countdown, the same deal on both, each run to its end and the result, on the board and in 3D.

On a phone the numbers (level or depth, score, what's left of the quota or the charges), the queue, the board or the shaft, the line saying what's happening and the buttons stand in a column, the board as large as the room allows at its shape; the buttons keep their room when there's nothing to press, so the board never changes size. Held sideways (README, Bigger screens) the board or the shaft takes the left with all the height, the numbers, the queue and the buttons beside it, low where a thumb is. `tests/pipes.mjs` checks 600 boards (repeatable and fair, as above), the queue's bags, every rule on boards laid by hand (the quota, each kind of spill, a loop through a crossing, pumps, tanks, the terminal past and short of the quota, blasts, Oil now, Hurry, unused pieces), that the route shown is the way the oil goes, and the board bot's curve; and for the descent, 30 shafts of 120 layers (repeatable, a way down, no rock near the surface, no layer all rock, the boons), the pieces' ways and turns, laying against the cap, rock, the wall and your own pipe, the boons, Hold, Scrap, Blast, the oil's pace and Hurry, the catch, a descent saved and restored playing on the same, and how far the bot gets.
