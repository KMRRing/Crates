# Dou Dizhu
斗地主 (dòu dìzhǔ, "fight the landlord"), China's most played card game (October 2026), against computer players or at a table with friends each on their own phone (README, Seats): three players, one against two. The rules are as pagat and the Chinese sources set them down. All 54 cards, both jokers in: seventeen each and three face down, the kitty (底牌, dǐpái); a card turned up as they're dealt (never one of the kitty) picks who bids first, as pagat's deal does. The bidding is for points (叫分, the classic, as JJ and pagat play it: once round, each bids 1, 2 or 3, more than the last bid, or passes; a 3 ends it at once) or, in the menu, calling and robbing as Tencent's Happy Dou Dizhu (欢乐斗地主) plays it (叫地主, 抢地主: the first to call is landlord unless robbed; each player after the caller may rob once, a player who declined to call can't, each rob doubles the stake, and when anyone has robbed the caller has the last word). Nobody bids: the cards are thrown in and dealt again, the same deal. The landlord takes the kitty, shown to everyone, and leads; play goes round to the right. Big joker, small joker, 2, A, K down to 3; suits never matter. The plays: a single, a pair, three alone or with one card or a pair (三带一, 三带一对), a straight of five or more from 3 to A (顺子), three or more pairs in a row (连对), two or more threes in a row (飞机, the airplane) alone, with a card for each three (its wings) or with a pair for each, four with two cards or two pairs (四带二), the bomb (four alike) and the rocket (both jokers). Each play beats the last with the same kind and as many cards, higher: a chain by its lowest card, a three or a four by its own rank, the kickers (the cards a three, an airplane or a four takes along) never counting. A bomb beats anything but a higher bomb, the rocket everything; two passes in a row end the trick and its last player leads anything. Where the sources differ on the small print pagat's stands: a kicker can't share the threes' or the four's rank, a four's two cards are of different ranks and so are its two pairs, an airplane's wings may pair but not triple and can't be both jokers, and cards that read as more than one play read as the strongest (twelve cards of four threes in a row are an airplane of four, not three with wings). Scoring: the stake is the bid (or 1, doubled by each rob), doubled for each bomb and rocket played, and doubled again for a spring (春天: the landlord out before either peasant has played a card) or an anti-spring (反春: the peasants out with the landlord having played only their first lead); the landlord wins it from each peasant or pays it to each, twice the stake either way, so the scores always balance. A game is 3, 6 or 9 deals, the most points winning.

The table talk is the apps' voice: the narrator calls each play in Mandarin, Cantonese or English (Menu, Table talk): 对K, 三个七, 顺子, 连对, 飞机, 炸弹！, 王炸！, the bids (2分, 不叫, 叫地主, 抢地主, 不抢), "剩两张！" and "剩一张！" for the last two cards. A pass is 要不起 ("can't afford it") when the player has nothing that beats the play and 不要 when they choose to pass: the apps say it so, it tells the table something true, and the computer players count it (below). Cantonese calls the jokers 大鬼 and 細鬼 and the two Ｄ, as Big Two's table does. The English lines name a seat where no verb has to agree with it ("Dou Dizhu: You."). The Rules sheet has the plays and the talk with pinyin and Jyutping.

The computer players (`landlord-bot.js`) first organise the hand into plays (拆牌, "taking the hand apart"), as Big Two's do: the rocket and the bombs kept whole (a four may also be let go to a chain), then whichever straights, pairs in a row and airplanes leave the rest cheapest, the rest grouped into threes, pairs and singles, the weakest singles and pairs riding on the threes and airplanes as kickers; each play is weighed by how likely it is to need a lead of its own (a low single nearly always does; a two, a joker or a bomb takes the lead back), and the arrangement with the least weight wins. Leading, the weakest play goes first, the long ones before the short; when every play but one is sure (nobody can beat it, from the cards still unseen and what each opponent holds), the sure ones go first; a peasant whose partner is nearly out feeds them a low single or pair; nobody leads what an opponent with one or two cards could follow. Following an opponent, the play that leaves the hand best: the plan's own, or a structure broken when it costs little or an opponent is close to out; a bomb when an opponent is close to out or it wins the deal outright. A peasant lets a partner's play stand once the landlord has passed on it, and tops a weak one with a cheap play of its own when the landlord is next. 要不起 is counted: a player who couldn't beat a single nine holds nothing above a nine, nor a bomb, nor the rocket. Hard looks ahead. In the middle of a deal, each choice worth weighing (the rules' own, the next best two, passing) is played out by Medium's rules in up to 24 deals of the unseen cards, each dealt within what the seats have shown they can't hold and the kitty in the landlord's hand, and the choice that wins most often is made (as many deals as fit in 150 ms, so a slow phone weighs fewer rather than keep the table waiting). Near the end (fifteen cards or fewer in all, someone within six of out), each sample deal is worked out exactly instead, every seat playing perfectly (a solver: the side to move wins if any move of theirs leads to a win, positions remembered), perfect-information Monte Carlo as bridge and skat programs play their ends. Bidding weighs the jokers, twos, aces and bombs against the weight of the plan: `ARENA=bid node tools/landlord-arena.mjs` plays 4,500 hands with each seat made landlord in turn and shows the landlord's chances by that strength (a half at about 0.7, 58% at 1.9, 65% at 2.9, four in five from 4.6), which set the bids (1 at even chances, 2 and 3 as they grow, a rob at 2.2). Easy is a beginner: it leads its lowest card with all its like, beats with the lowest that does, ignores its partner, and bids by feel. `tools/landlord-arena.mjs` plays each deal six times, each strength in each seat with the other in the other two, so the luck of the cards evens out: Medium against Easy, 300 deals, Medium took 1.6 points a deal a seat and sat on the winning side of 57% of the deals against Easy's 31%; Hard against Medium, 200 deals, Hard took 0.33 points a deal a seat and sat on the winning side of 47% of the deals against Medium's 41% (as landlord it won 74% of the deals it bid for, Medium 62%). A move of Hard's takes 20 ms on average on a laptop and at most a fifth of a second; the others' a fraction of a millisecond.

The page: the felt in persimmon (the game's enamel), the two others at its top corners, each with their name, their side (地主 or 农民, landlord or peasant, as the talk is), the cards they hold (a card's back and a count, red at two or fewer), their points and what they did this trick (their cards, 不要 or 要不起; their bid while bidding); the kitty between them, face down until the landlord is known; the narrator's latest line and what's to beat in the middle; your seat at the foot with your play. Under the table, the counter (记牌器, as the apps have it; Menu turns it off): how many of each rank you haven't seen, the jokers and twos first. Your cards in a row along the bottom, biggest first as the apps hold them (Menu: smallest first), a joker with JOKER down its corner so it reads in the fan; a tap picks a card up, × puts them all back; Play names what the picked cards make (Play airplane with wings) and plays them when they're legal; Pass becomes 要不起 when nothing beats the play; Hint picks Hard's play, and each tap after picks the next play that's possible, as the apps' 提示 does. While bidding the buttons are the bids: 不叫, 1分, 2分, 3分, or 不叫 and 叫地主, 不抢 and 抢地主. The strip above the table keeps the stake as it stands, and why (bid 2, 1 bomb). When a deal ends every hand shows with what it pays, the winner's side and the stake, and a bar running down to the next deal; a long play or a hand left over is laid out in rows. Held sideways, the table takes the left and the counter, your cards and the buttons stand beside it. On `seats.js`: Invite players opens a party with Dou Dizhu chosen, friends taking the computer players' seats. `landlord:game` keeps the game as it stands, `landlord:opts` the menu, `landlord:record` the games won, and the games screen shows the run of wins. `tests/landlord.mjs` checks every kind of play and what isn't one, what beats what, the plays a hand can make, the deal and the face-up card, both biddings (rising bids, a 3 ending it, the cards thrown in and dealt again; each rob doubling, the caller's last word, no rob for one who didn't call), the trick, 要不起 and what it tells, the spring, the anti-spring and the rocket doubling the stake, the hand organised into plays, whole games of computer players at every strength and both biddings with every card accounted for and the scores balancing, the state through the database, and Medium against Easy.

The course (Learn, in the header, as Xiangqi's; October 2026): Dou Dizhu taught from nothing, for a newcomer who means to sit down at a real table in China, in 18 lessons in three chapters on a sheet over the game. The game: one against two, the deal and the kitty, the cards' order, the simple plays, passing (不要, 要不起) and the lead, the bids and the stake. The plays: threes with kickers, straights, pairs in a row and airplanes, four with two, bombs and the rocket. Playing well: counting a hand's plays (手数) and taking it apart into them (拆牌), following without breaking it up, leading (the weakest first, the long ones first, the sure ones at the end), a player down to one card (报单), playing as peasants (letting a partner's play stand, feeding a partner who is nearly out, topping a partner's low lead ahead of the landlord), counting the cards (记牌), and when to bid; then a first game against Easy. A step is words over cards (examples with their names, a play to beat, your hand), a play to make from your hand (tap the cards, then Play: a wrong one is answered with why, "That's a single. To beat a pair, play a higher pair.", "That breaks your pair of queens: the other queen would need a lead of its own."; after two misses the answer is picked for you), or a choice of answers; Hint picks the answer. Chinese terms come with their pinyin the first time. Where you are is kept (`landlord:course`), so the course opens where you left it and its map ticks what's done. The lessons are data (`landlord-course.js`), on the course sheet shared by the games that aren't played on a board (`course.js`, `course.css`: Xiangqi's course's look; Big Two's and Liar's dice's too) and its card stage (`course-cards.js`: cards written as text, "7 7 7 4", "SJ BJ", drawn as the table draws them and as large as the stage allows, a tablet's larger). A choice whose options are all short (a number, a word or two) sets them side by side, so the words above keep their room on a short phone. `tests/landlord-course.mjs` works every exercise through the rules: every card real, every answer in the hand and judged right, every wrong pick it has words for judged wrong with them, and of every pick the hand allows only those the lesson means judged right; where a lesson teaches what the computer players do (follow with the card that costs least, lead the weakest, lead a pair against a last card, top a partner's weak lead, feed a partner, let a partner's play stand), Medium's rules choose it from the same seat; the bidding questions' answers are the computer's bids by the strength the lesson describes, and the hand counted in plays goes in five and no fewer. The Rules sheet, every rule on one page, opens from the course's map and the menu.
