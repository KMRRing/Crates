# Lode
Strands' genre on the knowledge base (October 2026). A board of 48 letters, eight rows of six, hides a theme's words: each is a path of touching letters (sideways, up and down, or diagonally), no letter used twice, and between them the words use every letter on the board exactly once. One of them, the spanning word or mother lode, names the theme and touches two opposite sides of the board (left and right, or top and bottom). A title, a clue to the theme, heads the board. Trace a word by dragging a finger through its letters, or tap them one at a time and tap the last again; slide back to take the last letter off, and tap off the letters to clear. A theme word found locks in lapis, the spanning word in gold, and the line under the board says what links it to the theme. Any other English word of four letters or more (`wordlist.js`, every tier) counts towards a hint, three to a hint, as in Strands. A hint rings the letters of the hardest word still hidden; a second while it's ringed numbers them in order. The score is the seconds on the clock plus 30 a hint, lower being better.

The themes are the knowledge base's countries and commodities: 148 of Crates' 159 answers can make a board (122 at Easy). A theme's words are its clue links in Crates' bank (`bank.js`, built from `kb/`), so they follow the knowledge base as it grows: every clue whose name is one word of 4 to 10 letters once accents are dropped (Björk as BJORK, Weißwurst as WEISSWURST, Ørsted as ORSTED), never one holding the theme's own name (Copperbelt never comes under Copper, though Copper comes under Zambia), each with its link's difficulty, topics and reason. The spanning word is the one thing no source gives, so `lode-themes.js` writes it by hand: the theme's name where it's long enough to cross the board (six letters side to side, eight top to bottom), else its people's name or a longer form (PERUVIAN, JAPANESE, MESOPOTAMIA for Iraq, CUBALIBRE; GOLDRUSH, CORNBELT, CARBAMIDE for urea, GREENDIESEL for HVO, CASHEWSHELLOIL for CNSL), and two where both read well (NORWAY or NORWEGIAN), Easy taking the plainer. Nine themes have too few one-word clues to fill a board (RINs, the THG quota, LCFS credits, EREs, RTFCs, recycled carbon fuels, tyre pyrolysis oil, ETBE, MTBE), and Lumber's and Black mass's word lengths can't add up round any spanning word; they come in by themselves as `kb/` gains clues. The title is another of the theme's clues, one not on the board, as a riddle ("Awarded in its capital, unlike the other Nobels"; "The pots of ash that named it"), never one that says the theme, its people or a word on the board: a well-known link at Easy, a specialist's at Hard. Easy and Medium say whether the theme is a country or a commodity; Hard doesn't.

What a board deals from is the menu's Content: the themes (Mixed, Countries or Commodities: the header's choice, as in Crates, mirrored there), a line and a link for the topics (below), and the regions and sectors (Crates' groups, ticked in or out, All or None; with nothing left to deal the board comes from every theme, and says so). A random board passes over the 30 themes dealt last.

Topics (2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed"). The menu no longer has a topic control of its own. Where Content had one there is a line saying where the suite's one set of topics stands ("Topics: STEM (5 of 23 on)") and a link, Change topics, which opens the same sheet as Settings' Topics part over the game; the line adds that a word of a topic left out comes only when a theme can't fill a board without it, as it always did. Each of the 23 subjects is on or off, and Lode's topics, Crates' country and commodity ones (each link's aspects), belong to them (`TOPIC_OF.kb` in `topics.js`): a topic is in, at weight 1 as the old Balanced had it, when its subject is on, and out when it isn't (`topicsOf` in `lode-engine.js`); a word is as likely as its likeliest topic. With every subject on a board is therefore the one Balanced dealt, seed for seed, and a deal keeps no mix at all (nor with none on, where no word fits and the board is of every topic, `dealMix`). The page reads the subjects when it deals a board, never once at start-up, so a change reaches the next board (the board dealt ahead for the one after is dealt again if the topics changed under it) and a board under way keeps what it was dealt; a table deals from the dealer's. A link carries the mix of the board it names (`m=`) and still reads a weight of 2 and the topics out among them from an old link. The topics are no longer kept in `lode:settings` (a mix an older version saved there is never read, and the next save leaves it out). Lode's bests are kept by level and board shape as before, but a random board counts for them only when it was dealt on a preset of the topics (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade, All off), not a mix of your own, as in every knowledge game; today's board and a linked board that deals every topic count as they did, and a run kept by an older version counts too. The menu's line says when your own mix won't count.

The generator (`lode-engine.js`, pure) makes a board from its deal alone, the same on every device. It picks the words (the spanning word, then words drawn by the level's and the topics' weights whose lengths add up to the rest of the 48, five to seven of them), then packs them: the spanning word as a walk from one side to the side across, then the rest cut into paths of the other words' lengths, always from the free cell with the fewest free neighbours (it has to be the end of whatever path takes it), every patch of free cells checked to be fillable by the words still to place, and no two diagonals crossing, so every word draws as a line. It writes the words along their paths and climbs towards a lettering where each word traces only along its own path: a word that traces a second way almost always does so at an end (BALI's I with another I beside its L), which turning that word or a neighbour round, or swapping two words of a length, puts right. Last, a solver counts the ways the words cover the board, up to two, and the board is kept only if there's exactly one: the board's one solution. A word may still trace a second way through another word's letters (at most two a board; on about three hex boards in ten and two square ones in three, where eight neighbours give letters more twins): traced there it's found all the same, and shows where it really runs, the only cells the one cover leaves it. That rule is Lode's own, since knowing the word should never be what fails you. On this machine (two cores shared with several others) a hex board takes 2 ms to deal and a square one 9 to 13 (the medians), nine in ten within 8 and 40 ms; a phone is a few times slower, and the next random board is dealt in the background as you play, so New board is at once.

Difficulty. What makes a board hard is knowing its words and finding them. Knowing: each link carries the knowledge base's difficulty, 1 (well known) to 3 (specialist). Finding: a word is as hard to find as its path turns, runs against the reading direction (leftwards or up) and offers false starts, the traces of two or more of its letters that a search for it would follow but aren't the word (every A beside an F, looking for FJORDS). A board's grade is its words' mean link difficulty (×100) plus a quarter of their mean of turns, backward steps and false starts (×100), the quarter putting the two on one scale. The levels set their boards apart by how they're made: Easy deals no specialist link and no acronym, its paths run straight on and read rightwards or down, its title a well-known clue; Medium mixes; Hard leans on specialist links, turns its paths and runs them backwards. Each level keeps a board only inside its band of grades (Easy up to 390, Medium 360 to 480, Hard from 450), making it again if not, and a theme that can't make a board near its band (Freight's CLARKSONS, DEMURRAGE and WORLDSCALE make no Easy board) gives way to the next the seed picks. Over boards as dealt (`tests/lode.mjs` prints the spread), Easy's 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles are 279, 332 and 367, Medium's 374, 419 and 465, Hard's 480, 533 and 589: no Easy board is as hard as any Hard one. Whether the bands feel right is for Korbi's thumb.

Twists, both in Settings and from the next board: Hex (Board), the same 48 letters as a honeycomb, rows of six with every other one set half a cell over, each letter touching six: the paths bend differently and there's no diagonal to read along, and the spanning word still has to reach two opposite sides. Every word (Clues), every word's clue with its length in place of the title, one at a time with arrows, the spanning word's last: the board becomes a clued word search, the words worked out from what you know and then found, which makes the knowledge base the game rather than its backdrop. Considered and left out: the classic straight-line word search with a message in its leftover letters (another, easier genre), a bigger board (a phone has no width for another column of letters a thumb can hit), and two themes on one board (a word fitting both would have no right answer).

Together, from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six: one table (`lode-table.js`, on `together.js`), the board itself kept in the room so every phone has the same one, in one of two modes chosen in the menu's Together part for the next board; a pair's first board is Team, a party's Race.
- Team: one board for the table, its letters shared out. The t-th letter of the j-th word goes to seat (t + j) mod n, so every word runs through every player's hands, each sees one letter in n of every word, and no word of four letters or more can be read alone; each holds a fair share (24 each of two, 16 of three). The others' letters show as sealed tiles marked with where they are (C4), to ask for. Anyone traces through any letters, sealed or not, and what you trace shows on the others' boards as it grows, ringed in your colour with your initial at its head (written to your seat at most every 90 ms, as in Delta), so whoever holds a letter can say what it is. A word found opens its letters to everyone, in its finder's colour; the hints and the meter are the table's. A seat nobody has, or whose player is away, has its letters open to all, so a sleeping phone never holds the others up. The pair's record gets the team's score.
- Race: the same board on every phone from the same moment (after a three-second count, its letters sealed till then), a run each, kept on the phone (`lode:table`) and reported as it goes; the strip shows how far each of the others has got, and once every run that began is done, the standings (`standings.js`), the lowest score first. One who comes late runs on a clock of their own. For the pair's record, the winner is first alone.

Why not `duel.js` for the race: a table holds one kind of state and a pair's request always opens the game one way, so a race on `duel.js` and a team on `together.js` couldn't share a request, nor let a pair switch between them for the next board as Manifest's two modes do; the race keeps `duel.js`'s shape (one start, late runs on their own clock, a finished run standing, the suite's standings).

No computer rival (3 November 2026, Korbi: "'against the computer' makes little sense"; "probably drop from all"), solo or at a race table.

Scoring, bests and the daily. Giving up (Show the words still hidden, in the menu) adds 120 seconds a word shown: more than earning and using a hint ever costs, so it's never the quick way out. Bests are kept for each level and board shape (`lode:times`, lowest wins), not for a board given up or one dealt on a mix of topics of your own. Today's board is the same for everyone (the suite's day): Medium, square, its title, from every theme and topic. It counts the moment its last word is found (`noteDayTime`), and the games screen's tile shows today's time.

Learning: a word you needed a hint for, or were shown, goes to the pile with its theme and its entity, so Deck asks it back (which country or commodity: WORD, its link), with fresh wrong answers each time, and the other games' learning modes deal it too; one found unaided that was due counts as known.

Saved state: `lode:run` holds the board itself and your run (the words found, the other words, the hints, the clock), so a reload, another device or a partner watching sees it as it stands, and a newer generator never re-deals a board under way; `lode:settings` what the next board deals and how it shows; `lode:times` the bests; `lode:seen` the themes dealt lately; `lode:table` this phone's run in a race. A partner's watching frame draws the board as you left it, runs no clock and writes nothing. The clock stops with the menu and the games screen (the pause), and while the page is hidden.

The page fits the screen from 390 by 600: the theme, the count and the clock, the board as large as the room left (drawn in an SVG to its own units, it never changes size as you play), then the line and the tools, the result taking their room at the end. Held sideways the board takes the left with all the height, and the theme, the words found so far with their links, the line and the tools stand beside it, the tools low.

The rules (October 2026) are a sheet of their own, from the header's Rules or the menu's The rules, as the other games' are: in plain prose, how the words fill the board and the mother lode spans it, where a theme and its words come from, the drag and the taps, how other words earn hints and what a hint shows, the score and giving up, the levels, the board's shapes, the clues, today's board, and the team's sealed letters asked for by their place (C4). Its numbers come from the engine as it opens, so the sheet can't drift from the game, and a solo board stands still while it's open, as for the menu. Looked at on a phone (390×664, 390×844) and an iPad both ways, in Codex day and night and in Office.

`tests/lode.mjs` checks the themes and their words; a board for every theme tried at every level and shape (every letter used once, each word on its own path, the spanning word across, no crossing diagonals, one cover by the solver, which counts two on a hand-made board that has two); the grading and its bands; the same board from the same deal, and today's on every device; the suite's topics (every subject on the old Balanced board seed for seed, STEM's or Energy's words coming first, nothing on every topic, an old link's weight of 2 still dealing); a run's rules (theme words, other words towards hints, both hint steps, giving up, the score, a word traced through its twin letters); the shares, every word through every seat; and a table of three through the database's own round trip (Team: who reads which letters, traces, one player's find for all, a stale move refused, the team's result; Race: starts, reports, a finished run standing, the end, the standings, the pair's record). In Chromium: three phone sizes and an iPad both ways, in Codex day and night and in Office; played by mouse and by touch (drags, taps, sliding back, other words, both hints, giving up, today's board from the tile and again on a second phone, a watching frame); two phones through Team and then Race on the stand-in database, the pair's record written; and a party of three through a race, a team of three and a race with Ace. The suite's topics (November 2026): the menu's topics line and its Change topics sheet at 390 by 664 and 1180 by 820 in Codex day and night and in Office, and a change in the sheet reaching the next board: STEM's words first, a mix of your own unranked (its link carrying the mix), nothing on a board of every topic.

Not done: the knowledge base's collections as themes (rivers, gods, painters of a movement, wines), which need spanning words of their own; a computer team mate in Team, which would have to hold a share of the letters and answer for them; a hint that gives only a word's first letter.
