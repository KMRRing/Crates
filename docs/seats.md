# Seats
The card, dice and board games for a few seats round one table (October 2026: Liar's dice, Big Two, Dou Dizhu, Meld, Pegs, the trump games, dominoes, backgammon, Quest's hidden roles, and Cartel): each seat is a person or a computer player, and one runner, `seats.js`, plays any of them solo or at a table without the game knowing which. Solo, you sit in seat 0 and the computer players in the rest, on this device, with no connection needed; the game is kept as it changes (`<game>:game`), so a reload, another of your devices or a partner watching sees it as it stands. Invite players (the game's menu) opens a party with the game chosen (README, Party): when its host starts it, every phone comes to a table in the party's room, each in their own seat, and the host's page deals with its own options. A friend who joins mid-game sits down in the lowest free seat, which is a computer player's, and takes it over, hand and all; one who leaves gives it back to the computer, and one whose phone has been away for half a minute is played for until it's back, so nobody's sleeping phone holds up the table. The host deals the next game, with as many seats as asked and at least one for everyone at the table, and Back to the party (the menu) takes every phone back to the lobby; each phone's solo game stays as it was.

A game is a module of pure functions: `deal` (a fresh game for so many seats), `toMove` (whose decision it is), `act` (a move made, or why it can't be), `bot` (a computer player's move, from what its seat may know), `over`, and for a timed step (a trick left on the table a moment, the dice shown) `wakeAt` and `wake`; `restore` makes a state read back from the database whole again (it keeps no empty lists); `anytime`, where a game has it, names the moves a seat may make whoever's decision it is (Meld's and Pegs' "next", on to the next hand), and every other move is refused out of turn. Every move's randomness comes from the game's seed and the move's number, so a move worked out twice, as a transaction may be on a fresher copy, comes out the same. The device that runs the table (the host's, or the next in line) makes the computer players' moves, each a short while after the last move so the people can follow it (a game can set that by the step: Quest's secret votes go in at once), and checks the table hasn't moved on meanwhile; a person's move is checked on their phone and again in the transaction. A state may carry a line for the narrator, spoken by the phone that runs the table. `seats-ui.js` wires a page: the solo game, the table in the address, the invitation, the narrator and the menu's table actions. A page may make a move as it draws (a choice given before its turn, sent the moment it comes, as Banquet's throws and Quest's votes are): the runner then looks at the table afresh before setting the next computer player going. Until October 2026 it went on with the view it had drawn, in which it was still the person's turn, and cancelled the move the person's own move had just set going, so the table stood still. `tests/seats.mjs` plays a toy game both ways: solo, the computer players moving in turn until it's yours and waiting while a menu is open, a move made as the page draws not stalling it; at a table on the database in memory, two people and two computer players, only the running phone moving the computer, a friend leaving and another sitting down mid-game in the computer's seat, a phone gone away played for after its grace, a solo game carried to a table and back.
