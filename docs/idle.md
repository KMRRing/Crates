# Idle
Games to play without much thought (October 2026): no puzzle to work out, but as much thinking as Block Blast, a bubble shooter or match-3 ask for, which is more than it looks. What those have in common, and what every idle game here keeps: a real choice every move among a dozen or so, a board that remembers a careless one, a reward for setting up combinations, and pressure that ends the game when you're careless. A game with less in it (a tune-the-plate idea, one slider and a pattern of sand) was turned down as too little. Each idle game changes one rule those games share for one nobody has used, and each was prototyped and tried in hand before the next. Each game's own file (`docs/<game>.md`) has its rules and traps; this is the index.

- **Heliograph** (aim by building the path): a beam fired through mirrors you set in a glass tube, through colour crystals into a chandelier, in 3D. A bubble shooter aims the shot; this builds the shot's path, and every mirror changes the paths after it.
- **Shunt** (push in, line up): a rail yard of wagons; match-3's swap replaced by the push of the board game Labyrinth, which moves a whole row and with it the lines in every column it crosses.
- **Polder** (enclose to clear): dyke pieces on an 8×8 sea; a pocket of sea cut off drains into farmland and clears. Block Blast completes lines; this completes areas.
- **Topple** (set up the chain, then push it): standing dominoes on a table of hexagons in 3D, set up and then pushed; waiting for a bigger chain fills the table.
- **Columbiad** (aim and fire one shot): a cannon on a real-gravity Earth, later with a moving Moon, one drag back like a slingshot and a Fire button; a design doc (`docs/columbiad.md`), built in milestones.
- **Orrery** (real-time, in 3D, on a beat): four hoops of planets always going round a sun, their speeds set so that planets of a colour come into line.
- **Avalanche** (you set off the chain reaction, and where it runs matters): a slope of snow on the sandpile model.
- **Netting** (lines replaced by loops): obligations between six counterparties; a cycle nets out and scores; a party past its credit limit defaults.
- **Settle** (you pick the tank, density picks the level): parcels sink below everything lighter and lift it a level.
- **Ingot** (2048 itself): four swipes on a four-by-four board of metal bars that double; the day's seed for a daily, limited undo, a hint and a par from a small search.
- **Drift** (2048 turned round): the forecast wind picks the direction, you pick the square.
- **Guilloché** (a rose engine: engrave the target plate), asked for as a game where you make something, in 3D, with guilloché as its base. Each plate teaches one part of the machine, so that someone who can set the last knows how every guilloché is made. Replaced Rosette.
- **Slipway** (carving), asked for as something more casual and braindead, still about putting something together in 3D: a puzzle of saw, plane and drill levels, each part of a ship's cog a ghost in a block of timber (`docs/slipway.md`).
- **Loom** (weaving, as a nonogram of floats), asked for on 19 October 2026 as a sewing game: "Fix buttons, disattached belt loops, holes, whatever." At every crossing of a warp thread and a pick of weft one yarn lies over the other, and a line's clue is how long its own yarn shows in a row; the plain cloths are laid thread by thread (tap where the warp comes up and where it goes back under), the other chapters (weaves, colour and weave, gold thread) are painted crossing by crossing (`docs/loom.md`). Replaced Thimble.
- **Courier** (run the quay, answer over the water), asked for on 2 November 2026 as a spiritual clone of Blades of Brim, an endless runner in real 3D, in the Codex's colours, with the knowledge base's questions as what stands in the way ("Ok go yea put it into idle"): three lanes, four swipes, a Hanseatic quay; now and then the quay breaks at a gap no jump clears and three gulls hang over it, each carrying an answer (`docs/courier.md`; its files, ids and saved keys keep the name Reckoning).
- **Landfall** (celestial navigation from a chart table), asked for on 3 November 2026 as a new idle game, from a design doc: take sights of real stars with a sextant and watch the fog round a dead-reckoned position collapse along each line, learning the constellations by using them (`docs/landfall.md`; built in five milestones, the first is the Polaris watches).
- **Harbour** (plan, then run), moved here from Puzzles on 3 November 2026 (Korbi: "Harbour should go to idle"): a Hanseatic metal trade whose levels are programs for ships and whose campaign is a season board; its own file has the rules.
