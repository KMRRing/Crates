# Idle
Games to play without much thought (October 2026): no puzzle to work out, but as much thinking as Block Blast, a bubble shooter or match-3 ask for, which is more than it looks. What those have in common, and what every idle game here keeps: a real choice every move among a dozen or so, a board that remembers a careless one, a reward for setting up combinations, and pressure that ends the game when you're careless. A game with less in it (a tune-the-plate idea, one slider and a pattern of sand) was turned down as too little. Each idle game changes one rule those games share for one nobody has used, and each is prototyped and tried in hand before the next.

Four were bookmarked, to be prototyped one by one, and all four now have a first draft to try in hand:
- **Heliograph** (aim by building the path): first a flat field of gems under a beam fired from the foot through `/` and `\` mirrors laid on a grid; rebuilt in 3D (October 2026) as a glass tube with a chandelier of crystals hanging in its middle and white light falling from the top, which you carry with mirrors set anywhere in the tube through colour crystals and into the chandelier. A bubble shooter aims the shot; this builds the shot's path, and every mirror changes the paths after it. The first prototyped (below).
- **Shunt** (push in, line up): a rail yard of tracks holding wagons of grain, timber, steel and oil; shove the incoming wagon onto any track from either end and the train rolls along to close its first gap; three or more alike across or down couple up and depart; a full track pushes its end wagon off the rails, a life lost. Match-3's swap replaced by the push of the board game Labyrinth, which moves a whole row and with it the lines in every column it crosses. The second prototyped (below).
- **Polder** (enclose to clear): an 8×8 sea with the coast along the foot; three dyke pieces at a time, placed anywhere on open water; when dykes and coast cut a pocket of sea off from the open sea, it drains into farmland and clears with its ring of dykes, more for a bigger polder but more of the board held up meanwhile; a piece that fits nowhere ends it. Block Blast completes lines; this completes areas. The third prototyped (below).
- **Topple** (set up the chain, then push it): three pieces of standing dominoes at a time (chains of one to five) on a table of sixty-one hexagons, each domino facing a neighbouring hexagon or the line between two, and turned as you like; when the table's knock comes you push one domino along its axis, and it falls on what it faces (a domino facing a line on both beyond it), and so on down the chain, everything that falls clearing, the score the dominoes felled times the steps they took; waiting for a bigger chain fills the table. Played in 3D on a mahogany and birch table you can tilt and zoom into, with the clack of each domino. Classic, or fourteen tables that add mixed colours, heavy dominoes and gates, stones, water, springs, hedges, portals, floors, balls and cups, each beaten at a goal score (the first, a plain table of five colours, at 14,000). The fourth prototyped (below).

Five more were bookmarked after those (October 2026), to be drafted in this order:
- **Orrery** (real-time, in 3D, on a beat: four hoops always going round a sun, their speeds set so that planets of a colour come into line): four brass hoops of planets on a pivot axle round a sun, stepping on a metronome you set from 1 to 20 beats a minute (1, 2, 4, 6, 12 or 20); every hoop is always going, and a tap on one and a button set it a place on every beat or two, either way (or every hoop's, with none chosen); four colour buttons tip the hoop chosen into a colour's plane, or every hoop at once with none chosen (a tap anywhere but a hoop or a button lets a hoop go); three or four planets of one colour brought together in one direction from the sun, however their hoops are going, are struck on the next beat and score: at twelve or six o'clock any colour, anywhere else only on hoops in that colour's plane; each hoop drifts a tip by itself now and then, at its own pace, until its colour is pressed again; a new planet lands every few beats, quicker as the game goes on (so the faster the tempo, the faster the planets come), and no room for one ends it. It began as a turn-based match-3 and was rebuilt three times (below). The fifth prototyped (below).
- **Avalanche** (you set off the chain reaction, and where it runs matters): a slope of snow on the sandpile model (each square holds three layers, a fourth slides one onto each neighbour, which can slide in turn); snow falls at random each night, and each day you lay up to three charges and fire them as one avalanche; a slide you trigger scores the square of what it counts for, its size and the bridges it builds across bare squares, a slide the night sets off that reaches the road at the foot costs a life. The sixth prototyped (below).
- **Netting** (lines replaced by loops): six counterparties; each obligation arrives from one of them and you choose who it's owed to; a cycle (A owes B owes C owes A) nets out by its smallest leg and scores; a party past its credit limit defaults. The seventh prototyped (below).
- **Settle** (you pick the tank, density picks the level): six tanks, parcels of four grades from water to naphtha; a parcel sinks below everything lighter and lifts it a level; three of a grade level with each other in neighbouring tanks ship out, and what floated above drops a level, which can line up more; an overflow spills. The eighth prototyped (below).
- **Drift** (2048 turned round: the forecast picks the direction, you pick the square): lay the next tile on any empty square, then the forecast wind blows and everything slides downwind to the wall or the next tile; three alike in a line clear, and the wind keeps pushing. The ninth prototyped (below).

A tenth followed (October 2026), asked for as a game where you make something, in 3D, with guilloché as its base:
- **Guilloché** (a rose engine: engrave the target plate): the machine's rosette, throw, passes, index and feed are bare, and the plate is the target's when what you cut is what is shown. Eleven plates from a single wave ring to a face of five cuts, each a different watch in 3D; each teaches one part of the machine, with a lesson and three tiers of clue, so that someone who can set the last knows how every guilloché is made. Replaced Rosette, below.

An eleventh followed (October 2026), asked for as something more casual and braindead, still about putting something together in 3D:
- **Slipway** (carving: a ship's parts worked by hand from blocks of timber): each part's ghost lies in its block; saw, plane, chisel, drill and sandpaper take the wood away, and the part is graded on the waste left, any cut into it and any knot in it. The cog's eleven parts, from a mast wedge to the masthead. The eleventh prototyped (below), rebuilt as a carving game on 19 October 2026 and given a yard on 23 October: the parts fastened into five assemblies and tried in a gale.

A twelfth followed (19 October 2026), asked for as a sewing game: "Fix buttons, disattached belt loops, holes, whatever."
- **Loom** (weaving, as a nonogram of floats): at every crossing of a warp thread and a pick of weft one yarn lies over the other, and a line's clue is how long its own yarn shows in a row; paint the crossings until every clue is met. Four chapters, 26 cloths: plain cloth, the weaves a weaver names (their shafts tie crossings together), colour and weave (a crossing of one colour is settled and a float runs across it) and gold thread (a third yarn that hides both). Stars for a cloth with no slip and no hint; hints say which line settles what. The first Loom, the draft (thread the warp, lift shafts for each pick, choose each pick's yarn until the cloth matches), is the menu's second game. Replaced Thimble, below.

A thirteenth followed (2 November 2026), asked for as a spiritual clone of Blades of Brim, an endless runner in real 3D, in the Codex's colours, with the knowledge base's questions as what stands in the way ("Ok go yea put it into idle"):
- **Reckoning** (run the quay, answer over the water): three lanes, four swipes, a Hanseatic quay running away to the horizon; crabs on the planks, gulls in the air, walls to run up, gaps in the quay. Now and then the quay breaks at a gap no jump clears and three gulls hang over it, each carrying an answer; cut down the right one and it throws you across. The first slice (boxes for models, one world); see the section below.
