# Ladder
Lewis Carroll's Doublets (October 2026), the word ladder he invented on Christmas Day 1877, called Word-links in his diary and published as Doublets in Vanity Fair from 29 March 1879: from a first word to a last of the same length, one letter changed at a time, each letter keeping its place and every rung a word. Par is the fewest steps it can be done in, found by searching every word there is, so it's proven, not estimated; and every ladder dealt can be done at par in words a person knows. Korbi once turned down a word-ladder mode as "just finding words with letters again". What makes this one more is par (you always play against the best there is, and learn how many routes reach it), the route (the end shows the commonest ladder at par, how many there were, and whether yours was it), and pairs with a link between them: Carroll's own, the word golf Nabokov gave Kinbote in Pale Fire, and pairs chosen for what joins them (COLD to WARM, LEAD to GOLD, BULL to BEAR, SINK or SWIM).

Playing. The ladder is drawn as one: brass rails, the first word pinned at the top of the card and the last at its foot, a rung for every word, and between them the rungs still to go at par as short rungs with a brass dot, so you see how far par is. Tap a letter of the working rung, then its new letter on the keys: if that makes a word it is the next rung; if not, the next rung shows the word in red for a moment, the line says why (not a word here, more than one letter, already on your ladder), and the letter stays chosen, so another key can be tried at once. A whole word can be typed instead, Enter placing it, which suits a keyboard (the arrow keys move along the letters, up and down switch ends). The letter each rung changed is underlined in cobalt. You work from either end, as on paper: Work up (or a tap on the last word) builds from the bottom, Work down from the top, and the ladder is whole the moment a word made at one end is a word of the other; any rungs past the join, a way that end needn't have gone, are dropped. Undo takes off the last rung made, at whichever end, and costs nothing, since a step is counted in the finished ladder, not in the tries; a word already on your ladder can't come back (Undo back to it instead). Hint, 30 seconds, puts on the next rung of the commonest shortest route from where you are, and says how many steps are left. A real word is never refused: a rung may be any word of three to six letters up to SCOWL 55 (tier 5 of `wordlist.js`), British and American spellings both.

The words. The two ends are the commonest words (SCOWL 10), never a plural, a past tense or an -ing form of another word, a word like ALSO or FROM, an irregular past (CAME, KNEW), a comparative, an -ly adverb, computing's jargon (BYTE, CURSOR), or one spelling's alone (COLOUR). Par counts any word a rung may be; the generator keeps only ladders that can be done at par in words no rarer than SCOWL 35, so par never hangs on a word nobody knows. The word list leaves out offensive words but kept some of their plurals and past tenses (CUMS, CUNTS, FUCKS, KIKES and about forty more): Ladder treats those as no words at all, and a few crude ones a player may use (BUM, PEE) the game never shows itself, as an end, a hint, a route or the computer's rung. That belongs in `wordlist.js` for every word game, and is for its owner.

Par and its routes. The words of a length are a graph, two words a step apart when they differ in one letter (found through patterns with one letter blanked, kept as numbers, which is quicker on a phone than strings: a length's graph takes about 25 ms in Node). A breadth-first search from each end gives par and every word on a shortest route; the routes at par are then counted exactly, layer by layer, up to a million, and the test checks the count against a walk of every shortest route by hand. The commonest route at par is the one whose rarest word is commonest, plurals and past tenses counting a shade rarer: it's what the result shows and what a hint follows. Searching takes under a millisecond.

Difficulty. What makes a ladder hard is the search it takes, not its length alone. A ladder whose letters can each be changed straight to the last word's, in some order, has no detours: trying the obvious change at each rung finds it. One that needs a letter changed to a go-between and changed again later (HEAD to TAIL changes its third letter twice: HEAD, HEAL, TEAL, TELL, TALL, TAIL) has a detour, which hides par from the obvious; and one with a single route at par hides it best. The measure is `effort`: how many words a careful person's search tries before it holds a ladder at par (best first by the steps taken plus the letters still wrong, in common words). On 1,800 ladders, a ladder with no detours took par plus one words (straight there); one detour at par 5, about 9; two at par 6, 23; three at par 7 or 8, 56 to 113. The levels, at four letters (three, five and six letters have ranges of their own): Easy is par 3 or 4, at most one detour, at most 12 words tried and two routes at par or more; Medium par 5 or 6, one or two detours, 8 to 60 words; Hard par 6 to 8, two detours or more, 25 words at least, and exactly one route at par, so on Hard there is one way to do it in par and the count proves it. A test run of 360 ladders climbs as it should: par 4.1, 5.6 and 7.2 on average, 6, 16 and 73 words tried. The generator draws a first word, takes every end a par's distance away that can be reached at par in common words, and grades them until one fits: 1 to 15 ms at four letters, 70 at six. These figures come from the search, not from people: whether Easy is easy is for Korbi's thumb.

Ladders, in the menu: Today's, Random, Linked or The book. Today's is Medium, four or five letters, Classic, made from today's date (`today()`), the same on every phone, and counts for the games screen the moment it's whole, as its score in seconds (`noteDayTime`; the tile shows it as a time and opens today's while it's still to play). Random deals at the menu's level from the word lengths ticked (3, 4, 5, 6; four and five to start). Linked deals 160 pairs in six themes, ticked in the menu (Opposites, Trade, Food & farm, Nature, Creatures, Life & play), each with its line in Carroll's manner ("Turn a BULL into a BEAR", "Render FAT into OIL", "Roll SPOT into TERM"), at the level its par puts it, none twice until every pair at that level has come up (`ladder:seen`). The book is Carroll's own, his doublets in his book's order (Macmillan, 1879), his example HEAD to TAIL first, each a chapter that opens once the one before is climbed, with his line ("Drive PIG into STY", "Evolve MAN from APE"); then Kinbote's three. Seven of Carroll's thirty-eight are left out: ROGUE to BEAST, ELM to OAK, HARE to SOUP, PITCH to TENTS, PITY to GOOD and EEL to PIE need words nobody uses now (BEAUT, OIK, SOUK, TENCH, POOS, PEE), and BUY an ASS a word the list leaves out. The rest may use the words of his day, up to SCOWL 50 (GIRD, GIRT, RICK, as in his own solutions). CAIN and ABEL are names: they join the graph as guests, the ends of their own ladder and rungs of no other. Kinbote's records are "hate—love in three, lass—male in four, and live—dead in five (with lend in the middle)": the first two are par, and the third can't be done in five through any word of SCOWL's widest list (it takes six, through LEND as he says), which the result points out. The knowledge base supplies none of the pairs: its things are names and phrases the word list doesn't hold, and the few plain words among them (gold, coal, corn, cotton) are in Trade by hand.

Score, bests and the games screen. A finished ladder's score is its seconds, a minute for every step over par and half a minute a hint; lower is better. A step costs more than speed does, since par is the point. Bests are kept by kind, level and rules (`ladder:bests`, lowest), and clean pars in a row (at par without a hint, `ladder:pars`). The book keeps each chapter's steps (`ladder:book`).

Rules: Classic, or Grow and shrink. The genre's own variant, word ladders with additions, lets a step also add or take away a letter: it earns its place by opening pairs of different lengths, which Classic can't play at all (HOT to COLD in three, SUN to MOON, WAR to PEACE, MAN to WOMAN, ORE to METAL, IRON to STEEL, CRUDE to FUEL, GRAPE to WINE, SHEEP to WOOL), and by routes through shorter and longer words. 35 of the 160 linked pairs need it, and every pair plays by it. Tap a gap between two letters (or before or after them) to add one there, ⌫ on a chosen letter takes it away, or type the whole word. Its graph is every length at once, 15,378 words and 48,570 steps, built in about half a second in Node (the rete says Setting out the words), and its search's lower bound is the edit distance. Weighed and left out: an anagram as a step (rearranging every letter at once makes most ladders a step or two and the puzzle another game), a word the ladder must pass through (two ladders end to end: more length, nothing new), and a timed run of ladders (the race below is three against the clock already).

The computer, in Settings (3 November 2026, Korbi: "'against the computer' makes little sense"; "probably drop from all": no rival that races you), is off or a partner, at a level: Novice, Club or Master. It searches as a person does, with `search()`: a novice knows only the commonest words and rushes at the end word, so it often takes a step or two more than par; a club player rushes less; a master searches exactly and finds par. As a partner it takes the seat nobody has: it holds every other letter of a random or linked Classic ladder, dealt as a team's are (the letters that must change first, so both of you are needed), and the two of you work down from the first word. Whenever the ladder changes it looks for a step it can make with its own letters (`partnerMove`): a master takes one on a shortest route whenever there is one, a club player or a novice follows the route its own search finds, which may be longer; it makes the step after a person's thinking, or waits for yours, and the line under the figures says which. You can change only your letters (ringed in your colour; its rungs are marked in copper), and you see the whole of the last word, since the computer can't talk. Undo and Hint work as alone; the result counts each one's steps; a partner's ladders keep bests of their own. With a master and a careful player, sixteen ladders in the test all came out at par.

Together: the pair from the partner sheet, or a party of up to six. One table plays either game, chosen in the menu's together part for the next game and in the lobby for this one until it starts; a pair plays as a team by default and a party races. Everyone says Ready, and a countdown starts the game for everyone at the same moment by the database's clock, as the head-to-heads on `duel.js` do; anyone ready may start without waiting for someone who never comes (in a team, that player's letters are let go to the rest until they arrive). Ladder runs its own table rather than `duel.js` because the table also holds a team's ladder and a request carries no mode, so the page decides, as Manifest's does: it is `duel-core.js`'s table (the same Ready, countdown, runs and standings) with the team's ladder beside the runs, every change a transaction on it (`ladder-table.js`, pure; `ladder-room.js`, the page round it).

The team: one Classic ladder, long enough for everyone to hold a letter (four letters for a pair or three, five for five, six for six). The letters are dealt round the table, the letters the first and last words differ in first, then the rest, starting a step further each game; and the dealer makes sure there are as many letters to change as players. So everyone holds a letter that every ladder between the two words must change, and nobody can climb it alone. Only you may change your letters, which are ringed in your colour (another's are faded, and a tap on one says whose it is); and each of you sees only your own letters of the last word, the others' sealed, so you know where your letters must end and your partner knows where theirs must, and the team has to talk. A rung made on a ladder that has moved on is refused, and the page shows it as it is. Undo and Hint are the team's (a hint's rung may be anyone's letter). A player who is away, asleep or gone, lets go of their letters, and anyone may change them until they're back, so a sleeping phone never holds the team up. Each rung's changed letter carries its maker's colour, and the end counts each player's rungs. The team's score is its time from the start, a minute a step over par and half a minute a hint, kept for the pair as a team's best.

The race: the same three ladders for everyone, dealt by whoever deals at their level, letters and rules, each climbed on their own phone from the shared start, Next ladder after each, the clock running through. The others' progress shows under the figures (Sarah 2 of 3, then her score). The lowest score wins (seconds from the start, a minute a step over par, half a minute a hint), recorded for the pair as a head-to-head; Rematch deals three more, the one who asked ready. A reload carries on where it was (`ladder:match`). In a party it's listed as a race for up to six.

Saved state. The ladder on screen is `ladder:run`: its pair and par, the rungs at each end and the order they came in, the hints, the clock, and the computer's climb or a partner's letters and who made each rung, saved at every rung and every five seconds, so a partner watching sees it as it stands. Choices are `ladder:prefs`. Links: `#d=DAY` today's, `#s=SEED&l=LEVEL&n=LENGTHS&r=RULES` a random ladder, `#p=FIRST-LAST&r=RULES` a pair (`&c=` a chapter of the book).

The screen. On a phone: the figures (par, steps, the clock), the pair's line, the ladder's card taking what's left, one line of what to do or what happened (one line always, so nothing moves), the tools, the keys. A tile is as large as lets the whole ladder at par fit the card, and never smaller than a thumb's target (2.2rem); a longer ladder scrolls inside the card with both ends pinned. Held sideways, the ladder takes the left with all the height and the figures, the line, the tools and the keys stand beside it, the keys at the bottom; an upright tablet draws it all larger. Checked at 390×600, 390×664, 390×844, 820×1180 and 1180×820, in the four themes by day and night, played through with taps and touches.

The rules (October 2026) are a sheet of their own, from the header's Rules or the menu's The rules, as the other games' are: in plain prose, what a doublet is, Carroll's HEAD to TAIL worked through (five steps, par, the third letter changed twice: a detour), what par is and how the card shows it, the taps, the keys and working from either end, which words a rung may be, the hint and the score, the four kinds of ladder, Grow and shrink (HOT to COLD in three), the computer, today's ladder and the two ways together. Its numbers come from the engine as it opens, so the sheet can't drift from the game, and a solo ladder and the computer stand still while it's open, as for the menu. Looked at on a phone (390×664, 390×844) and an iPad both ways, in Codex, Modern and Kontor by day and night.

`tests/ladder.mjs` checks Carroll's rules, par for known pairs, the route counts against a hand walk, the generator at every level and length across 360 seeds (each fits its level; Hard's single route counted by hand up to two; the same seed, the same ladder), the grading's climb, a run through JSON, hints and scores, the computer players at their three levels, the computer partner (it changes only its own letters, waits when the next step is yours, and finds par with a careful player), the daily, every pair of the book and the linked pairs, and the table through the database's round trip: the letters dealt round two, three and four players with everyone holding one that must change, the last word's letters each sees making the whole between them and neither alone, one player stopped at the other's letter, the pair climbing at par together, refused rungs, undo and hints, an away player's letters let go, a race's standings and its end. Two phones in one browser on the stand-in database played a team game (each seeing only its own letters of HOST: H _ _ T and _ O S _) and a race of three.

Not done: the computer in a seat at a table (an away player's letters go to the others instead, which keeps people central), Grow and shrink for a team or the computer partner (a letter keeps its owner only while letters keep their places), a daily drawn from the linked pairs (today's is made, so it never runs out or repeats), and the words' meanings (Slate's dictionary could give them, a tap on a rung).
