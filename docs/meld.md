# Gin rummy
Gin rummy for two (31 October 2026, Korbi: "meld"), against the computer or at a table with a friend on their own phone (README, Seats). It is the first of the games that had a placeholder (README, Coming soon) to be built, on seats.js as Big Two and Landlord are: `meld-rules.js` the pure rules, `meld-bot.js` the computer player, `meld.js` the page.

The rules are the usual ones, set down in the file's head. Ten cards each, the next card turned up; the player who didn't deal may take it or pass, then the dealer, and if both pass the first draws from the stock. A turn draws from the stock or takes the pile's top card, then throws one card, and the card taken from the pile can't be thrown back the same turn. Melds are three or four of a rank, or three or more in a suit in a row, the ace low only (Q-K-A is not a run). The cards in no meld are deadwood, an ace 1 point, the face cards 10. With 10 or less in deadwood after the throw a player may knock instead of throwing; with none it is gin, and all eleven cards melded with no throw is big gin. When the stock is down to two cards and the player who drew doesn't knock, the hand is a draw and nobody scores.

Scoring: the knocker scores the difference in deadwood, once the other has laid off (put cards on the knocker's melds: the fourth of a set of three, the card above or below a run). If the other has as much or less it is an undercut: the other scores the difference and 25 more. Gin scores the other's deadwood and 25, with no laying off; big gin 31. A game goes to 50, 100 (the default) or 200; the winner adds 100 for the game (200 if the other scored nothing, a shutout), and each side 25 for every hand won. Two choices are made for the player: laying off is done for the defender, as well as it can be done, because a person choosing among layoffs is only a chance to get it wrong and there is nothing hidden in it; and the knocker's melds are set to leave the defender the least to lay off, as a careful player would.

The engine is bitmasks over the positions in a hand: every meld a hand holds is a mask, and the least deadwood is a search over the first card not yet placed (deadwood, or one of the melds it starts), memoised by the cards used, so a hand of eleven is a few hundred steps and the computer can ask it hundreds of times per move. It can also list every way of melding down to the least deadwood, which is what the knocker's choice of melds and the display of your hand use. `tests/meld.mjs` checks it against a plain search that shares no code with it, over 400 hands.

The computer player has three strengths. Easy only takes the pile's card when it completes a meld, throws its highest loose card (three times in ten another loose card) and knocks at 5 or less. Medium takes a card that saves 3 points of deadwood, throws the card that leaves the least deadwood and, among those, the one that leaves the most unseen cards that would make a meld, and knocks at 8 or less. Hard also remembers the whole pile, counts what the other has picked up from it (the other's melds in the making: it doesn't throw a card of that rank, or next to it in suit), takes a card that saves 2 points, or 1 if it joins a meld, and knocks when the other is likely to hold more, which it reckons from the turns gone and the pile cards taken. Over 160 games Hard beats Medium about 3 in 5 and Medium beats Easy about 3 in 4; the knock rule was tried several ways and mattered little, how much weight it gives to cards still to be drawn into a meld mattered most.

The page: the other hand as backs with what it has picked up from the pile beside it, the stock and the pile between, the latest thing done, and your cards along the foot, the melds set apart from the rest (always melds first since 4 November 2026; by rank and by suit were menu choices), the card you have just drawn ringed. A tap on a card picks it; Throw and Knock sit under the cards, Knock showing the deadwood the pick would leave (Gin at none, Big gin when all eleven are melded) and grey while the pick leaves more than 10. The status line says what is asked, and what the pick would leave. At a hand's end every card is shown: each side's melds and deadwood, the cards laid off in brass, and a line saying what was scored; the next hand deals itself after a quarter of a minute or when either player taps Next hand. The game's end gives the tally: points, hands won, the game. Random's round is a hand; a finished game counts for the streak on the games screen.

The 3D table (Menu, View; 3 November 2026, Korbi: "an aesthetic 3d mode ... pretty much just like dandan if it's a card game"): `meld-3d.js` on the shared cloth (`table3d.js`); the flat table stays the default and the choice is the device's own (`suite:meld-view`). The stock and pile are the flat table's own buttons, tapped on the cloth.

Lessons (Menu, Learn; 4 November 2026, Korbi: lessons are "a lessons tab accessible there same as any other lesson for these games"): `meld-course.js` on the suite course sheet, progress `meld:course`. course-cards.js runs ranks ace high, so `toGin` translates a card before the rules look at it. Every count and outcome a lesson states is recomputed by `tests/meld-course.mjs`; a new example must pass it.

**4 November 2026, Korbi: the menu is the minimum it needs to be.** The menu is View (flat or 3D). The computer's strength, A game ends at and how your cards sort are gone: the game ends at 100, the cards are sorted by melds, whatever was saved (a game in hand keeps its own); the computer's strength is the hidden ramp (`meld:ramp`, up a step after a game won, down after one lost; its first step is the level that was saved; a lesson's game plays Easy and leaves it alone).
