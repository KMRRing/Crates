# Reckoning
A runner (2 November 2026), in Blades of Brim's way, in `reckoning.html`, `reckoning.js` and `reckoning.css`; its rules in `reckoning-engine.js` (pure functions on one plain object, tested in Node by `tests/reckoning.mjs`), its drawing in `reckoning-3d.js` and its questions from `reckoning-questions.js`. It sits in Idle because it is played on reflex, with a question now and then rather than a puzzle to work out. This is the first slice: every shape is a box standing in for a model to come, there is one world (the Hanseatic quay), and no sound.

**The controls are four swipes**, a finger going far enough in a direction (22 px) acts at once, and the arrow keys or WASD and space do the same on a computer. A swipe is read by what is near when it lands, counted in seconds of running to a foe (so the windows are the same at 10 m/s and at 16, which the run climbs to over a kilometre or two) rather than in metres:
- *A foe in front, in my lane, on the ground:* up, very close (0.28 s), cuts it down; sooner (up to about 0.55 s) is a plain jump that clears it; later, or no swipe, and it hits. A swipe in the last moment still counts.
- *A foe in the next lane, at a medium range (0.08 to 0.6 s):* a swipe sideways into it changes lane and cuts it down, on the ground against a foe on the ground, or in the air against one in the air; the other way dodges it. Coming down, the same swipe swoops onto a foe on the ground from up to 4.5 m above it (2 November 2026, Korbi: "while in air when swiping left or right on descend should enter attack mode"), and counts as an attack in the air. With none in the next lane, a foe two lanes across (0.16 to 0.6 s off: the hero crosses both lanes to it, twice the way) is cut the same way (2 November 2026, Korbi: "swiping left/right attacks units 2 lanes across as well when in attack distance"); a foe in the next lane comes first. A foe attacks the lane it aimed at when it launched (below), and only a foe within a lane of me aims at all, so the far lane is always safe from a foe in an outer lane.
- *One swipe, two foes* (2 November 2026, Korbi: "if player is on leftmost tile and two enemies on right and rightmost, swiping right will attack both"): a cut sideways does not stop at the foe it was aimed at; when it lands it goes on, in the same direction, to the next foe in the next lane along if that one is within half a second (`SWEEP_WIN`) and in reach the way a swipe sideways picks one (a foe on the ground from the ground, one in the air from the air), with no hop between. From the left lane, with a crab in the middle and one on the right, one swipe right takes both; in the air, gulls in a row are a chain. It never goes on to the gulls of a question: those are chosen.
- *A foe above, in my lane:* up is a jump attack, from the ground or the air, within 0.55 s, and a swoop onto a gull just below me counts the same: no more than 1.2 m (`UP_ABOVE`) over its base (2 November 2026, Korbi: "if the player is just slightly above in the same lane, up will work"). From higher it is down that works, as a bonk (below); it used to reach a gull 2.8 m under me.
- *The bonk:* down, in the air, directly over a foe lands on it and bounces into another jump. It is what reaches a gull I am far above in its own lane (2 November 2026, Korbi: "otherwise down slam will work if the player on the way collides with the flying mob in which case it's a bonk and player will jump back up after attack"): it works within 0.22 s of the gull, or a dive that crosses it on the way down.
- *Down in a jump, with nothing under me:* neither a bonk nor a slam, only a quick drop, and it lands in a roll (2 November 2026, Korbi: "if down during jump and not [bonking] or slamming, do a roll on land"), with the roll's 0.45 s of cutting down what reaches me in my lane and going under a gull's dive. The engine knows the drop by its speed on landing (a dive starts at 24 m/s, which no fall of its own comes near), so nothing has to be remembered and a bonk, a lunge or the water cancels it by themselves.
- *The roll:* down on the planks (2 November 2026, Korbi: "rolling attacks unit in front if in attack distance") is a roll, a forward somersault of 0.45 s (four and a half to seven metres of quay; it was 0.32 s until Korbi said on 2 November 2026 that rolls "should last a bit longer") in which every foe on the ground that reaches me in my lane is cut down, and no hop afterwards; sooner than that is too early and it ends before the foe arrives, a foe in the next lane still hits a rolling hero, and up in the middle of it jumps out of it. It is the plain way to meet a foe without jumping, where up is the sharper one, and its window the wider. Down again in the middle of a roll (2 November 2026, Korbi: "repeatedly rolling down gives no window where a ground enemy in front of the player would be able to successfully attack") queues the next roll to start the moment this one ends, so a run of them has no gap at all, and each swipe guards my lane for 0.4 s besides. A roll also goes under a gull's dive; it does not dodge a lunge from another lane ("rolling on a lane that the attacking enemy isn't on won't dodge their side lunges").
- *The slam:* down, in the air, from more than one jump height (1.6 m; so after a wall jump, or a kill in the air, which throws me higher than a jump does) falls hard, and on the planks (never on a foe, which is a bonk instead) a shockwave cuts down the foes on the ground in my lane and the ones beside it, three metres ahead. Down from a plain jump's height is only a quick drop.
- *A flyer's dive:* a gull that has launched at my lane (below) can be cut down from the ground by a swipe sideways towards it (2 November 2026, Korbi: "shortly before the player would take damage from a diving range attack while on the ground from the side, the player has a window where if they attack left/right, they can target and kill the diving flyer"): from the launch, 0.3 s before it reaches me, until just before it hits (`DIVE_MIN`, 0.03 s, though the hit itself starts at about 0.05 to 0.07 s, so the window is in practice the whole dive). Before the launch a gull is out of reach of a cut from the ground, as before. It counts as an attack in the air: my jump comes back and the chain counts.
- *The wall:* with a wall beside me in the outer lane, a swipe into it runs up it, lifting me to 2 m and along it for as long as it goes; jump first and swipe into the wall in the air and I keep my height. Up on the wall is a wall jump, higher than any jump from the ground (peak 4.4 m), and away or down lets go. A wall carries me over a pit no jump clears.
- *The air chain:* every foe cut down in the air throws me up again, so gulls hung a lane or less apart over a pit carry me across it, and killing in the air keeps the chain count (the points rise with it) until I land. Every attack in the air, a jump attack, a lane change into a gull, a bonk, the right gull over the water, also gives my jump back (2 November 2026, Korbi: "after every aerial attack the hero jumps and resets his jump"): one more up in the air, with nothing to cut, is a second jump from where I am (a whole jump height more), and sideways swipes carry on the chain as before. The jump is taken away again by landing, the wall, the water and a wrong gull; a second one needs another cut.
- A swipe with nothing yet to work on waits 0.14 s (a jump swiped as I come down jumps as I land).
- *Moving a cut in flight* (2 November 2026, Korbi: "it should be possible to jump up, then left and right mid air to correct the answer bird" and "up -> left -> left if done fast enough should go from attacking the right bird to the left bird"): while I am flying at a foe, a swipe sideways does not wait for the cut to land; it moves the cut to the foe in the next lane (a gull of a question counts from a second off, any other foe within 0.6 s), swapping the lane at once, and the one after moves it a lane further. So up, then left, left, in the right lane, goes from the right gull to the middle one to the left one, so long as each swipe is made before the cut lands (the last 0.03 s, `RETARGET_MIN`, is too late), and in the air, jumping up then swiping left or right does the same before any cut has started. Up and down in the middle of a cut wait their 0.14 s for it to land, as any swipe with nothing yet to work on does.
Three hearts. A foe's hit and the water each cost one, with a second and a half of grace and a blinking hero.

**Flyers follow my height** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "quite easy to gain vertical height in this game. Enemy flyers slowly rise or fall closer to the player if the player is far above or below them. If the player is a bit above and next to them then the player can hit them with left/right swipes, or if the player is just slightly above in the same lane, up will work. Otherwise down slam will work"). Going up had been a way out of everything: a bonk and a second jump together take me to about five and a half metres, over every gull. `drift()` makes a gull within four seconds of me that has not launched climb at 1.6 m/s while I am more than 1.9 m over its base, until I am that near, and sink while I am more than 2.4 m under it (never below its own 1.35 m), so it comes up to where a swipe reaches it and the height gained is not a way out; it is slow, so a hero who comes back down finds it much as it was. What reaches a gull, by how far over its base I am (`SIDE_ABOVE` 2.0 m, `UP_ABOVE` 1.2 m): a swipe sideways from the next lane up to 2 m over it (and 2.5 m under it, on the way up), up in its own lane up to 1.2 m over it, and above that down, which is a bonk. The gulls of a question and the ones over a pit do not move: they are an answer and a bridge. Two things are untouched and worth a look on a phone: the height each kill and each second jump gives is as it was, so how high I can get is the same and only what is up there has changed, and a chain over a pit that goes from a high gull to a low one in the same lane now asks for a down (a bonk) in the last 0.22 s, where an up used to do (the bot knows it: the test plays four seeds of 1,800 m, and sixteen were played once, all without a fall).

**The foes telegraph** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "enemy units should telegraph their attacks"; he had first said there was no need). A foe within a lane of me goes through three stages in `think()`, counted in seconds to contact so they last the same at any speed: from 1.1 s away it *sees* me (it turns its head to my lane, and a brass "!" pops up over it), from 0.6 s it *winds up* (a crab rears with its claws up and apart, a gull climbs and spreads its wings; the "!" turns red and shakes), and from 0.3 s it *launches*: a crab springs across to the lane I am in at that moment, a gull dives onto it. The launch is a commitment, `aim`, and the hit lands there: a lane away, a jump, a roll (against a dive) or a cut after the launch is a dodge, and a swipe away in the last moment (the lane change takes 0.13 s) is too late. Leave its reach before it winds up and it loses me and never attacks. A foe I only come within a lane of at the last moment goes through the three in a step or two, as it did before. A gull in my own lane never attacks (I run under it), and none attacks me while I am in the air or on a wall: it waits for me to come down. A foe over a pit and the gulls of a question are the bridge, not enemies (`think()` leaves them). The rules' events (`notice`, `wind`, `launch`) are for the drawing and the swipe log.

**Grace** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "should get more grace time: immediately after swipe is complete, hero should be immune to attack from that direction"). A swipe that does something guards me for 0.4 s, from the moment it is made and again from the moment its cut lands, against the foes on the side it was made towards: left, the lanes to the left of the one I swiped from; right, the ones to the right; up and down, the ones in my lane. So a swipe a moment too late to cut a foe, towards it, no longer leaves me hit by it, and a lane change is not undone by a foe I was swiping at. The foe beside me, on the other side, hits as it did, and a foe that arrives after the 0.4 s too. The grace after a hit (1.4 s, the blinking hero) is as it was.

**The question.** Every few chunks of quay (the first by about 100 m) the quay stops at a gap wider than a jump, always, and over it hang three gulls, one to a lane, each with an answer; the question and the three answers (left to right as the lanes lie) stand over the top of the quay, under the hearts, from twelve seconds of running before the gap (2 November 2026, Korbi: "questions should be above the player near the top of the screen somewhere under the hearts with the answers" and "more time to think about right answer"; it was seventy metres before, four to seven seconds, under the quay), and the card of the lane I am in is ringed. Nothing is up between questions, so the quay has the whole screen; held sideways the question stands over the middle of it. The run's end is a sheet over the quay, the questions missed and their answers listed in it. The rules make the gap in time (`LOOK`, how much quay is made ahead, is 200 m, twelve seconds at the top speed). The right gull, cut down by any of the attacks above, throws me across (and gives my jump back, so one more up over the water carries me further); a jump up to a second in front of a gull of a question is a jump attack on it (a plain gull needs 0.55 s), so a jump swiped anywhere near the edge, in the lane of the answer, flies to the gull and the throw after it clears the gap by itself (the tests swipe from 0.2 to 0.95 s off, at the lowest speed); a wrong one stops me dead (no bounce) and I drop in the water, and back on the far bank; going under them unanswered does the same and counts as missed. A wrong or a missed answer costs two hearts, one for the answer and one for the water (2 November 2026, Korbi: "getting a question wrong costs 2 lives; right restored one"); with two left or fewer it ends the run. A right answer is worth 500 and gives a heart back, up to the three (it was every third right answer in a row). A wrong answer's right one shows on the cards, and at the run's end every question missed is listed with its answer and the knowledge base's explanation. The slam never answers a question (a gull is in the air, and the shockwave runs on the ground), so there is no shortcut. The questions are the choice banks Punt asks from, cut down to ones that fit a gull's banner (one right answer, answers of 34 characters or fewer, a question of 70 or fewer: 2 November 2026, Korbi, "the questions should be capped in length"; seventy is two lines over the quay on the narrowest phone, so a question is never cut short, and it dropped the pool from 687 to 621): the Hanseatic quay's are merchants, cities, economics, refining and titles (621 now); Punt's own grades, 1 to 10, set how hard they are, a notch harder every two gaps from 3. Each carries the entities it is about; a miss is kept with them under `reckoning:miss` for the pile (not yet read by Deck or the learning modes, which is a change to `pile.js`, a shared file, still to ask Korbi about).

**The quay** is made from the seed, chunk by chunk, a hundred and twenty metres ahead of the hero, whatever the clock does, so a run is the same quay from the same seed (a daily and a duel will want this; neither is wired yet). The chunks, by distance: crabs (one to three, a lane each), gulls over the lanes with a crab among them (from 60 m), an air chain (gulls over a pit of 14 m or more, from 140 m), a wall over a pit (from 220 m), a wall run ending above rows of crabs, for a wall jump and a slam (from 330 m), and the question. The test plays four seeds of 1,800 m with a bot that swipes by the rules, and it must get through all of them, the questions included; that is the proof that every chunk can be won with the swipes the rules give (it still takes the odd hit: the bot is not perfect).

**Cut down, they fly back** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "units when hit should fly back"): a foe the hero cuts down is thrown the way the cut came, tumbling, and shrinks away in 0.85 s: a cut from in front sends it on ahead and up, one from the side to that side, a roll along the planks, a slam outward from me, a bonk squashes it first. Korbi wrote "Rennt units"; this is read as every unit, there being no kind of that name. It is the drawing's only (`reckoning-3d.js`, `knock` and `flyBack`); the rules have already counted the kill.

**The crab** was redrawn (2 November 2026, Korbi: "redesign the enemy melee unit, idk what that's supposed to be but looks weird"): the boxes and flat slabs it was made of did not read as anything. It is now a boiled-red shell, wide and low, over a pale belly, two white eyes on stalks (they stand up when it has seen me), six legs that scuttle, and two big claws with a thumb and a finger each that snap while it looks at me and go up, apart, when it winds up. Under 1.5 m across, as the asset list says. At night it is lifted a shade so it is not lost in the dark.

**The look** is Blades of Brim's, held back: a perspective camera behind the hero, and a world that curves away below the horizon (a patch to every material's vertex shader: the further from the camera, the lower), fog to the sky's colour, a hemisphere light and one sun, a soft disc under each thing instead of shadows. The colours are read from the page's own tokens each time the theme changes (`--yard`, `--sheet`, `--brass`, `--plate`, the enamels `--c0`..`--c3`), the enamels held down a third toward their grey and pulled toward the page, so the three themes, day and night, each get their quay: a pale stone one in the day, a moonlit one at night. The warehouses along both sides are stepped gables in brick, ochre, slate, cream and sage. It needs WebGL (there is no flat fallback; without it the page says so). Three.js comes from `vendor/three-run.js`, a second bundle beside Harbour's (`vendor/three.js`, shared and without a perspective camera, fog, textures or glTF): the same r186 package bundled with esbuild, with what the runner needs, glTF and skeletons and clips included for the models.

**Fitted to the asset list** (2 November 2026, the Docs page "Reckoning: models and assets", which the modeller works from): the grey boxes were brought to the sizes it states, so a model replacing one changes nothing about what a lane holds: a crab is at most 1.5 m across with its claws, a gull's wings span 1.6 m (three hang side by side over a gap, 1.7 m apart), and only the gulls of a question carry a banner and its rope (every gull did at first). The gull's beak pointed away from the hero, because foes are turned half round to face him and it was drawn on the wrong side; it now points at him.

**The hero** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "overhaul player character: make them a pretty model"): the grey figure became a young merchant-adventurer of the Hanse, seen from behind as the camera has him: a tricorn with a plume, hair tied back in a tail, a navy coat with gilt trim and two tails, a red sash and scarf (its ends streaming behind), cream breeches, tall boots, a rapier in the right hand and its scabbard at the left hip. He is built round joints (hips, knees, shoulders, elbows, neck, head, hat, plume, the coat's tails, the scarf's ends, the scabbard; the `J` table in `reckoning-3d.js`) and every pose is only numbers on them, in `poseHero`: the run's stride follows the distance run, not the clock, so he never slides at any speed; a jump tucks the knees and throws the arms up, a fall spreads them; a lunge thrusts the rapier at the foe, a bonk and a slam have their own stoop; a roll folds him into a ball; on a wall he leans into it. The coat's tails, the scarf's ends and the plume flutter, and stream out further in the air and in a slam. The coat is navy by day and a lighter blue by night, so he is seen against the water; the colours that come from the page's ink (boots, hair, scabbard) would turn pale at night and, under the moon's blue light, grey, so by night they are given brighter, warmer browns of their own. The joints carry the names an animator will want, so a glTF with joints of these names can take over without the rules changing; nothing in the rules depends on the shape.

**Left and right** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "swipe directions are inverted"): the camera looks down +z, where a right-handed world's +x falls on the screen's left, and the rules put lane +1 on the hero's right, so a swipe right went to the left of the screen, and the answers (lane 0, 1, 2 for the first, second, third card) ran backwards. The view now draws everything inside a group mirrored in x (`world` in `reckoning-3d.js`) and gives the camera the mirrored x; the rules, the saved runs and the tests are untouched.

**The swipe log** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "add logs to the game that shows and indexes the swipes and events; I want to copy what happened and explain what I wanted to happen in those cases"). Every swipe and every event the rules make is a numbered line in `reckoning-log.js` (pure, tested in Node): `#41 t=12.30 z=96.1 | swipe LEFT | me: lane R, flying y=2.10 at gull R +0.21s y2.4 gate4 (up, 0.21s left), guarded up 0.31s | foes: gull R +0.21s y2.4 gate4; gull M +0.37s y2.4 gate4`. A swipe's line says where I was and what I was doing, and the foes about to reach me with the seconds until each does (the swipe windows are in seconds, so that is the number that explains what a swipe did); the lines after it are what the rules did about it (a lane change, a cut, a kill and how, a hurt, a clang, an answer with its question, a swipe kept 0.14 s, late or dropped, and a foe seeing me, winding up or launching, with the lane it aimed at). A swipe's line also says what each foe near is about to do ("crab L +0.41s winding up"). It is under Menu → Swipe log and a button on the run's end sheet: a dialog with the run's lines in a text box (so a note can be written beside a line, "#41: I wanted the left gull") and Copy, and This run / Last run when there is one before it. A legend heads it. It keeps the last 900 lines, stands the run still while it is open, and notes every pause, so the gaps explain themselves. It is kept in the tab's `sessionStorage` (`reckoning:log`), not under the synced `reckoning:` keys: it is long, and the other device's run is not this one's swipes. Reopening the page picks the same run's log up where it was, with a first line saying the swipes before it are not kept if it could not.

**Kept** under `reckoning:`: `run` (the run, every three seconds and as the page is left; the run carries on from a swipe), `best` (points), `stats` (runs, right and not) and `miss`. A partner watching reads `run` and draws it without a clock. Random mode deals it; a round is a run over.
