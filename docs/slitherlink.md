# Slitherlink
Draw one closed loop along the lines between the dots of a grid; each number says how many of the four sides of its cell the loop uses; the loop never crosses, branches or touches itself (31 October 2026, Korbi's list of genres to add). Every puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning, never by a guess. Tap an edge to draw it, again to cross it (a small mark for "not part of the loop", a note for yourself), again to clear it; hold an edge, or right-click it, to cross it at once. Only the lines count: the puzzle is solved the moment exactly its answer is drawn, so crosses are notes. A finger means the nearest grid line within 0.4 of a cell, so a tap in the middle of a cell does nothing (a miss never marks a wrong edge). A rule you break shows at once, with a line under the board saying which, from the rules alone and never from the answer: a number with more lines round it than it says (in oxblood), three lines at a corner or a line running into a dead end (the dot in oxblood), a loop closed round the wrong cells (its edges in oxblood). A number goes pale once it has all its lines. Undo and Redo step through your marks. A hint (30 seconds) first puts a wrong mark right, and otherwise makes the next step a person would take and says why ("this number has all its lines: the other sides are crossed"), the edges it settled in gilt and the number or corner that says so ringed.

Sizes 5×5, 7×7 and 10×10 (the menu); three levels (the header, mirrored in the menu). Nothing comes from the knowledge base: Slitherlink is numbers, and has no content to choose.

Making a puzzle (`slitherlink-engine.js`). Grow a random region of cells, one cell at a time, that stays in one piece, has no hole and touches itself at no corner (a pair of diagonal cells in with the other two out would give a corner four lines meet at), up to half to seven tenths of the board: its outline is a single loop that never meets itself, and is the answer. Number every cell with how many of its sides are on the loop. Then take numbers away in random order, each one only for as long as a solver of the level asked for can still finish the puzzle from what is left. That proves the answer unique, since every step that solver takes is forced (a solver that completes a puzzle by forced steps has found the only answer there is), and it is the whole of the uniqueness argument: there is no search in the generator. `tests/slitherlink.mjs` checks the solver's rules against a plain walk of every simple loop on five small boards (it counts exactly as many answers as that does, on 180 sets of numbers, 144 with an answer), and 15 puzzles across every size and level against a counting search that guesses. 186 more were checked outside the suite the same way (none had two answers or none, every one landed on its level).

Difficulty is the hardest step a solver needs that reasons as a person does, never how many numbers there are. The rules: a number has the lines it says (all its lines drawn means its other sides are crossed; as many sides left as lines wanted means they are all drawn), a corner of the loop has two lines or none (two means the rest are crossed; one with one way on takes it; none, with only one side left, crosses it), and a line may join the two ends of one path only when that closes the whole answer. Easy: those rules alone. Medium: a trial as well, an edge supposed a line or crossed and the rules followed for two rounds; if they break the edge is the other. Hard: a trial followed to the end. A puzzle's level is the lowest that solves it, and a puzzle no trial of one edge can finish is never dealt. In 186 puzzles every size and level came out at the level asked for the first time; on a 10×10 the Easy has about 42 numbers of 100, the Medium 37, the Hard 36, so the levels differ in how the numbers have to be used, not how many there are. The generator tries up to eight times and then deals the nearest level, and the line says so. Puzzles are made in a worker (`slitherlink-worker.js`) with a spare of each kind lately played kept, so a new puzzle is instant; measured in Node, a 10×10 Hard takes up to three quarters of a second (a phone is slower), a 7×7 up to a third, and the rete shows only for a kind with no spare yet. The same seed makes the same puzzle on every device.

Score: your time plus 30 seconds a hint, lower better. Bests by size and the level a puzzle came to, in `slitherlink:times` (not `:best`, which the games screen reads as highest-is-best). Today's puzzle is a 7×7 at Medium made from the date (`today()`), the same for everyone; its first solve is today's time on the games screen (`noteDayTime`, `slitherlink:today`), counted the moment the lines are the answer, and the tile opens today's while it's still to play, asking before leaving a puzzle you've started. A link (Copy a link to this puzzle) carries the seed, size and level. The run in play is `slitherlink:run` (the puzzle and its answer, your marks, undo and redo, the clock, hints, the hint showing), saved at every mark and every five seconds, so a reload or a partner watching sees exactly what's on the screen; `slitherlink:prefs` is the size and level, `slitherlink:spares` the spare puzzles. The clock stands while the menu, the rules or the games screen is open, and while the page is away. Random mode counts a solve as a round (`roundOver`).

Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height, the time beside it at the top, the line and the tools low. The rules are a sheet of their own, from Rules in the header or The rules in the menu.

`tests/slitherlink.mjs` (about two and a half seconds) checks the board's edges, corners and cells, the solver against every loop, 15 puzzles across every size and level (one answer each, a loop meeting every number, the answer the solver finds, the level asked for), a seed's puzzle the same every time and today's the same for a day, each level needing its hardest step and no harder, marks and the rules they break, hints (every step true of the answer and enough alone to solve a puzzle), a run through JSON and the database's round trip, and the page wired to the engine.

Not done: the head-to-head and a split board on `duel.js` (the table is the same one Hitori uses; the race is the cheaper of the two: everyone gets the seed's puzzle and reports how many of the answer's lines they have right), the computer rival (the solver's steps, paced like a person, as Hitori's), boards past 10×10, and Expert (a trial inside a trial: it costs seconds on a phone at 10×10, and few puzzles need it). Masyu builds on this engine.

## Shaded puzzles

Added in November 2026 (Korbi: "Slitherlink one spin good"; I could not recover which spin was proposed, so this one is my choice and his thumb sets whether it stays). The menu's Clues choice is Numbers (the game as it was) or With shading: some cells carry no number but a mark saying which side of the loop they are on, a **tinted** cell inside it, a **dotted** one outside. The loop is exactly the line between inside and outside, so a shaded cell beside a dotted one has a line between them, two of the same kind have none, and that carries through the lines and crosses you have drawn: a tinted cell with a crossed edge to its neighbour makes the neighbour tinted too. The outside of the board counts as outside, so a tinted cell on the border has a line on its outer side. A mark that clashes with what is drawn turns oxblood, with a ring round the cell.

How it works (`slitherlink-engine.js`, generalised, not forked). A cell's clue is its number (0 to 4) or code 5 (inside) or 6 (outside), "i" and "o" in a puzzle's grid string. The sides rule is one more step in the solver's local rules, run only when a puzzle has shading, so plain puzzles never touch it: the cells and the outside of the board are nodes, a line between two says they are on opposite sides and a crossed edge the same side, a tinted cell is opposite the outside and a dotted the same, and any edge between two cells whose sides are known is settled (a union-find that keeps each node's side relative to its root, in module scratch because it runs inside every trial). It is an Easy step: it is the plain consequence of the corner and loop rules, and a hint says it in words ("the shading and the lines so far put the two sides of this edge on opposite sides of the loop"), with both cells ringed. When a loop would close, it must also leave every tinted cell inside and every dotted one outside (`closes`, which floods outward from the board's edge). Uniqueness is proved as before, by the solver finishing from the clues by forced steps.

Making one. The loop is made as always; then 40% of the cells have their number swapped for the side they are on in the answer; since a shading says less of a cell than its number does, that puzzle may not be solvable at the level, so numbers are put back on the shaded cells one at a time until it is (the puzzle with every number always is); then clues are taken away in random order for as long as the level's solver can still finish, but never below an eighth of the cells shaded or dotted, or the shading would pare away and leave a plain puzzle. Measured, 12 puzzles of each size and level: every one had exactly one answer and came out at its level; the shading kept is about 3 cells of 25 at 5×5, 6 of 49 at 7×7 and 10 of 100 at 10×10, and on 10×10 fewer numbers than the plain puzzle (35, 30 and 30 numbers at Easy, Medium and Hard against the plain 42, 37 and 36, beside the marks; an Easy 7×7 has 17 numbers and 6 marks). A shaded Hard 10×10 takes about 0.7 s on average and 1.4 s at worst in Node, against 0.75 s at worst for a plain one, so the spare puzzle made in the background matters on a phone. A link to a shaded puzzle adds `&t=shade`; its bests are kept apart (`slitherlink:times` keys end `:shade`) as are its spares; today's puzzle stays plain. `tests/slitherlink-shade.mjs` checks the rule against every loop on five small boards by a plain even-odd count (180 sets of clues, most of them with shading, many with several answers: the solver counts exactly as many answers as that does), shaded puzzles across every size and level (one answer, the shading true of it, enough of it kept, on level), hints (each step true of the answer, 71 of 829 from the sides) and the rules the marks break, that plain puzzles are what they were (18 seeds, sizes and levels by hash), and the page's wiring.

**The loop kernel** (`loop-engine.js`, 31 October 2026, taken out of `slitherlink-engine.js` for Masyu; Slitherlink makes the same puzzles for the same seeds as before). What the two loop puzzles share: the board of corners and edges (`geometry`), the corner rule (`vertexRule`: two lines or none), the loop rule, the trials, the grade and the counting search (`loopSolver`, which takes a kind of puzzle's own `sweep`, what its clues settle, and `closes`, whether the lines drawn meet them all), and the loop itself (a random region's outline, `randomLoop`, `outlineMarks`). A new loop puzzle brings its clues and those two functions.

## Co-op

A paired partner can join a Slitherlink you are playing and draw on the same board (3 November 2026, `docs/coop.md`). A shared solve counts for neither of you: no best in `slitherlink:times`, no today's time, no Random round. The host's `slitherlink:run` follows the shared board and is theirs again when it ends; the guest's is never written. `slitherlink-coop.js` says how a run becomes a board and back; `tests/coop-request.mjs` plays it.
