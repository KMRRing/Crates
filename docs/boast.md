# Liar's dice
Liar's dice with Chinese rules (October 2026: the dice game of Chinese dinners and Hong Kong bars, and of a supplier meeting in China), against computer players or at a table with friends each on their own phone (README, Seats). It is 吹牛 (chuīniú, "to brag") on the mainland and 大話骰 (daai6 waa6 sik1, "big-talk dice") in Hong Kong, Macau and Guangdong. Two to six players, five dice each under a cup.

The rules are the Hong Kong bar rules as zh.wikipedia's 大話骰 sets them down, the commonest set; the mainland plays the same game with house rules of its own, the two commonest were offered in the menu until 4 November 2026 and are off now (the engine still plays them). In turn, each bids how many dice of a face lie under all the cups together ("three fives": at least three 5s on the table). The opening bid names at least as many dice as there are players (起叫). Each bid beats the last: more dice, or as many of a higher face. Ones are wild, counting as the face bid, unless the bid is plain (斋, zhāi, "vegetarian", i.e. nothing added): "four fives, plain" needs four real 5s. A bid on ones is plain by nature and the highest plain face. A plain bid may follow a wild one at the same count, any face; going back to wild after a plain bid (飞, fēi, "fly") takes two dice more, since a wild bid is about twice as easy to make true. Instead of bidding, the next player may open the last bid (开, kāi): every cup is lifted and the face counted, with the ones unless the bid was plain; as many or more and the opener loses a life, fewer and the bidder does. 劈 (pī, "chop") opens at two lives, and the bidder may answer 反劈 (fǎn pī, "chop back") for four. Whoever lost starts the next round. Everyone starts with 3, 6 or 10 lives (6 unless chosen); a game ends when someone has none left, and whoever has the most wins. (The state's `cups` counts lives lost: the name stays for saved games.) No drinking in the games (4 November 2026, Korbi: "remove the drinking references across the games (PG), just say 'loses' or 'loses a life'"): a loss is a loss or a life; `tests/no-drinking.mjs` keeps the words out. House rules (once Menu, off unless chosen; gone from the menu on 4 November 2026, so always off; a game in hand keeps its own): 豹子 (bàozi, "leopard"), five alike under one cup counting seven (six with ones among them, as Hong Kong counts 圍骰 and 純爆); 散 (sǎn, "scattered"), a cup of five different faces rolled again before anyone bids, as many mainland tables play. 劈 can be turned off.

The table talk: every bid is shown as it's called and spoken by the narrator (the phone that runs the table) in Mandarin, Cantonese or English (Menu, Table talk): 三个五 (sān gè wǔ) and 三粒五 (saam1 nap1 ng5) for three fives, 四个三斋 for four threes plain, 开, 劈, 反劈 and 飞, and the count when the cups are lifted ("开！一共五个六。Mei丢一条命。", open, five sixes in all, Mei loses a life). Mandarin counts "two" of a thing as 两 (liǎng) and the face two as 二 (èr); Cantonese counts dice with 粒 (nap1, "grain") where Mandarin uses 个 (gè). The Rules sheet has the table of them with pinyin and Jyutping (the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong's romanisation, tone by number). In English the narrator names a seat where no verb has to agree with it ("Mei loses a life.", "Round 3. First to bid: You."): playing solo, your seat's name is You.

The computer players (`boast-bot.js`) reason by probability, as Cartel's do. Each samples what could lie under the other cups, weighting each sample by how likely its owner was to bid what they bid with it (a player calls the face they hold most more often than not: a softmax over the faces in the bid's own counting, so a cup full of ones, which helps every face alike, says nothing about the face called), and from the samples the chance any bid holds. Hard looks one player ahead: for each bid it could make, in each sample the next player reckons it on their own cup and opens it when it looks unlikely to them; opened, a bid costs a life if false and the opener one if true; raised past, the round goes on. So a plain bid on a face its own cup holds, true but unlikely-looking, draws a wrong 开, and Hard bids plain where that pays (more than half its bids at a table of two, a few per cent at four). It opens the last bid when that's worth more than its best raise, chops when it's nearly sure the bid is false, and chops back when it trusts its own bid. Medium doesn't read the others' bids and decides by the chance alone, noisier; Easy is noisier still. In `tools/boast-arena.mjs`, at a table of two Hard and two Medium players over 300 games, Hard lost 22% of the rounds it sat in and Medium 28% (at four, 25% each would be even); against Easy, 21% to 29%; head to head at a table of two, Hard won three games in four against Medium. A move takes about 10 ms.

The page: the others' cups across the top (their name, their lives left as pips against the limit, their last bid, "thinking" on their turn), the bid in the middle as large as the room allows (with its words and romanisation, who bid it and the bids before), your five dice and your bid at the foot: the count with − and +, 斋, the six faces, and Bid (named as it will be called), 开 and 劈; it starts from the face your cup holds most at the least count that follows. When the cups are lifted every cup's dice show, those that count lit and the rest faded, the count against the bid, who loses, and a bar running down to the next round (five seconds). The odds line (once Menu, Show the odds; always off now) added the chance the last bid holds on your dice and the bids, as Hard reckons it. Held sideways, the table takes the left and the round's bids in full stand at the top of the right, over your dice and your bid. The dice are Chinese dice: the one's single pip large and red, the four's four red. Menu: the table talk (a language, or Off for the narrator's voice) and View; Invite players opens a party with Liar's dice chosen (README, Party). A game kept as it changes (`boast:game`), the menu's choices in `boast:opts`, the games won in `boast:record`, and the run of wins the games screen shows in `boast:streak`. `tests/boast.mjs` checks what may follow what (the opening, plain and wild, 飞, ones), the count (wild ones, plain, the leopards), a round from the first bid to the next (开, 劈 and 反劈 at one, two and four lives, the loser starting), the end, the words in three languages, the state through the database, whole games of computer players at every size with the house rules, and Hard against Easy.

The course (Learn, in the header, as Xiangqi's, Dou Dizhu's and Big Two's; October 2026): the game taught from nothing in 15 lessons in two chapters, on the course sheet Dou Dizhu's and Big Two's use (`course.js`; README, Dou Dizhu) with a stage of its own for dice (`course-dice.js`: cups lifted, cups still down, bids shown large, drawn with the game's own dice). The game: five dice under a cup (骰盅), bidding up (more dice, or as many of a higher face; the opening at least one die a player), ones wild (百搭), plain bids (斋/齋) and flying back to wild (飞/飛, two dice more), opening (开/開) and who loses a life, chopping (劈, 反劈), the house rules (豹子, 散). Playing well: what to expect under the cups (a third of the dice you can't see for a wild bid, a sixth for a plain one, and your own), how likely a bid is (the binomial odds: with fifteen dice unseen, a wild bid needing their average of five holds 6 times in 10, needing nine 1 in 30), opening the bidding on your best face near what you expect, raising or opening (open when the last bid is likelier false than your raise would be), reading the bids (a face bid twice is likely held), the plain trap (a plain bid on a face you hold, at the same count, which looks thin to a player without it), chopping when sure; then a first game against Easy. A step is words over cups, a cup to count (tap every die that counts for a bid: wrong ones are dimmed, with why; Hint marks the rest), a bid to make on a setter as the table's (fewer, more, 斋, the faces; Bid stays off until it beats the last bid, and a bid that doesn't do what's asked says why), or a choice; terms come in Mandarin with pinyin and Cantonese with Jyutping. Where you are is kept (`boast:course`). `tests/boast-course.mjs` checks every cup and bid written, that every cup to count holds as many dice that count as its words say, that every bid asked for can be made and of all the bids that could be made only the meant ones are judged right (exactly one where it asks for the fewest dice), and every figure quoted, expectations and chances alike, against the binomial odds from the cup shown. The Rules sheet opens from the course's map and the menu.
