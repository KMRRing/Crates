# Chess
Chess against Caissa (MIT, `vendor/caissa/NOTE.txt`) at one of eight strengths, two players on one phone, or a match with your partner. The same engine is the coach (hints, a word after a mistake) and reviews the finished game. The rules are chess.js's (`vendor/chess.js`); `gambit-rules.js` keeps a game as plain JSON, changed only by `act()`. The puzzle rush (Menu, Puzzle rush) is a page of its own, `rush.html`, under Gambit's header, on 20,000 Lichess puzzles (CC0) in `puzzles/`.

## Rulings
- 2 November 2026 (Korbi, licences): prefer a permissive engine, and in the worst case no engine at all, only puzzles. Stockfish (GPL v3) is gone from Gambit for that reason; `tests/gambit-licences.mjs` fails if a GPL engine file comes back.
- 3 November 2026 (Korbi, 3D): "add a 3d mode (both for puzzle and real)… a highly optimized interface". Menu, View: Board (the default) or 3D, for the game and the rush alike; the flat board stays.

## Traps
- **Every search needs a limit.** The engine is single-threaded (GitHub Pages can't send the headers `SharedArrayBuffer` needs) and a `go` runs to its end inside the worker's call, so `go infinite` or a bare `go` wedges it. The levels, the hint and the review all search a set number of positions, never a time, so a slow phone plays as strongly as a fast one.
- **Caissa has none of Stockfish's options.** `Skill Level`, `UCI_LimitStrength` and `UCI_Elo` are dropped by `gambit-caissa.js`; a level's strength is its node count and, for the weaker ones, `choose()` drawing among the MultiPV lines at a temper. A search that stops on its node limit stops part-way through an iteration, so its last lines are of two depths and may repeat a move (about 150 positions per line at depth 1, so a level that draws among 12 moves needs about 2,000 nodes). For up to 4 lines `gambit-caissa.js` gives whole depths only (the hint and the coach's looks); the weak levels' draw among more was set with the mix, so leave it. `bestmove` can be a move none of the last lines starts with; `levelMove` uses `best` only when no draw is made.
- **Caissa's table stays within a game.** `gambit-caissa.js` clears it for any search that isn't a later position of the last search's game: cleared before every move, Caissa lost to Stockfish 19 lite at twice its positions; kept, it came level at equal ones. A puzzle or a review position is cleared, so it answers the same whatever came before.
- **The levels were set by games, not by arithmetic**, against the Stockfish levels they replace (the records are `tools/engine-levels-gambit-caissa.jsonl`; the old engine is in the history, before the commit that removed it). Caissa needs about 1.9 times Stockfish's positions for the same strength, so Full strength searches 1.5 million (about 6 s on a desktop core; 800,000 would be about 100 Elo weaker). MultiPV shares the positions among its lines: 8 lines at 2,500 positions play no better than about 1,300 at any temper, so Club and up draw among 6 at 3,000. A node limit is soft (the search finishes its iteration), so 800 and 1,500 positions are two steps about 500 Elo apart, not a slope.
- **The 76 MB network is held once**: the worker unpacks `caissa.pnn.z` straight into the engine's own memory (`net_alloc`). Changing the network or the build is a deliberate act: re-measure the levels (`vendor/caissa/NOTE.txt` has the hashes the test checks).
- **WebAssembly SIMD** needs Safari / iOS 16.4; the page's engine can't start on older ones ("The computer player couldn't start").
- A Lichess puzzle's first move is the opponent's; the solution starts at the second.
- The rush's wanted-move square was once classed `hint`, which picked up the suite's clue-button style and grew far past the board; it is `want`.
- The rush keeps its own identity (keys `rush:`, what a partner sees, the battle's requests): they go by a page's name, so it isn't a part of `gambit.html`.
- **3D (`gambit-3d.js`, geometry in `gambit-3d-geo.js`) is a second drawing of the same board**, never a second board: `chess-board.js` keeps the rules of touch and the marks, and hands the scene a model to draw. The choice is `suite:gambit-view` (this device only; a watching frame draws flat and never writes it). The vendored three.js has no `PerspectiveCamera` or `LatheGeometry`, so the camera is an orthographic one given a perspective matrix and the men are lathed by hand; `tests/gambit-3d.mjs` fails on a three.js name the bundle lacks.
- **Marks (squares, dots, arrows, shadows) must face up**: their material is one-sided, and a disc written the other way round simply isn't drawn (`createMarks.tri` turns each one up).
- **A finger picks a man by a stick from his foot to his head, not by his mesh**; a man in front hides the one behind, so a press on several takes the one that can move (`accept`), and a second tap on a picked man means the square behind it (it hides it). Promotion's four choices stand in a row across the middle of the board, since a column hides the far ones.
