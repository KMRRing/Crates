# Chess
Chess against Arasan (MIT, with its MIT network; `vendor/arasan/NOTE.txt`) at one of eight strengths, two players on one phone, or a match with your partner. The same engine is the coach (hints, a word after a mistake) and reviews the finished game. The rules are chess.js's (`vendor/chess.js`); `gambit-rules.js` keeps a game as plain JSON, changed only by `act()`. The puzzle rush (Menu, Puzzle rush) is a page of its own, `rush.html`, under Chess's header, on 20,000 Lichess puzzles (CC0) in `puzzles/`.

## Traps
- **Every search needs a limit.** The engine is single-threaded (GitHub Pages can't send the headers `SharedArrayBuffer` needs) and a `go` runs to its end inside the worker's call, so `go infinite` or a bare `go` wedges it. The levels, the hint and the review all search a set number of positions, never a time, so a slow phone plays as strongly as a fast one.
- **Arasan has none of Stockfish's options.** `Skill Level`, `UCI_LimitStrength` and `UCI_Elo` are dropped by `gambit-arasan.js`; a level's strength is its node count and, for the weaker ones, `choose()` drawing among the MultiPV lines at a temper. Arasan prints a depth's lines together, but a search that stops on its node limit prints the ones it got to and leaves the other ranks at the depth before: the wrapper holds a depth's lines until it is whole and drops a cut-short one, so the lines `choose()` sees are of one depth. `bestmove` can be a move none of the last lines starts with; `levelMove` uses `best` only when no draw is made. MultiPV costs Arasan more than it costs Caissa (six lines at 12,000 positions reached depth 2 in a quiet position), which is why the weak levels keep their small node counts.
- **Its centipawns are larger than Caissa's** (the start 92 against 61, a queen up 1,700 against 668), so `gambit-arasan.js` says every score times `CP_SCALE` (0.4, which fits at both ends) because the coach's words, `choose()`'s clip at ten pawns and the levels' tempers all assume the smaller scale (the tempers were re-fitted by games after the scaling, not multiplied: the clip made the same numbers play 200 Elo stronger). Mate scores are not scaled.
- **Arasan's table stays within a game.** `gambit-arasan.js` clears it for any search that isn't a later position of the last search's game: kept, it scored 15.5 of 24 against cleared before every move (10,000 positions; that was measured before the network fix below, on the same engine both sides). A puzzle or a review position is cleared, so it answers the same whatever came before.
- **The network's first layer is summed in plain integers** (the patch, `sparselinear.h`): its SSE and AVX2 code gave other numbers than the generic layer's on the same input and weights, and played like an engine 400 Elo down (puzzles at depth 8: 58 of 80, against 75 and, at 20,000 positions, 80 of 80). Don't put the SIMD version back unless the layer's trace, built with `-DNNUE_TRACE`, matches the generic build's on a position; the rest of the SIMD (accumulator, pairwise, the small layers) does match.
- **The levels were set by games, not by arithmetic**, against Caissa's, which were set against the Stockfish levels before them (`tools/engine-levels-gambit-arasan.jsonl`, fitted with the older records: the command is in `tools/engine-levels.mjs`'s header). The six weak levels draw among the engine's lines and came out level with Caissa's at the same positions, so they kept their counts; the single-line ones did not (Arasan needs about twice Caissa's positions at the bottom and is far stronger per position higher up), hence Master at 3,000 and Full strength at 100,000 (about 0.8 s on a desktop core, which sets the hint's 100,000 and 50,000 and the review's 20,000). A node limit is soft: the search finishes its iteration.
- **The 28 MB network is held once**: the worker unpacks `arasan.nnue.z` straight into the engine's own memory (`vendor/arasan/NOTE.txt` has the hashes the test checks). The module asks for 128 MB to start and may grow to 1 GB, the hash is 16 MB. Changing the network or the build is a deliberate act: re-measure the levels.
- **WebAssembly SIMD** needs Safari / iOS 16.4; the page's engine can't start on older ones ("The computer player couldn't start").
- A Lichess puzzle's first move is the opponent's; the solution starts at the second.
- The rush's wanted-move square was once classed `hint`, which picked up the suite's clue-button style and grew far past the board; it is `want`.
- The rush keeps its own identity (keys `rush:`, what a partner sees, the battle's requests): they go by a page's name, so it isn't a part of `gambit.html`.
- **3D (`gambit-3d.js`, geometry in `gambit-3d-geo.js`) is a second drawing of the same board**, never a second board: `chess-board.js` keeps the rules of touch and the marks, and hands the scene a model to draw. The choice is `suite:gambit-view` (this device only; a watching frame draws flat and never writes it). The vendored three.js has no `PerspectiveCamera` or `LatheGeometry`, so the camera is an orthographic one given a perspective matrix and the men are lathed by hand; `tests/gambit-3d.mjs` fails on a three.js name the bundle lacks.
- **Marks (squares, dots, arrows, shadows) must face up**: their material is one-sided, and a disc written the other way round simply isn't drawn (`createMarks.tri` turns each one up).
- **A finger picks a man by a stick from his foot to his head, not by his mesh**; a man in front hides the one behind, so a press on several takes the one that can move (`accept`), and a second tap on a picked man means the square behind it (it hides it). Promotion's four choices stand in a row across the middle of the board, since a column hides the far ones.
