# Atoll

Shade the sea (31 October 2026, Korbi's list of genres to add). Each number is an island of exactly that many cells, joined edge to edge, with that one number in it; every other cell is sea, which is one connected piece and holds no 2×2 block. Two islands never touch edge to edge. Every puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning, never by a guess.

Playing (`nurikabe.js`). A tap on a cell shades it as sea, again dots it as island (a note for yourself: only the sea counts), again clears it; a right click dots at once. Drag and the cells the finger crosses are painted with the mark a tap on the first one would have made, a quick drag stepped cell by cell so it leaves no gaps; a number can't be marked. A stroke is one step to undo, so is a hint's and so is Clear the board (an undo entry is `[cell, from, to, stroke]`, the stroke a time, as Pearl's). The puzzle is solved the moment exactly its sea is shaded. A rule broken shows at once, from the rules alone and never the answer (a 2×2 pool of sea, sea cut off from the rest by islands, an island with two numbers or too many cells, all in oxblood), a number turns green once its island is whole, and the solved sea turns gilt from the top left.

Sizes 6×6, 8×8 and 10×10 (the menu); three levels (the header, mirrored in the menu). Nothing comes from the knowledge base. Today's puzzle is an 8×8 at Medium. Bests, the daily, spares made in the background, a link with the seed, the run in `nurikabe:run`, Random's round and the clock work as Pearl's do (`nurikabe:times`, `nurikabe:today`, `nurikabe:prefs`, `nurikabe:spares`).

The rules a solver follows (`nurikabe-engine.js`). Easy: an island with all its cells is walled in by sea; a cell touching two islands that would join into one with two numbers, or one too big for its number, is sea; an island short of its number with one way out takes it; three sea cells in a 2×2 leave the fourth island; the numbers add up to a count of island cells, so once that many are found the rest is sea (and once only that many cells are left, they are all island); a cell no island can reach with the cells it has left (counted round other islands' cells and round cells that would touch another number) is sea, and an island piece without a number that none can reach is a contradiction. Medium: the sea kept in one piece as well, a cell that is the only link between parts of the sea being sea (found with the lowpoints of a depth-first walk, one pass). Hard: a supposition too, a cell supposed sea or island with the Medium rules followed until they break, which settles it the other way. The hint says which, simplest first. A solver that only reasons can't count a wrong answer, so a puzzle it finishes has no second one: `tests/nurikabe.mjs` checks the solver's count against a plain search of every filling of 4×4 and 5×5 boards.

Making a puzzle (`nurikabe-engine.js`, off the page in `nurikabe-worker.js`). The placeholder's warning was right that this is the risk: a board dealt at random, with a number in a random cell of each island, was finished by reasoning in about one try in ten at 6×6 and fewer at 10×10, and with the plain rules alone in none. So the board is dealt as an answer (islands grown a cell at a time, never beside another and never cutting the sea in two, then cells added wherever a 2×2 of sea is left), and then searched: a number moved inside its island, an island given another cell, a new island of one cell, one cell given back to the sea, each kept when the solver of the level has no more cells left undecided than before (or, now and then and less as it goes on, a few more: plain arithmetic, no `Math.exp`, so every device deals the same board for a seed). Every change keeps the board an answer, so it never has to be solved to be known. When the level's reasoning finishes the board, whole islands and then single cells are given back to the sea for as long as it still does, so a puzzle has about the fewest numbers its level allows; a Hard board is then nudged, its numbers moved about in their islands, until Medium's rules can no longer finish it but a supposition can. The level a puzzle came to is what it needed, and when the tries (24, 12, 8 boards for Easy, Medium, Hard, each given up early if it is far from done two fifths of the way) run out, the nearest level's puzzle is dealt and the line says so; if none comes at any level, the seed is moved on and the levels tried again, so one is always dealt.

What it costs and where it falls short. Islands average two to three cells, and a third to a half of them are single cells: the 2×2 rule needs an island in every 2×2 of the board, and a minimal puzzle keeps the ones that stop a pool. Bigger islands made Easy and Medium boards much rarer (the search runs out of ways to tell the solver more). A Hard 10×10 takes a second or two on a computer, several on a phone, which is why the page keeps a spare made in the background; and a request for Medium or Hard comes back as the level below about one time in five at 8×8 and one in three at 10×10 (Easy at 10×10 about one in four comes back as Medium).

Score and bests as Pearl's. Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height. The colour is umber (`--nk-in`), the sea a deep slate (a lifted steel by night), the islands paper.

`tests/nurikabe.mjs` (about three seconds) checks the board, the solver against a plain search of every filling for 99 sets of numbers over two small boards (58 with several answers, so a solver that guessed would show), 7 puzzles across the sizes and levels (one answer each, found by a search that guesses, the answer the rules allow and the solver finds, the level asked for), a seed's puzzle the same every time and today's the same for a day, each level solving a puzzle and the one below not, marks and the rules they break, hints (every step true of the answer and enough alone to solve a puzzle), a run through JSON and the database's round trip, and the page wired to the engine.

Not done: the head-to-head, a computer rival, boards past 10×10, bigger islands, and Expert.

Co-op (3 November 2026, `docs/coop.md`, `nurikabe-coop.js`): a paired partner joins and shades the same sea. The dots are shared too (one board; each is a note both of you see). A shared solve earns no best, today's time or Random round, and the host's `nurikabe:run` follows the shared board. Undoing a stroke puts back each cell only if it is still as you left it.
