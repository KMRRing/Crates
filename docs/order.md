# Order

Put things in order. A card is dealt and you tap the gap where it goes in your line, which starts with one card face up and grows a card at a time; a wrong place costs one of three lives and the card goes where it belongs, so the line is always right, values showing. Dates deal 16 of Quote's 434 dated questions (a painting by its name, with its picture on the card, fetched once per card, and a small one beside it in the line; the name is the knowledge base's, which the build gives the question, since a Wikipedia page's title can carry the year: Number 1, 1950 (Lavender Mist)); Quantities deal one family a run, Quote's questions of one dimension (19 families: lengths, areas, numbers of people, masses, volumes, sums in dollars, pressures, then %, kg/m³, °C, cetane, kWh and more), so the comparison means something: a hectare and a square kilometre are both areas, so the Sahara, Lake Geneva and a tennis court share a line, each card in its own unit ("Height of Everest (in metres)" beside "Length of the Suez Canal (in km)") and the line ordered by what each is in the dimension's base (`kb/units.js`; a unit nothing else shares is a family of its own, as every unit once was). A saved best under a single unit (`q:metres`) still counts towards its family's. Two quantities within one per cent of each other fit either side of each other (Egypt's 116.5 million and the Philippines' 115.8: the sources round differently); years are exact. Quote's Scale questions, softball Fermi questions for calibration, give Quantities families in hectares, litres, kWh, MW, kg, km and euros. Random or today's, a daily for each mode by the suite's day (the UTC date, the same deck for everyone); bests by mode. After a finished run, or from its tile, today's comes first while it's still to play. The cards and the rules are `order-engine.js`'s, pure; `tests/order.mjs` checks the families and plays runs through. Stories (October 2026) deal one story's events instead of numbers, and what's ordered is the story itself: Heracles' twelve labours as Apollodorus gives them, Odysseus's voyage home, the Trojan War, Ragnarök, the deeds of Perseus, Jason and the Argonauts, Rome's seven kings (kb/items/sequences.js, checked by `tests/stories.mjs`). A placed card shows its place, 1st, 5th, and the end of the run says which source the order follows. Picture cards say what happened to the picture, from the question: a painting was painted, a building completed.

Topics (set in Settings, 2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed"; until then Order had a control of its own, each of Quote's categories out, in or more, with the presets Balanced, Trader and Culture night, and a mix kept under `order:topics`, which is no longer read): Dates and Quantities deal from the subjects that are on (README, The knowledge base), as Quote's categories at their usual weight: Dates holds Trade, Wine, Art, People and Markets, Quantities all nine, and each category is in when its subject is (Quote's mapping: Trade to Trade & shipping, Cities to Cities, Wine to Food & drink, Art to Art & architecture, People to People, Geography to Geography, Scale to Science, Markets to Finance, Refining to Energy). The menu says where the topics stand and links to the control, shown whether today's run or a random one is picked (22 October 2026: they showed only once Random was, and each game opens on today's, so Korbi never found them); today's run deals every topic. Dates draws its sixteen cards by their topic's weight; Quantities draws its family by the mean weight of its cards, among the families with six cards or more in the topics in, and deals only those. All subjects on deals exactly as Order always did. A mix with fewer than eight dated cards in, or no family, or none of the mode's topics on, deals from every topic of the mode and says so. A run counts for your best on today's or a preset of the subjects (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade, All off), not on a mix of your own, higher or lower included. History's stories have no topics. At a table, a new game comes from the topics of whoever deals it. Read at deal time: a change in Settings reaches the next run, and a run already dealt keeps what it was dealt.

No repeats (31 October 2026, Korbi: "no-repeat dealer: good"): a run of your own deals the cards you haven't seen before any you have, so the same sixteen dates don't come round while hundreds wait. The page keeps the cards you've played in `order:seen` (so it follows you between your devices like the rest of your saved state) and hands them to `dealRun` as `seen`; a card is noted as you place it, and the one face up at the start. Dates draw their sixteen from the unseen ones by their topics' weights as before; quantities choose a family by the share of its cards you haven't seen (times its topics' weight) and put its unseen cards first. When too few are left (fewer than a run's sixteen in the dates, or in the family) the deal takes all that are left, reaches back for the rest and says `recycled`, and the page then forgets that pool's cards, so a card comes round again only after the rest have, not on a clock. A daily, a table's deal and the stories pass nothing: the seed alone decides them, so everyone has the same cards, and a story is dealt whole in any case. `tests/order.mjs` checks that an empty memory is the old deal exactly, that no date comes twice until the pool runs short, and that a table's deal never reads one phone's memory.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the card in hand stands on the left, its picture large (asked of Wikipedia at 960 pixels there, rather than a phone's 500), and your line beside it, a column you scroll down.

Together (October 2026): one run for the table, played as a team, from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six, each on their own phone (`together.js`). Every phone shows the same card in hand and the same line, and whoever taps a gap first places it, for everyone: the transaction places it only if that card is still the one in hand on the line that phone showed, so a tap made a moment after someone else's does nothing and says so ("Too late: Sarah placed it"). The three lives are the team's, and a miss is told on every phone ("Tom missed: 1886 goes last"). Each card in the line has a dot in the colour of whoever placed it (README, Rooms; the first, dealt face up, none), and a row under the strip shows who's at the table in those colours (initials for five or six), anyone away greyed. Nobody waits for anybody, so a phone that sleeps or leaves holds nothing up. The menu's Together part deals a new game for the table in Dates, Quantities or History (asked first while one is under way), says who's there, and has Back to solo. The end, on every phone: the count placed together and each player's right and wrong, by seat, not ranked, with Another game and Back to solo. The pair's record gets a team result, the count placed (a win with the whole deck placed); a Party's game isn't the pair's. Your solo run, bests and today's stay as they were, and a table in the address is joined under the rete, with no solo run dealt beneath it. It replaced, the same day, a head-to-head on `duel.js`, everyone building their own line from the same deck: Korbi wanted it friendly, anyone placing. `tests/order.mjs` plays a table of three through the database's own round trip (a stale tap turned down, the shared lives, the tallies, the record, a new game); three phones in one browser played it end to end, and a pair from the partner sheet's address.

Higher or lower (2 November 2026, Korbi: "order: add the higher or lower mode. in settings, user can switch. when enabled, user just choses which is the higher of two, then the old choice disappears and a new choice replaces it. until failure"): a second way to play Dates and Quantities, the Play setting in the menu's Settings (Line, or Higher or lower), for one player. Two cards stand side by side with their names, a painting's or building's picture on its card as in the line; the left card's value shows, the right one's is a dashed "?". Tap the one you think is higher (later, for years). Both values then show for a moment, the higher marked ▲ in verdigris and a wrong tap ✕ in oxblood (the marks and the words "Right" and "Not quite" say it, not colour alone), and if you were right the left card goes, the card that was on the right takes its place with its value showing, and a new one is dealt beside it. The first wrong tap ends the run: one life, and the score is the taps that were right. A tap while both values show moves on at once, unless it comes within a third of a second of the answer, which is a double tap. The cards that have gone are listed under the pair (sideways, in the column beside it), latest first, values showing: what the run has taught you. The end of a run says "N right in a row" and shows the pair that ended it, the tapped card marked, with Today's run (while it's still to play) and Another run.

The card beside the other takes its place whichever of the two was higher, so the run is a chain of fresh pairs. Korbi's words could also be read as the higher card staying and the lower being replaced; that makes the card in hand, within a few rounds, the highest seen so far, and the run a string of taps on it (always tapping it survives n taps with a chance of 1 in n + 1, so a score would be luck and a memory of which card is the largest of all). The chain is also what his earlier note for this mode described ("a right guess puts it face up and it becomes the one to beat"). Why it plays differently from the line: it asks less of each card, one comparison against a value in front of you, with none of the narrowing gaps of a growing line, and more of the run, one life where the line gives three; a player who is right nine times in ten runs nine cards on average, one right nineteen in twenty, nineteen.

The whole run is dealt at the start, from the seed, as one chain (`dealHigherLower` in `order-engine.js`; `answerHigherLower` and `advanceHigherLower` are the pure steps, a tap and the move on after a right one), so it is the same for everyone with the same seed, can be saved as it stands, and never needs a card to be found while you wait. Dates chain the whole pool of dated cards; Quantities one family, as the line's do, so the comparison means something, from the seven of the nineteen with 15 cards or more (sums in dollars, lengths, masses, percentages, volumes, numbers of people, areas; a smaller family is over before it is a run), drawn by the square root of the weight of its cards you haven't seen (every card equally likely would give the biggest three families three runs in four). No card comes twice in a run, and no pair is too close to call: not the same year, and not quantities within one per cent (the rule the line gives its ties, in whatever unit each card is in, so 193 km and 400 metres compare by size); a card too close to the one before it waits for the next turn. The cards are drawn by your mix of topics, as the line's are (Balanced, Trader and Culture night count for your best, your own mix doesn't), and a mix that leaves too few cards deals from every topic and says so. History's stories have no value to compare, so the menu offers this mode Dates and Quantities only.

What it keeps, all under `order:` so it follows you between devices and a partner's watching frame draws the same screen: `order:hl`, the run as it stands, the moment both values show included (a reload while they showed goes on to the next pair); `order:hlbest`, bests by `dates` and `q:<family>`, apart from the line's `order:best` so the games screen's tile, which shows the line's best, doesn't mix the two; `order:play`, which way you play. Today's run is dealt from the day's seed, its own, the same for everyone, and counted the moment the run ends: `order:daily`, as `YYYYMMDD/hl-dates` or `YYYYMMDD/hl-quantities`, so it counts as today's run for the games screen (the tile's today is the best of the day's runs, in whatever mode). Random runs use `order:seen`, the line's memory of the cards dealt (a card is noted when its value shows; a daily passes none): unseen cards come first, and when fewer than 20 are left the deal reaches back, says `recycled`, and the page lets that pool go. A run ending is a Random-mode round (`roundOver`), told 0.9 seconds after the tap; the moment both values show stands still while the menu is open (`onPause`). Alone only: a table, a duo match or a Party plays the line (the Play switch isn't in a table's menu, and the menu says so while Higher or lower is on). `tests/order.mjs` checks the chain over many seeds (no card twice, no pair too close, one family), that a seed deals the same run, the tap and the move on, always-right clearing the pool and a wrong tap ending the run, the topics and the memory, and the page's keys.
