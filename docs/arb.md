# Arb

On the games screen with every other game, in its one list that scrolls (it once sat behind a More button, with Survey, Blend, Refinery, Brut, the Lexicon and Ledger, while the main screen kept a fixed number of games).

From one thing to another through the knowledge base's links, as cheaply as you can. Every step costs 1 + log2 of the links of the thing you step onto: an obscure thing costs 1, the United States about 9, so the cheap route runs through what you specifically know rather than the hubs. Par is the cheapest route there is (Dijkstra over the 4,329 linked things); matching it scores 100, more spent scores in proportion. Each choice shows its cost and kind, every hop the reason the two are linked, and the end the cheapest route. Dead ends aren't offered: a link appears only if it leads somewhere new (a thing linked only to where you stand, or only back onto your route, would force a step back), the goal always does. Routes run between things with 2 to 25 links, three to five hops apart and of different kinds; five a run, random or today's. The graph and the routes are `arb-engine.js`'s, pure; `tests/arb.mjs` checks them.

**Help** (1 November 2026, Korbi: "the user should also be able to hint and reveal the next best word to take (or go back if there's true dead end and show what should have been)"). Three things, two of them paid in the steps' own currency, which comes off the score as part of what the route cost (the header says "spent 8.5 (1.5 for help)"):
- **"?" on any thing**: both ends and every link on offer. Its description a clue at a time, as in Nexus (README, Nexus, "Asking what a thing is"), and it goes to the pile as not known. Free in points; an end has its hints, a link has none, and no line names the things of the cheapest route between the ends.
- **Hint**, half a step each: three, about the best next word from where you stand (the way on that costs least without crossing your own route): what kind of thing it is; one more fact about it, or its cost when it has none; its first letter and how many words. A step either way starts the hints again. In a dead end the one hint says so and how many steps back.
- **Show**, a whole step: marks the best next word in the list and scrolls to it (looking again from the same place is free). In a true dead end, where every way on to the goal runs back into your own route, it takes you back to the last place a way on starts, for the same price, and says which word to take instead. A route that can go on is never told to back out (`bestOn` and `backOut` in `arb-engine.js`).

Topics (set once for every game, 2 November 2026): a random run's routes come from the topics that are on in Settings, the one set every knowledge game shares (README, The knowledge base, "Topics, set once for every game"), not from a control of Arb's own. The menu shows where they stand ("Topics: Culture, 10 of 23 on") with a Change topics link that opens the same sheet over the game; a change reaches the next run, since the topics are read when a run is dealt, and one made with today's picked turns the next run to a random one. Arb's own topics are still Crates' country and commodity ones, and each belongs to a subject (`TOPIC_OF.kb` in `topics.js`: Production and Trade & logistics to Trade & shipping, Markets to Finance, Uses and Specs & science to Science & tech): `topicsOf` makes the mix, a topic in when its subject is on and out when it isn't. There is no more or less any longer (Korbi: on or off is all that's needed), so Balanced, Trader and Culture night are gone from the menu; all on is what Balanced dealt, seed for seed, and the engine still draws by a weight of 0, 1 or 2, so a link carrying an older mix still deals. A thing is about the topics of its links: a clue link's aspects; a typed link's (a painting's painter, museum, movement and style, a building's architect, a book's author and lines, a style's successor: Culture & arts; standing in a place: Geography); a thing linked by none of those, its kind's (a person People, a company Companies & brands, a god or a hero Culture & arts). A route's two ends are drawn by their likeliest topic's weight, so a thing only of topics out never ends a route; the route between them runs through whatever links there are. Today's run is always every topic, and says so in the menu. A random run counts for your best when the topics are a preset (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade or All off); a mix of your own doesn't, and the menu says so. Nothing on deals from every topic, as a mix that leaves no route always did, and the line says so. A match carries the dealer's mix, so every phone deals the same routes. The old `arb:topics` key is no longer read or written, and is left alone.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), your route stands on the left with room to show all of it, and the links to choose from (or how it went) beside it.

Together (October 2026): the same five routes for everyone at the table, each finding their own way on their own phone (README, Head to head), from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six. The others' scores and how many routes they've done show in the strip. The highest total wins, then the quicker; the match counts the moment your last route is decided. Your solo run, your best and today's stay as they were.
