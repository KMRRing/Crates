# Manifest
A stack of cargo (crates, barrels, timber, ingots, chests, sacks: `manifest-rules.js` says what weighs what and what may stand on what) comes down onto a quay in a Hanseatic harbour, holds, and is shipped; then you're asked about it: counts, the cargo at a spot, weights and worths, what a column bears, which of four stacks is yours, turned. Every fourth round of Random the stack comes back changed and you tap what changed. Three modes: Random (twelve rounds or three lives, coins of the League), Levels (32 in 8 chapters, stars: `manifest-levels.js`) and Today's manifest (twelve rounds from the day's seed); alone or at a table of two to six (Random's rounds). `manifest-engine.js` makes the rounds from a recipe, with the prices and table rules (pure); `manifest-shapes.js` is the cargo as solids; `manifest-art.js` draws.

## Traps
- **The stacking order is the weights.** `CARRIES` is made from `w` in `manifest-rules.js`: a piece may stand on one that weighs at least as much (a chest carries nothing, a sack only sacks), so changing a weight changes what may stand on what, and the key printed on a question is the same table (`legendRows`; `tests/manifest-observer.js` reaches the answer from the key alone). A hidden piece in a `column` question is named only where the rules leave one way (`deduceColumn` tries every way, among the round's cargo), which mostly means between two of a kind.
- **The turned question's picture is plain boxes, so what shows of its marked piece is counted on boxes** (`clearSet(cells, b, true)`, a sixth of a piece and not `visibleSet`'s hair of it), and its outline is drawn over the stack, through what stands in front. Every question that points at a piece in a picture (the turned one, the change rounds, the which-stack's changed pieces) takes `clearSet`.
- **A piece stands inside the box of its cell, and what you could see through a stack is what the model shows, not the cell.** `visibleSet` fills each piece's outline (`outlineOf`: the hull of its mesh seen from the camera) and needs `MIN_SEEN` pixels of it, so a barrel's empty corner is not the barrel. Change a mesh and the rounds change with it. A piece that is replaced, swapped, or put into a decoy stack must be seen again in the stack that comes back (a sack in the place of a crate can hide itself): `makeChange` and the `which` decoys check it.
- **Two drawings of one picture.** 3D (`manifest-3d.js`, `manifest-glsl.js`, `manifest-pictures.js`; three.js, one WebGL context for the page, the shapes from `manifest-shapes.js`, the packing worked out in the shader) and a simpler flat SVG one per type in `manifest-art.js`, which a device without WebGL2 plays from the start. The page waits for the 3D drawing before it draws anything (`ready`), so a visit is never flat and then 3D. `manifest-model.js` is the geometry both share; `tests/manifest.mjs` checks the camera against the engine's `isoPoint`, so a change there can't move a piece. The mesh and the shader share their numbers (`LOGS`, `INGOT_SHRINK`): never copy one into the other.
- **Timber's axis is a function of the tier**, `axisOf(z, turns)`: x on an even tier, y on an odd one, swapped by a quarter turn. The rule, the 3D yaw (`yawOf`), the flat drawing and the engine's outline all use it; that is why timber on timber always crosses and why a turned stack needs no state.
- **A stack under tarpaulin (the turned spot's grey stack) is plain boxes** in grey with ropes: the cargo's own shapes would say what is under it. A marked piece is outlined by an enlarged back-face copy of its own shape, so the outline vanishes where another piece stands in front of it.
- **No turning animation, on purpose.** Turning the stack in front of the player shows what the all-at-once view hides and does the mental turn the turned question asks. The engine's turn is already a layout, so a yaw 0 to 90 degrees could end on it if that's ever wanted.
- **The types must stay apart by outline first, colour second.** `tests/manifest-shapes.mjs` fails if two silhouettes overlap more than .90; `tests/manifest.mjs` measures CIEDE2000 (normal, protan, deutan, tritan) under both drawings' shading and in shadow, and fails if two types with close outlines are less than 9 apart for any eye. The questions say "cargo", never "colour".
- **A level's wanted question is dealt again from the same seed's stream** (up to 40 tries, then the best stack): small stacks may not hold one (a column question needs hidden pieces), so a chapter that wants a kind gets the room for it (`z` in `manifest-levels.js`).
- **Saved keys:** `manifest:run3` (the run in hand, any mode), `manifest:camp` (stars, the level in hand, how often each was played), `manifest:track` (last mode), and `manifest:best` (Random's or Today's), `manifest:daily`, `manifest:record` as before; Levels write no best (their scores aren't comparable).
- **The hold stands still with the menu, the rules or the games screen open** (the drop, the hold's clock and its bar); a table's clock never stops.
- **The questions come least-revealing first** (counts, cargo, the turned spot, which-stack last: its four stacks show nearly everything), and nothing is judged until the round's review, so no later option or earlier verdict helps. Number answers are five evenly spaced values with the truth at an evenly drawn place, so the middle is no safer a guess. Prices grow 12% a round and follow your record per kind (0.6 to 1.4 of the price, marked ↓ ↑). Every question is deducible from what was shown and the rules (`tests/manifest-observer.js` proves it for every round the tests deal); none is a guess.
- **A showing starts again from the beginning** when a saved run resumes or the page is hidden.
- **The page never scrolls.** The quay shows for the drop, a change round (the stack is the answer: tap it) and the review; while answering an ordinary round the questions have the room. A question about a place carries its own picture.

## Together (October 2026)
- A table's round is the room's moment (on the database's clock): the tally, the drop and the hold are worked from it on every phone, and a phone that comes in late draws the pieces already down landed. Keys are `s<slot>` and `q<i>`; the rules are pure in the engine.
- Against: everyone sees the whole stack, answers their own questions; Ship it ends only your hold, for your own bonus; a player out of lives watches. Together: one run, one score, three team lives; the first answer counts.
- **Together splits the stack in whole columns of the floor, never by tier**: the bottom tier has every column and the top a third of them, so one player would get three times another's, and a tier above the floor would hang in the air. A part stands on the floor as it was stacked and reads the same in every view, the parts go round a step each round, and "you could see" means any of you. A change round shows each their part and brings the whole stack back to everyone.
- Prices are plain at a table (your record moves your own prices only). Your solo run, bests and today's are never touched.
