# Manifest
A stack of the League's packages (columns of varying height) comes down onto a quay in a Hanseatic harbour, holds, and is shipped; then you're asked about it: counts, the ware at a spot, a spot seen from the side, which of four stacks is yours, turned. Every fourth round the stack comes back changed and you tap what changed. Twelve rounds or three lives (coins of the League), alone or at a table of two to six. `manifest-engine.js` makes the rounds, prices and table rules (pure); `manifest-art.js` draws.

## Rulings
- 2 November 2026, Korbi: a re-skin for Hanseatic trade; "the rules, the engine, the prices, the change rounds and what is asked are as they were, only the skin, the wording and the drawing changed". Words: package (a bale, sack, chest, bundle or cask by its ware), ware (cloth, salt, amber, iron, herring; was colour), the tally (was the order book), "Land the cargo". `colour` became `ware` in the engine and nowhere else: every id and saved key (`manifest:run2`…) is as it was, and a saved run holds a ware's number.
- 3 November 2026, Korbi: the packages "nice, 3D, animated and beautiful"; a turning stack maybe later, packages may change if they don't read as a package.

## Traps
- **A package is the box of its cell and no smaller; its packing is drawn on the faces and never changes a silhouette.** What shows through the stack is what the engine says (`visibleSet`). The engine finds it on a raster of 14 px per cell, so a sliver thinner than that counts as hidden but still shows in the picture (in both drawings): not a bug in the picture.
- **Two drawings of one picture.** 3D (`manifest-3d.js`, `manifest-glsl.js`, `manifest-pictures.js`; three.js, one WebGL context for the page, packing worked out in the shader) and the flat SVG one in `manifest-art.js`, which a device without WebGL2 plays from the start. The page waits for the 3D drawing before it draws anything (`ready`), so a visit is never flat and then 3D. `manifest-model.js` is the geometry both share; `tests/manifest.mjs` checks the camera against the engine's `isoPoint`, so a change there can't move a package.
- **No turning animation, on purpose.** Turning the stack in front of the player shows what the all-at-once view hides and does the mental turn the turned question asks. The engine's turn is already a layout (the packages lie the other way), so a yaw 0 to 90 degrees could end on it if that's ever wanted.
- **A stack under tarpaulin (the turned spot's grey stack) is grey with ropes and says nothing of what's under it.**
- **The wares must stay apart for colour-blind eyes.** The first version's yellow and orange sat about 1 ΔE apart for a green-blind eye. A ware differs in hue, in lightness (five steps, near-black iron to pale salt) and in packing, and an answer is a small package with the ware's name, never a swatch. `tests/manifest.mjs` measures CIEDE2000 (normal, protan, deutan, tritan) under both drawings' face shading and in shadow, and fails if any pair is nearer than the first version's colours were. The questions say "ware", never "colour".
- **The hold stands still with the menu, the rules or the games screen open** (the drop, the hold's clock and its bar); a table's clock never stops.
- **The questions come least-revealing first** (counts, wares, the turned spot, which-stack last: its four stacks show nearly everything), and nothing is judged until the round's review, so no later option or earlier verdict helps. Number answers are five evenly spaced values with the truth at an evenly drawn place, so the middle is no safer a guess. Prices grow 12% a round and follow your record per kind (0.6 to 1.4 of the price, marked ↓ ↑).
- **A showing starts again from the beginning** when a saved run resumes or the page is hidden.
- **The page never scrolls.** The quay shows for the drop, a change round (the stack is the answer: tap it) and the review; while answering an ordinary round the questions have the room. A question about a place carries its own picture.

## Together (October 2026)
- A table's round is the room's moment (on the database's clock): the tally, the drop and the hold are worked from it on every phone, and a phone that comes in late draws the packages already down landed. Keys are `s<slot>` and `q<i>`; the rules are pure in the engine.
- Against: everyone sees the whole stack, answers their own questions; Ship it ends only your hold, for your own bonus; a player out of lives watches. Together: one run, one score, three team lives; the first answer counts.
- **Together splits the stack in whole columns of the floor, never by tier**: the bottom tier has every column and the top a third of them, so one player would get three times another's, and a tier above the floor would hang in the air. A part stands on the floor as it was stacked and reads the same in every view, the parts go round a step each round, and "you could see" means any of you. A change round shows each their part and brings the whole stack back to everyone.
- Prices are plain at a table (your record moves your own prices only). Your solo run, bests and today's are never touched.
