# Drift
The ninth idle game (October 2026; README, Idle): `drift.html`, `drift.js` and `drift.css`, its rules in `drift-engine.js`. A field seven squares by seven, tiles of the four enamels lying on it, each with a mark of its own (a dot, a ring, a bar, a chevron) so they're told apart without their colour, and the wind, forecast three turns ahead under the field beside the tile to lay and the one after. Each turn you lay the tile on any empty square; then the wind blows and every tile slides downwind until it meets the edge or another tile, keeping its order (the edge it piles against is drawn in brass). It's 2048 turned round: there you choose the way and the game lays the tile, here the forecast chooses the way and you lay it, so along the wind each row is a queue and where you lay a tile is where it slots in. Three or more alike in a line, across or down, clear, and the wind blows again, which can line up more; a turn scores ten times the square of all it clears (three 90, six 360). Press a square and the turn is shown before you let go (the field after the wind, the lines that would clear ringed, what the turn would score); slide to another square to see it, and let go to lay the tile (a mouse shows each square's turn as it passes). Every few turns a squall blows stray tiles in on empty squares before the wind takes them, none making a line where it lands: every two turns at first, every turn after 15, two at a time after 40, three after 80 and four after 150. A squall is forecast a turn ahead (October 2026): its strays are drawn faint on the squares they'll land on, no tile can be laid there (never on the last empty square), and the turn shown before you let go has them in it, so it's the turn you'll get; the line under the field says when the next squall comes, or that one is about to. Stakes (October 2026): every sixth tile to lay comes with a stake, a pin in its corner (on the tile to lay, and on the next when it's the one). Laid, it stays put whatever the wind, and the tiles the wind brings pile against it as against an edge, those beyond it sliding on to the field's edge as before; it clears in a line like any tile, and its stake goes with it. So a stake is a wall you place: something to build a line against in the middle of the field, or a way to hold a pair apart from the tile that would spoil it, and a nuisance where it blocks the line you wanted. The turn shown with a staked tile holds it in place, and the first time one comes the line under the field says what it does. A field with no empty square left after the wind ends the game; the strip counts the room left. The score and your best are in the strip, the best kept as `drift:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the game in play is kept (`drift:run`) and opens again where it was. A first game's first turns say what to do, written on the field. Held sideways the field is on the left with all the height, the score, what's coming and the line beside it.

Why the squall is forecast: blown in unseen, its strays undid whatever a turn set up, so a player laying where the turn shown clears most and otherwise dropping the tile in from the windward side lasted as long as one weighing the room left or looking at the next tile too (about 99, 97 and 98 turns, scoring 13,700, 12,900 and 15,000), and every way of playing that took the clears shown ended alike. (The tuning players below were first run on what a turn really did, squall and all, which the page never showed; judging only what it shows, the careful one lasted about 91.) Forecast, the habit lasts about 168 turns and scores about 34,500, the careful player about 184 (104 to 350) and 40,700, and the one looking at the next tile too about 189 and 52,100: reading the field pays again, and laying beside a stray to line it up is a move of its own. Players that never take a clear still fill the field in about 50 turns.

Why the squalls grow to four at a time: the wind packs every tile against one edge each turn, and packed tiles line up easily, so a player laying for the wind lasted 3,000 turns with a squall only every fourth turn, and one laying anywhere about 110. Four strays a turn late in the game is what ends the careful player's.

Why these numbers: played out by simple players (`moves` lists the squares a tile may go on), one laying anywhere fills the field in about 53 turns; one laying where the turn clears most and leaves the most room lasts about 143 (53 to 276) and scores about 26,000; one that also keeps pairs of a colour together lasts about 184, and looking at the next tile too about 189. (Before the forecast the second of these read 235 turns and 58,000 points: it judged each square by the turn it would really make, squall and all, and since where the strays landed hung on where the tile went, it could pick its luck, which no person could.) Stakes, when they came in, left the players about where they were (a stake every sixth tile, four or five standing at most; every fourth tile or every eighth, much the same), so they change what a turn can do rather than how long a game lasts. Every random thing (the tiles dealt and coming, the winds, the squalls' strays) comes from the game's seed and the number of the draw, so a game opened again goes on as it would have.

`tests/drift.mjs` checks the deal from a seed (no line standing, two tiles and three winds forecast), the wind (to the edge or the next tile in order, east, west, south and north, and what moved told), lines (across, a gap breaking one, down, a square in two counted once), a turn (a line made by the wind and its 90, nothing made where the wind takes the tile elsewhere, a clear and a second gust lining up more, the game laid on left as it was, the next tile and the forecast moving on, empty squares only, the gale as the page shows it), the points, squalls (forecast a turn ahead, their strays on empty squares where no tile can be laid, landing there before the wind so the turn shown is the turn played, the next forecast once it's a turn away, never on the last empty square, a game saved before forecasts given one; the count starting again, more often and more of them), stakes (a staked tile staying put and the wind piling tiles against it either way, only what moved told, down a column the same, a stake keeping a line apart, a staked tile laid staying where it's laid, one in a line clearing with its stake, the count to the next, a game saved before stakes given none), the end with no room, a game saved and opened again, and whole games: laying anywhere full in under 100 turns, laying for the wind lasting half as long again or more.
