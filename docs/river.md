# River
Xiangqi (象棋, xiàngqí, Chinese chess) against the computer at a strength you choose, two players on one phone, or a match with your partner (October 2026: Korbi meets Chinese suppliers and wants to hold his own at a real board); and a puzzle rush, its tactics against the clock (below). Red moves first; the computer's side is chosen in the menu (Red, Black or either), and the board turns so your side is at the bottom.

The rules are the Asian Xiangqi Federation's (亞洲象棋聯合會, AXF) laws as Chinese clubs play them. Nine files by ten ranks of points; the river (楚河 漢界) across the middle; each side's palace (九宮), the nine points marked with a cross. The general (帥/將) steps one point along a line inside its palace; the advisor (仕/士) one point diagonally inside it; the elephant (相/象) exactly two points diagonally, never across the river, and not at all past an occupied point between (塞象眼, the elephant's eye blocked); the horse (傌/馬) one point along a line and one diagonally outward, not past an occupied first point (蹩馬腿, the horse's leg hobbled); the chariot (俥/車) any distance along a line; the cannon (炮/砲) like a chariot, but it takes only by jumping exactly one piece of either side (炮架, the screen); the soldier (兵/卒) one point forward, and once across the river sideways too, never back. The two generals may never face each other on an open file (將帥不能照面), so a piece alone between them may move only along their file. A side with no legal move loses, in check (將死, checkmate) or not (困斃): there is no stalemate draw.

Repetition is where River simplifies the AXF's laws. Those judge a repeated position by what each side's moves were doing: perpetual check (長將) loses, and so does perpetually chasing (長捉) an unprotected piece, with tables for every mix of checks, chases and idle moves, which an arbiter applies. River judges only the checks: the third time a position stands, a side that gave check with every one of its own moves since the position first stood loses; any other repetition is a draw, chases included. So a player who chases a chariot round the board for ever gets a draw here where an arbiter would make him change his move. The engine plays the full AXF rules (Fairy-Stockfish has the chasing rule built in), so it never leans on the simpler one. Sixty moves by each side without a capture end the game drawn (自然限著, the natural move limit of the Chinese Xiangqi Association's rules), and so does a board where neither side has a chariot, horse, cannon or soldier left. The referee is River's own move generator (`river-rules.js`), not the engine: it gives the counts from the start position (44, 1,920, 79,666 and 3,290,240 at one to four moves deep, the published perft numbers) and agrees with Fairy-Stockfish's own legal moves in all 4,729 positions of forty random games, both checked by `tests/river.mjs`.

Moves are written two ways, each move in the list with both. In Chinese notation (中文記譜), the piece, the file it stands on, how it moves and where to: 炮二平五 is the cannon on file two across to file five. Each side counts the files from its own right, Red in Chinese numerals (一 to 九) and Black in figures (1 to 9); 進 is forward, 退 back, 平 across; a piece moving straight along its file gives the ranks moved (俥九進一), a horse, elephant or advisor the file it lands on (傌二進三); two like pieces on one file are front and rear (前炮, 後炮), three soldiers front, middle and rear, more by their order from the front. WXF notation (the World Xiangqi Federation's) is the same in letters: K A E H R C P, + forward, − back, = across (C2=5). The opening is named as you play (`river-openings.js`): sixteen systems a club player names, from 中炮 (zhōng pào, the central cannon) and 飛相局 (the elephant opening) to 中炮對屏風馬 (the central cannon against screen horses) and 五七炮, each in traditional and simplified characters with its pinyin and English name, matched by position and counting its mirror image (炮八平五 is as central as 炮二平五); the Rules sheet lists them with their moves.

The pieces are the traditional discs: ivory with a brass rim, Red's characters 帥仕相傌俥炮兵 in cinnabar and Black's 將士象馬車砲卒 in navy, each inside a ring of its colour. Menu, Characters: Traditional, or Simplified as a mainland set prints them (帅仕相马车炮兵 and 将士象马车炮卒, Black's chariot, horse and cannon the same characters as Red's), the notation following (进, 退). Menu, Facing each other turns the far side's characters, and its file numbers, to read from the far side, for two people with one phone flat between them. Menu, Pieces: Pictures (October 2026, for a player still learning the characters, who found them hard to tell apart at a glance) draws each piece as a picture in the same disc and colours, a chess cousin where there is one: the general a crown, the advisor a shield crossed on the diagonals it moves along, the elephant an elephant's head, the horse a knight's head, the chariot a tower (it moves as a rook), the cannon a cannon on its wheel, the soldier a pawn (the knight, tower and pawn are the cburnett chess pieces' shapes, CC BY-SA 3.0, as Gambit's are); the pieces taken beside each seat, the Rules sheet's discs and the course's board follow it, and the notation keeps its characters. The board is drawn in SVG (`river-board.js`): the frame, the river's words, the marks where the cannons and soldiers start, each side's file numbers at its own edge; tap a piece and then a point, or drag it, its legal points dotted and the pieces it can take ringed; a second tap puts a piece down; on your move, a tap on one of the other side's pieces shows where it could go (hollow dots in its colour, a ring in check's red round each of your pieces it could take) and which of its own pieces it guards (dashed rings), and says so in a line ("Black's cannon 砲: 12 points to go to, 1 of them a take; guards nothing"; `reachOf` in `river-rules.js`: its legal moves were it its side's move, and the pieces its paths reach, a pinned piece guarding all the same), and a second tap, or a piece of yours picked up, puts it away; the last move is marked where it came from and where it went, and slides in; a general in check glows red. Hint shows the engine's strongest moves for the side to move as arrows on the board, with what it makes of the position in words and figures ("Hint: 相七進五 E7+5 · about level (+0.3)"; a little better, better, much better, winning, or mates in n; below, the coach); Take back undoes to your last move (with the computer, its reply goes too). The Rules sheet has each piece with both its characters, its pinyin and its English name, small boards showing the four rules a newcomer trips on (the general's file, the elephant's eye, the horse's leg, the cannon's screen), how a game ends, how to read the moves, and the openings.

At the end, a sheet says who won and how, the opening and, against the computer, the strongest level you've beaten; then it reviews the game: each position searched once by the engine at full strength (20,000 positions each, some ten seconds for a long game on a phone, the sheet counting them off), and your three costliest moves listed with how much each gave away by the engine's reckoning and its better move, in both notations (two players on one phone get both sides' moves).

The coach (Menu, Coach, October 2026, as Hong's and Tribute's) speaks in one of four ways. Hints, the default and what there was before: nothing unless you ask, and Hint shows the strongest moves, the engine's best and any within a fifth of a soldier of it (in an opening often two or three, at a combination one), as arrows. Check: Hint too, and after each move of yours that gave a good deal away, the coach's word on it under your seat, its label in oxblood so it isn't taken for advice, until your next move: what the move gave away and the better one ("2. 炮五進四?? gives away about 4.0 soldiers' worth; better was 傌二進三 H2+3, or 2 more as good"), the move marked ? (a mistake) or ?? (a blunder) in the list; Show puts the board back as it stood before it, the coach's moves drawn in green and yours in oxblood, until Back to the game. Always: the same, and the strongest moves as arrows at each of your moves without asking ("Coach: 傌二進三 H2+3 · about level"). Off: nothing, and no Hint. How bad a move is goes by winning chances, not soldiers: Lichess's curve, 2 / (1 + e^(−0.00368·cp)) − 1, the share of the game a score is worth from −1 (lost) to 1 (won), a fall of 0.2 a mistake and 0.3 a blunder as Lichess marks them, so a soldier thrown away in a level game counts and the same soldier a chariot up doesn't (the game was won either way); a move that lets in a mate, or misses one, says so. Xiangqi's engine scores a soldier at about a quarter more than chess's engine scores a pawn, so the coach reads its scores at four fifths. Each position is analysed once, the engine at full strength showing its best three moves (MultiPV 3) on 250,000 positions, and the analysis is shared by the hint, the advice and the check; a move outside the three is searched on its own from the same position (UCI's searchmoves, 125,000 positions), so it's scored as the best was. Under Check and Always the coach looks at your position while you think, so its word comes quickly, and the computer's reply waits for it. The judgement is `uci-coach.js`, Gambit's too; the hint, the word and the marks are kept with the game (`river:game`), so a reload shows them. There's no coach at a table together, as there are no hints. A coach that speaks unasked keeps its two lines under your seat (four beside the board, held sideways), so the board doesn't change size as it speaks and falls quiet; with three moves named in both notations it names the best and counts the others, which Show draws. `tests/river.mjs` checks it with the engine: the central cannon thrown away for a soldier is a blunder, its better moves legal, and a horse brought out is no word.

The computer player is Fairy-Stockfish (Fabian Fichter and contributors, GPL v3), Stockfish taught Xiangqi and some ninety other variants, among them those River may get later (Shogi, Makruk, Janggi). Every WebAssembly build of it published (the npm package fairy-stockfish-nnue.wasm 1.1.12, lila-stockfish-web's fsf14, fairy-stockfish-web) is multi-threaded, so it needs SharedArrayBuffer and with it cross-origin isolation headers, which GitHub Pages can't send: it wouldn't start on Korbi's iPhone. River's is built from the official source (commit 9f778da, 23 September 2026) as a single-threaded WebAssembly reactor: `vendor/fairy-stockfish/single-threaded.patch` starts no thread (a search runs to its end in the caller), takes UCI commands one at a time from the page's worker instead of standard input, and adds the entry points the worker calls; no rule, search or evaluation is changed. `tools/fairy-stockfish.sh` rebuilds it byte for byte (clang 18 for wasm32-wasi, with Ubuntu's WASI C library and libc++; the SHA-256 is in `vendor/fairy-stockfish/NOTE.txt`, with the source's address and the licence). It has no NNUE net for Xiangqi (those are on GitHub's release pages, which the machine that built it couldn't reach), so it plays its classical evaluation, still far stronger than any club player at full strength. It runs in a worker (`uci-fairy.js`, through the small WASI shim in `uci-wasi.js`), spoken to in UCI by `uci.js`, and is loaded only when a game against it needs it: the rete shows while it starts (the first time it downloads its 1.2 MB), and it is kept offline with the rest of the app. The tests run the same file in Node through the same shim.

Eight levels (`river-levels.js`), named by rough rating: Beginner 700, Novice 950, Casual 1,150, Club 1,450, Strong club 1,700, Expert 1,900, Master 2,200 and Full strength 2,700; a new player starts at Novice. The four weakest draw their move among the engine's best after a small search, each weighted e^(−loss/T) by the centipawns it gives up against the best (`choose()` in `uci.js`, as in Gambit): Beginner among its 12 best after 800 positions at T = 300, Novice 10 after 1,000 at 140, Casual 8 after 1,500 at 70, Club 6 after 2,500 at 35, so each now and then gives a piece away or misses a mate, as people do, and each level up less often. The four above play the engine's own move after 1,500, 4,000, 20,000 and 500,000 positions (Full strength a second or two on a phone). A level is a number of positions, not a time, so it plays alike on a slow phone and a fast one. Fairy-Stockfish's own limiter (UCI_Elo) isn't used: its ratings are calibrated for chess, and in Xiangqi its 1,700 scored 7 of 24 against a drawing level that comes out at about 1,350 here.

How they were measured: `tools/engine-levels.mjs` plays matches in Node, the page's engine file (through the same shim) and `uci.js` playing both sides, each game opened with two random sensible moves and the colours alternating: 430 games among the eight levels and ten candidates tried on the way (16 between each pair of neighbours up to Master, Full strength ten against the engine at 80,000 positions, ten for each other pairing), fitted together to one scale by maximum likelihood under the Elo model (`node tools/engine-levels.mjs fit`; the games are kept in `tools/engine-levels-river.jsonl`). Above Beginner, the levels came out 340 ± 100, 660 ± 130, 1,030 ± 160, 1,410 ± 180, 1,670 ± 190, 2,110 ± 210 and 2,820 ± 290 points up. Nothing ties a Xiangqi rating to these matches, and matches between versions of one engine overstate the gaps (its own deeper search finds its own mistakes more surely than a person would), so the scale's ends are a judgment: Beginner at Gambit's Beginner's 700, the two made alike, and Full strength at 2,700 (the Chinese Xiangqi Association's top ten were all above 2,600 at the end of 2022), which shrinks the measured gaps by 29%.

The course (Learn, in the header; October 2026, for a beginner who means to sit down at a real board in China): Xiangqi taught from nothing, in 24 lessons in three chapters, on a sheet over the game with a board of its own. The rules: the board and its palaces and river, then each piece in turn (tap every point it can reach; take the piece it can take, the elephant's eye and the horse's leg in the way), the generals that may never face each other, check, mate, no move left losing, perpetual check. Ideas: what the pieces are worth (開局炮，殘局馬: the cannon early, the horse late), the fork with a horse and with a soldier, the pin, the discovered check (抽將), the generals face to face (對面笑) as a weapon, the named mates (馬後炮, 重炮, 雙車錯, and since the rush's Patterns came, 臥槽馬, 掛角馬 and 悶宮), and how to open (三步不出車: the chariots out in three moves). Openings: the central cannon, the screen horses (from Black's side), the river-crossing chariot of the main line, same- and opposite-direction cannons, the elephant opening and the pawn opening, each played through move by move, you making your side's moves and the other side's answered for you, a note on every move; then a first game against Beginner. A step is words over a position, a tap of every point a piece can reach (counted as you find them, a wrong point crossed out), a tap of the piece the question asks for, a move to find (a wrong one shown a moment, then taken back with why: "Check, but there's a way out: 將5進1 (K5+1)"; after two misses, an arrow), an opening line, or a choice of answers. Hint shows the answer. Words name points by what they are (the central soldier, the centre file), not by coordinates the board doesn't show; Chinese terms come in the characters the menu sets. Where you are is kept (`river:course`), so the course opens where you left it and its map ticks what's done. The lessons are data (`river-course.js`), drawn by `river-learn.js` on the game's own board (which gains the course's marks: points dotted and crossed, rings, arrows, the palaces and the river shaded), and `tests/river-course.mjs` plays every exercise through the move generator: every position legal, every "tap every point" with as many points as its words promise, every move asked for there to be made (each mate in one with exactly one mating move), every opening line legal and named by the openings table. The Rules sheet, every rule on one page, opens from the course's map and the menu.

The puzzle rush (Menu, Puzzle rush; October 2026, as Gambit's chess puzzles): Xiangqi tactics, as many as you can, with the settings every game's rush shares (README, Puzzle rush): Random, Today's or River's own Patterns (below); 3 min (a puzzle's first five seconds don't run the clock, and nothing is added back), 5 min or Survival (no clock); three misses end the run. It opens on an easy warm-up, and solving that starts the clock; the warm-up counts for nothing. Each puzzle shows the position from the side that solves it, the other side's mistake plays after a beat, and you move by dragging a piece or tapping it and then a point. Every move has to be the puzzle's, except that where its line mates, any move that mates counts (or leaves the other side no move, which wins as well). A move made while the reply is coming is a premove, its points ringed in violet, played the instant the reply lands if it's legal then (the same move again takes it back). A miss draws your move in oxblood and the line's in green, and names the line in Chinese notation; after each puzzle, its rating and what it comes to ("Rated 1,240 · wins a chariot", "a chariot for a cannon", "mate in 2"). Your rating is a running one across every run (`river:rushRating`), Elo-style against each puzzle's: quick for your first twenty puzzles, slow after, a solve's gain halving with each miss earlier in the run so a careless run can't climb; it shows by the count, with a ? while it settles, and resets from the menu. A run starts about 250 below it and climbs 40 a solve to about 150 above (a miss doesn't raise it); today's run is the same puzzles for everyone that day, from 800 and 60 more a solve. The results give the count, accuracy, your rating and how it moved, your best in the mode (or today's best), the hardest solved, and every miss with its line in both notations and Show, which plays it out on the board a move a second; the menu has them again until the next run. The menu, the rules and the games screen stop the clock; a timed run ends if the app goes to the background or when you go back to the game (you're asked), while a survival run, or a warm-up, waits where it was, after a reload too. The rush takes the game's place on the page, its count, clock and strikes over the board (beside it, held sideways), and the game waits as it was, the computer's move too; the menu's Pieces and Characters hold for its board and its notation as for the game's. The run is kept after every move (`river:rush`: the puzzle, how far into it, the count, the strikes, the clock and how much of the puzzle's grace is used), so a partner watching sees it as you play; your bests by mode are `river:rushBest`, today's runs `river:rushDaily`. The page's part is `river-rush-ui.js`, the rules of it without a page (the bank's lines, whether a move answers, what a run aims at, the rating's steps) `river-rush.js`. Misses don't go to Deck yet, as Gambit's do: Deck would need a Xiangqi board.

The bank (`river-puzzles.txt`, 2,321 puzzles, 916 of them mates and 308 with more than one move to find; 1,510 until the patterns came, when the finder ran again) is River's own (Gambit's chess puzzles come from Lichess, which has no Xiangqi): they come from games River's levels play among themselves, checked by Fairy-Stockfish as Lichess checks the puzzles it finds in players' games (`tools/river-puzzles.mjs`). Each game is between neighbouring levels, Beginner to Master; at each of its positions, the moves the side to move might make are tried as mistakes (the one played, the ten a weak player weighs, a strong level's own five), and where one leaves the other side three soldiers' worth ahead from no more than three before (its winning chances up 0.3 or more, by the coach's curve) or lets in a mate, the position after it is a puzzle if the other side's best move is the only one that wins: its winning chances 0.5 above the next best's, or for a mate in more than one, no other move that mates and none winning about a chariot's worth. The line follows with the engine's best defence while each of the winner's moves is still the only one, up to four, and ends on the winner's move. A puzzle is rated by how River's levels fare at it: the four drawing levels' chance of playing the line, as they play, and the engine at full strength after a ladder of searches from 1,000 to 20,000 positions (Strong club, Expert and Master among them), each solver rated on River's scale (the rungs between the levels by the doublings of their search); the rating is the one likeliest to give those results under the Elo model, drawn a little toward 1,300 so a puzzle everyone gets isn't rated minus infinity. So the ratings are on the menu's scale of strengths, not a human pool's as Lichess's are. The levels' mistakes are mostly simple (a piece left where it can be taken, a mate in one let in), so the bank is heavy at the easy end: 1,578 below 1,000, 658 from 1,000 to 1,600, 85 above; a run past the top is served the hardest there are. Trying only a strong level's own mistakes, searched deeply, or only moves that look sound at a closer look found no harder puzzles, at a quarter of the pace. The generator runs again to add to the bank (`find`, then `merge`; a native Fairy-Stockfish built from the same commit searches twice as fast, and two at once use both cores: about ten puzzles a minute), and a puzzle's id comes from its position and line, so it stays the same as the bank grows. `tests/river.mjs` checks the bank (every line legal from its position, the other side's mistake first and the solver's move last, its theme right, easiest first), that where a line mates any mate counts and nothing else does, what a run aims at, that today's run is the same thirty puzzles on every device, and the rating's steps.

Patterns (October 2026, asked for: the bank's puzzles were good practice but taught no names): the mates and tricks a Xiangqi player learns by name, found in each puzzle's line and the position it ends on by the board's own rules (`river-patterns.js`): 馬後炮 (the cannon behind the horse: the horse is its screen and holds the general's flight), 重炮 (doubled cannons, the front one the rear one's screen), 臥槽馬 (the horse checking from beside the elephant's home, on the general's second rank), 掛角馬 (the horse checking from a corner of the palace), 悶宮 (a cannon's check with every point the general could step to filled by its own pieces), 天地炮 (one cannon down the general's file, one along its rank), 雙車錯 (one chariot checking, the other holding the line beside it), 大膽穿心 (a chariot given up in the palace's centre), 對面笑 (a point the general needs held by the other general down an open file), 困斃 (no move left, not in check: a loss), 雙將 (double check), 抽將 (the discovered check that takes as it goes) and 捉雙 (the fork the line then cashes). A puzzle can be several. They're written into the bank with its theme ("mate;mahou,duimian": `tools/river-puzzles.mjs merge` works them out, and `merge --add` adds new finds to the bank as it stands, the finds that made it not being kept), and named over the board once each puzzle is done ("Rated 885 · mate in 1 · 臥槽馬"), in the characters the menu sets. The rush's menu has a third kind of run beside Random and Today's: Patterns, the named puzzles alone, with no clock, each one said after it's done ("✓ 臥槽馬: the horse checks from beside the elephant's home"; after a miss, the line first, then what it was), with time to read it; its best is kept apart. The bank grew for it: the finder ran again on 210 new games with a native Fairy-Stockfish (built from the WebAssembly's commit, twice as fast), adding 811 puzzles, and the named ones went from 280 to 423 (臥槽馬 51, 雙車錯 50, 重炮 45, 馬後炮 41, 悶宮 26, 天地炮 24, 掛角馬 23, 大膽穿心 9; 對面笑 at work in 102, 雙將 in 64). `tests/river.mjs` checks that every puzzle's patterns are the ones its line shows, worked out again, that a line of the bank reads back whole, that a run of patterns draws only on named puzzles, and finds each pattern on a position built for it (a mistake, then the mate or the move that leaves none).

Your game is kept as it changes (`river:game`: the moves, who plays which side, the level, a hint shown, the review), so a reload, another of your devices or a partner watching sees it as it stands; `river:opts` keeps the menu's choices, `river:best` the strongest level beaten (the tile's "Best"), and `river:record` your wins, draws and losses at each level (the menu's About). A game counts once, as it first ends: Take back after the end lets you play on to another end, with its own review, but the record keeps the first, so a take-back can't turn a loss into a level beaten. The computer's move waits while the menu, the Rules or the games screen is open, and comes no sooner than 0.7 seconds after yours, so you see it arrive.

Upright, the computer's seat is over the board (its general's disc, its name and rating, "thinking" while it searches, and what it has taken), the board as wide as the phone, your seat under it saying whose move it is, check, the hint or the opening, then the moves in a row, the latest at the right, and Take back and Hint at the bottom (once the game is over, New game in Hint's place: two buttons at most, all a sideways column has room for). Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height and the seats, the moves (in rows) and the buttons stand beside it, the buttons low.

Together (a duo match from the games screen, `together.js`): the colours are drawn by lot, Red moves first, each device moves only its own side and sees the board from it; there are no hints or take-backs, and either of you can resign from the menu. A finished match goes to the pair's record, a win each way. `tests/river.mjs` checks the rules move by move on hand-built positions, perft, the move generator against the engine's, the notations, every ending (checkmate, no move left, perpetual check, repetition, sixty moves, no attackers), the openings, the state as the room keeps it (every move of whole games between levels read back through `tests/rtdb.js`'s model of the database), and the stronger levels beating the weaker.
