# Survey
A 3D puzzle (October 2026; it was a budgeted guessing game about a mining concession before, which Korbi found weak). A block of rock, 3, 4, 5 or 6 cubes each way, hides a body of ore. Every straight row of cubes through it, in all three directions, has a reading printed on the face it comes out of: how many of that row's cubes are ore. With the seams marked (the default) the plate also says how the ore lies: a plain plate is one seam (one unbroken run), a ring is two seams, a box is three or more; Counts only leaves that off and is the harder reading. Your job is to chip away all the waste. Only a cube with a side open to the air can be chipped, so you work from the outside in, and each hollow you cut lets you reach, and see into, what lies behind it. A cube that turns out to be ore stays, shows in gold and costs a strike; Mark keeps cubes you are sure are ore safe from the chisel (free, and only a cube you could chip can be marked); Hint names a cube the readings force right now, with the row that says so, and lights both.

Your score is your time in seconds plus 20 for each strike and 30 for each hint; lower is better. A row whose cubes left are all ore has nothing more to chip and its plate dims; one with nothing left at all disappears. The last waste cube ends the block (counted the moment it goes, as for every daily), and the ore left is shown in a wave of gold while the block turns on its own for a few seconds.

Every block has exactly one answer, and it can be reached by reasoning along rows alone, never by guessing: `survey-engine.js` generates each one and checks that a solver which only ever uses what one row forces (the rows that fit its reading and what is already known) settles every cube. That also proves the answer is unique. The generator grows one to three lumps of ore to about half the block, breaks them up a little (a tenth of the ore removed, a few waste cubes added beside it), keeps the layout only if ore is between 28% and 60% of the block and no waste cube is sealed inside (one you could never reach), and takes the first that needs at least as many rounds of reasoning as the size asks (Small 2 with the seams marked and 3 counts only, Medium 3 and 4, Large 3 and 5, Vast 4 and 6; a round uses only what the round before settled), else the hardest of up to sixty it tried. With the seams marked every random shape is solvable this way; counts alone are, in a measured run, about 98% of Small, 92% of Medium, 69% of Large and 40% of Vast layouts, so the larger counts-only blocks take the generator more tries (a few milliseconds, never a wait). The puzzle is made from its seed, size and readings alone, so the same link is the same block anywhere. These numbers come from the generator's statistics, not from play: whether Medium is the right default and the depth targets are right is for Korbi's thumb to say.

The block is drawn by `survey-3d.js` on a canvas (three.js, kept on the device): every cube is one instance of a rounded cube, so a whole block is one draw; a chipped cube pops and shrinks away with a burst of shards and leaves a hollow with its shadows; a strike cube swells and turns gold; a marked cube is tinted blue with a diamond on each face. The camera goes round the block under a finger (one finger turns it all the way round and tips it from 72° below, where the plinth steps aside and the light follows, to 86° above; two fingers pinch to zoom and slide it; a mouse wheel zooms, shift-drag slides; a turn coasts and dies away smoothly, none of it with reduced motion; the view, zoom and slide are kept in `survey:view`) and is a perspective one fitted once, to the block at its first tilt, and never again while it moves: nothing is refitted under a finger. A touch chips only if it stays put, is quick (a longer press only lights the cube's rows) and did not land on a coasting block (that touch just stops it); a second tap in the empty air, or the round arrow at the card's corner (shown only when the view is away from home), brings the block home. The readings are small plates of the page laid over the canvas, each at the face where its row comes out of the block on the side turned toward you, so they stay upright and legible while the block turns; a plate fades out as its face turns edge-on and the other end of the row takes over. A tap works the tool on the cube under the finger (the nearest cube still in the block: ray against the cubes' boxes); pressing a cube lights its three rows' plates, which is the way to read it. The light turns with the camera so every face you see is lit the same from any side. The light, the plinth and the gold follow the theme and day or night.

Tools and the line under the block follow the tool: tap a cube; Chip, Mark, Hint. A marked cube cannot be chipped until it is unmarked, so a wrong mark costs nothing but a tap; a wrong chip costs 20 seconds. Hints use only what is certain (cubes chipped, ore shown), never your marks. If every cube a hint could name is still hidden inside, it lights the row and says to chip towards it.

The page fits the screen: the block takes all the room under the score, the tools and the line under it; at 390×600 and up nothing scrolls. Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the block takes the card's left side with all the height, and the score, the line and the tools stand at the right, low where a thumb is. Where there is no WebGL the page says so (there is no flat version).

In Free play (what Survey was before levels): sizes Small, Medium, Large and Vast in the header; Readings (Seams marked, Counts only, Runs given) in the menu; a random block or today's, which is the same for everyone (Medium, seams marked) and counts for the games screen's tile as a time, the first clear's score (`survey:today`). Bests, one per size and readings, are in `survey:times`, not `survey:best` (the games screen reads a best as highest-wins). The free block in play is `survey:run` (saved at every change and every five seconds, so a partner watching sees it), your view `survey:view`, the readings `survey:readings`; the old game's `survey:best` and `survey:daily` were cleared once (`survey:era`). Links are `#s=SEED&z=SIZE&r=READINGS` (`#d=` for a day's). The clock runs while the page is on screen and stops with the menu, the rules and the games screen.

## Twists
Korbi, 4 November 2026 (above): "not enough interest elements to the puzzle. think of some twists and make it more fun, levels very boring rn". Four twists, each a chapter of its own that brings it in gently and ends on a block that mixes it with what came before; every block of every level still has one answer by reasoning along the rows (`survey-twist.js` is the solver for two ores and faults; `tests/survey.mjs` checks each twist's blocks against a search that may guess).
- **Granite**: grey cubes on the surface, seen from the start, that can't be chipped, marked or struck and are never ore; the plates count the ore only. Always on the surface, never buried: a granite cube you could not see would be a hidden fact the readings depend on. They are not waste to clear, so the block ends with them still there.
- **Lanterns**: waste cubes on the surface glowing amber. Chipping one lights its six neighbours: waste crumbles, ore shows gold or silver with no strike, and a lantern among that waste lights its own neighbours (a chain). The solver starts from the block with every lantern chipped, which costs nothing, so a block may need its lanterns to be solvable; a hint that finds nothing points at one.
- **Silver and gold**: two ores, a plate with two numbers (silver underlined blue-grey, gold amber), all of both stays, a strike on either costs the same. One answer here means one split of ore and waste: which ore a cube is can stay open, nothing asks for it. A hint that shows ore shows its true kind, which the rows may not have settled.
- **Faults**: a plane across the rock; a row that crosses it gives two numbers, one for each side, with a bar between. The brass plane and the hairline gap it opens are only a picture: the rules see no gap, and a cube beside the crack is no more exposed for it.
- A level's `id` is what its seed and block come from and never changes; the ladder's order is the order of the chapters (`PLAN`), so the first 25 levels (the ladder before twists) are the same blocks wherever they now stand, checked by hash. What was saved under the old numbers is moved once into `survey:camp2` and `survey:lruns2` by where each level stands now. A level is open up to one past the furthest beaten, so nobody who got past a level finds it locked.

## Levels

Added in November 2026, after Korbi's note that Survey was a very fun base but needed progression, and that the smallest cube played itself (mine the zeros, mark the fours, and the game finishes). The menu's Play choice is Levels or Free play; someone with a free block in hand from before carries on with it, anyone else begins on the ladder (`survey:track` keeps which). The ladder follows Topple's Levels: a list in the menu of every level as its rock in miniature (`survey-mini.js`, drawn isometrically with the faces another cube hides left out), three to a row under their chapter, each with its number, stars, name and size. Beating a level opens the next, any open level can be played at any time and is kept as it was left (`survey:lruns2`, one block per level), and the header shows the level's number in place of the size dropdown (a level's size is its own) with the par beside the score. The level being played and the stars won are `survey:camp2`; a link to a level is `#l=LEVEL&s=SEED&v=2`, and one to a level you have not opened yet is ignored.

A level is a recipe (`survey-levels.js`), not a block: its size, the shape of its rock, how its rows are read, how much fog and how much reasoning to ask for. The first time a level is played it is the same block for everyone (the level's own seed), so levels can be talked about; after it is cleared, or on New block, it is a new random block of the same recipe, which is how a level is replayed for its stars. There are 44 levels in ten chapters, each bringing one new thing to the one before (the four twists, below, come between these six, which are the ladder as it first was):

- **Test pits** (four levels, 3 to 5 cubes): the small blocks as Survey always was, with the seams marked. The first level is 3×3×3 with a line of what to do, and the next three are boxes and cubes of 4 and 5.
- **Blind counts** (four): the plates say only how many cubes in the row are ore, nothing of how they lie. This is the game's old Counts only reading.
- **Runs** (four, up to 7×7×7): a plate gives the length of every seam in the row, in order (`2 1`: a seam of two, a gap, then one). Runs is a third reading, giving away more than the seams marked (their lengths and order, not only how many there are) and more than the counts, and it is what lets the blocks grow: the six- and seven-cube levels are solvable by reasoning because of it. It is in Free play too.
- **Cuttings** (five): rock that is not a cube: a ramp (a wedge), a corner (a pyramid on a triangle), an arch, an elbow, a ziggurat. A cube that is not there is air, so a cube beside a gap can be chipped at once and a row can begin or end at a gap; a row with no rock in it has no plate; the plates sit at the ends of the rock's rows, not the block's. Every shape fills its block's length along each axis, so the block fits the view as a cube does.
- **Fog** (four, one of them a table on four legs): some plates are missing, shown as a dashed `?`. The generator takes readings off one row at a time in random order and keeps each one off only while the block can still be settled by reasoning along the rows, so the rest still settle every cube; the share asked for is a cap (15% to 30% of the rows that have rock), which is reached or nearly.
- **Master surveys** (four: a tower, a deep arch, a stepped ziggurat and the 7×7×7 mother lode): shapes, readings and fog together.

Stars are for the score, as everywhere in Survey (seconds plus 20 a strike and 30 a hint, lower is better): one for clearing the block, two for a score within twice the par, three within the par. The par is 8 seconds plus 2.2 seconds for each waste cube, more for a block that asks for deeper reasoning (12% more for each round beyond two). These two numbers are guesses, set so that the first level's par is 37 seconds and the mother lode's is 12 minutes; whether three stars should be that easy or that hard is for play to say, and `PAR_PER_CUBE` and `PAR_BASE` are the knobs. A level says what is new when it is first met (the chapter's idea, on the chapter's first level), and the line under the block says how to read the plates for the readings in play.

How it works underneath: `survey-engine.js` was generalised, not forked. A row's reading is now one of three kinds (seams, counts, runs) with an index of every row pattern of n cubes by its reading, and the solver takes a frame (size, kind, which cubes there are): a pattern fits a row only if its ore is in the rock, and a row with no reading (fog) can be any pattern the rock allows. Uniqueness is still proved the same way, by the solver settling every cube from the rows alone. Blocks from before levels are unchanged: the random generator draws in the order it always did, so the same seed, size and readings is the same block (checked by hash for 32 of them in `tests/survey-levels.mjs`, which is what keeps today's block and old links valid). Measured: the 25 levels at four seeds each make in about 1.2 s together, the slowest block 50 ms, and 91 of those 100 blocks need at least the reasoning their level asks (the rest have a little less; the generator tries up to sixty layouts for it); Free play at Runs takes 4 ms on average at Vast, 7 ms at the worst.

Not known yet: how the levels feel in the hand. The seven-cube levels are dense, every plate in a row of seven sits close to its neighbours, and on a phone the 7×7×7 blocks may want a zoom; the ramp's default view stacks plates at its thin end (turning the block clears it). The chapters are in the order I would learn them in; Korbi's thumb sets the order, the pars and which levels go.

Together: the same block for everyone at the table, the same size and readings, each chipping it on their own phone from when the table starts (README, Head to head), from the partner sheet or a Party of up to six; the header's size deals a new match at that size. The others show under the score as the rock they have left (sent every second and a half, not every chip), and as their score once they have cleared it. The lowest score (time plus penalties) wins. Your own block, bests and today's stay as they were. `tests/survey.mjs` checks the rules, the solver, the generator (a puzzle of every size and reading), uniqueness by exhaustive search on small ones, chipping, marks, strikes, hints and scores; `tests/survey-levels.mjs` the ladder: every shape spans its block and is one piece, every level makes a block at four seeds with one answer by reasoning, ore only in the rock, nothing sealed in, true readings, fog as asked and the same block twice, every one chipped clear from the outside in, the stars and pars, the blocks from before levels, and the miniature.
