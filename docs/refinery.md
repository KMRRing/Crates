# Refinery
A course in how a refinery works (October 2026, rebuilt on Calibre's pattern at Korbi's request): chapters of levels from the simplest ideas to the most involved, each level a lesson, then a plant to run, then an exam. Thirteen chapters: the barrel (the crude unit, crudes' assays, blending to a limit), sulphur (hydrotreating, jet fuel, the sulphur plant), octane (the reformer, isomerisation, vapour pressure, aromatics and E10), cracking (the vacuum unit and the FCC, light cycle oil, alkylation), hydrogen and the hydrocracker, the bottom of the barrel (fuel oil after IMO 2020, the visbreaker, the coker, bitumen), blending to spec (diesel's density window, petrol to every EN 228 limit with its benzene splitter and ETBE, fuel oil to ISO 8217, the whole refinery to full specification), economics (cracks in two markets, choosing a crude, what a barrel is worth, the bottleneck), renewables (tickets and the blend walls, HVO against diesel's density floor, co-processing in the diesel hydrotreater and in the FCC, sustainable aviation fuel against its mandate), two whole refineries taken unit by unit, Alder Point and Brackwater, and co-processing at both, and a third, much smaller one, Kestrel Flats.

Alder Point is an invented fuels refinery of about 120 thousand barrels a day, on a light sweet crude (Saharan Blend). It has crude and vacuum units; an FCC with ZSM-5 for propylene, its naphtha cut light (to a selective post-treater and petrol), intermediate (to the naphtha hydrotreater and the reformer) and heavy (to the kerosene hydrotreater and diesel), its cycle oil drawn light and heavy; a semi-regenerative reformer that is its only hydrogen, with no hydrogen plant and nothing bought; a benzene splitter; and two stages of gas oil desulphurising, the first to heating oil's 0.1% and the second to 10 ppm. It makes no jet and has no hydrocracker, coker, isomerisation or alkylation, and it makes VLSFO by its crude slate and desulphurised cutter. Its five levels add the plant a part at a time; the last holds every product to its full specification, with FAME, ethanol and ETBE bought for their tickets. To build it, a level can give a unit feeds and yields of its own (`feeds`, `yields`: its diesel hydrotreater takes only the light gas oil and the first stage's product, its FCC cuts its naphtha three ways) and place its units on the flowsheet (`layout`), and a crude can give its cuts properties of their own (`props`: Saharan Blend's gas oils are lighter and higher in cetane than the textbook's, which is what lets the plant make mostly diesel).

Brackwater is an invented refinery of about 200 thousand barrels a day in two works a few kilometres apart, joined by pipelines, on a medium sour crude (Arab Light). The east works distils 80 and cracks: a VGO hydrotreater ahead of an FCC with no naphtha post-treater, so the cracker takes only treated feed (its naphtha carries about a tenth of its feed's sulphur into petrol); MTBE from the isobutene in its C4s and bought methanol; alkylation of the rest of the C4s, the raffinate; and cumene from its propylene and bought benzene, sold to a phenol plant. The west works distils 120 and does the rest: a naphtha hydrotreater, a CCR reformer and isomerisation, the naphtha cut so that the C6s that would make benzene go to isomerisation, which saturates them (reformate under 1% benzene, and no benzene splitter); four distillate hydrotreaters, one for kerosene, two for the lighter gas oils and one at higher pressure for the heaviest, which takes the cracked gas oils too; the vacuum unit, taking both works' residue, and a second VGO hydrotreater (each cracks a little of its feed to diesel on the way, a mild hydrocracker); and the residue chain, a visbreaker, a solvent deasphalter (its oil to the VGO hydrotreaters, its pitch left behind) and a gasification power plant, an IGCC, which turns the pitch into power for the grid and hydrogen for the treaters. A hydrogen plant and a sulphur plant serve both. Its five levels: the two works and their distillates (11.1); the heavy end, vacuum gas oil treated and sold as low-sulphur VGO and the residue made into deasphalted oil and power (11.2); the cracker and its light ends (11.3); a turnaround, the east works' VGO hydrotreater down for its catalyst change and treated VGO on offer (11.4); and the whole of it to full specification with five crudes to choose from (11.5), where the residue chain, built for a medium sour crude, is what decides the best. To build it a level can run a second crude unit (`cdu2`, its share of the crude in kb/d) cutting the same crude the same way, so each cut is one stream whichever column made it, drawn as two columns side by side; a unit can use a reactant bought in (`uses`, priced in `REAGENTS`: methanol for MTBE, benzene for cumene), counted in the margin and shown on its card; a treater can set a property by its feed (`setFeed`: deasphalted oil comes out at 300 ppm, gas oil at 150); a level can give a unit's product properties of its own (`props`: the reformate's benzene); and a product can sell by the megawatt hour (power, shown in MW).

Co-processing, the last chapter (Korbi, October 2026: "lessons on co-processing of different feedstocks there"), runs renewable feeds through the units the two refineries already have. Each level starts from the plant as the level before left it at par (the first at each refinery from its chapter's last level, 12.3 from Alder Point before the tallow, since its tallow is scarcer, and 12.6 from Brackwater's summer plan), as co-processing is a change made to a running plant: `start` reads `refinery-par.js`, so `--write-par` is run twice when a level's plan changes. Five feeds: used cooking oil, Category 1 tallow and rapeseed oil (triglycerides), crude tall oil (fatty and rosin acids from kraft pulping) and fast pyrolysis oil (from forest residues), each with its price (tallow under used cooking oil, as Category 1 trades; pyrolysis oil a little over the fuel oil it replaces in a boiler, heat for heat), its saving as the fuel it becomes (RED II's default values for HVO: used cooking oil 83%, animal fat 77%, rapeseed oil 47%), its hydrogen (rapeseed oil, the most unsaturated, the most; tallow the least; every fat six or seven times a gas oil's), and its cold flow. A unit takes each feed up to a share of its own feed, and its renewables together up to a share of it too (`co`, and `coTotal`, as the heat, the hydrogen and the carbon oxides are theirs together): the diesel hydrotreaters fats up to a tenth; the FCC fats up to 7% and pyrolysis oil, through a nozzle of its own, up to 5%, a tenth together; Brackwater's heavy gas oil hydrotreater, built in stainless steel, tall oil and fat up to a tenth; the kerosene hydrotreater fat up to 5%, as ASTM D1655 allows for jet (the UK's Def Stan 91-091 raised it to 30% in 2025). What a scheme credits co-processed is the scheme's: Germany, since 2024 (37. BImSchV, section 12), credits co-processed oil only from Annex IX Part A feeds and Category 1 and 2 animal fats, so co-processed used cooking oil and rapeseed oil earn no ticket there; 12.3 is played in Germany and where all co-processing counts, a market being able to set what a feed saves as well as prices (`decks` with `props`, through `asPlayed` in the engine, which every page and test now uses to make the level as played). Diesel is held in its autumn levels to the transition grade's cold filter plugging point, −10 °C, which Germany sells from October to mid-November (summer's grade B is 0 °C, winter's F −20 °C): a fat's straight C15–C18 paraffins plug a filter near +15 °C whichever fat they came from, and treated tall oil, whose rosin acids become ringed molecules, about ten degrees colder. Jet in 12.5 and 12.6 holds 2% renewable, ReFuelEU's share from 2025. Its six levels: a fat and Alder Point's short hydrogen (12.1), the autumn grade (12.2), which fat in which unit in two markets (12.3: the FCC needs no hydrogen for a fat, but its naphtha and cycle oil do, from the same reformer, and a third of the fat's renewable carbon ends in propylene, LPG and slurry; par leaves it out); tall oil (12.4), co-processed jet (12.5), and pyrolysis oil with all of it (12.6: it barely pays, as each barrel in Brackwater's full FCC is a barrel of gas oil not cracked).

Kestrel Flats (the last chapter, 13; Korbi, October 2026: "another fictional refinery"), an invented small refinery of 27 thousand barrels a day on two domestic crudes, is the other end of the scale from Alder Point and Brackwater: nine units, no jet, no isomerisation, no hydrocracker, nothing to convert the residue. Its crudes are Mesa Shale, a tight-shale crude (API 44, 0.08% sulphur, over half of it boiling below 240 °C, paraffinic, so its gas oils are high in cetane and a little waxy), and Plateau Waxy from an old basin (API 26, 0.22% sulphur, more than half residue, waxy in every cut), run four to one by volume as the plant's pipelines and contracts deliver them. The blend (Kestrel Blend, built in `refinery-data.js` by `blendOf` from the two crudes' own cuts, so the three agree) is a better crude than either: Plateau's light gas oil, 1,196 ppm sulphur, is over heating oil's 1,000 on its own and in the blend it is 867. The works is a crude unit, a vacuum unit, a naphtha hydrotreater, a semi-regenerative reformer with a benzene splitter, one hydrotreater for all its distillates, a small FCC (6 thousand barrels a day at most) with a post-treater for its naphtha, and a sulphur plant. The reformer is the plant's only hydrogen (`h2Import: false`), cleaned by a pressure swing adsorption unit, and what the plant doesn't use goes to fuel gas.

The distillate hydrotreater is a liquid-phase one (`lpHt`, a CoMo catalyst to under 10 ppm): the hydrogen is dissolved in the feed and a recycle of product carries it and the heat, so there is no recycle gas compressor. It takes kerosene, light gas oil, light vacuum gas oil and the FCC's light cycle oil, and the hydrogen it draws rises through them (180, 400, 600 and 1,200 standard cubic feet a barrel), the cycle oil's aromatics taking the most, because oil dissolves only so much; it doesn't touch the wax, so its product keeps its feed's cold flow (that is what 13.4 is about). Kestrel Flats' diesel is the autumn grade of chapter 12 (CFPP −10 °C), with a renewable mandate on top: at least 6% renewable by volume, against EN 590's 7% cap on FAME, so the plan lives between the two, and the premium of diesel over heating oil has to pay for the FAME or the plant sells its diesel as heating oil. Its prices are its own, set so that both the mandate and the waxy crude's discount bind: diesel $102, heating oil $90, VLSFO $70, HSFO $62, FAME $175 a barrel. Petrol is the country's regular grade, RON 91 and MON 81 with aromatics to 42%: the EU grade's 35% is out of reach for a plant with no alkylation and a reformer for its octane.

The six levels: the two crudes and their blend with only the two distillation units (13.1: the choice of crudes, and the blend beats both), naphtha and the hydrogen (13.2), the liquid-phase hydrotreater with kerosene, gas oil and light vacuum gas oil (13.3), wax (13.4: autumn diesel can't be made from Plateau at all, and Mesa's gas oil needs kerosene to pull its plugging point down), the whole works with the FCC and the mandate (13.5), and soybean oil co-processed at a hard 2.5% (13.6). Soybean oil (`sbo`, $132 a barrel bought, refined and bleached to under 5 mg/kg phosphorus and 1% free acids) is a feed of the liquid-phase unit only, up to 2.5% of its feed (`co` and `coTotal` on the unit, which the level can set): the unit was designed for 15% and runs at 2.5% for want of hydrogen and a reactor with room for a fat's carbon oxides. 13.6 has two price decks, which differ in what the law counts: as the rule stands, co-processed content earns nothing towards the mandate (soybean oil's `bio` is 0 in the deck) and par leaves it unbought, the same $6.50 as 13.5; if co-processing counts, each barrel of it replaces a barrel of FAME, par buys it to the cap and earns $6.75. Raising the cap would not help much: the plan's spare hydrogen runs out at about 3.4% of the feed. The level starts from 13.5's par (`running` in `refinery-levels.js`, so rerun `--write-par` twice after changing 13.5) with the soybean oil already in the hydrotreater, so that neither market starts at par: as the rule stands, the oil adds diesel and no FAME, the diesel's renewable share falls to 5.9% under the mandate's 6 and the plant is off spec (−$6.65 a barrel) until the oil goes; where it counts, the plant is in spec at $6.25 and has FAME to cut. It is invented throughout, with no real plant's name or figures, and the hint's roles (`ROLES` in `refinery-practice.js`) make no claim about buying soybean oil.

Every level opens with its lesson (`refinery-lessons.js`), its ideas a step at a time, each with a picture (`refinery-scenes.js`): the crude column with its cuts drawn off at their heights and temperatures, crudes' boiling curves, molecules in skeletal formulae (n-heptane against iso-octane, hexadecane against methylnaphthalene), bars of values with a spec's limit as a line, a blend's parts on a property's scale with where the blend lands against its limit, or a small plant with the units in question lit and running. A picture shows the idea, never the level's answer. A chapter's first level opens with what the chapter is about; the last step is the task, with what to watch out for and how to play folded away. Lesson, in the brief and the menu, opens it again.

The plant is its flowsheet (`refinery-sheet.js`), drawn as a block flow diagram: crude in at the left, products out at the right, each unit in its column of the process (distillation, residue, cracking, treating, octane) and at the height of what it handles, light at the top and heavy at the bottom, as on the crude column itself, which is drawn as a column with each cut leaving it at its own height. Every stream is an arrow as wide as its flow, coloured by what it is (gas, naphtha, middle distillate, heavy oil, residue, renewable), flowing; each unit shows what it's running at against its capacity, each product its volume, its price and its key specs, value by value, ticked or crossed, as many as its box holds: the failing first, then those nearest their limits, and a count of the rest (petrol held to all ten of EN 228's ran far past its box); an off-spec product says what it's sold as instead. The layout comes from the level alone, so nothing moves as the routing changes. Drag to move it, pinch, the wheel or + and − to zoom, a double tap zooms in; tap an arrow and that stream's sheet opens: its volume and properties, then every destination that takes it with a slider and a typed share, All to send the whole stream there, and what sending all of it there would do to the margin. Shares are the point: real refineries split streams all day (heavy naphtha between the reformer and the naphtha market, kerosene between jet and diesel, gas oil cut into fuel oil as far as its sulphur allows), and blending exactly to a limit, with nothing given away, is how they earn. Each destination has a lock (Korbi, October 2026: a stream split three ways, one share to hold while another moves): a locked share stays as it is whatever the others do, a change is taken up by those not locked, no slider passes what the locked shares leave it (its track is brass beyond), one left alone with everything else locked can't move, and All becomes Rest, everything not locked; the spec edges are found along the locked path, and the locks last while the level is open. Tap a unit for its card (purpose, how it works, operating conditions, yields, hydrogen, running cost, and what it's running now), a product for its specification as judged in the level and as published (EN 228, EN 590, Jet A-1, ISO 8217 and the rest) and what's in it, the crude for its assay. A card's streams are rows to tap for their sheets, each in its family's colour with its flow and a line on where it goes ("→ NHT 62% · Naphtha 38%", and "nowhere else" where the level gives it one way out) or where it comes from: where the lines are too thick to tap the one you want, the unit's card is the way to it, and it says at a glance which of a cracker's products are yours to place. The funnel under the zoom buttons opens the lines panel (Korbi, October 2026: a whole refinery's flowsheet is a hundred lines, and most of a cracker's products have only one place to go). Streams, All or With a choice, fades every stream with one destination in the level; Family fades all but one family (gas and LPG, naphtha, distillates, heavy oils, residue, renewables, and cumene and power as other). What fades stays faint, so the plant still reads, and can't be tapped, so the lines left are easy to hit; the stream whose sheet is open is drawn full whatever the panel says, the funnel is lit while anything is faded, and the setting is kept (`refinery:lines`). In 11.3, With a choice fades 23 of the hundred lines, and with Distillates too, 35 are left. Undo takes a change back. Hint goes by how standard a pattern is (Korbi, October 2026: start with the obvious, fine-tune at the end; `refinery-practice.js`). Over the course's 78 optimal plans (par for every level, crude and market) it counts, for every stream and every place it can go, how many plans send it all, most, part or none of it: all 21 plans with alkylate send it to petrol, 38 of 40 the bulk of the light naphtha to the isomerisation unit, 53 of 56 the bulk of the light gas oil to the diesel hydrotreater, 44 of 61 the bulk of the atmospheric residue to the vacuum unit (all of it in 36), and 3 of 78 the bulk of the kerosene to gas oil. What counts as practice is refining's, not the course's (Korbi, October 2026: the statements must make sense in the real world). The course's counts follow its levels: 25 of its plans sink most of the kerosene in fuel oil (cutter for a sour residue, or no treater to take it), where a refinery treats it for jet or diesel and cuts fuel oil with only a share; its alkylation units and coker fill, so only 6 of 24 plans send the bulk of the FCC's C4s to alkylation and 3 of 8 the bulk of the vacuum residue to the coker, both their usual homes; and two plans leaving rapeseed oil unbought make no practice at all. So each way a stream can go has its standing in refining, set by hand (`ROLES`): its home, where it goes as a rule when the unit or market is there; a common outlet; a share up to a limit (a cutter in fuel oil, butenes to a vapour pressure); or seldom right. Buying a renewable feed, and co-processing one in a fossil unit, make no claim. Where the level's choice of crude has a better one than yours, the crude comes first, since no routing of a worse one reaches the level's par. Then, of the ways your plan differs from par, where the bulk of a stream goes comes before how much: refining's standard first, then the common, then what refining has no single answer for, and what it wouldn't do last, each then by how common par's pattern is in the course. A hint says it in refining's terms with the course's count beside it: "Standard practice: 38 of the 40 optimal plans in the course where it can go there do the same (95%)" for the light naphtha to isomerisation, "Standard practice, though only 9 of the 26" for the light vacuum gas oil to the FCC, "Refineries usually send it to the delayed coker; here the HSFO 3.5% pays more" where par passes the coker by, "Refineries usually send only a share there; here more of it pays" for the bulk of the kerosene in fuel oil where a treater could take it, and the count alone where refining makes no claim; how much a share should be (fine-tuning) is the plan's own, and gets the course's count alone. Each hint says too what taking par's row alone comes to here (alone it can cost: it pays with par's other changes). Trims come last, the biggest first, and only while the plan is short of par: par blends to its limits and fills its units, so a few thousandths of a point of heavy vacuum gas oil kept from 8.1's full hydrocracker spill into fuel oil and put it off spec, and three hundredths of a point more butane lift 12.1's petrol over its vapour pressure. Do it takes par's row for the stream. Followed from any level's start, the hint reaches par in at most 22 steps, about 12 on average (`tests/refinery.mjs` follows it). Par's plan lists its rows in the same order, refining's standard first, each with the share of the course's optimal plans that send the bulk of the stream the same way, and marks yours where it differs, to as many decimals as tell them apart. A hint caps the level at two stars, taking par's plan at one.

Off spec (Korbi, October 2026: an off-spec product should show the blend inputs causing it). A product that misses a limit names the streams in it that push it over (`culprits` in the engine). A blend is the weighted mean of its streams' blending indices (by volume, or by mass for a mass property), so a stream's push is its weight times how far its index lies past the limit's, and the streams on the right side give the rest room; the misses are taken furthest first, against the limit (sulphur 613 ppm against 10 before cetane 50.96 against 51). The flowsheet draws the streams with a tenth or more of a miss's push in red, whatever the lines panel fades; the margin's line names the worst miss and where it comes from ("Diesel off spec: sulphur 613 ppm over 10 ppm, from the LGO"); the product's card lists, miss by miss, each stream pushing it, a tap from its sheet, with its value and its share of the excess or the shortfall; and a culprit's own sheet says what it's doing there. Where taking something out would mend it, it says the least that would: the streams that push most a barrel first, each wholly, the last in part ("taking out all 2.4 kb/d of the light gas oil and a trace of the kerosene meets the limit"). Where that would take out more than a quarter of the product, the product's own base stock is what misses (hydrotreated diesel at cetane 50.9), taking out is no remedy, and none is offered. A miss finer than the figures shown is told to as many places as part it from the limit, each compared with the limit at as many: "cetane 50.97 under 51.0" read "51.0 under 51.0", and petrol's "RVP 60.004 kPa over 60 kPa" read "60.0 over 60".

Spec edges (Korbi, October 2026: where a share makes or breaks a product's spec the margin jumps, and that edge is generally where a plan earns most). Along a slider a pool can go on or off spec: past the share where the diesel's CFPP goes over its limit, all of the diesel sells as the next grade down (gasoil, or worse), and the margin falls by dollars a barrel in one step. The sheet finds every such break and marks it on the slider's track, a short fade of green on the side that earns more and red on the side that loses; breaks closer than the eye can part (two treaters each full on either side of par, the pool off spec on both, leave a window a few thousandths of a per cent wide) show as one edge, red both ways round a green line at its best point. Dragged, the thumb moves in whole per cents, but within a fingertip of an edge (three quarters of a rem, 12 px on a phone) that earns more than where the finger is, it stops on the edge, a millionth inside the better side, and a label over it names the product, the grade it sells as if that's another, and the spec that holds there ("diesel: CFPP", "gasoil as VLSFO: flash point"); a share typed in the box is never moved. Only an edge that earns more pulls: in 12.2 the tallow to the diesel hydrotreater has a window between the diesel's FAME (too little tallow and it's over 7%) and its CFPP (too much and it's over −10 °C), and par stands inside it short of the CFPP edge, where the hydrogen runs out, so a drag near that edge from par stays put. Of the 719 shares at par between none and all, 514 sit on an edge: par is mostly made of them. The breaks are found along the slider's own path (its share from none to all, the stream's other destinations scaled to make up the rest, as a drag moves them): 32 samples, then halving to a millionth of the share wherever the plant's state changes between two. The state is every pool's grade and the limits it fails, every unit's capacity and co-processing limits, hydrogen bought and sulphur over: the limits are in it so that a window bounded by two of them is found though no sample lands in it. That's about 90 runs of the plant a slider, and up to 280 (half a second) on the biggest levels, so a worker does it (`refinery-worker.js`), the sliders in use first, each marked as it comes, and again after every change. A drag holds the stream's other shares as they were when it began, so it stays on the path its breaks were found on (dragged to all and back, the other destinations come back as they were). The sheet is built once while it's open and only brought up to date as the plan changes: rebuilt on every move, it replaced the slider under the finger, and a drag across the whole track stopped at 8%. The slider is drawn by the page (a narrow index on a thin track) rather than left to the browser, since the edges are placed by the thumb's width; the index is a fingertip wide to the touch, its sides transparent, as iOS moves a range only by its thumb.

The engine (`refinery-engine.js`) runs the plant as a real one is planned. Every stream carries its blending properties: octane (RON and MON), vapour pressure, aromatics, olefins, benzene, oxygen, ethanol and ethers, cetane, density, sulphur, viscosity, flash point, FAME, renewable content. Each blends linearly in its own index, by volume or by mass: sulphur and viscosity by mass, vapour pressure as RVP to the power 1.25, viscosity by the Refutas index, flash point by the Wickey-Chittenden index (the vapour's, so the lightest part dominates: kerosene at 42 °C can be only about a fifth of a 60 °C blend), the cold filter plugging point by Hu and Burns' cloud point index, the absolute temperature to the 20th power (the waxiest part dominates: a tenth of a fat's paraffins lifts a −12 °C gas oil to about −6), octane by volume. Straight-run cuts take their sulphur from the crude's assay (light naphtha a fiftieth of the crude's, residue half as much again, scaled so the cuts weigh up to the assay exactly) and the residues their density and viscosity. Units run in process order, each taking its share of every feed up to its capacity (what a full unit can't take goes where the stream would have gone without it), carrying their feeds' properties on (a hydrotreater keeps its feed's cetane, density and octane, shifted, and sets its sulphur; a cracker's products are as sour as its feed). Pools are judged on the specs the level sets (the early chapters only the core ones, sulphur and octane or cetane; later all of them) and, in every level, on the limits no product can be sold without: each distillate's flash point (diesel 55 °C, gasoil and fuel oil 60, jet 38), fuel oil's viscosity and density (380 cSt, 991 kg/m³), diesel's density window and its distillation (EN 590's 95% recovered by 360 °C: at most 5% of it boiling above, each cut's share carried through the treaters, so vacuum gas oil desulphurised is still vacuum gas oil), gasoil's cetane, and petrol's vapour pressure at the winter grade's 90 kPa until a level teaches summer's 60. Without them (Korbi, October 2026: par's plans should make sense in the real world), par found plans no refinery could sell: vacuum residue as HSFO with no cutter, at tens of thousands of cSt; "VLSFO" nine-tenths kerosene; petrol a tenth butenes; and, once co-processing's light paraffins made room under diesel's density limit, road diesel a third desulphurised vacuum gas oil (the distillation limit took up to a quarter of a dollar a barrel off 8.2's heavier crudes, and seven cents off 10.4). A level with a sour crude starts with its distillates in the HSFO with the residue, which they cut, so its start is a plan that runs. An off-spec blend sells as the next grade down: petrol as naphtha, diesel as 0.1% gasoil, gasoil as VLSFO, VLSFO as HSFO, what's left as slops; a market of limited size (bitumen) sells its excess the same way. Hydrogen comes from the reformer, then the hydrogen plant up to its capacity, then bought in at half as much again (or not at all, where a level says so); spare hydrogen is burned. A level can offer a feed to buy, at its price (ethanol, ETBE and FAME to blend; imported vacuum gas oil to fill a cracker; used cooking oil to hydrotreat or co-process): what isn't used goes to Not bought, each stream's price refunded, so leaving it costs nothing. Chapter 9's levels price tickets: every renewable blendstock carries the greenhouse gas a barrel of it saves, its energy times how far its carbon intensity sits under fossil fuel's 94 g CO2e/MJ (about 430 kg for HVO from used cooking oil, 415 for SAF, 230 for rapeseed FAME, 215 for ethanol, 100 for ETBE), and a road fuel's pool (diesel, petrol, HVO100) earns that saving at the level's price, $350 a tonne (about where Germany's quota traded in 2026, €310–340); jet earns a quarter more, for SAF's premium. An off-spec pool earns what it's sold as earns, and what a market can't take earns none. Renewable carbon follows its feed: a treater's product carries its feed's share, a cracker spreads it over everything it makes by volume, so the FCC's propane and slurry take theirs and earn nothing. A unit may co-process a renewable feed only up to a share of its own feed (the diesel hydrotreater a tenth, the FCC 7%, about what they take without a revamp); what's over goes where it would have gone without it. The rules behind the lessons are as they stood in 2026: RED III, Germany's quota law passed by the Bundestag in April 2026, ReFuelEU Aviation's shares, ASTM D7566's half for HEFA-SPK and D1655's 5% for co-processed jet, and Delegated Regulation 2023/1640's mass balance checked by carbon-14. The sulphur the treaters and crackers strip goes to the Claus plant, sold at $120 a tonne less its $40 cost, within its capacity. The margin is products less crude, bought-in blendstocks, running costs and hydrogen, plus sulphur, per barrel of crude. Prices are a coherent North-West Europe deck at Brent $75; yields, properties and prices are textbook-typical and labelled so. The coker and bitumen levels (6.3, 6.4) price HSFO at $65, a weak month for sour fuel oil: at $70, cutting vacuum residue into fuel oil earned as much as coking it, and par left the coker idle in its own level.

Par is the LP's plan (`refinery-lp.js`, a two-phase simplex with duals; `solve` in the engine), as a refinery's planners make it. A refinery LP is linear except where a stream's quality depends on the plan (a treater's diesel is as good as the mix of feeds it was given), so it's solved by successive linear programming: at each step the LP carries how each blend's quality would move as its sources move, the plan moves to the solution, every stream's quality is recomputed there, and it solves again; spec rows are elastic at a penalty far above any margin, so an infeasible start walks out. It starts from several plans (the start, every stream into its first unit, seeded random ones) since the blends' nonlinearity has local optima, and exact local moves on the real plant then find what the linear view misses. A plan counts only if every pool really meets its specs when the plant runs it, with a hair of margin inside each limit. Tickets are valued along the whole chain rather than one step back: each stream's saving at what a kilogram of it earns downstream as the plan now routes it (worked back from the pools), every column credited with the saving it moves on at its destination's value, less what it was worth where it was. With the specs' one-step gradient the plan never sent fats to the FCC, since their ticket is earned two units on, when the FCC's naphtha has been through its post-treater into petrol. The duals are the plan's marginal values: what one more barrel of each stream, or one more barrel a day of each unit's capacity, would add to the margin. Levels that teach them (8.3 and 8.4) show them, each stream's sheet with its value and each full unit's card with its capacity's, from par, where they're kept. Par's plan is kept as the shares that actually flowed: where a full unit sent some of its feed back to where it would have gone without it, the plan says so, so following par's plan never leaves a unit overfull (11.5's FCC was, by 8.7 kb/d). The plan kept in `refinery-par.js` is one of the solver's starts when par is worked out again, so a plan once found is never lost to a search that wandered elsewhere (11.5 on Arab Heavy once lost a dollar a barrel that way). Par for every level, crude and market is worked out ahead and kept in `refinery-par.js` (`node tests/refinery.mjs --write-par` writes it; the test fails while it's stale), so a level opens at once.

Stars: three at par (within $0.10/bbl), two within $0.50, one within $1.50, and only with the plant running: every pool in spec, the sulphur plant and hydrogen within their limits. A level of several markets (8.1, petrol strong and diesel strong, each a price deck with its own par) is played in each and holds the fewest stars any market has: the first market done says so, and the level counts once the other is. A level done offers its exam (`refinery-exam.js`): about ten questions on what carries over to any refinery, never on what one plan made (Korbi, October 2026: a figure that holds for one plan teaches nothing to take elsewhere). The level's own questions on its ideas; the bank's (`refinery-facts.js`) on the units, products, specs and bought feeds its plant is about, its chapter's own first (chapter 12's on co-processing), then those it brings in: the cut ranges, each product's limits and which of them usually binds, what each unit does and which unit a situation calls for, typical sizes, yields and operating conditions; the unit that does a job, tapped on the flowsheet, and a cut's path through the units put in order, both on par's plan; a word from the glossary; and Punt's refining bank on the chapter's subjects. Three in four pass; the best is kept. The course is a map of every chapter, folded, the one you're in open, with Continue to the first level not done, each level's line, stars and exam. Progress is `refinery:progress`, your plan on each level `refinery:work` (the sixty most recent), the level you're on `refinery:at`; the old campaign's save is gone.

Chapter 8's crudes are CPC Blend, Brent, Johan Sverdrup, Arab Light, Arab Heavy and Maya: Urals, priced as it traded before 2022, stays only in chapter 1's comparison of sweet and sour, since a lesson whose answer was to buy it would teach a trade the EU's embargo on Russian crude by sea ended in December 2022.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the flowsheet takes the left, all the height under the header, and the strip, the brief and the margin stand beside it.

Together (October 2026): the same level for everyone at the table (README, Head to head), the same crude (or choice of crudes), units and prices, from the same start, each planning on their own phone; the menu's level deals a new match. Par shows as ever, hints don't. Submit is your answer, asked once and final: the highest margin wins, then the quicker. Your own plans, stars and exams stay as they were.

`tests/refinery.mjs` checks the data (units, streams, pools and crudes agree; every producer of a stream runs before every unit that takes it; a pool's streams have every property it's judged on; the cuts' sulphur adds up to each crude's assay), the simplex on textbook LPs with their duals, that every level and crude has an in-spec par above its start and that `refinery-par.js` is still what the solver finds, the chemistry (untreated cuts off spec and treated ones in, the hydrocracker's swell and hydrogen against the FCC's, blending by the indices), what puts a product off spec (untreated gas oil in diesel: the sulphur first, the gas oil all of it, and taking out what it says meeting 10 ppm exactly), that a slider's spec edges are found and each stops on its better side (11.3's window at par, a few thousandths of a per cent wide with no sample in it; 12.2's tallow between the diesel's FAME and CFPP), that every lesson ends in its task and every exam, dealt three times, holds six to twelve questions whose right answers are right and wrong ones wrong, none giving itself away, that every question of the bank is tagged with things that exist (units, products, specs, chapters, feeds bought in), fits some level and gives nothing away, and that a lesson's figure par decides (what a barrel of vacuum gas oil earns in 8.3's hydrocracker) is within $1.50 of par's.
