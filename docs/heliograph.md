# Heliograph
The first of the idle games (October 2026; README, Idle), rebuilt in 3D the same month: `heliograph.html`, `heliograph.js` and `heliograph.css`, its rules in `heliograph-engine.js`, the camera in `heliograph-view.js`, the tube drawn by `heliograph-3d.js` (three.js) or, without WebGL, flat by `heliograph-flat.js` through the same camera. It began on a flat field of gems with `/` and `\` mirrors on a grid; Korbi asked for it in 3D, the grid gone, the light coming from above and the time spent setting mirrors to do something plain to see.

A glass tube on a brass-rimmed plate, open at the top under a frame of spokes. In the middle of the frame a crown on a spindle, skirted in smoked glass, hangs a chandelier: strings of crystals under the places of a hexagon of nineteen, in a shape of its own (a column, a cone longest in the middle, a bowl longest round the edge, a hollow tube, a star, a slant, a spiral; each new chandelier another), so which string reaches the line first and where to cut differ from one to the next; the crystals wear the four enamels, each colour with a cut of its own (round, lozenge, square, hexagonal prism) so they're told apart without their colour. White light falls from a port in the frame beside the crown. Two colour crystals stand low in the tube. Light that passes through a colour crystal takes its colour and the crystal is spent (another appears somewhere, its colour shown coming); light through both carries both colours, drawn as two beams side by side a hair apart, one in each colour (it was once one beam in the last colour taken with a glow in the first, which Korbi found didn't read as both), each colour kept to its side from leg to leg. In the chandelier, light shatters every crystal of a colour it carries and stops at the first of another; white light stops at the first it meets and does nothing. Everything on a string below a crystal shattered falls, so a cut high up brings a long string down. The skirt hides the top two layers from the side: light reaches them only from below, up through the chandelier along a run of its colour. (Korbi found that aiming at the top layer from the side, which brought every string down at once, left no reason to aim anywhere else.) A shot scores ten times the square of the run the light shatters (one 10, five 250) and ten for each crystal that falls: cutting high buys time, but the points are in long runs of a colour, low or across; a chandelier cleared is 1,000 more and a new one hangs.

You build the light's way with up to six mirrors, discs silvered both sides on brass stems from the floor, set anywhere in the tube: under the port, beside a colour crystal, right up against the chandelier to send light up into it or across a layer. Drag one from the tray into the tube: near the light it settles onto it (the light as it would be without that mirror), turned to send the light level toward the middle of the tube; elsewhere it moves on the upright plane facing you. A tap on a mirror chooses it and shows its handle; drag the chosen mirror to move it, drag the handle to turn it (across the screen turns it about the screen's upright, up and down about its level, slowly, so light can be laid on a crystal by hand). There is no aiming by pointing: the light's whole way is drawn as you move and turn, white then in its colours, and every crystal it would shatter is ringed, with the line under the tube saying what Fire would bring down ("Fire brings down 12 (7 falling): +1,440"). Only the chosen mirror moves: a drag that starts on another turns the camera, as from empty space, because a drag meant to look round the tube once moved whatever mirror it began on and spent a move (Korbi lost moves that way). Mirrors stay where they're set from shot to shot. Turning one is free, as often as you like; setting, moving or taking one back is a move, and there are two a shot (the squares in the strip, before the drop's pips), which is what makes it a puzzle: the light's way has to be rebuilt for the new colour crystals with two changes and as much turning as you need. The first shot has three, since the tube is empty and the light wants a mirror under the port, one past a colour crystal and often a third to reach into the chandelier; a game kept on its first shot from before then got its third when it opened. A mirror set or moved this shot moves again free, and taking back one set this shot gives its move back. Take back returns the chosen mirror to the tray. Only Fire takes a turn. A turn can be passed for nothing but the shot: turn a mirror so the light misses both colour crystals (turning is free) and fire, and the white light shatters nothing and spends no colour crystal, while the moves come back and the chandelier's count goes on. That's how a way needing more moves than one shot has is built without spending the colour crystal it passes. A Skip button did the same for a day (October 2026) and went, since it added nothing a free turn of a mirror doesn't. A drag on empty space turns the camera round the tube and tips it a little up or down.

Every six shots the chandelier comes down a layer, a crystal more at the top of every string of its shape (every five after 30 shots, four after 70, three after 120, two after 180; the strip's pips count down); a mirror it comes down on goes back to hand, and a colour crystal in its way moves. A crystal reaching the line low on the glass ends the game. The score and your best are in the strip, the best kept as `heliograph:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the game in play is kept (`heliograph:run2`) and opens again where it was. The flat game's run and best (a different scale of score) were cleared once when the 3D one first opened (`heliograph:v2`). A first game's first shots say what to do next in the line under the tube. Held sideways the tube is on the left with all the height, the score, the line and the tray beside it.

Why these numbers: played out by simple players (`plan` in the engine sets a mirror under the port turned to a colour crystal and one beyond it turned into the chandelier), keeping to two moves a shot, one setting no mirror is overrun in 30 to 40 shots (sooner under a deep shape), and so is one picking a crystal of the colour at random, since every string must be kept short and a random cut seldom shortens the longest; one taking the best of four lasts about 55 to 80 shots, and one taking the best-scoring shot each turn about 140 and scores about 10,000. Before the skirt and the new scoring that best player cut at the top layer in more than a third of its shots; now its cuts spread down the chandelier, and the colours of a string are less often the same from one crystal to the next (a fifth of the time, from two fifths), so a run straight up to the top is rarer. Strings rather than a lattice joined every way: joined every way almost nothing fell, a shot brought down two or three crystals, and the best player was overrun as fast as one doing nothing. The colour crystals stand clear of the glass so light through one has room to run on to a mirror beyond it. The engine is pure (the light is a ray reflected off mirror discs, R − 2(R·n)n, tested against spheres for the crystals, the crown's disc, the glass, the floor), and every random thing (the chandelier, each new layer, the colour crystals) comes from the game's seed and the number of the draw, so a game opened again goes on as it would have; the drawing is on demand, still when nothing moves.

`tests/heliograph.mjs` checks the deal from a seed (a chandelier in a shape, each string as deep as the shape has it, games of different shapes, two colour crystals of colours the chandelier has, clear of the glass and the chandelier), the light (from the port to the floor; reflection; off a mirror at 45° level to the glass; turned or moved out of the light; taking a crystal's colour; across a layer through five crystals of its colour and out; stopped by another colour; white light doing nothing; through both colour crystals carrying both colours and shattering either), a shot (everything below on the strings falling, the points for the run and for each fall, the crystals spent and replaced), the skirt (level light at the top layers stopped by it, lower down running through; no mirror against it), where a mirror may stand, the moves a shot (three on the first and a fourth mirror refused, one set this shot moving again free, turning always free, a mirror set this shot taken back giving its move back, two after the first shot), a shot turned away from the colour crystals (nothing shattered, no colour crystal spent, the moves back, the chandelier's count going on), the chandelier coming down (a crystal more on every string of the shape and none elsewhere, a mirror under it back in hand, the end, the pace), a cleared chandelier, a game saved and opened again, firing leaving the game passed in as it was, and whole games: no mirrors overrun in under 50 shots, a glance lasting longer, the best shot longer still and every game ending.
