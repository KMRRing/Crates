# Loom
Weaving as a puzzle that stays calm (23 October 2026; README, Idle): `loom.html`, `loom.js` (the page the two games share, and the menu) and `loom.css`. Two games on one loom: *the cloths* (1 November 2026, below), a nonogram of floats, and *the draft* (23 October), the first Loom, kept as it was: `loom-draft.js` plays it, on `loom-engine.js` (no page: the tests run it in Node) and `loom-draw.js`; `loom-kit.js` is what they share (what is saved, the sounds, the dialog a finished cloth opens). Korbi, of Thimble: "entirely unfun and linear ... something on fabrics: weaving and/or knitting", then "replace thimble with weaving". Thimble's mending was a line of steps with a grade for how near you tapped; nothing in it was decided by the player. Weaving has something to work out, and the same few ideas make every cloth, so playing it teaches how a loom is set up (a weaver's *draft*).

I like Loom, I'm just wondering: is it too similar to nonogram to justify it being a separate app. Maybe loom should be the pretty 3D version of the game, as each game should have"). The cloths are a nonogram of floats on Nonogram's own solver (below), so Loom stops being a tile of its own and becomes Nonogram's *calm 3D mode*: the cloths and their layers (colour, shafts, gold thread) as a kind of picture in Nonogram's menu, drawn as woven cloth that grows row by row, beside Nonogram's plain mode as the pure puzzle (README, Classics: each classic gets one of each). Its ladder (26 cloths in four chapters), stars and saves move with it; the `loom:` keys are kept so nothing is lost. Not built: it is a merge of two games' pages and menus, and the 3D drawing is new work.


**The cloths** (1 November 2026). Korbi, after the draft had been played: "a nonogram-like puzzle is I think the way to go. Different thread materials/colours ... a fun way to add layers to nonogram and theme it in a loom style". What is solved is a cloth's *face*. At every crossing of a warp thread (a column) and a pick of weft (a row) exactly one yarn lies over the other and shows. A yarn that stays over several crossings in a row is a *float*, and a line's clue is the lengths of its own yarn's floats, in order, as a nonogram's: "3 1 2" on a pick is its weft showing over three crossings, then one, then two. The two brushes (Warp, Weft) paint what shows; every clue met is the cloth. The line logic is the nonogram's own (`nonogram-engine.js`'s `settle` and `overlap`, so it is tested there), which is why every cloth is solved by deduction, not by guessing; the rest is how the loom adds layers to it:
- **Colour and weave.** The clue counts the colour that shows, not the yarn. Where a warp thread and a pick are one colour the crossing shows that colour whichever is over, so it is *settled* before you start (drawn with a brass dot) and a float runs on across it. That is how a tartan's stripes and a houndstooth's blocks come to carry a cloth's floats: the colours are free information, the structure is what you work out.
- **Shafts.** A weaver's draft ties crossings together: threads on one *shaft* rise together, picks lifted alike are alike. A puzzle woven on a named weave carries its shaft numbers (above the columns, beside the rows), and a crossing then stands for every crossing with the same pair of numbers: one tap sets them all. Plain weave on two shafts is only four crossings to find, whatever the size; twill, herringbone, satin, diamond and rosepath have sixteen to twenty-five, and their pattern repeats the way the real weave does. The weaves are written down as a weaver writes them (`WEAVES` in `loom-levels.js`: the tie-up, the threading, the treadling), with the starting point, the direction and the colours of the tie-up taken from the seed.
- **Gold thread.** A third yarn laid over the cloth: where it lies it hides both, so a clue lists gold's floats (gilt chips) among the line's own, in the order they come, and a third brush paints it. The gold chapter's cloths are never woven in ochre, which would look like it.

The ladder is four chapters, 26 cloths (`LEVELS` in `loom-levels.js`): *Plain cloth* (six, 5×5 to 12×12, two yarns and a float), *Weaves* (eight named weaves, each with its story), *Colour and weave* (gingham, pinstripe, houndstooth, a tartan, madras, a Glen check), *Gold thread* (brocade to the Field of the Cloth of Gold). Every cloth is made from a seed (`makeLevel(level, seed)`: the first cloth of a level is canonical, the same for everyone, and *a fresh one* is another seed) by rejection: the cloth is woven at random in the shape the level's recipe names, the clues are checked for exactly one answer (`count`, the line solver and then a search), and where they have two the level may pre-weave a crossing or two (drawn like a settled one) until they have one. The logic the cloth needs is graded 1 (the overlap rule alone finishes it), 2 (full line logic), 3 (it needs a guess), and a level that asks for 2 or more keeps trying until it gets it. The honest limit: the colour and weave chapter is easy, since settled crossings and shaft ties give so much away (its cloths grade 1 however big); its length is the challenge, not its logic. On the canonical cloths, the sampler (plain 6), hopsack, twill and the last two gold cloths need a guess; the rest fall to line logic.

Each cloth opens the next; the first of each chapter is always open, so the other chapters can be tried at once. Stars are for how clean the cloth was: a crossing painted the wrong way is a *slip* (marked with a red cross in the cloth and said in the line below it, unless the menu's option is off, and counted either way), and a *hint* is a help; none of either is three stars, up to three of them together two, finishing one. A hint fills in the crossing that one line's logic settles from what you have set right (so a wrong crossing never misleads it), brackets the line and the crossing, and says why ("Pick 3 reads 5 2, so at thread 4 the weft lies over"); when no line settles anything alone it says the lines together do, or that a guess is wanted. Tapping a crossing that already shows the brush clears it, a drag carries the brush along, and a stroke is one Undo (the slip stays counted). A line's clue dims once the line is whole and right. A finished cloth is woven out row by row, the shuttle crossing it, and a dialog gives the slips, the hints, the crossings to set and the crossings pre-woven, with the cloth's story.

The page: `loom-cloth.js` draws one canvas that fills its box (clue chips in each line's own yarn, the shaft numbers, every crossing as the yarn over the other with a shadow and an end that dips where the next crossing is the other yarn's, an unset crossing as the two yarns laid thin), `loom-play.js` takes the fingers and keeps the work, `loom-float.js` is the puzzle and its solver and `loom-levels.js` the ladder, all four without a page except the first two. The sizes are the screen's: cells and chips grow with the root font on a tablet and the cloth fills the bench; sideways, the buttons stand in the side column. A cloth of 16 columns is about 19 px a crossing on a phone, and the clue chips never go under 14 px.

**The draft.** The first Loom, now the menu's second game. A cloth is shown to you (the target, drawn thread by thread over and under, with the yarns it uses) and you build the draft that weaves it, in three tables, the way a handloom is set:
- **Threading** (a row under the target): each warp thread, the lengthwise ones, goes through one *shaft*, a frame that lifts its threads together. Tap the shaft a thread goes through.
- **Lifting plan** (a column of picks beside the cloth): for each *pick*, one pass of the weft across, tap the shafts to lift. A warp thread whose shaft is lifted shows on the face, over the weft; the others sink under it.
- **Yarn:** the weft's yarn for each pick, tapped through the yarns the cloth uses.

The cloth you are weaving is drawn as you set it (the face is the warp yarn where the thread's shaft is lifted, else the weft's), and every place that differs from the target carries a red cross, with a count ("32 of 64 right") so a change is seen at once; the menu can turn the crosses off for the stricter game. The draft is not unique: any threading and lifting that weave the same face are right (a plain weave woven on four shafts is as good as on two), so the cloth is what is checked, never the draft. Painting a stroke across cells sets them all, and a stroke is one undo.

The eight cloths, each opening when the one before has a star: plain weave (two shafts, over one under one), hopsack (blocks of two), twill (a diagonal, four shafts), gingham (warp and weft stripes of two yarns crossing in checks), herringbone (the twill turned back on itself, ten threads), satin (five shafts; one thread in five shows the other yarn in each pick, never two side by side, so the face is smooth), diamond (a point draft, the threading run up and down again) and houndstooth (a two-colour twill, in broken checks). Each is made by the draft that names it, with the shafts, the stripe positions, the yarns and the turning points taken from the seed, so no two plays are the same cloth. `makeJob(index, seed)` keeps the answer draft hidden and works out the *par*, the taps that draft takes from a bare loom (one for each thread, each shaft lifted, and each pick's yarn after the first).

The grade is stars, by taps against par: up to a third above par (and two taps) is three, up to twice par (and four) two, any finish one; it is a puzzle of knowing the weave, not of speed, so there is no clock. A finished cloth is woven out row by row, the shuttle crossing it, and a dialog gives the taps, par, how many shafts the draft needed and how many picks it takes to repeat, with a line on what the weave is for ("Over one, under one: the cloth of a shirt"). *A fresh one* weaves the same kind again from a new seed; *Next cloth* moves on.

Saved state is under `loom:`: `loom:track` (the cloths or the draft), `loom:run2` (the cloth in hand: its level, seed, crossings as a string, slips, hints, brush and result, so another device can watch it), `loom:camp` (the stars by cloth), `loom:run` and `loom:jobs` (the draft's run and stars), `loom:best` (the stars of both, for the games screen) and `loom:opts` (the crosses, sound). Sounds are made as they play, like Slipway's. Nothing has a clock, so there is nothing to pause beyond the drawing, which stands still while the menu is open (a cloth being woven out when the menu opens is finished when it closes). A partner's watching frame draws all of it from those keys and its taps go nowhere.

`tests/loom.mjs` checks the drawdown (a cell shows the warp where its shaft is lifted); that all eight cloths build the same from a seed, that the hidden draft weaves the target and costs the par, that the cloth fits the screen's grid and uses two yarns at least; that each cloth is what it's named for over sixty seeds (plain is a checker, hopsack goes in pairs, a twill moves one thread along each pick, a herringbone's diagonal runs both ways, satin shows two places in ten, a diamond reflects, houndstooth is four and four, gingham stripes of two); setting and undoing; the stars; and that other drafts weaving the same face are accepted.

`tests/loom-float.mjs` checks the cloths: a clue is the lengths of a yarn's floats (gold's listed among them, and a float running across settled crossings), the solver and its hints (following them from a bare cloth gets to the cloth, every one right), that a shaft ties crossings into one variable and a settled crossing joins none, that every level of the ladder makes a cloth from any seed whose clues have exactly one answer, the same from the same seed, with the grade it reports and no more pre-woven crossings than it allows, within a few seconds, that the recipes use only yarns they list, that a named weave is what it is called, the stars, and that every id the page's scripts look up is in `loom.html`.

Not yet: knitting, which Korbi named too (a stitch is a loop rather than a crossing, so it is another game, not another cloth), a free loom to design on, and cloths with more than eight shafts. For the cloths: harder cloths for the colour chapter (colour and shafts only give information, so the way to difficulty is smaller clues, not more), chapters that combine the layers (shafts and gold, colour and gold: a "grand cloths" chapter), a cloth shown woven in three dimensions like Bridges' as the reward for finishing one, and a first cloth of the day. Generating a cloth takes up to about 0.4 s on a computer for the biggest gold cloths and will take longer on a phone, with nothing on the screen meanwhile. Seen at 390×664, 390×844, 820×1180 and 1180×820, in all three themes, day and night; played by real taps in a browser, not yet on a phone. The draft's cells are 17 to 28 px at the smallest, so a tap is forgiven by a slop of 15% of a cell.
