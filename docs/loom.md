# Loom
Now the Cloth mode of Nonogram (4 November 2026; `docs/nonogram.md`, Cloth): `loom.html` only redirects to `nonogram.html?mode=cloth`. Weaving as a nonogram of floats (`loom-mode.js`, `loom.css`; the cloths are `loom-levels.js` (26 in four chapters: plain cloth, weaves, colour and weave, gold thread), `loom-float.js` (the puzzle and its solver, on `nonogram-engine.js`'s line logic), `loom-thread.js`, `loom-cloth.js`, `loom-play.js` and `loom-kit.js`). At every crossing of a warp thread (a column) and a pick of weft (a row) one yarn lies over the other and shows; a *float* is a yarn showing over several crossings in a row, and a line's clue is the lengths of its own yarn's floats. Every cloth is made from a seed and has exactly one answer; stars are for a clean cloth (no slip, no hint), a *slip* being a crossing set the wrong way.

## Traps
- Only the plain chapter is laid as threads (`threaded(p)`): a shaft ties crossings together, so a float in one column would be floats in others, and gold hides both yarns, so a single float is neither. Weaves, colour and gold keep their brushes.
- A float is not stored. The cells are all there is and a float is a run of warp cells in a column, so floats laid end to end are one (which is how a clue counts them); the clues, solver, grading and saved cloth are what they were when plain cloths were painted.
- `loom:run2` keeps crossings as characters ("0" warp over, "1" weft, "2" gold, "-" undecided); a plain cloth keeps in the same ones, so a run begun when it was painted opens as floats (warp painted is a float, anything else weft), and an older copy of the game reads a laid cloth as painted. A thread waiting for its end is not saved. `loom:track`, `loom:run` and `loom:jobs` were the cut draft's and are left unread.
- Tapping a float with nothing waiting pulls out the whole float, not the tapped end (a tap cannot say which end); to shorten or split one, drag along it. To extend one, start on the weft beside it.
- A plain cloth has lost *unknown*: an undecided crossing shows the weft, as a worked-out one does. So a hint skips steps that would only say "the weft stays" and goes to the first that lays, extends, joins, shortens, splits or pulls out a float; and weft you wrongly believed is no slip, only unfinished. What the player has worked out and what is merely undecided can't be told apart in the cloth: that is what the notes are for (next line).
- A plain cloth's notes (`run.notes`, `run.mode`: extra fields of `loom:run2` that an older copy ignores) are the player's pencil: a vertical stroke for a warp thought to come up, a horizontal one for the weft known, in an array apart from the cells, so no slip, hint, grade or star reads them. Only a clue's darkening counts them, as laid (`asMarked`: warp notes are warp, everything else weft, since the field starts weft), right or wrong: it is a display of the player's marks, not a verdict. They sit only where the weft shows: a laid float takes a note away in its own Undo step, and a float or a pre-woven crossing refuses a new one. The mouse's right button pencils weft in any mode.
- Not every cloth falls to deduction. The colour chapter is easy by construction (settled crossings and shaft ties give so much away that its cloths grade 1 however big; its length is its challenge), and on the canonical cloths the Sampler (plain 6), Hopsack, Twill and the last two gold cloths need a guess.
- Making a cloth by rejection takes up to about 0.4 s on a computer for the biggest gold cloths, and longer on a phone, with nothing on the screen meanwhile.

## Not yet
Knitting (a stitch is a loop, not a crossing: another game), a free loom to design on, cloths on more than eight shafts, the other chapters laid as threads (a float on a weave could set every column on the same shaft; gold could be a third gesture), harder colour cloths (smaller clues, since colour and shafts only give information), chapters that combine the layers, a finished cloth shown woven in 3D like Bridges', and a first cloth of the day.
