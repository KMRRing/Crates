# Sic Bo
Sic Bo (骰寶) as Macau deals it (October 2026): `sicbo.html`, `sicbo.js` and `sicbo.css`, its rules in `sicbo-engine.js`, the layout drawn by `sicbo-layout.js` (the page bets on it and the course shows it), the course in `sicbo-course.js` on the stage `sicbo-stage.js`, and the rules on one page in `sicbo-rules.js`. The die (ivory, the one's pip large and red and the four's red too, as Liar's dice draws it) is in `casino-dice.js` and `casino-dice.css`, new files the dice games after it (Craps) share, so that `casino.js` and `casino.css` stayed as they were; the roll's tumble (`tumble`) is there too, and the strip above the dice leaves room for the newest ball's ring. Sic Bo's tile and its Modern and Kontor logos are in `apps.js`.

The game, by the gaming regulator's rules for it (the DICJ's Despacho 57/2004): three dice under a cup, which a croupier (莊荷) covers, shakes and rings the bell on, and the bets the rules list. Small (小, the total 4 to 10) and Big (大, 11 to 17) pay 1 to 1 and a triple takes both (通吃, Article 7); a single number (三軍) pays 1, 2 or 3 to 1 for one, two or three dice showing it; a total (點數) pays 50 on 4 or 17, 18 on 5 or 16, 14 on 6 or 15, 12 on 7 or 14, 8 on 8 or 13 and 6 on 9 to 12 (no total of 3 or 18 but as a triple); a double (短牌) 8, two numbers (長牌) 5, a named triple (圍骰) 150, any triple (全骰) 24. Nothing is rounded: the house's edge on every bet is worked out over all 216 ways the dice fall, and every bet keeps its own: 2.78% on Small and Big (6 triples in 216), 7.87% on a single number, 9.72% on a 7 or 14, 12.5% on a 10 or 11, 16.67% on two numbers, 18.98% on a 9 or 12, 29.17% on a 4 or 17, 30.09% on a named triple, 30.56% on any triple, 33.33% on a double and 47.22% on a 5 or 16. That is the point of the game, against roulette's one 2.70% for every bet. For the totals on 5 or 16 and 6 or 15 the rules give a range (18 to 30 and 14 to 18 to 1); this table pays the least of each, and the rules page says what the most would keep (13.89% and 12.04%). The rules list three bets more that no layout seems to carry, a double with a different single (50 to 1), three numbers (30) and any three of four (7), and 單 and 雙, an odd or even total at 1 to 1; since Article 7 takes only Big and Small on a triple, 單 and 雙 as written would be a fair coin (108 throws in 216 are odd), so none of these is played, and the rules page names them and works out what each keeps. A round the croupier finds with a die resting on another is void (無效) and thrown again at once (Article 3). The table: at least HK$100 on each bet, in HK$50s; the most HK$100,000 on Small or Big, HK$50,000 on a single number, HK$20,000 on two numbers, HK$10,000 on a double or a total, HK$5,000 on any triple and HK$2,000 on a named triple. The rules set no limits and no table's were found, so these are the game's own (a stake whose win stays a good deal under the most a table could pay out). You start with HK$20,000.

The page: the strip has what you hold, the table's minimum and the round. Under it the screen over the table: the last twenty totals as coloured balls, newest first and the latest ringed (Big red, Small blue, a triple green), and what the last hundred came to (Big, Small, triples, the three hottest totals and the three coldest). Under that the tray with the three dice and the croupier's call: the total, Big, Small or the triple, and what the round paid you. The layout, as big as the room left allows, in the order a Macau table lays it out: Small, Any triple and Big across the head; the six triples and six doubles; the totals, 4 to 10 under Small and 11 to 17 under Big; the fifteen pairs of numbers; the six single numbers at the foot, nearest the player. Each cell shows its dice and what it pays; a tap puts the chip on it, with its amount at the cell's corner and a toast saying what it bet and what it pays. On a short phone the layout scrolls inside its own box rather than shrinking a cell below a thumb. Held sideways the layout takes the left of the screen and the screen, the tray, the chips and the buttons the right. The rail: the chips (HK$50 to HK$5,000), Clear, Undo (the last chip; Rebet, the last round's bets, when the layout is empty) and Roll. Roll: the dice tumble in the tray and land one after another, then the cells they win are ringed and every bet's corner says what it won or lost. Menu: a new table (the screen starts again), Top up, the course, Sic Bo on one page, quick rolls. The game is kept as it stands (`sicbo:run`: your bankroll, the bets on the layout, the rounds so far), the chip and quick rolls in `sicbo:opts`, the course in `sicbo:course`. The screen is dark by day and by night, as an electronic one is; the lettering of Big, Small and triples is lighter at night.

The course: the dice (three dice and the 216 ways they fall, with the totals as a chart; the cup, the bell, the call to open the dice and the void round); the bets (Big and Small and why a triple takes both; single numbers; totals; doubles and pairs; triples; the rules' others); what the house keeps (each bet its own edge from least to most, what 100 rounds of HK$100 on Big cost, what the same bet keeps at the top of the rules' range; the screen and why a run on Big is no likelier to go on); on the floor (screens and machines, and the words, Cantonese with Jyutping). Where it speaks of Macau it rests on the DICJ's rules and on Inside Asian Gaming's May 2011 report that Sands China's Rapid Sic Bo, live croupier and electronic bets, started a row because electronic sic bo could pay up to 190 to 1 on any triple while a live table's most is 150, and that the regulator moved every automated table onto live pay-outs. Not found, and so not claimed: any Macau table's limits, the layout's printed Chinese beyond the rules' own words, and what the Venetian's tables pay on the totals within the rules' ranges.

`tests/sicbo.mjs` checks the dice (216 ways, the totals' ways, a 3 and an 18 only as triples), every bet the layout carries and the rules' others, what each pays by the regulation's articles, that a triple takes Big and Small and nothing else, every bet's edge worked out by hand, the ranges, which bets may go down, a round from its seed and 21,600 of them landing as three fair dice do, and the screen's counts. `tests/sicbo-course.mjs` checks every answer, payout, edge and count a lesson states against the engine, every bet a picture puts on the layout, and every throw it shows.
