# Mines
Minesweeper by its usual rules, on fields proven to clear by reasoning alone from a marked start (no guess, ever). Difficulty is the reasoning a field takes (`mines-solve.js`), not only its size. Flat fields are squares and hexes (the classics); 3D puts the field on the Moon, in space, as a shape on a sphere. There is a field a day, a computer partner, and play together as one team or a race.

**Traps.**
- A start is a cell with a whole neighbourhood (`most` neighbours: the commonest count on a solid, all of a flat shape's), never one of a solid's odd cells; the generator lays no mine on it or round it.
- The solver reads only who touches whom, so any graph works. A solid has no edge, where flat fields stick, so it carries more mines than its flat tiling for the same reasoning: `MINES` in `mines-engine.js`, measured on samples (`tests/mines.mjs` keeps the bands honest). The rhombicuboctahedron takes the squares' bands, the football the hexes' (on hexes two numbers hardly ever prove more than one, so Hard there is the densest that still clears, and its "deeper" is the mines-left count).
- Solids (`mines-solid.js`): cube n×n per face (4, 7, 9); rhombicuboctahedron every face cut m×m (2, 3, 4: 104, 234, 416 cells, the triangles cut into m² triangles); football GP(g,g), g 2, 3, 4 (122, 272, 482 cells; GP(1,1) is the truncated icosahedron itself, 32 faces, too few). The football's points come from a lattice laid on each icosahedron face and its Delaunay triangulation (convex hull), then smoothed; exactly twelve pentagons, tested. The cube's lattice goes through `tan` before the push onto the sphere, or its corner cells are four times smaller than the middle ones.
- Corners are named by their coordinates to seven places: two faces' shared point must come out as one name, or cells stop touching across an edge.
- 3D is dark in every theme: `mines-3d.js` paints its own sky and palette (the page's tokens would put a dark number on a dark moon), the card goes dark (`.mn-card.moon`) and `#app` takes `plate` so the menu button reads. Hand-drawn 2D canvas, no WebGL or SVG filter: nothing to go wrong on iPhone Safari; only Chromium could be checked.
- A cell seen edge-on is a sliver: a tap on one turns it to face you instead of digging.
- Today's field is the week's table in `WEEK` (Thursday: small cube); always alone.
- Saved: `mines:run`, `mines:view` (the turn as a quaternion), `mines:times` (by shape:size:level), `mines:prefs` (`shape`: square, hex, cube or football; an older file's `solid` and `body` are read into it, the rhombicuboctahedron as the cube), the size and level step `mines:ramp`. An old save of a retired shape fails `valid()` and is dropped; a retired pref falls back to squares or the cube.

**Not done.** Minesweeper Flags (turn-taking on one field). A computer player at a table. A table of three or more as one team is tested in its parts, not played through. Untried on a real phone: the hold and the pinch on iOS Safari.
