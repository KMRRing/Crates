# Parley
Four beginner courses from an English speaker's side: Chinese, French, German and Spanish, on the pile's schedule. A course is units of about 14 words, each unit opening with its grammar pattern (three genders and *sein*; the accusative *den*; verb second; the partitive *du / de la*; *aller* and *au*; measure words and 块; 在 and 去; 了) and closing, once all its words are met, with a short text written only from words learned so far, read aloud by the phone, with three questions. Every word card carries the word with its article (French and German) or its characters and pinyin with tone marks (Chinese), the meaning, an example sentence, and a button that speaks it; a new word is shown on its card, spoken, and drilled at once. Study brings what's due first, then up to 12 new words a day, unit by unit. Every unit is open: Learn on its row teaches all of its words not yet met, whatever the day's count (choosing a unit is choosing the pace), its pattern opens from the row any time, and Read appears once its words are all met; a reading reached by jumping ahead says it may use words from units not yet finished.

A word is drilled harder as it climbs the piles: recognise it (word and voice, pick the meaning); pick it by ear (voice only), or for Chinese name the tone of its first syllable; produce it (type the word, accent-tolerant, and tap its article for a gendered noun; for Chinese type the pinyin with tones, ni3 hao3 or nǐ hǎo, and the sounds right but the tones wrong is a miss with a note); fill it into its sentence from four choices. The right answer sits anywhere among the choices, and no wrong one means the same or sounds the same (a Chinese homophone can't be told apart by ear). Right moves it up a pile, wrong drops it to the first, as everywhere; Deck reviews Parley's cards too. Six units each for now: hello, numbers and time, eating and drinking, places and going, shopping, work and trade (oil, prices, the boss). `parley-courses.js` is the content, written by hand and meant to grow; the German course is for a learner from English.

The page fits the screen: the units list, a drill's card and a reading (its passage and questions together) scroll inside themselves. The voice lives in `voice.js`, which Deck uses too when it reviews Parley's words.

A study session is kept as it goes (October 2026): a reload, or another device on your solo code, opens the card you were on, and a card already answered comes back with its verdict and Next, its answers locked so it can't count twice. A partner watching sees the same card (it used to see your home screen), silently. Changing course ends the session.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the course and Study stand on the left and the units beside them; a card on the left and its answers beside it; the reader keeps to a reading line's width.

- Traps: a word's example must hold the word as a whole token (the cloze gaps its first match, so `euro` in `euros` breaks it); two words that differ only by an accent (qué, que) are kept apart in options by comparing without accents; typed articles per course are in `ARTICLES` in `parley.js`.
