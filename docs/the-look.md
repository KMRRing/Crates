# The look

Brass instruments on a codex's page, with an Art Deco hand: the precision of a sextant or an astrolabe (brass on navy, engraved scales, enamelled detail) set the way the 1930s set gold on black, in wide geometric capitals and fine double rules, as in an ocean liner's chart room or Griffith Observatory. Light is the page: parchment (`--yard`), cream paper for cards and dialogs (`--sheet`), navy ink (`--ink`), brass for lines, frames and scales (`--brass`). Dark is the instrument itself: a night-navy dial, parchment lettering, bright brass. The four crate colours are enamels: ochre, verdigris (copper's green patina), cobalt and oxblood, and every game's own accent is retuned to an enamel of its colour, with its soft fill mixed from it and the paper (`color-mix`), so both schemes follow from one value.

All of it lives in `style.css`'s tokens; a game names colours only for what is its own. Headings (a game's name in its header, dialog titles, menu headings, crate names, the tiles on the games screen) are Archivo at its widest (`--wide`, 125%), in spaced capitals: the font the suite already serves offline, so the look costs no download. A header sits on a graduated rule, a hairline with ticks every nine pixels, like an instrument's scale. Buttons are brass-framed with square corners; a primary action is a plate, navy lettered in gilt on the page and brass lettered in navy on the dial (`--plate`, `--plate-ink`), with a gilt hairline inside its edge, and every game's main action button is that plate. Tiles are paper with a brass edge, and a picked tile wears a brass frame. A game's stage (the card it plays on: Chart's map card, Quote's question, Manifest's floor, a card of Punt's Wine level) is a panel with square-ish corners framed in a double rule, a brass edge and a hairline four pixels inside it, drawn as an inset shadow so it sits under the content and never crosses a map or a board; Cartel's felt table has a gilt rim and a gilt line inside it. Category labels are tags, not pills: small rectangles in the game's enamel, lettered in the same wide capitals; boxes, options and inputs take a tile's corners, and the only round things left are dots, discs and round icon buttons. Every game's logo is its object drawn as the instruments are (README, the games screen): navy, brass, parchment and its enamel. Each logo's contour is drawn at its width times `--wire`: 1 in a game's header and on the games screen, 1.5 on the selected game (the one you're in, or a tile under your finger or pointer), the contour thickening as it's chosen while the engraved detail keeps its fine weight.

The logo, the menu button and the loader are the Codex mark, and loading is drawn with it (`loading.js`; README, "The mark"). Whenever a page or a long task loads, the mark turns large in the middle of the screen, the task's words under it: while a page loads (it fades in only after a moment, so a page that's ready at once never shows it; it lets taps through, so a page can never be locked behind it), while Delta deals a hard board, while Crates sets up or fetches a synced run, and while Codex updates itself. A wait with stages says where it has got to (October 2026): under the mark's words a bar fills as it goes, a glint running along it so a slow stage never looks stuck, and under the bar the stage in plain words (joining a table, a party or a partner, opening a party, an update downloading: README, Rooms and Offline). Whatever does a stage says it (`progress(words, at)`, `at` how far along the whole wait is, from 0 to 1, the bar never going back), so every game shows them without code of its own. A short wait inside a line or a box shows the tiny mark, turning (`loader-small.svg`): a dictionary lookup, a reconnecting room, a toast that says it's still working (one ending in "…"), and a picture on its way. `busy(label)` returns the function that ends a wait; `waitMark()` is the tiny one as an element.

Four themes, chosen in Settings (More, Settings), each with a day and a night that follow the phone unless set: Codex (above, the default), Modern (Codex as it was before its Art Deco dress), Kontor (dense and technical in an office's green, with line icons) and Office. Office looks like Outlook: Microsoft's Fluent design, light greys and white surfaces by day and charcoal at night, one blue (`#0F6CBD`, `#479EF5` at night) for what can be pressed and what's selected, Segoe UI in sentence case at 14 px, hairline borders, 4 px corners and dialogs with a soft shadow, no ornaments and no marks: the games screen is names with thin monochrome line icons, and each game's board (Pipes' tiles, Harbour's chart and 3D skin, Cartel's table, the chess board) has its Office colours by day and by night. The themes beyond Codex live in `themes.css`; `theme.js` sets the theme on the page before it draws. At night the suite's dropdown (`dropdown.js`) reads in all four (October 2026): Modern's logo tints stay light, so its dropdown takes a deep face of the logo's edge, lettered in the ink, and Kontor and Office keep Codex's deep face, lettered in the ink. Until then the three beyond Codex lettered it in the logo's tint on a face of the same tint, so a choice like Refinery's crude couldn't be read at night.
