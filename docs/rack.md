# Rack

A word game on a board for two to four (31 October 2026, Korbi: "rack"; "scowl perfect" when asked which word list it would use; built 1 November), against the computer or at a table with friends on their own phones (README, Seats). A 15 by 15 board and a bag of 100 tiles, two of them blanks; each player holds seven. A turn lays tiles in one row or one column, so that the word joins the tiles already down and every other word the new tiles make is a word as well, or swaps tiles with the bag, or passes. The first word covers the star. A tile scores its value, a word the sum of its tiles'; a new tile on a double or triple letter (2L, 3L) counts twice or three times, one on a double or triple word (2W, 3W, the star a 2W) doubles or triples the whole word, and a square counts only the turn the tile is laid. A blank scores nothing. Seven tiles in a turn add 50. The game ends when a player has used the last tile with the bag empty (that player adds the others' tiles to their score, each of the others loses theirs) or after six turns running without a word (everyone loses their own tiles). The rules are the board game's own and are set down in the head of `rack-rules.js`; the tile counts, values and premium squares are the usual ones, and `tests/rack.mjs` checks the board is symmetric with 8 triple-word, 16 double-word, 12 triple-letter and 24 double-letter squares and that the bag is 100 tiles worth 187.

The word list is the suite's own English list (`wordlist.js`, SCOWL, tiers 10 to 70, 113,000 words of three to fifteen letters, which Assay and Codeword already carry, so the offline cache grows by nothing), with the 126 two-letter words of Collins (SCOWL keeps nothing under three letters) and sixteen short words it lacks that a word-game player expects (`qat qin qis zax zzz tsk` and ten more, `EXTRA` in `rack-rules.js`). There is no challenge: a word that isn't in the list is refused, and named, before the turn is spent, so nobody is penalised for a word they were unsure of, and the line under the board says as you lay tiles what the word is worth or what is wrong with it. The limit is the list's: it has no proper names and none of the stranger words (it lacks, for instance, `qaid`, `xyst`, `yird`, `zoea`, `kvas` and `zen`, and a few thousand more rare words the tournament lists have), so a word a Scrabble expert would play may be refused, and a word the list has that the tournament lists don't is allowed. A bigger SCOWL size would close most of the gap (it is built into `wordlist-data.js` by a tool in `tools/`, a shared file); that wasn't done for this.

The board is a string of 225 characters (`.` empty, a capital a tile, a lower-case letter a blank standing as that letter) and racks and the bag are strings, which the database keeps whole; a swap puts the thrown tiles in the bag and reshuffles it from the move's own seed, so a move worked out twice comes out the same. `evaluate(board, put, dict)` is the one place a play is judged and scored: it looks for the words of two letters or more that any new tile is part of, along the row and the column, so a single tile, a word through tiles, a word beside a word and a word that makes three all fall out of one rule, then checks the tiles lie in one line with no gap, join the board (or cover the star), and are all words.

The computer finds every play with Appel and Jacobson's method (`rack-bot.js`): for each row, and the board turned on its side for each column, take the squares a word could start from (the empty squares next to a tile; the star for the first word), build the part of the word before it out of the rack, then run on to the right through the tiles there, putting a rack tile on an empty square only if it makes a word with what is above and below it (that square's cross-check, a set of letters worked out once per row). The words are one sorted list, so the words that begin with some letters are one stretch of it found by bisection and no word is tried that can't be finished. A whole position takes 20 to 90 milliseconds on a laptop, the first move 120 more while the list is sorted (the page does that while you look at the board). `tests/rack.mjs` checks it against a plain search (every word of a small list in every place it could go, tried with the real scoring): on 21 positions it finds well over a thousand plays, none illegal and none missed. The three strengths differ in the words they know (Easy the 40,000 commonest, Medium 69,000, Hard all) and in how they choose: Easy plays a middling move, Medium one of the best six (usually the best), Hard the best by score plus what the tiles it keeps are worth (a blank 25, an S 8, a Q −7, doubles and an all-vowel rack penalised) and, with the bag empty, less what it is left holding; Hard swaps when its best play is poor and its rack worse, Medium only when nothing scores five. It doesn't look ahead to what its play opens for you, so Hard is a strong casual player and no more; it beats Easy in six games of six when tried.

The page: the scores across the top (the player to move boxed) and the bag, the board as large as the width allows (the premium squares labelled 2L, 3L, 2W, 3W by letters as well as by colour, the star a star; Codex, Modern, Kontor and Office all draw it), then a line saying what the computer did and what your laid tiles make, your rack, and Shuffle, Recall, Swap and Play. Tap a tile and then a square; tap a laid tile to take it back; the blank asks which letter it stands for; Swap opens a sheet with the rack to tick and the Pass button, so a pass is never one tap by accident. The tiles the last play laid have a red edge. Menu: Show me the best word (solo only: it lays the best play on the board to look at, change or play), the computer's strength, the players in a new game (2, 3 or 4; the other seats are computers called Quill, Sable and Pip). At a table the host's phone moves the computers and everyone sees their own rack only on their own phone (the state holds every rack, as every card game here does). Held sideways the board stands on the left and the rack and buttons in a column. Random's round is a game; a finished game counts for the streak.

`tests/rack.mjs` (under five seconds) checks the board and bag, the scoring on worked cases (QUIZ from the star 44, HELLO 24, a seven-tile play 68, a blank, the star counted once, a word beside a word making three), what a play is refused for, the turn (tiles you don't hold, swapping only from a full bag, six scoreless turns, going out), the play-finder against the plain search, six whole games of the three strengths and two to four seats with every tile accounted for after every move, the state through the database, Hard against Easy in four games, and the pages wired up.
