# Shunt
The second idle game (October 2026; README, Idle): `shunt.html`, `shunt.js` and `shunt.css`, its rules in `shunt-engine.js`. A rail yard six tracks across and six down, crossing on every square; wagons stand on it in the four enamels, each cargo with a mark of its own (grain's ears, timber's log ends, a steel beam, oil's drop) so they're told apart without their colour. Round the yard is an arrow at both ends of every track: twenty-four ways to shove the incoming wagon in, the one after it shown beside it. Shoved in, it pushes the wagons ahead of it along the track until the first gap closes; wagons beyond the gap stand where they are. On a full track the wagon at the far end goes off the rails, and a derailment costs one of three lives (the strip's pips). Three or more alike in a line, across or down, couple up and depart, a wagon in two lines at once sending both; a shove scores ten times the square of the wagons departing (three 90, five 250), so one shove that lines up two runs beats two shoves that make one each.

Press an arrow and the shove is shown before it's made: the yard as it would stand, the wagons that would depart ringed, a wagon that would go off the rails drawn beyond the end and crossed, and a line under the yard saying what it would do. Slide along the arrows to see each in turn, let go on one to shove, let go anywhere else and nothing happens (a mouse shows each shove as it passes). An arrow is red where its track is full. Why show it: a shove moves up to six wagons and with them the lines in the six tracks they cross, too much to see in the head at a glance, and the thinking is meant to be in choosing among the shoves, not in imagining them (as a bubble shooter draws its aim).

Every few shoves a goods train leaves a wagon of its own on an empty square, one that makes no run where it stands: every three shoves at first, every two after 25, every shove after 60, two at a time after 120 and three after 180 (the line under the yard counts down to it).

Scrap flats (October 2026): about one wagon in ten the goods train leaves is a scrap flat, a rusty grey with twisted ends on it, never one of the wagons coming. It matches nothing, so it never departs and breaks any line it stands in; the one way out is off the end of a full track, and a scrap flat shoved off costs no life. So a scrap flat is a small job of its own: shove it along to a track's end, fill that track, and send it off from the other end. An arrow whose full track would send scrap off is drawn grey rather than red, the shove shown says the scrap goes for nothing, and until you've shoved one off the line under the yard says what one is for while one stands there. The yard fills, the gaps close, full tracks multiply, and the game ends at the third derailment. The score and your best are in the strip, the best kept as `shunt:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the game in play is kept (`shunt:run`) and opens again where it was. Held sideways the yard is on the left with all the height, the score, the wagons coming and the line beside it.

Why these numbers: played out by simple players (`shoves` in the engine lists all twenty-four with what each would do), one shoving anywhere goes off the rails three times in about 25 shoves; one shoving at random among the shoves that don't derail (or only scrap), and among those that score or scrap when there are any, lasts about 140; one taking the shove that scores most and leaves the yard emptiest and clearest of scrap lasts about 167 (126 to 193), scrapping about 11 flats, and scores about 11,000. Before scrap flats those two lasted about 175 and 182 (scoring about 16,000) with the goods train a little quicker (every two shoves after 20, every shove after 50, two after 100, three after 160); scrap at one in ten on that pace cut the careful player to about 140, at one in eight 138, one in twelve 139, so the pace was eased to give the scrap room. With the goods train bringing no more than a wagon a shove, a careful player outlasted 2,000 shoves, so later it brings two and then three, and a game ends soon after. Every random thing (the yard dealt, the wagons coming, where the goods train leaves its wagons and what they carry) comes from the game's seed and the number of the draw, so a game opened again goes on as it would have.

`tests/shunt.mjs` checks the deal from a seed (no run standing), a track's squares from each end, a shove rolling the wagons up to the first gap and leaving the rest, one from the far end, a full track's derailment and its life, a shove shown without being made, runs across, down and both at once, the points, the goods train's wagons (on empty squares, more and more often, never in a run over thirty games played at random), scrap flats (never in a run and breaking one; shoved off a full track for no life, a cargo wagon at the far end still costing one; from either end; about one of the goods train's wagons in ten, never one coming), the end at the third derailment, a game saved and opened again, and whole games: shoving anywhere off the rails three times in under 60, shoving with thought lasting past 100.
