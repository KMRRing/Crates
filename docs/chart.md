# Chart
Pin a place on the map: a city, a country, a port or chokepoint, a mine or oilfield, a wine village, a museum (or where a painting hangs), a birthplace, a mountain or a strait.

Every question says what it asks: Pin the Hermitage, Pin Balkh with what the name carried as a line of its own (Where Rumi was born; In Rioja; The town, for Guernica), and for an outline or a line what counts (Anywhere inside it counts; Anywhere on it counts); at the reveal it takes its full name again. An instruction rather than a question, so the pyramids of Giza and the Alps read right. Deck asks Chart's places in the same words.

What a set deals is the topics that are on in Settings, in a region. The topics are set once for every game (2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed"; in October 2026 Chart had a mix of its own, each topic out, in or more, with the presets Balanced, Trader and Culture night, and before that a dropdown of six; README, The knowledge base). The menu shows only where they stand ("Topics: Culture (10 of 23 on). Chart deals 7 of its 13 topics") and a Change topics link that opens the same sheet as Settings over the game, whether today's set or a random one is picked (22 October 2026: the old control showed only once Random was, and each game opens on today's, so Korbi never found it); a change made with today's picked turns the next set to a random one. The region is not a topic and stays Chart's own, in the header's dropdown and the menu; a region chosen deals a random set in it (on today's it once dealt today's again, worldwide, keeping nothing of the region). The topics are Chart's categories: Cities, Countries, Commodities (ports, terminals, refineries, mines and oilfields), Geography (peaks, islands, deltas and the far places), Physical (the rivers, ranges, deserts, plateaus and lakes drawn on the map), Art, Museums, Architecture, Myth, History, People, Wine, and Local places, a region's own (Bavaria's towns, the Alps' resorts, Lake Geneva's sights), dealt only in that region. Each belongs to a subject (`TOPIC_OF.chart` in `topics.js`): Cities to Cities; Countries, Geography and Local places to Geography; Commodities to Trade & shipping; Physical to Nature; Art, Museums and Architecture to Art & architecture; Myth to Literature, music & myth; History to History; People to People; Wine to Food & drink. A topic is in when its subject is on and out when it isn't, so Geography off takes Countries, Geography and Local places together, and a subject Chart has no places on (Sport, Maths) changes nothing here. The subjects are read when a set is dealt: a change reaches the next set, a set already dealt keeps what it was dealt, and the next set after a finished one (Again, A random set) deals from the topics as they stand, in the finished set's region. With every subject on a set is Balanced, which deals exactly the sets Everything did, at most two of a kind worldwide, seed for seed; today's set is always that. A mix of only in and out (one topic, or the old Nature's two from a link) deals the sets the dropdown's topic did, a little over a category's share each and one category all ten, topped up so a set is short only when the selection is. Nothing on, or only subjects Chart has no topic in, deals every topic and the menu says so; so does a mix with nothing in the region chosen, with a toast, as it always did. The region: Worldwide, or Europe, the Middle East, Asia, Africa, North America, South America or Oceania, each listed with how many places it holds for your mix (a short set below ten). In a region every question opens zoomed on its map window. A place counts in its country's region, by the flags bank's regions with a few made to match what the windows show: Turkey is Middle East and Europe, Cyprus Europe and Middle East, Egypt Africa and Middle East, Russia as a country both Europe and Asia, and a place in Russia or Turkey by its side of the Urals or the Bosphorus (Norilsk is Asia, Istanbul Europe); Antarctica and the far ocean islands are in no region. A new region deals a new set in it at once (asking first if one is under way; together, a fresh set for everyone); new topics apply to the next set. A set's link carries both (#s=…&t=art.1,wine.1&r=europe; a link from before may carry a 2, which still deals that topic twice as often and up to twice as many of it in a set, or an old topic's name, &t=nature, which deals the set it always did). Bests are kept per selection, in each region: Everything's (every topic worldwide, where the old Balanced's and Everything's bests are), a single topic's (the Physical set STEM leaves, the Commodities set Trade leaves), the Culture preset's, or an old topic's or earlier preset's where a link or a saved set carries one (nature, Trader, Culture night); your own mix of several keeps none, and the menu says so. The games screen's best counts today's set (every topic, worldwide) and a random set on a preset (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade or All off) that deals more than one topic, worldwide, learning mode or not; one topic alone keeps a best of its own, as the dropdown's topics did, and none for the games screen's. The old `chart:topics` is left alone and no longer written.

A country's outline is its target: anywhere inside it scores in full (1,100), anywhere outside nothing, with the distance to its border shown. The outlines (`chart-countries.js`, 168 countries) are Natural Earth's 1:110m, named as the flags bank names them and rounded to a tenth of a degree as `world.js` is, so the outline is the border drawn on the map; the inside test is even–odd over all of a country's rings, so a hole is outside (Lesotho isn't South Africa); Northern Cyprus and Somaliland are inside Cyprus and Somalia (the recognised borders); rings across the antimeridian (Russia's east, Fiji) are cut there; French Guiana is part of France, as in the source. Microstates aren't in the 1:110m set, nor territories the flags bank has no flag for. A country's clues can't name it, so they are its land neighbours' count (with its region), then two of its neighbours, then its flag (an island nation: its flag, then its first letter). A pin outside a country, or any clue, sends it to the pile, carrying its knowledge-base id, so the other games' learning modes deal it too. One map: drag to pan, pinch (or the wheel) to zoom about your fingers, from the whole world down to 1.5° across, with a graticule that tightens as you zoom in; tap to place the pin, tap again to move it, then pin it. The reveal frames your pin and the truth together, with a line and the distance, and a description of the place. Points fall off smoothly with great-circle distance: 1,000 on the spot, about 600 at 1,000 km, 135 at 4,000 km, plus 100 within 100 km. Physical features too, 172 of them, drawn on the map rather than pinned to a point: rivers as lines (the Nile, the Rhine, the Yangtze, the Paraná), mountain ranges, deserts, plateaus and basins, and lakes as shapes (`chart-geo.js`, from Natural Earth, simplified), each with a note. Pin anywhere on the feature for full marks; the distance is to its nearest point, and at the reveal the feature is drawn and the map frames it with your pin. Clues, at a price: the first names the region and keeps 70% of the points, the second the country (45%), the third shows the place's description with its own name hidden (25%); in a room each player's clues discount their own score. Ten places a set, random sets or today's set (for the selection), bests kept, links to a set.

Together (October 2026): a pair's duo match, or a party of up to six, each on their own phone. Everyone pins the same place, seeing only their own pin, and takes their own clues, which discount only their own points; a line under the map shows who's at the table, who has pinned (ticked) and who's away. The reveal comes once everyone at the table has pinned: the map shows every pin in its player's colour with their initial, each joined to where it should have been, and under it this place's results, the most points first (each one's distance, any clues, the points), then the standings (standings.js: each total and this place's change), then the place's note. The table moves on once everyone at it has pressed Next, and the hint says who it's waiting for. Someone away (another game open, the phone asleep) never holds it up, and nor does someone slow: once those who have pinned are at least as many as those still to, and half a minute has passed since the last pin, they may reveal without the rest (a missing pin scores nothing); Next the same way, so a phone put down at the reveal doesn't stop the table either. The last place's reveal ends the set, its result final there and then (the pair's record counts it); Final standings shows who won, every total with how many places each was best on, every pin of the set on one map, Play again (a fresh set for the table) and Back to solo. The menu at a table holds your topics and the region: a new region deals a fresh set for everyone at once, and every fresh set comes from the mix of whoever deals it. A pair plays the same way with two seats, and the higher total counts for their duo record. Opened for a table (a request, Join, a party), the page shows the rete (Joining your partner, or Joining the table at a party) until the table's set is drawn, and deals your own set only if the join doesn't come off (October 2026): it used to draw your own set first, so for the first seconds, ten on a slow connection, each of you was on a place of your own. Back to solo returns to your own set as you left it, and nothing at a table touches your own run, bests or today's. A set dealt as you sit down is worldwide, from the mix of whoever deals it, the menu changing it for the next. The table's rules are pure functions in `chart-table.js` (everything kept by seat and by question, never in a list by number, which the database would read back with holes), tested in `tests/chart.mjs` through the database's round trip: scoring a place for several, the reveal's order, who the table waits for and the grace, people coming and going, the standings and the record. Three phones in one browser played a set through (each pin private until the reveal, the reveal on each, Next from all three, one going back to solo mid-set without holding the other two up, the grace for a slow pinner, the final standings and Play again), and a pair from the partner sheet's address played one with a reload on each side and its record written.

The map is drawn on canvas from `world.js`: Natural Earth's country polygons (with their holes, each drawn on its own so nothing wraps the antimeridian; the rings that crossed it, Russia's far east and Fiji, are cut there) and interior borders at 1:110m (public domain, via the world-atlas package), rounded to a tenth of a degree, 160 KB, so it works offline. Mercator clipped at ±78°. `chart-countries.js` holds the countries (outline, flag, regions, land neighbours from the source's shared borders, knowledge-base id) and every flags-bank country's regions; `chart-bank.js` holds 415 places with coordinates and, for each, a description of what it is and why it matters (shown at the reveal), with the trade angle where there is one; `chart-engine.js` does distance, scoring (a country by its outline), sets, topics, regions and their views, and the projection; `tests/chart.mjs` checks them, the countries' outlines (each holds its own anchor; Paris is in France and Madrid isn't; Maseru is in Lesotho, not South Africa; Chukotka past 180° is Russia) and the regions (Ras Tanura is Middle East, Norilsk Asia).

The page fits the screen: the map takes the room left and keeps its shape, and under it a fixed space holds the clues and buttons or, after the pin, the result (its note scrolling inside), so the map keeps one size from pinning to the reveal. At a table the map keeps the world's shape at the card's width (smaller only where the screen is short) and the space under it, all that's left on a tall phone, holds the clues and buttons and then the results and standings, scrolling inside; held sideways they take the side column under the place.

The end of a set shows every pin on one map, each joined by a dashed line to where it should have been (for a river, range or lake, the nearest point of it, with the feature drawn), numbered and framed so all ten fit; under it a row a place with its miss and points. Tap a row to frame that place's miss alone (the others fade); tap it again for all of them. At a table everyone's pins are drawn, each in their seat's colour, and each row gives a pair's both misses, or a bigger table's best and yours. A region can also lie within a country: Bavaria (October 2026) is played on its own map window, with its outline, the Danube and Lake Constance drawn faintly for bearings. Its places are those marked with its state in kb/ (Munich and Neuschwanstein as much as Landshut), and its own category of towns, peaks and sights is dealt only there, never in a round of the world. Britain and Scotland (October 2026) are sub-regions too: Britain takes every place in the United Kingdom, Scotland the places marked with its state; Scottish towns are dealt in both. They're drawn on a detailed coastline of the British Isles (chart-detail.js, Natural Earth at 1:50m), in place of the coarse world map, so the Hebrides, Orkney and Shetland are there to pin; with the Anglo-Scottish and Anglo-Welsh borders, the Thames and the Severn. China (October 2026) is a sub-region the same way, by country, drawn with the Yangtze and the Yellow River. The Alps (October 2026) cross five countries, so their places join by a mark in kb/ (marks: Alps) rather than a state or a country; Garmisch and Oberstdorf are marked Alpine and Bavarian, and turn up in both. The range is drawn as a dotted outline. Lake Geneva and the Watch Valley (October 2026) are drawn with 1:10m lakes and borders (chart-detail.js), the fine borders in place of the coarse ones; Lake Geneva is played at city scale, so a small region opens, and reveals, at its own width (regionSpan) rather than the 6° a continent needs. The map's night palette (October 2026) sets the land well apart from the sea, every coastline is outlined, and the islands the coarse world map leaves out come from Natural Earth at 1:50m (chart-islands.js, written by kbwork/islands.mjs): oceanic ones (the Azores, Cape Verde, the Maldives, Tonga…) show as a speck at any zoom and as land once they're big enough, coastal islets only then. On the iPhone, Safari's own pinch is refused on the map, so a pinch zooms the map, not the page. The map listens to pointer events alone (October 2026), as every other map in the suite: it once also cancelled a second finger's touches, and a browser given both can lose that finger's lift, after which a one-finger drag pinched against the lost finger, zooming and hardly panning, and a tap no longer placed the pin. `fingers()` in `chart-engine.js` counts the fingers on the map and starts again with the first finger of every new touch, so a finger the browser lost can't outlast its gesture; `tests/chart.mjs` checks a pan, a pinch, a tap and a lost lift.

Museums: the world's most significant museums, art and otherwise, are all in the knowledge base with a position and a note (196 of them). Art museums are pinned under Art (141 pins); the rest under Museums (67): history and archaeology (the Egyptian and Grand Egyptian Museums, the Acropolis Museum, Mexico's anthropology museum, the Terracotta Army), science and technology (the Deutsches Museum, the Science Museum, Air and Space, the Computer History Museum), natural history (London, New York, Paris, Berlin, Chicago), ships and polar voyages (the Vasa, the Mary Rose, the Fram, the Kon-Tiki) and memorials (Anne Frank House, Auschwitz-Birkenau, Yad Vashem, Hiroshima). Where a painting hangs is asked among art museums only.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the map takes the card's left side, all its height, and the place to find, its picture and the buttons stand at the right, the buttons at the bottom.
