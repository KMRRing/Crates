# Cue
A party game (October 2026): tap anywhere on the card and a simple word pops up, to act, draw or describe without saying it, or for someone holding the phone to their forehead to guess, with a countdown under it (below). The party keeps its own rules, teams and score.

The words (`cue-words.js`) are 553 simple, everyday, concrete ones in twelve kinds: animals, food and drink, things, clothes, nature, places, actions, people, sports and games, getting about, the body, feelings; British spellings, as the rest of the suite. The kind shows small over the word in the game's enamel, a raspberry, so whoever acts it knows what sort of thing to show; Menu, The kind over the word, turns it off for a harder game. Menu, Words, ticks the kinds in play (none ticked is all of them).

The deck (`cue-deck.js`) is the words of the kinds ticked, shuffled (Fisher–Yates: every order equally likely) and dealt one a tap, so no word comes twice until every one has; then it is shuffled again, never starting with the word just seen. The count on the card's foot says how far through you are (12 of 553, and the round after the first). Back steps to the word before, for a tap that came too soon, and a second tap within a third of a second counts as the same tap, so a double tap doesn't skip a word nobody saw. Menu, Shuffle them all again, starts a fresh round. The deck is kept on the device (`cue:deck`, the menu's choices in `cue:opts`) and syncs like every game's keys, so a reload, another of your devices or the next party carries on where it was; a deck kept from a list that has since changed is dealt afresh. On a keyboard, → (or Space on the card) is the next word and ← the one before.

The countdown (Menu, Count down 3, 2, 1; on unless turned off, October 2026): a word comes up alone for a second, so everyone has read it before anything is counted, and then a dial under it counts down three seconds, its numeral 3, 2, 1, a second each, and an arc in the game's enamel running out with the time. At nought the ring and the numeral turn raspberry and the card flashes once (a tint and a frame in the enamel, fading over most of a second), so the end is seen from across a room; the 0 stays until the next tap. For the first second the dial is only its empty ring, there from the start so nothing moves when the count begins; in Codex three brass ticks on the ring mark the seconds. A tap at any moment is the next word with its own second to read and its own count, and Back gives the word before a fresh one. The count stands still while the menu or the games screen is open and goes on from where it stood (the suite's pause). The moment a word came up is kept with the deck (`when` in `cue:deck`), so a reload mid-count, another of your devices or a partner watching shows the same count; a page opened after a count has ended shows the empty ring, without a flash. Turned on in the menu, it starts with the next word: the one up has been read. The timing is in `cue-clock.js`, the time since the word came up in and the phase, numeral and share of the arc out, which the page draws each frame and tests/cue.mjs checks.

The word is drawn as large as the card holds it: at most a third of the card's height, no more than the kind and the dial leave of it, and nine tenths of its width. A long word (WEIGHTLIFTING) whose wide capitals would come out under 3rem takes narrower letters instead, which draw it about a quarter larger in Codex's lettering. Held sideways the card takes all the height under the header, Back and the count lying on its foot, so a phone held to a forehead shows the word as large as the screen is high. tests/cue.mjs checks the words (each one word of small letters, none in two kinds, at least 20 of each kind, none over 13 letters, so every one fits large) and the deck (every word once a round, a new round not starting with the word just seen, back a step and on again to the same word, a kept deck read back, and dealt afresh when the list has changed under it, a fair shuffle) and the countdown (nothing counted for the first second, then 3, 2 and 1 a second each, nought at four seconds, the arc running out evenly).
