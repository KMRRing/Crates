# The mark

**The name** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "change Almanac to Codex"). The suite was Crates, then Almanac, and is Codex: a codex is the book the games are bound in. Only the words changed. The repository and the site's address stay `Crates` (installed copies live at that address, and GitHub Pages doesn't redirect a renamed one), and so do every game's id, its saved keys, the rooms and the theme's id (`deco`; its name on the Settings screen is Codex). Two strings keep the old name on purpose: the offline party's link-code prefix (`AL1:` in `lan-link.js`, so a phone not yet updated still reads a new one's code) and the words "almanac", "yearbook" and so on in the word-puzzle data, which are words. The computer in Sudoku, Kakuro and Calcudoku plays as Codex. The app a phone installs is Codex for now; Korbi means to give the app its own name later, "something on top of Codex", which is one string in `manifest.webmanifest` and each page's `apple-mobile-web-app-title` (a name on an iPhone's home screen changes only when the app is added again).

**The picture** (2 November 2026, from Korbi's wish that the astrolabe's rete become the C and the o, and a mechanism that turns when a page loads). A C of three Geneva-striped brass tiers (côtes de Genève, the radial stripes of a fine watch's bridges, one every four degrees, each tier ending on a stripe, the ends stepped like an Art Deco terminal) round a chapter ring (the minute track of a watch, the "o"), round a watch movement: a great wheel of 20 teeth, two pinions, and an internal ring gear of 40 teeth behind them, held by a bridge with three ruby jewels and screws. The movement is real, with one module (tooth size) for every wheel so they mesh. The great wheel turns clockwise in 3 s and carries a bar to the inner tier of the C; the two pinions (a wheel of 10 teeth meeting the great wheel and another of 10 meeting the ring, on one shaft) turn the other way in 1.5 s; the ring turns the same way as the pinions in 6 s and carries a bar to the outer tier. The middle tier, the o, the bridge and the jewels stand still. The speeds follow from the teeth, and the great wheel turns twice for the ring's once, so everything is back where it began every 6 s: at rest the C is whole, and while it turns the C comes apart and re-forms. Shaded as brass, not outlined (3 November 2026, Korbi: "add gradients and shadows that make it look 3D and remove those massive white outlines"): every gradient is the same all the way round, because the layers turn, so a light that stays put on a turning layer would swing with it. The menu button's mark and the tiny loader stay flat. Reduce Motion stops every animation on the page except the mark's turning (`style.css` says so with `!important`): it is the loading sign, and a phone with Reduce Motion on showed a mark that never moved. No SVG filters in the mark: Safari draws a filter at a coarser grid than the screen and the layers came out blurry on an iPhone, so a shadow is the part's silhouette stamped faintly at a few offsets (`shadow` in `tools/build-mark.mjs`). The loader's layers are turned by script from `theme.js` as well as by CSS (CSS left them still under Reduce Motion, from an older stylesheet, and sometimes on an iPhone when the box was shown again), restarted each time the loader comes into view. 3 November 2026, Korbi: the arms must not pass through each other, so the great wheel's long bar is the topmost layer and the ring's shorter bar lies under every other part but the ring.

**One generator** (`tools/build-mark.mjs`). It draws every file the mark is in: `mark/*.svg` (the large cut in nine layers, bottom to top: base, ring, the ring's bar, the two pinions, great wheel, bridge, jewels, the great wheel's bar), `logo.svg` (the small cut at rest: the games screen's title), `icon.svg` (the same on a rounded navy tile: the favicon, and Modern's title), `loader-small.svg` (the tiny cut, turning), `mark.js` (the small cut inline, for the menu button and Settings) and `tools/mark-3d/params.json` (the teeth, for the 3D scene). Tooth phases are found by scanning for the middle of the longest run with no overlap through a mesh cycle, not set by hand; `tests/mark.mjs` regenerates everything in memory and fails if a file differs (so nobody edits one by hand), checks that no teeth overlap, that a pinion half a pitch out of mesh does (the check can fail), that every tier ends on a stripe (the first drawing's inner tier ended between stripes and stopped short at the bottom), and that style.css's speeds are the movement's.

**The loader is on the GPU.** A page loads with its own thread busy, which is when an animated SVG (SMIL, which repaints the whole picture on the page's thread) stutters: measured in headless Chrome at three times a phone's pixel density, the one-piece mark dropped to 26 frames a second while the thread was busy, and even when it wasn't it took 159 ms of rasterising (turning the drawing into pixels) a second against the old rete's 64 ms. So the large mark is nine stacked pictures (`mark/*.svg`), put into every page by `theme.js` from its first moment (so it's there before anything else has run, and shows only once all nine have come, never half a mark), each moving part its own layer that `style.css` (`.cx-load`) turns with a CSS animation the compositor runs as a texture: 60 frames a second whatever the page is doing, and nothing repainted. The pinions turn about their own axes (`transform-origin` at 150 and 30 degrees round the hub). Reduced motion shows it still. In the Codex theme the menu button and Settings draw the small cut inline (`mark.js`), standing still, and while the menu or Settings is open the same four parts turn at the same speeds (`.cx-a`, `.cx-r`, `.cx-b`, `.cx-d`); that one is small enough to turn on the page's own thread. The games screen's title and every theme's brand: the logo in Codex and Kontor, the tile in Modern, nothing in Office. In a game's header the menu button is the mark in Codex, Kontor's three rules in Kontor and Office, and the word Menu in Modern, as before.

**The app icon is a render** (`tools/mark-3d/`). A real 3D scene of the same movement in thin brass (the tiers as polished slabs with Geneva flutes on top that catch the light in bands, the gears as bevelled slabs, the bars on posts above the wheels, ruby jewels), lit like a product photograph, drawn once into the PNGs: the disc fills 79% of the square, inside the 80% circle a maskable icon keeps clear, so one picture serves the iPhone's `icon.png`, `icons/icon-192.png`, `icons/icon-512.png` and the maskable `icons/icon-maskable-512.png`. Nothing of it runs in the app: it needs the full three.js (the page's vendored copy is cut down to what Harbour draws) and Playwright, installed beside it, never in the repo (`cd tools/mark-3d && npm i --no-save three@0.170.0 && NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node render.mjs`). A live 3D loader was weighed and left out: the scene is light for a GPU, but it would load a 692 KB engine (171 KB compressed) to show the loading screen, and below about 40 pixels the shading can't be seen. A pre-rendered film of it came to 463 KB for 6 s at 360 px and 2.3 MB at 720, blurred on a phone's screen. Not built: the nine layers rendered in 3D, each rotated the same way; its highlights and shadows would turn with the parts.

**What went before.** The logo was a sextant whose frame is the A, and loading was an astrolabe's rete turning over its plate (`rete.svg`, with a small sextant for short waits, `sextant.svg`, and Modern's and Kontor's own drawings of it). `sextant()` is now `waitMark()`; the old name is still exported from `loading.js` so branches written before the rename load, and goes when they have merged.
