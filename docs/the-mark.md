# The mark

**The name** (2 November 2026, Korbi: "change Almanac to Codex"). The suite was Crates, then Almanac, and is Codex: a codex is the book the games are bound in. Only the words changed. The repository and the site's address stay `Crates` (installed copies live at that address, and GitHub Pages doesn't redirect a renamed one), and so do every game's id, its saved keys, the rooms and the theme's id (`deco`; its name on the Settings screen is Codex). Two strings keep the old name on purpose: the offline party's link-code prefix (`AL1:` in `lan-link.js`, so a phone not yet updated still reads a new one's code) and the words "almanac", "yearbook" and so on in the word-puzzle data, which are words. The App Store name is Codex Gizmo (2 November 2026, Korbi: "Codex Gizmo - perfect", after asking for a casual second word that Latin would not give); the strings change only after a trademark search for CODEX and GIZMO. What a phone installs from the web is still Codex: one string in `manifest.webmanifest` and each page's `apple-mobile-web-app-title` (a name on an iPhone's home screen changes only when the app is added again).

**The picture** (2 November 2026, from Korbi's wish that the astrolabe's rete become the C and the o, and a mechanism that turns when a page loads). A C of three Geneva-striped brass tiers (côtes de Genève, the radial stripes of a fine watch's bridges, each tier ending on a stripe, the ends stepped like an Art Deco terminal) round a chapter ring (the minute track of a watch, the "o"), round a watch movement: a great wheel, two pinions and an internal ring gear behind them, held by a bridge with three ruby jewels and screws. The movement is real, with one module (tooth size) for every wheel so they mesh. The great wheel turns clockwise and carries a bar to the inner tier of the C; the pinions turn the other way; the ring turns the same way as the pinions and carries a bar to the outer tier. The middle tier, the o, the bridge and the jewels stand still. The speeds follow from the teeth and everything is back where it began every 6 s: at rest the C is whole, and while it turns the C comes apart and re-forms.
- Shaded as brass, not outlined (2 November 2026, Korbi: "add gradients and shadows that make it look 3D and remove those massive white outlines"). Every gradient is the same all the way round, because the layers turn, so a light that stays put on a turning layer would swing with it. The menu button's mark and the tiny loader stay flat.
- The arms must not pass through each other (2 November 2026, Korbi): the great wheel's long bar is the topmost layer and the ring's shorter bar is the rearmost part, on the dial and under the ring gear, all three tiers and everything else (so it shows only between them); the long bar runs into a boss on the arbor with the centre jewel set in it.
- Reduce Motion stops every animation on the page except the mark's turning (`style.css` says so with `!important`): it is the loading sign, and a phone with Reduce Motion on showed a mark that never moved.
- No SVG filters in the mark: Safari draws a filter at a coarser grid than the screen and the layers came out blurry on an iPhone, so a shadow is the part's silhouette stamped faintly at a few offsets (`shadow` in `tools/build-mark.mjs`).
- The loader's layers are turned by script from `theme.js` as well as by CSS (CSS left them still under Reduce Motion, from an older stylesheet, and sometimes on an iPhone when the box was shown again), restarted each time the loader comes into view.

**One generator** (`tools/build-mark.mjs`). It draws every file the mark is in: `mark/*.svg` (the large cut in nine layers), `logo.svg` (the small cut at rest: the games screen's title), `icon.svg` (the same on a rounded navy tile: the favicon), `loader-small.svg` (the tiny cut, turning), `mark.js` (the small cut inline, for the menu button and Settings) and `tools/mark-3d/params.json` (the teeth, for the 3D scene). Edit the generator, never a file it writes: `tests/mark.mjs` regenerates everything in memory and fails if a file differs. It also checks that no teeth overlap, that every tier ends on a stripe, and that `style.css`'s speeds are the movement's (so a change to the teeth changes the speeds there). Tooth phases are found by scanning, not set by hand.

**The loader is on the GPU.** A page loads with its own thread busy, which is when an animated SVG (SMIL, which repaints the whole picture on the page's thread) stutters. So the large mark is nine stacked pictures (`mark/*.svg`), put into every page by `theme.js` from its first moment (it shows only once all nine have come, never half a mark), each moving part its own layer that `style.css` (`.cx-load`) turns with a CSS animation the compositor runs as a texture, whatever the page is doing. The pinions turn about their own axes (`transform-origin` at 150 and 30 degrees round the hub). In the Codex theme a game's menu button draws the small cut inline (`mark.js`), standing still, and while the menu is open the same four parts turn (`.cx-a`, `.cx-r`, `.cx-b`, `.cx-d`); that one is small enough to turn on the page's own thread. The games screen's title: the logo is the C of CODEX in Codex, nothing in Office; its Settings button is a gear (2 November 2026, Korbi: the mark would show twice), not the mark. In a game's header the menu button is the mark in Codex and three rules in Office.

**The app icon is a render** (`tools/mark-3d/`): a real 3D scene of the same movement in thin brass, lit like a product photograph, drawn once into the PNGs (the disc fills 79% of the square, inside the 80% circle a maskable icon keeps clear, so one picture serves `icon.png`, `icons/icon-192.png`, `icons/icon-512.png` and `icons/icon-maskable-512.png`). Nothing of it runs in the app: it needs the full three.js (the page's vendored copy is cut down to what Harbour draws) and Playwright, installed beside it, never in the repo (`cd tools/mark-3d && npm i --no-save three@0.170.0 && NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node render.mjs`). A live 3D loader was weighed and left out (it would load a 692 KB engine to show the loading screen, and below about 40 pixels the shading can't be seen), and so was a pre-rendered film (463 KB for 6 s at 360 px, blurred on a phone's screen). Not built: the nine layers rendered in 3D, each rotated the same way; its highlights and shadows would turn with the parts.

**What went before.** The logo was a sextant whose frame is the A, and loading was an astrolabe's rete turning over its plate. `sextant()` is now `waitMark()`; the old name is still exported from `loading.js` so branches written before the rename load, and goes when they have merged.
