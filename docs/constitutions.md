# Constitutions and institutions

A course of 64 lessons in 23 chapters on the shared course sheet (`course.js`): a constitutional-law spine (what a constitution does, separating and checking, rights and emergencies, where power is divided, the regime typology), a world tour (old republics, imperial bureaucracies, Islamic, Persian and South Asian orders, Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia, Africa, the Americas and the steppe), the two deep dives Korbi asked for (the US and the EU: who starts an act, who can stop it, how long it takes), Westminster, Germany, France and Japan, the Party-state, Russia and Singapore, Iran, Turkey, Egypt and Pakistan, Brazil, South Africa, Nigeria, Indonesia and Malaysia, orders beyond the state, constitutions without a document, electoral systems, civil-military relations, the economic constitution, Switzerland (chapters 18 to 22, after the first seventeen so cited chapter numbers hold), and a comparison. The outline and its fact checks are `docs/political-theory-outline.md` (sections 9 to 14). Tile "Constitutions", door `constitutions.html`; words and questions in `constitutions-c1.js` to `constitutions-c8.js`, their exercises in `constitutions-x1.js` to `x8.js` (helpers in `constitutions-x.js`, the kit in `lesson-kit.js`), built into steps by `constitutions-lessons.js`; the stage (terms card, source to read, ordering) in `constitutions-stage.js`. Keys: `constitutions:course`, `constitutions:lessons` (`{ done, of }` for the tile), `constitutions:pile` (the review: a miss returns after a day, three days, a week, three weeks, via `music-pile.js` as Art history does).

## Traps

- **A contested number is never asked as a number** (Kesavananda's 7 to 6 is stated, not asked; Athens' citizen share, the Kouroukan Fouga's age, Haudenosaunee dating, the millet are asked as "which statement is defensible"). A figure the outline marked `(check)` and nobody opened stays out of questions (`tests/constitutions.mjs` lists them: the ostracism quorum, Ibn Khaldun's 120 years, India's "105", the IMF's 16.5 percent exactly).
- **A perishable fact carries its date and source in the words** (counts of laws, shutdown length, the number of MEPs, the Politburo, ASEAN's members, the WTO's Appellate Body, the Pakistani 27th Amendment). Pull each again the week it is shown; the test fails a 2025 or 2026 fact with no "as of" or source.
- **Exercises are the kit's** (`lesson-kit.js`, `docs/lesson-kit.md`), drawn by `constitutions-stage.js`: `course.js` is shared and untouched. A step flagged `check: true` shows a small tag until someone verifies its facts; `tests/constitutions.mjs` lists them. A review step is a `Object.create` of its step, so the stage must never store state on the step, only on `st`.
- **The right answer is not usually the longest** (the test fails a question where it is clearly longest, or the only one with a digit); wrong options are a neighbouring idea or the answer with one thing changed. When editing, keep the four options within a few words of each other.
- **Extracts are short and quoted from memory of public texts** (Federalist 51, the Constitution's Article V and I section 7, Marbury, the Basic Law's Articles 79(3) and 67, South Africa's section 36(1), the UN Charter 27(3), Japan's Article 9, India's Article 1, Legge's Mencius): check each against its text when a connected machine is to hand; a short extract only, never a whole clause.
- **Iran and any current regime:** describe the structure, never the person in office; events change faster than a lesson.
