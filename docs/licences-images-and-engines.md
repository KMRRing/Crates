# Licences: images and engines

Decided on 2 November 2026 (Korbi, after the library review; not legal advice; of what follows only Gambit's engine is changed so far).

**Paintings** (Punt's art bank, 201 of them): public-domain works only. A faithful photograph of a public-domain painting gets no new copyright in the EU (Article 14 of the 2019 copyright directive) or the UK (the IPO's 2014 guidance), and the Met and the Art Institute of Chicago publish their images as CC0. A painting by an artist who died after 1955 (or first published after 1930, for the United States) is still in copyright, and paying a picture library for the photograph does not pay the painter's estate. Those are to be swapped for a pre-1931 painting of the same movement (`kb/` already says which movement). Not yet counted: how many of the 201 fail.

**Buildings**: the building and the photograph are two things. An old building is free, but its photograph belongs to the photographer, so only CC0 or public-domain photographs, or our own drawings. A building by an architect who died after 1955 is itself protected, and the freedom to photograph it in public differs by country: the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany (the outside only), Switzerland and China allow it; France and Italy allow no commercial use. The suite can be opened in France, so such buildings are left out.

**Chess**: Korbi's call (2 November 2026): prefer a permissive engine, and in the worst case no engine at all, only puzzles (Gambit's puzzle bank is the Lichess database, CC0, so that part is clean already). Gambit's engines, evaluated 3 November 2026, one line each:
- Stockfish 19 lite (stockfish.js 19.0.1): GPL v3. Was Gambit's; out of the tree (the history keeps `vendor/stockfish/`, for measuring against).
- Caissa 2.0.12 (324f1f9): MIT, about 3600 on CCRL, a 263 KB single-threaded WebAssembly module with SIMD. Was Gambit's until 3 November 2026 (the history keeps `vendor/caissa/`): its network (eval-ml-11a-100B-spsa-transplant, from Caissa-Nets, 76 MB, 38 MB packed) sits in a repository with no licence file, though the author releases the engine as MIT and its build fetches that network itself. A licence line from the author would have settled it; Korbi chose the clean engine instead ("yea we go for the weaker one").
- **Arasan 26.1 (84a6787): MIT, its network included (28.5 MB, 13.3 MB packed), the engine Gambit plays** (`vendor/arasan/NOTE.txt`). The licence file in its repository grants the same terms to the `network` directory by name. It builds to an 840 KB single-threaded WebAssembly module with SIMD; about 2.5 times the time per position of Caissa's, and weaker at the same positions (the levels are re-set by games, `tools/engine-levels-gambit-arasan.jsonl`).
- kishi (chess, 9f06237): MIT, Rust, already WebAssembly. About 2,000 Elo: too weak for Gambit's top levels.
- Leorik: MIT, C#, about 3,400 on CCRL Blitz. Not evaluated: it would need a .NET build.
- Viridithas (AGPL from v21) and Coda (GPL): not usable.

**Xiangqi (River)**, 3 November 2026: River plays its own engine (`river-engine.js`), written for the Codex in plain JavaScript, so nothing of anyone's is in it and nothing is carried. The engines looked at, one line each:
- Fairy-Stockfish: GPL v3 (Fabian Fichter and contributors). River used it until now; it is out of the tree (git history only). The sandbox kept a copy to measure the new engine against, and to label positions for its evaluation: the weights were fitted to the scores it gave (numbers, no code and no tables), and they are River's own set.
- Pikafish: GPL v3 (a Stockfish derivative, its network trained for Xiangqi). Not used, not run.
- kishi (Shogi, MIT, in Rust for WebAssembly, as the plan has it): not adopted. River has no shogi, and its licence and strength were not checked (the sandbox could not reach its repository).

**Four in a row (Silo)**, 3 November 2026: Silo's solver (`silo-solver.js`) is written for the Codex in plain JavaScript after the method of Pierre Pons's tutorial on solving the game (bitboards, a filter for non-losing moves, a transposition table folded by the mirror, columns tried from the middle out); no code of his is in it, and a method is not protected by copyright. The weights of its judgement are its own. Nothing was run or measured against another program except a plain negamax written for the test.

**Draughts (Crown)**, 3 November 2026: Crown plays its own engine (`crown-search.js`: alpha-beta search, a transposition table, a hand-made evaluation), written for the Codex in plain JavaScript; nothing of anyone's is in it and nothing is carried. No other engine (Scan is GPL) was run or read. The rules are the federations' published rules, which belong to no one.

**The Earth's map (Columbiad)**, 3 November 2026: `columbiad-earth.jpg` is NASA's Blue Marble (Next Generation, with topography and bathymetry, January), NASA Earth Observatory / Visible Earth, which NASA publishes without copyright (NASA media usage guidelines); downsized to 1024 x 512 (66 KB) from the 4096 x 2048 copy in the MIT-licensed repository vasturiano/three-globe (`example/img/earth-blue-marble.jpg`), which is where it was fetched; the repository's licence covers its own code, not the picture. The clouds and the Moon's map are drawn by the page from noise and craters, from no picture.

**Court cards (J, Q, K of the ordinary pack)**, 7 November 2026: the twelve double-ended figures in `court/` (drawn by `court-art.js`) are Byron Knoll's "vector-playing-cards" (Google Code, released by him into the public domain), taken from the notpeter/Vector-Playing-Cards collection on GitHub, whose README puts the images, scripts and their output into the public domain (the WTFPL where that is not recognised). Changed here only by cropping to the inside of the card's frame, rounding the numbers (svgo, one decimal) and mapping every blue, green and olive to black or yellow, so the pack has the suite's four colours. A look-alike of a stock-vector sheet Korbi sent was refused: it is a commercial licensed artwork, so neither traced nor copied.
