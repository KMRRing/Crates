# Netting
The seventh idle game (October 2026; README, Idle): `netting.html`, `netting.js` and `netting.css`, its rules in `netting-engine.js`. Six trading houses round a baize table (GVA, LON, SIN, HOU, RTM, SHA), an arrow for each obligation between two of them, from the debtor, its amount on it. Three new obligations wait under the table, each from a given debtor for a given amount; you choose who each is owed to, in the order you like, and three more come once all three are booked. Drag one onto the house it's to be owed to (an arrow follows the finger from its debtor), or tap it and then the house; while it's held over a house the loops it would net are lit, with what they'd score or the default it would cause in the line above the obligations, and letting go there books it. A loop of obligations (A owes B owes C owes A) is netted out by its smallest leg: every leg pays down by that much, and a leg paid off goes, as a clearing house's multilateral netting does; owing each other is the smallest loop, two legs. Netting scores ten times the square of the gross it cancels, the amount times the legs, so a long loop scores most (a loop of three paying down 2 cancels 6, 360; six houses paying down 3 cancel 18, 3,240). If a booking closes more than one loop, the one cancelling most goes first. A house may owe up to its limit, ten to begin with (the gauge round it, red from two short); past that it defaults, its debts are written off and a life is lost, and the third default ends the game. A booking only ever adds to its debtor, so the way to keep a house within its limit is to book its obligations where they close a loop through it. Interest: an obligation that stands for four hands without being added to or netted grows by one as the next hand comes. A ring round its amount fills a quarter a hand and is red when the next hand will charge it, and the line under the table says how many are due. Contagion: a default cuts each of the defaulter's creditors' limits by what it owed them, to no less than five (the cut limit red after the slash), and a creditor now owing more than its cut limit defaults too, a life each, which can run on round the table. Limits recover by one with each new hand, and a defaulter's is whole again. While an obligation is held over a house the houses that booking would bring down are ringed in dashes and the line names them, including any the new hand's interest would tip over when it's the last of a hand; a cascade plays out house by house, each flashing as its debts go and its creditors' limits fall. Obligations come for one or two at first, up to three after 8 hands, four after 20, five after 40 and six after 70. The score and your best are in the strip, the best kept as `netting:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the game in play is kept (`netting:run`) and opens again where it was. Held sideways the table is on the left with all the height, the score, the line and the three obligations beside it, low where a thumb is.

Why it plays as it does: no loop is ever left standing (each booking nets out everything it closes), so every loop is made by a booking, and a loop can only be closed by an obligation from a house that something leads back to. A player who books only for what nets now does worse than one booking at random (about 48 bookings against 74 when it was tuned), because with nothing to net it piles everything onto one house and no ways back form; one that also weighs how many future bookings would close a loop lasts about 200. That's the game: lay the ways back, then close them. Interest and contagion (October 2026) make the ways back cost something to keep: a way back left standing too long grows, so a debt parked where no loop will come for it isn't free, and a default is no longer one house's affair, so it matters who a strained house owes as well as how much (a house near its limit owed by another near its own is a fuse). The floor of five keeps a cascade from cutting a limit to nothing, and the recovery of one a hand lets a cut limit mend if it's given room.

Why these numbers: played out by simple players (`moves` lists the fifteen bookings a hand offers), one booking anywhere has three houses default in about 54 bookings (75 to 80 before interest and contagion); one weighing what nets now, how near the houses are to their own limits and how many loops are waiting to be closed lasts about 183 (93 to 426) and scores about 26,000 (about 200 and 28,000 before). Over 30 games the random player set off 16 cascades and the careful one 6: the threat is there, and care keeps it rare. Interest every three hands with a floor of four took the random player down to 43 and the careful one to 165, too harsh; every five hands played much like four. With obligations never larger than four the careful player once lasted up to 825, so they keep growing. Every random thing (each obligation's debtor and amount) comes from the game's seed and the number of the draw, so a game opened again goes on as it would have.

`tests/netting.mjs` checks the deal from a seed, a booking (the debtor owing it, the game booked from left as it was, never to its own debtor, five houses for each of three obligations, more to the same house adding up), loops (owing each other paying down by the smaller; a loop of three and its 360; the loop from the debtor round to it again; of two loops the one cancelling most first; no loop left standing after any of 300 random bookings), the points, a default (debts written off, a life; netting first keeping a house within its limit; owing exactly ten within it), contagion (a default cutting its creditors' limits, the one just booked to as well, and tipping one over; never below five; a defaulter's limit whole again; a creditor owing exactly its cut limit holding; limits recovering one a hand), interest (an obligation ageing a hand at a time, growing by one after four, its clock starting again; what was added to this hand not charged; netting starting the clock again; interest tipping a house over as the new hand comes; a game saved before all this given ages and limits), a new hand after three, the pace, the end at the third default with no new hand, a game saved and opened again, and whole games: booking anywhere defaulting three times in under 90 bookings, booking to build loops lasting two and a half times as long or more.
