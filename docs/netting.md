# Netting
The seventh idle game: `netting.html`, `netting.js`, `netting.css`; rules in `netting-engine.js`, levels in `netting-levels.js`. Trading houses round a baize table owe each other; each new obligation is from a given debtor and you choose who it is owed to. A loop (A owes B owes C owes A) nets out by its smallest leg and scores ten times the square of the gross it cancels. A house whose net debt (owes less is owed) passes its limit defaults, a life is lost, and its creditors lose what it owed them, which can tip them too. Twelve levels in four sections (one currency, two currencies, credit lines, interest), each section adding one thing, each level a charter (gross, loops, longest loop, clear every debt, or survive every hand with enough gross cancelled); and the endless game, three lives, obligations growing.

- 4 November 2026, Korbi, of the Game Bench prototype: "netting: not changed drastically. currencies are new, the idea stays largely the same." The bench's look was a template only; the game keeps its own.
- The endless game keeps `netting:best` (what the games screen shows); levels' stars are `netting:stars`, the game in play `netting:run`. A run saved before the rework (v1) is dropped on opening.
- The rework dropped the old limit-cutting contagion, the limit recovering by one a hand and the lives-of-three for levels: net debt makes contagion a rule of its own (a default takes its creditors' receivables), and a line's cost is the only thing that lowers a limit.
- Trap: a level's `seeds` are games the lookahead bot won (`tests/netting-bots.js`); change a level's rules, hands or houses and regenerate them, or a game can be dealt that can't be won. A loop through houses in two currency zones needs a bridge house each way, because a loop can't visit a house twice; a middle house is allowed only where no direct booking exists, or routing would lengthen loops for free.
