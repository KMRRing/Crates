# The knowledge base
What the knowledge games know lives once, in `kb/`, and the banks the games read are built from it (`tools/build-kb.mjs`; edit `kb/`, never a built bank; CLAUDE.md, "Knowledge lives in kb/", has the working rules).

- **Entities** (`kb/entities.js`): every thing a game asks about or uses as a clue: Crates' countries and commodities, the rest of the world's countries (from the flags; a clue named for one, like Namibia under uranium, is that country), clue things (Fjords, Equinor, Trading Places, Gouda…), Chart's places and features, paintings with their painters, museums and movements. Each has its **sets** (what it is: country, commodity, group:eu, city, museum, painting, feature:river…; as many as apply) and its **facts** (position, note, region, year, picture…). Crates' answers keep their board position (`crates`, append-only) and the names accepted when typed.
- **Links** (`kb/links.js`): how things connect. A **clue** link is one of Crates' pairs, from the clue to the answer: the hand-written "because" (hint), its **aspects** (the topics you weight in Settings, which belong to the link, not the thing: Equinor is a company to Norway and a firm to natural gas), difficulty, the other answers it also fits, and its place in the answer's list (append-only). A word under several answers is one entity with several links, each with its own sentence; a clue that is itself an answer links a country and a commodity directly. Typed links: **in** a country, **painted-by**, **hangs-in**, **movement**.
- **Items** (`kb/items/`): the questions, each pointing at what it's about. **Pins** (Chart: the place's name, position, note and picture come from the entity), **estimates** (Quote: a painting's year comes from the painting), **choices** (Punt's knowledge banks, tagged with the paintings and countries they're about). `kb/geometry.js` holds the features' shapes.

Punt's skill banks (maths, reasoning, words, patterns) aren't knowledge and stay as they are; Refinery, Blend and Parley are parked outside `kb/` for now.

**Things of the same name become one entity only when something says they're the same thing** (the move into `kb/`, October 2026; `kb/MIGRATION.md` records it): Geneva the Chart city and the Crates clue are one; a natural feature joins a clue only if the clue is for a country it's in or its name or hint calls it a river, lake, falls…, so the Orange River stays apart from the Dutch colour, and rivers from the countries named after them. `kb/MIGRATION.md` and `kb/KINDS.md` list what still needs a person: facts two sources gave differently (the questions keep their own), words that may be two things under one name (Java: Indonesia's island, coffee's nickname, Sun's language) to check and split, and country strings that name no country (Svalbard, Antarctica…). `tests/kb.mjs` checks the banks are current with `kb/`, every link and item points at a real entity, Crates' order is unbroken, and nothing has fallen below the counts at the move.

**Knowledge travels between the games.** Every question the games read carries the entities it's about (Crates' answers and clues too), and a miss goes into the pile with them. Each game's learning mode then deals, after its own due questions, its own questions about what the other games found you weak on, most-missed first: miss who painted the Mona Lisa in Punt and Quote's next set asks the year it was painted; miss Geneva as a clue in Crates and Chart's next set asks you to pin it. `pile.weakElsewhere` and `pile.dealDue` do it (`tests/pile.mjs`); Punt adds up to five such questions a block, Quote and Chart fit them within the due half of a set. Not built yet: Crates' own learn mode taking in what the other games found you weak on, and more of these templates (a city's country, a country's capital) as their facts come in.

## Topics, set once for every game
2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed… all on, all off, culture and stem".
- Settings on the games screen (More, Settings, Topics; and from any game's menu, Change topics, which opens the same sheet) has 23 subjects, each on or off, with a Preset row (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade, All off; a mix of your own is "Your own"). Culture is history, people, language, religion and philosophy, the arts, film, craft, food and sport; STEM is science, maths, nature, energy and metals; Trade is the markets side (money, finance, trade, companies, energy, metals, agriculture, policy). Nothing on means every topic is dealt, as a mix too narrow always did, and says so. The choice is kept once (`topics:on`, `topics-on.js`) and syncs with your solo code; a subject added later starts on.
- Each game keeps its own topics (a clue link's aspect, a bank, a category) and says which subject each belongs to (`TOPIC_OF` in `topics.js`); a game's mix is made from the subjects that are on (`mixFromSubjects`): a topic is in, at the weight its game has always dealt it at, when its subject is on, and out when it isn't. **With every subject on that is each game's old Balanced, seed for seed, and today's run is always that.** A game reads the subjects when it deals, so a change reaches the next board, set or run, and a table deals from the mix of whoever deals. A run on a preset counts for your best; your own mix doesn't.
- A game's menu shows a line ("Topics: Culture, 10 of 23 on") and a link to the sheet, never a control of its own (`topics-ui.js`). The more and less of the earlier control are gone: a topic that was dealt twice as often keeps its weight inside its game, but you only choose on or off.
- `tests/topics-global.mjs` checks that every topic of every game belongs to a subject, that every subject is dealt by some game, that all on is each game's Balanced and what the presets are; `tests/kb.mjs` fails on a clue aspect that isn't a topic (two of Crates' clues once had an aspect no game knew, and fifty a word of the other list's for the same topic, so your topics never reached them). What a topic is follows what the game deals: Crates' clues and Origin's by their links' aspects, Nexus's things by their links' topics or else their kind, Quote's and Order's questions by their category, Chart's places by theirs, Punt's questions by their bank.
- Some commodity clues still carry a country topic (Energy, Metals & mining, Agriculture, Craft & style, Food & drink) where the commodity list has nothing quite the same (a gold mine is Production, a cotton jacket Uses, a pipeline Trade & logistics), and one country clue a commodity's; they follow those topics, which Crates shows beside each other, and are for a person to settle clue by clue.

## What the build writes
- **Kinds.** `tools/kb-kinds.mjs` reads each clue thing's hand-written hints and their links' topics and gives it a kind (person, company, place, food, currency…) only when the wording clearly says so, marked `kindBy: "words"` so it can be told from one set by hand; the rest were settled by reading each one's hints in turn (`kindBy: "read"`). The few still open in `kb/KINDS.md` are open on purpose: names that cover more than one thing (Java the island, the coffee and the language; Blue, Green and Grey as colours and as kinds of hydrogen or methanol) and bare descriptors (Ancient, Artisanal, Heavy, Lightweight). Kinds fold into the subjects (a company into Companies & brands, a festival into Culture & arts, a currency into Money & economy), so Deck's coverage by subject uses what a thing is where that's known and its links' topics otherwise; a thing counts once, whichever games asked about it. The build writes `kb-index.js`.
- **Questions the knowledge base writes itself.** A painting with its painter, museum, movement, year and picture gets Punt's who, where and movement questions and Quote's year, unless it already has hand-written ones, and every museum with a position gets Chart's pin unless it has one (art museums under Art; history, archaeology, science, natural history, ships and memorials under Museums): a painting added to `kb/` (one entity and three links) reaches all three games at once. Wrong options are things of the same kind, nearest first: painters of the same movement, then of the nearest years; movements of the nearest years; museums Chart can place, then the rest. Options' order and ids come from the entity, so every build writes the same questions and the pile keeps track of them. A painting with no museum (Salvator Mundi, privately held; the stolen Storm on the Sea of Galilee; Sunflowers in its many versions) is asked who and when, never where. A movement can be `within` another and the parent is never offered as a wrong answer; a painting can name movements it's `notWith` (View of Toledo is El Greco's Mannerism painted in the Baroque's years). Gothic is a painting movement too, with a painting card beside its building one (`archCard`). Dürer, a Crates clue before he was a painter here, is Albrecht Dürer in the art questions and stays Dürer on Crates' board (the link's `w`). `tests/kb.mjs` checks every painting is asked in Punt and Quote and every placeable museum is pinned.
- **Numbers a thing carries** (31 October 2026, Korbi: "quantity layer: good"). A fact that is a number lives on its entity (`area: 385207` on Norway, `length: 6400` on the Amazon) and `kb/measures.js` says what it is and how Quote asks it: its unit (one unit to a key, so a figure never needs its unit beside it), the sets it belongs to, the wording ("Total area of Norway", "Length of the River Orange": the Orange alone is a colour and a fruit), the market's ranges and the difficulty. The build writes one Quote question for each figure (`mq-area-norway`) unless a question is written by hand for the same fact (`about` and `fact` in `kb/items/estimates.js`: those take their truth from the entity, so there's one place to correct it). Figures are the sources' rounded ones where they agree to a few per cent and left out where they don't (the Brahmaputra, the Amur and the Mackenzie by their lengths: each source counts a different river; the Aral Sea and Lake Chad by area: they change with the year). They would crowd out the hand-written questions, so a question has a family (`fam`: country-area, river-length…) and a set takes at most one of each; Decoy leaves out the ones whose figure is exact to the digit (385,207 km²: the house's lies are round, so the truth would show). `tests/kb.mjs` checks each figure is a positive number on a thing of the measure's set, asked once in Quote with the thing's figure for its truth, and never named in its own question.
- **A question whose right answer is a word of the question** is left out of the banks, not out of `kb/` (2 November 2026, Korbi: "if the answer is a word that is in the question (e.g. where does the hannover mess take place, answer: hannover), just skip these questions. no need to rearrange the knowledge graph if it's worthwhile knowledge"). `give-away.js` judges it (an answer whose every telling word is in the question, accents and word endings aside; not when a wrong option is in the question too, as in "Brut or Extra Dry?"); `tools/build-kb.mjs` applies it to the banks built from `kb/`, Punt's deal of Crates' clues skips a clue that names its answer, and `tests/give-away.mjs` holds the built banks to it. Maths, wine, words, reasoning and patterns are edited in place and not filtered.
- **Party's Who am I? and Decoy** deal from it: `kb/items/whoami.js` names the decks, and the build writes `party-whoami-bank.js` (each identity with the names a guess may use and its crib: what the knowledge base says of it, its own facts first, then its links, six lines at most; a deck by set leaves out a thing the knowledge base says too little about) and `party-decoy-bank.js` (the sayings' meanings with their wrong meanings, Quote's numbers each with three house lies set round the truth, and the odd facts of `kb/items/decoy.js`). Its people are `PEOPLE` in the build, each marked for the party, for learning or both, with a crib written by hand; one the knowledge base holds keeps its id and names; the build fails on a person named as its own that is an entity, a mark it doesn't know, or a crib under four lines or over six.

## Subject areas
Written in October 2026 the same way, each as cards (what it traded, how it was run…), entities and links in `kb/`, from which the build makes a Punt level, Chart pins, Quote and Order dates and figures, Order's stories, clues for Origin and Crates, and Nexus's links: architecture, mythology, merchants and empires, titles and etiquette, the art market, Bavaria, Britain and Scotland, China, skiing, watches and law. Rules each of them keeps:
- Quote's years run from 1000: years before 1000 stay out of Quote (China's Tang and Song stay in the dynasties story).
- Where the knowledge base already had a thing (Hudson's Bay, Clive, Coen, Samarkand, the LME, Lloyd's, Culloden), the card joined it rather than adding a second; a clue for a country that takes no clues (Panama's Darien) is skipped. The fashion house and the god are different things: Hermès keeps its id, the god is `hermes-god`.
- Merchants: each card has its darkest chapter, stated as fact; the Rothschilds have none, since the only stories told about them are antisemitic myths.
- Titles and etiquette: no question about an order or a dress code names its answer. A founder of two orders, or one whose name echoes his order's, isn't asked, and the dress codes are described in other words (a cutaway coat, not a morning coat).
- The art market: houses are asked by where and when they began, since most bear their founders' names.
- Skiing: an Alpine resort's wrong countries are the other Alpine ones; the 3 Vallées and the 4 Vallées are written in words (Trois, Quatre), so no option stands out by its digit.
- Watches: every question is answerable without its picture.
- Law (October 2026; Korbi: "the most significant ~150 legal maxims and concepts that are just important to know. slight tilt towards trade law."): terms (maxims, mostly Latin with French, German and Italian ones, and concepts: Incoterms, demurrage, the bill of lading, force majeure…) in ten branches, which are Punt's stages. English law is the base, with the civil-law and international ideas a trader meets beside it. A term is one entity wherever it is asked (*caveat emptor*, FOB, demurrage… were already sayings or clue things and only gained the set), carrying its meaning, three wrong meanings and a note on why it matters. The build writes two questions for every term: what a maxim means (its literal translation shown unless the phrase is so well known that the translation gives the game away) or what a concept is, and, the other way round, which legal term a meaning describes. A wrong name is drawn from the nearest terms of the same kind and language, in the same branch and of about the same length; an acronym is set against acronyms (FOB against EXW and CIF); and what could fairly be said to mean the same is never offered against each other (`notWith`: frustration, *rebus sic stantibus* and force majeure; good faith, estoppel and *venire contra factum proprium*). Beside them questions are written by hand in `kb/items/choice/law.js` on the rules that have a number or a case (who bears the risk under FOB, why CIF is insured at 110%, UCP 600's five banking days and its 21 days, the Hague-Visby limit per kilo, *Hadley v Baxendale*). `tests/fair-options.mjs` holds the bank to the same length rule as the others and checks that no question or meaning says the term it is about.
- Difficulty in the choice banks is on Punt's scale of 1 to 10, which the house prices by. The banks written in October 2026 were first graded 1 to 4 and priced too short; they now keep their easy questions at 1 and 2 and put the rest at 5 and 7, and `tests/punt.mjs` holds the long-run odds across all topics between 1.85× and 2.15×, with the easiest twentieth at 1.25× or less.
- The maths bank is typeset: its maths is LaTeX between $…$, rendered by Temml through `rich.js`, converted from plain text by `tools/maths-tex.mjs` (which leaves prose, units and anything Temml can't parse as text, and can be run again on new plain-text questions). `tests/tex.mjs` parses every maths span in every bank; `tests/banks.mjs` judges an option's length as it renders, not by its markup.
