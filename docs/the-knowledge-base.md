# The knowledge base
What the knowledge games know lives once, in `kb/`, and the banks the games read are built from it (`tools/build-kb.mjs`; edit `kb/`, never a built bank).

- **Entities** (`kb/entities.js`, 7,687): every thing a game asks about or uses as a clue: Crates' 69 countries and 90 commodities, the rest of the world's countries (from the flags; a clue named for one, like Namibia under uranium, is that country), 3,671 clue things (Fjords, Equinor, Trading Places, Gouda…), Chart's places and features, 201 paintings with their 127 painters, 218 museums and 34 movements. Each has its **sets** (what it is: country, commodity, group:eu, group:soft, city, trade-place, museum, painting, feature:river…; as many as apply) and its **facts** (position, note, region, year, picture…). Crates' answers keep their board position (`crates`, append-only) and the names accepted when typed.
- **Links** (`kb/links.js`, 9,855): how things connect. A **clue** link is one of Crates' pairs, from the clue to the answer: the hand-written "because" (hint), its **aspects** (the topics you weight in Crates' settings, which belong to the link, not the thing: Equinor is a company to Norway and a firm to natural gas), difficulty, the other answers it also fits, and its place in the answer's list (append-only). A word under several answers is one entity with several links, each with its own sentence (1,215 such words); a clue that is itself an answer links a country and a commodity directly (449). Typed links: **in** a country (835), **painted-by**, **hangs-in**, **movement**.
- **Items** (`kb/items/`): the questions, each pointing at what it's about. **Pins** (Chart: the place's name, position, note and picture come from the entity), **estimates** (Quote: a painting's year comes from the painting), **choices** (Punt's knowledge banks, word for word, tagged with the paintings and countries they're about). `kb/geometry.js` holds the features' shapes.

The move into `kb/` (October 2026) read every bank and wrote them back identical before anything was replaced; `kb/MIGRATION.md` records it. Things of the same name became one entity (130: Geneva the Chart city and the Crates clue, Lake Titicaca's point and shape, Namibia the flag and the clue under uranium); a natural feature only when something says it's the same thing (the clue is for a country it's in, or its name or hint calls it a river, lake, falls…), so the Orange River stays apart from the Dutch colour, and rivers from the countries named after them (5 kept apart). Also listed: 20 facts that two sources gave differently (the questions keep their own, nothing changed), 78 words that may be two things under one name (Java: Indonesia's island, coffee's nickname, Sun's language) to check and split, and 12 country strings that name no country (Svalbard, Antarctica…). Joining the sources showed three Chart places filed in the wrong country, which the clues contradicted: Geneva (France), Jurong Island (Malaysia) and Kiruna (Russia), now Switzerland, Singapore and Sweden. `tests/kb.mjs` checks the banks are current with `kb/`, every link and item points at a real entity, Crates' order is unbroken, and nothing has fallen below the counts at the move. Punt's skill banks (maths, reasoning, words, patterns) aren't knowledge and stay as they are; Brut, Refinery, Blend and Parley are parked outside it for now.

Knowledge travels between the games. Every question the games read carries the entities it's about (Crates' answers and clues too), and a miss goes into the pile with them. Each game's learning mode then deals, after its own due questions, its own questions about what the other games found you weak on, most-missed first: miss who painted the Mona Lisa in Punt and Quote's next set asks the year it was painted; miss Geneva as a clue in Crates and Chart's next set asks you to pin it, and Punt's Cities level asks Switzerland's capital. Punt adds up to five such questions a block; Quote and Chart fit them within the due half of a set. `pile.weakElsewhere` and `pile.dealDue` do it; `tests/pile.mjs` checks it. Punt's Easy, Medium and Hard questions (built from Crates' clues) carry their clue's and answers' entities too, so a miss there travels like any other.

Topics, set once for every game (2 November 2026; Korbi: "the topics should be set in settings in the main menu, not for each knowledge game independently… remove the more/less thing, on/off is the only needed… all on, all off, culture and stem"). Settings on the games screen (More, Settings, Topics; and from any game's menu, Change topics, which opens the same sheet) has 23 subjects, each on or off: Geography, Cities, Nature; Money & economy, Finance, Trade & shipping, Companies & brands, Energy, Metals & mining, Agriculture; Policy & law, History, People, Language & words, Religion & philosophy; Art & architecture, Literature, music & myth, Film, TV & games, Craft & style, Food & drink, Sport; Science & tech, Maths & logic. A Preset row sets them at once (All on, Culture, STEM, Trade, All off; a mix of your own is "Your own"), and Choose below it ticks each, with All and None. Culture is history, people, language, religion and philosophy, the arts, film, craft, food and sport; STEM is science, maths, nature, energy and metals; Trade is the markets side (money, finance, trade, companies, energy, metals, agriculture, policy). Nothing on means every topic is dealt, as a mix too narrow always did, and says so. The choice is kept once (`topics:on`, `topics-on.js`) and syncs with your solo code; a subject added later starts on. Each game keeps its own topics (a clue link's aspect, a bank, a category) and says which subject each belongs to (`TOPIC_OF` in `topics.js`: the clue aspects, Quote's and Order's categories, Chart's, Lots' and Punt's); a game's mix is made from the subjects that are on (`mixFromSubjects`): a topic is in, at the weight its game has always dealt it at, when its subject is on, and out when it isn't. With every subject on that is each game's old Balanced, seed for seed, and today's run is always that. A game's menu shows a line ("Topics: Culture, 10 of 23 on") and a link to the sheet, never a control of its own (`topics-ui.js`). A run on a preset counts for your best; your own mix doesn't. A game reads the subjects when it deals, so a change reaches the next board, set or run, and a table deals from the mix of whoever deals. The more and less of the earlier control are gone: a topic that was dealt twice as often keeps its weight inside its game, but you only choose on or off. `tests/topics-global.mjs` checks that every topic of every game belongs to a subject, that every subject is dealt by some game, that all on is each game's Balanced and what the presets are. What a topic is follows what the game deals: Crates' clues and Origin's by their links' aspects (Origin the country ones), Nexus's things by their links' topics or else their kind, Quote's and Order's questions by their category, Chart's places by theirs, Punt's questions by their bank. Two of Crates' clues had an aspect no game knew (history for hist) and fifty a word of the other list's for the same topic (a country's clue under Companies rather than Companies & brands, a commodity's under Money & economy rather than Markets), so your topics never reached them: they're corrected, and `tests/kb.mjs` fails on an aspect that isn't a topic. 123 commodity clues still carry a country topic (Energy, Metals & mining, Agriculture, Craft & style, Food & drink) where the commodity list has nothing quite the same (a gold mine is Production, a cotton jacket Uses, a pipeline Trade & logistics), and one country clue a commodity's (Production, for cashew shell liquid processed in Mangalore); they follow those topics, which Crates and Arb show beside each other, and are for a person to settle clue by clue.

Party's Who am I? deals its identities from the knowledge base: `kb/items/whoami.js` names the decks (lists of things well enough known to be guessed by yes-or-no questions, or sets such as every country, every city, every painting), and the build writes `party-whoami-bank.js`, each identity with the names a guess may use and its crib, what the knowledge base says of it, its own facts first (what it is, its note, its years, a god's domain and symbol, a painting's year), then its links, the most telling first (what it made, its typed links, the clues it gives to other answers, the things linked to it, the clues to it, easiest first, and what lies in it), six lines at most; a deck by set leaves out a thing the knowledge base says too little about. Its people are `PEOPLE` there, each marked for the party, for learning or both and dealt by the mark (Famous people, People worth knowing), with a crib written by hand and a picture's page: one the knowledge base holds keeps its id and names, the rest are Who am I's alone, and the build fails on one that's named as its own but is an entity, a mark it doesn't know, or a crib under four lines or over six. Party's Decoy asks its questions from it too: the build writes `party-decoy-bank.js`, the sayings' meanings with their wrong meanings, Quote's numbers each with three house lies set round the truth, and the odd facts of `kb/items/decoy.js`.

Deck's overview shows coverage by subject, across the games: for each subject of the knowledge base, how many of its things you're learning (and how many are due) and how many you've learned, of how many it holds, with a bar for the learned share; a thing counts once, whichever games asked about it. A thing with a set is in that set's subject (Countries, Commodities, Paintings, Cities, Physical features…); a clue thing in the subject of its links' commonest aspect, by Crates' topic labels, with the country and commodity versions of one topic joined (Companies & brands, Policy & institutions, Trade & shipping, Culture & arts): 35 subjects in all. The build writes them to `kb-index.js`.

What each clue thing is: `tools/kb-kinds.mjs` reads each of Crates' clue things' hand-written hints and their links' topics and gives it a kind (27 of them: person, company, organisation, place, nature, site, event, law, market, film, book, music, art, food, drink, animal, plant, disease, currency, vehicle, sport, custom, word, technology, material, craft, date) only when the wording clearly says so: the best kind has to score 3.5 or more and beat the next by 1.25 ("A soup thickened with it" is food; "The Cannibal of cycling", with the People topic, a person; "A currency whose name means crown" a currency before a word). That gave 1,047 of 3,356, each marked `kindBy: "words"` so a kind given this way can be told from one set by hand; a sample of 40 checked by hand had 35 right (the misses: the Red Sea filed under ships, oil fires under sites rather than events, call centres under companies, the Purity law under drink, coal exports under places). All the rest but 11, 2,298 in all, have since been settled by reading each one's hints in turn (`kindBy: "read"`: Copernicus a person, HS 0901 a customs code, La Tomatina a festival, Krone a currency, Troll a gas field). The 11 still open in `kb/KINDS.md` are left open on purpose: names that cover more than one thing (Java the island, the coffee and the language; Blue, Green and Grey as colours and as kinds of hydrogen or methanol) and bare descriptors (Ancient, Artisanal, Heavy, Lightweight). Kinds fold into the subjects (a company into Companies & brands, a festival into Culture & arts, a currency into Money & economy), so coverage by subject uses what a thing is where that's known and its links' topics otherwise.

Questions the knowledge base writes itself: a painting in `kb/` with its painter, museum, movement, year and picture gets Punt's who, where and movement questions and Quote's year from the build, unless it already has hand-written ones, and every museum with a position gets Chart's pin unless it has one (art museums under Art; history, archaeology, science, natural history, ships and memorials under Museums); so a painting added to `kb/` (one entity and three links) reaches all three games at once. Wrong options are things of the same kind, nearest first: painters of the same movement, then of the nearest years (The Lacemaker: Rembrandt, Velázquez, Frans Hals); movements of the nearest years; museums Chart can place, then the rest. Options' order and ids come from the entity, so every build writes the same questions and the pile keeps track of them. The first four came in this way: The Lacemaker, The Laughing Cavalier, Netherlandish Proverbs and The Blue Boy, with Frans Hals, Thomas Gainsborough, and the Gemäldegalerie and the Huntington as new pins in Chart. Before that the art side was tidied: Chart's art places that aren't museums (Machu Picchu, Stonehenge, the Taj Mahal) are art places now, the seven museums that came in twice (Punt's answer and Chart's pin) are one each, and every museum has the short name its questions show, Punt's own wording where it had one. A painting with no museum (Salvator Mundi, privately held; the stolen Storm on the Sea of Galilee; Sunflowers in its many versions) is asked who and when, never where. `tests/kb.mjs` checks every painting is asked in Punt and Quote and every placeable museum is pinned.

Numbers a thing carries (31 October 2026, Korbi: "quantity layer: good"). A fact that is a number lives on its entity (`area: 385207` on Norway, `length: 6400` on the Amazon) and `kb/measures.js` says what it is and how Quote asks it: its unit (one unit to a key, so a figure never needs its unit beside it), the sets it belongs to, the wording ("Total area of Norway", "Length of the River Orange": the Orange alone is a colour and a fruit), the market's ranges and the difficulty. The build writes one Quote question for each figure (`mq-area-norway`) unless a question is written by hand for the same fact (`about` and `fact` in `kb/items/estimates.js`, as the Rhine's length, Estonia's area and Lake Baikal's already were: their questions kept their ids and now take their truth from the entity, so there's one place to correct it). First figures: every country's area (197) and the UN's 2024 population for 181; the length of 58 rivers; the area of 28 lakes and the depth of 25; the area of 20 deserts; the highest summit of 26 ranges. They're the sources' rounded figures where they agree to a few per cent and left out where they don't (the Brahmaputra, the Amur and the Mackenzie by their lengths: each source counts a different river; the Aral Sea and Lake Chad by area: they change with the year). Quote now has 1,345 questions, 523 of them written this way. They would crowd out the hand-written ones, so a question has a family (`fam`: country-area, river-length…) and a set takes at most one of each; Decoy leaves out the ones whose figure is exact to the digit (385,207 km²: the house's lies are round, so the truth would show). Almanac lists a thing's figures under Numbers, as it did its years. `tests/kb.mjs` checks each figure is a positive number on a thing of the measure's set, asked once in Quote with the thing's figure for its truth, and never named in its own question.

More paintings (October 2026): 116 more, to 201 by 127 painters, chosen to fill the story's gaps: the Gothic and early Renaissance (Giotto's Lamentation in the Scrovegni Chapel, Duccio's Maestà, Uccello, Piero, Mantegna), the Netherlandish masters (the Ghent Altarpiece, Rogier van der Weyden, Memling, Hugo van der Goes), Venice and Mannerism (Giorgione's Tempest, Titian, Parmigianino, Bronzino, El Greco, Arcimboldo), the Baroque (Caravaggio, Artemisia Gentileschi, Rubens, Velázquez, Poussin, Claude) and the Dutch beyond Rembrandt and Vermeer (Fabritius, Steen, Ruisdael, de Hooch), the Rococo, Neoclassicism and Romanticism (Watteau, Hogarth, David's Napoleon, Ingres, Fuseli, Friedrich, Turner's Slave Ship), the Hudson River School, the realists and the Russians (Courbet, Repin, Shishkin, Eakins, Sargent, Homer, Menzel), the Pre-Raphaelites, Impressionism and after (Caillebotte, Degas, Cassatt, Sorolla, Cézanne, Gauguin, Toulouse-Lautrec, Rousseau), and the twentieth century (Schiele, Matisse, Futurism, Mondrian, Kandinsky, Malevich, Magritte, Ernst, Kahlo, Miró, Pollock, de Kooning, Rothko, Newman, Lichtenstein, Hockney), with Hiroshige and more Hokusai. 22 museums came with them, each a new pin in Chart (the Scrovegni Chapel, Saint Bavo's in Ghent, Santo Tomé in Toledo, San Luigi dei Francesi in Rome, the Menil Collection, the Harry Ransom Center…), and three movements with their cards, Mannerism, Futurism and the Hudson River School; Gothic, until now architecture's, is a painting movement too, with a painting card beside its building one (`archCard`). A painting may carry a `note`, the one thing worth knowing about it (Washington Crossing the Delaware was painted in Düsseldorf, with American tourists as models; Fabritius was killed in the Delft gunpowder explosion the year he painted The Goldfinch), shown after its who question in Punt and its year in Quote; 132 do. Two facts keep the movement question fair, since a neighbouring movement isn't always wrong: a movement can be `within` another (the Rococo and the Dutch Golden Age within the Baroque, Pointillism within Post-Impressionism, the Hudson River School within Romanticism), and the parent is never offered as a wrong answer; and a painting can name movements it's `notWith`, ones it could fairly be said to belong to as well (View of Toledo is El Greco's Mannerism painted in the Baroque's years; L'Absinthe is Impressionist with a realist's subject), which aren't offered either. Dürer, a Crates clue before he was a painter here, is Albrecht Dürer in the art questions and stays Dürer on Crates' board (the link's `w`).

Law (October 2026; Korbi: "the most significant ~150 legal maxims and concepts that are just important to know. slight tilt towards trade law."): 168 terms, 82 maxims (Latin mostly, with French, German and Italian ones: *nemo dat quod non habet*, *pacta sunt servanda*, *treu und glauben*, *possession vaut titre*) and 86 concepts (Incoterms, demurrage, the bill of lading, the letter of credit, force majeure, the floating charge, kompetenz-kompetenz). They fall into ten branches, which are Punt's stages: selling goods, ships and cargo, paying, securing and insuring, contracts, courts and arbitration, property and equity, wrongs and crimes, reading the law, states and trade rules, companies and agents. English law is the base, with the civil-law and international ideas a trader meets beside it. Seventeen were already in the knowledge base as sayings or clue things (*caveat emptor*, *quid pro quo*, *bona fide*, *force majeure*, FOB, CIF, demurrage, anti-dumping…) and only gained the set, so a term is one entity wherever it is asked. A term carries its meaning, three wrong meanings and a note on why it matters (the case, the statute, where a trader meets it). From it: Punt's Law level (`law-bank.js`, weight 1 in the Trader preset), 355 questions. The build writes two for every term: what a maxim means (its literal translation shown unless the phrase is so well known that the translation gives the game away) or what a concept is, and, the other way round, which legal term a meaning describes. A wrong name is drawn from the nearest terms of the same kind and language, in the same branch and of about the same length, so the answer isn't the one with a different shape; an acronym is set against acronyms (FOB against EXW and CIF); and what could fairly be said to mean the same is never offered against each other (`notWith`: frustration, *rebus sic stantibus* and force majeure; good faith, estoppel and *venire contra factum proprium*). Beside them 31 questions are written by hand in `kb/items/choice/law.js`, on the rules that have a number or a case: who bears the risk under FOB, why CIF is insured at 110%, what the CISG says of an acceptance with changes, UCP 600's five banking days and its 21 days, the Hague-Visby limit per kilo, *Hadley v Baxendale*, the Convention on enforcing awards, the 12 and 200 nautical miles, the EU's duties on Chinese biodiesel. Also from it: 26 year questions for Quote (the Statute of Frauds, the Code civil, *Donoghue*, the GATT, the CISG, UCP 600…), Order's stories of a documentary credit, of a CIF cargo, of a London arbitration and of the law of trade through time, Nexus's links (161 links, each saying why two things connect; no Law end is left without a route to the rest of the graph), Lexicon's kinds ("legal maxim", "legal concept") and Deck's subject Law. `tests/fair-options.mjs` holds the bank to the same length rule as the others and checks that no question or meaning says the term it is about.

The maths bank is typeset (October 2026): its maths is LaTeX between $…$, rendered by Temml through rich.js, matrices as matrices; it was converted from plain text by tools/maths-tex.mjs, which leaves prose, units and anything Temml can't parse as text, and can be run again on new plain-text questions. `tests/tex.mjs` parses every maths span in every bank with Temml; `tests/banks.mjs` judges an option's length as it renders, not by its markup.

A question whose right answer is a word of the question is left out of the banks, not out of kb/ (2 November 2026, Korbi: "if the answer is a word that is in the question (e.g. where does the hannover mess take place, answer: hannover), just skip these questions. no need to rearrange the knowledge graph if it's worthwhile knowledge"). `give-away.js` judges it (an answer whose every telling word is in the question, accents and word endings aside; not when a wrong option is in the question too, as in "Brut or Extra Dry?"); `tools/build-kb.mjs` applies it to the banks built from kb/, Punt's deal of Crates' clues skips a clue that names its answer, and `tests/give-away.mjs` holds the built banks to it. Maths, wine, words, reasoning and patterns are edited in place and not filtered.
