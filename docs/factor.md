# Hanse (id `factor`)
An island-trading game (October 2026), three or four at a table, against the computer or with friends each on their own phone. It plays by the rules of the genre that Catan made well known and that Colonist plays in a browser; the names, pictures and code are its own. The name is a trader's: a factor was a merchant's agent on a far coast. The rules on one page are `factor-sheet.js`; its entry is in `rooms.js` (`GAMES`), its place at a party's table in `party-core.js` (`ELSEWHERE`).

## Where it differs from the genre, on purpose
- Where 6 and 8 would touch, the numbers are dealt again.
- Trading with the table is an offer each of the others answers once, in turn (on your own turn, at most four: `MAX_OFFERS`): no, yes, or a counter-proposal naming the cards; you take one answer or turn them all down. Korbi's words of 3 November 2026 (above) replace the old "not free haggling": a counter is one reply per seat per offer, never a run of them, so the table is still never held up. The answers come on the corner card; one sheet composes the rest: the tiles (five resources and a "?") and a Players | Bank toggle; the bank fixes the other side to itself at your rates and trades at once.
- 3 November 2026, 23:46, Korbi: "Hanse '?' tile: confirm it means 'any card of a kind chosen in the counter-offer'. yes". A "?" on the ask side is N cards of any kind, the answer names which (from its own hand); on the offer side, N cards of kinds you choose from what the proposer holds (the answer names N of the proposer's). At most three (`MAX_ANY`). At the bank the sheet fills a "?" before it sends (what you give: lots of each kind's rate from the piles you can best spare; what you ask: the kinds your best plan lacks), or the trade is off.
- 3 November 2026, 23:46, Korbi: "the 'ignore trades' should just be a permanent toggle in the top right of the screen". Ignore trades is a switch always on the map's top right (`#ign`, above the trade card), not a per-turn button; it and the menu's "Decline trades automatically" are one setting (`autoDecline` in `factor:opts`) and decline every offer to you unseen until switched off. A watching frame never answers.
- First to 8, 10 or 12 points wins on their own turn; a point that comes to a seat on someone else's turn (the longest road passing to it when a rival's road is cut) is counted when its turn begins.
- The say-lines are in the past tense and agree with "You" and with names, since the narrator speaks them on whichever phone runs the table.

## Traps
- Tap to build: a road, a post and a factory never share a spot, so a tap is never ambiguous; while roads and corners are ringed together the corner's target shrinks and the road's stops short of it (`mixed` in `factor-draw.js` and `factor-3d.js`), or a corner's circle would cover the road's whole length.
- Hard looks ahead only for its setup posts and the blockade. Playing whole turns out chose worse than its ranking (the sampled futures are too noisy to tell building now from building next turn); don't add it.
- Flat island: a tap on a corner is the fill of its circle alone, since a stroke would reach the neighbouring corners (they are one unit apart).
- 3D: the island is what turns, never the camera or the light, so every angle is lit from the same side of the screen and the ship (turned to face the camera) always shows her sails. Shaders that print on the table, sea or tiles read the island's own point (`TURN`), else the grain and the tile print swim as it turns. The dice follow the screen's corner, not the island (in 3D they are the only place the roll is shown).
- 3D: the numbers, harbour rates and every ringed place are an SVG over the canvas, projected through the camera at each pose; a ringed place's target is a patch of the table as the camera sees it, so it stays its own at any angle (below 30° they get thin: the floor is there for that).
- The camera the player arranged is kept as `suite:factor-view`, a `suite:` key so it never syncs between devices (a phone's screen is its own). A watching frame (`IN_FRAME`) reads it and never writes it. The fitted view is not kept: it refits to the box.
- Props stand in fixed places for their count (nothing is dealt), clear of the token, so the token sits in the middle of its tile in the flat island too; a prop that grows past its `FOOT` in `tests/factor-props.mjs` would touch the token or the tile's edge.
- Flying cards (`factor-flights.js`) are worked out from the state before a move and the state after it, never from anything the engine drew at random: a page opened mid-turn has no "before" and replays nothing, and a move seen twice flies once. What a seat may see decides the card's side: payouts, trades and played cards face up; a theft face down but to its two; a discard face down but to the one who gave it up; a bought development card face down but to its buyer. A watching frame flies them too (it writes nothing).
- The engine only ever moves specific cards: a "?" is filled by a counter (`counterWhy`) or by the bank's fit (`bankFit`) before anything changes hands. An answer is `ans[seat]`, ten numbers (the proposer's give, then get) beside `resp`, so the offer survives the database; `restore` reads an offer saved before the tiles.
- Not yet seen on a phone's own GPU or in Safari. Not yet: a course (Learn), and a result on the games screen.

Saved state: the game as `factor:game` (seats.js), the menu's choices as `factor:opts` and your record as `factor:record`.
- What a proposer holds is secret: no sheet or card shows it, and the engine never refuses a counter for it (a refusal is a probe); a counter the proposer can't cover is a quiet no when the answers are counted. A computer player's counter is still worked out from the real hands, so it can name a kind the proposer holds (`tests/factor-secret.mjs`). The plates' card counts are public, as at a real table.
