# Factor
An island-trading game (October 2026), three or four at a table, against the computer or with friends each on their own phone (Invite players: seats.js runs it, the computer taking the seats nobody has). It plays by the rules of the genre that Catan made well known and that Colonist plays in a browser; the names, the pictures and the code are its own (`factor.html`, `factor.js` and `factor.css`; the island's geometry in `factor-board.js`, the rules in `factor-rules.js`, the computer players in `factor-bot.js`, the island's picture in `factor-draw.js`, the rules on one page in `factor-sheet.js`). The name is a trader's: a factor was a merchant's agent on a far coast, which is what you are. Its tile and logos are in `apps.js`, its entry in `rooms.js` (`GAMES`) and its place at a party's table in `party-core.js` (`ELSEWHERE`).

The island is nineteen hexes in rows of 3, 4, 5, 4 and 3: four of timber and of wool, three of clay and of iron, four of grain, and one of dunes where the blockade starts, each with a number token from 2 to 12 (not 7), the odds shown as pips. Where 6 and 8 would touch, the numbers are dealt again, so the two likeliest are never neighbours. Nine harbours ring the coast: four that trade any resource 3 for 1 and five that trade their own 2 for 1. A game is dealt from a seed, so the same seed is the same island on every phone.

Each seat in turn places a post (on a corner, never beside another building) and a road beside it, then each again in the opposite order; the second post pays one card for each land hex it touches, so a seat puts it beside what it lacks. A turn: roll two dice, and every post at a hex with that number is paid one of its resource, every factory two (a factory is a post made larger: 2 grain and 3 iron, for 2 points); a post is 1 point. Then, in any order: build, trade, buy a card, play a card; then End. A road costs timber and clay, a post timber, clay, wool and grain, a card wool, grain and iron. A road joins your own roads or buildings and another player's building at its end stops it. There are 15 roads, 5 posts and 4 factories to a seat. The bank holds 19 of each resource; if a roll owes more than it has, a lone player gets what is left and several get nothing, as the genre has it.

A seven pays nobody. Anyone holding eight cards or more gives up half (rounded down; you tap the cards to give up), then the roller moves the blockade to another hex, which pays nothing while it is there, and takes one card, at random, from a player with a building on it. Trading is the bank's 4 for 1, 3 for 1 at a 3:1 harbour and 2 for 1 at that resource's own; or an offer to the table, on your own turn only: what you give and what you want, and each of the others in turn says yes or no. One yes settles it; several and you choose among them. This differs from the genre's free haggling on purpose: each seat has to answer on its own phone, so a counter-offer would have the whole table waiting on two people, and it is limited to four offers a turn (`MAX_OFFERS`) so a table of friends can't be held up by a bargain that never comes.

The 25 cards (bought blind from one deck, never played the turn they were bought, one a turn, a Privateer allowed before you roll): fourteen Privateers (move the blockade and take a card; three played make the **largest fleet**, 2 points, which passes to anyone who plays more), five Patents (a point each, kept hidden until they win), two Causeways (two roads for nothing), two Windfalls (any two resources from the bank) and two Corners (name a resource: every other player gives you all of it). The **longest road**, five roads in a line at the least, is 2 points and passes to anyone who gets a longer one; a rival's building on the road cuts it, and a road that loops counts each road once. First to 8, 10 or 12 points (the menu; 10 by default) wins, on their own turn: a point that comes to a seat on someone else's turn (the longest road passing to it when a rival's road is cut, say) is counted when its turn begins. The say-lines are in the past tense and agree with "You" and with names, since the narrator speaks them on whichever phone runs the table.

The computer players (`factor-bot.js`) are Medium and Hard (19 October 2026, Korbi: "ensure bots are medium and hard, and hard ones are extremely strong"; Easy went, and a menu still set to it plays Medium). They see what their seat may: its own hand and cards, the board, everyone's points without hidden patents, and what a player counting cards would know of the others' hands. Medium is the earlier Hard: it plans by the numbers (each corner by its pips, a resource it lacks and a harbour it can use; roads toward the best corner still open; the longest road when it can take it), buys what its hand can pay for or reach with the bank, makes one offer to the table a turn, plays its cards for what they win, refuses trades to a player within three points of winning, and puts the blockade where it costs the leader most.

Hard weighs every legal move of its turn (each road, post and factory, each bank trade, each card it could play, buying a card) by the position it leaves, and takes the best, ending the turn when nothing beats standing still. A position is worth its points first and far above all else; then what its land makes a turn (each resource's pips, the scarcer on this island the dearer, more for a new kind than another of the same, the robbed hex left out), less what the leaders make; the best corners it could build on now, one road away and two; how far its hand is from its nearest post, factory, card or road, counted in the rounds its land takes to make what's missing (the strongest single term: it keeps its hand pointed at its next build); its harbours, its road and fleet and the races for the two-point prizes; its cards; and the risk of a 7 (more than seven cards loses half, about two times in five before its next turn). Buying a card is valued without looking at the deck: its cost, a card in hand, and a patent's point at the odds of the cards printed. It trades with the table as a strong player does: it tries each offer of one or two of a kind for one of another on a copy of the others' minds (how Medium answers), offers the one worth most to it that someone would take, up to four a turn, and takes an offer only when it gains and the one offering isn't near winning. For its setup posts and the blockade it looks ahead: each of its best few choices is played on (the setup and fourteen turns, or eight after the blockade) twenty-four times, every seat by Medium's rules and its own by its own, with fresh dice and the cards nobody at the table can see (the deck, the others' unplayed cards) dealt anew, the same twenty-four futures for every choice (common random numbers, so the difference is the choice's and not the luck's), and takes the one whose futures end best. Playing whole turns out that way chose worse than the ranking (the futures are too noisy to tell building now from building next turn), so its turns don't. Its weights were tuned by playing thousands of games against Medium.

How strong: in four-player games of Hard against three Medium players, rotating the seats, Hard won 46.5% of 400 (a fair share is 25%; the earlier Hard won 33.5% against the same players, and Hard without its look-ahead 39%). It is quick for a turn and slower for a setup post (a second or two). Medium is no pushover either: it is what Hard was until this.

The page: a plate for each seat across the top (its colour, name, points, cards in hand, cards bought, the longest road and the fleet, with a ring on whoever's turn it is and a dashed one on whoever must answer), a line saying what is asked or what just happened (with the dice), the island, your five resources and your cards, and your buttons. Tapping Road, Post or Factory rings the places it may go on the island; tap one. The buttons that you can't press yet are dimmed and say, when tapped, what is missing. Your hand shows each change for a moment (+2 timber, −1 grain). Card opens your cards (the Windfall and the Corner ask which resources), Trade the bank's rates or an offer to the table. Held sideways, the island is on the left and the players, what things cost, your hand and your buttons beside it. The island is one picture sized to its box, whichever way the box is short; a tap on a corner is the fill of its circle alone, since a stroke would reach the neighbouring corners (they are one unit apart).

The island in 3D (19 October 2026, Korbi: "make Factor 3D, subtle table, real dice, the roads and towns proper there, tiles; a 2D mode in settings for battery saving"): `factor-3d.js`, on the vendored three.js the other 3D games use, with no model or texture files, so it's there offline. A quiet table (walnut in Codex, pale oak in Modern, baize in Kontor, grey linen in Office, its grain or weave worked out in the shader and kept low in contrast), a sea in a wooden frame with a brass line, and nineteen bevelled tiles standing a little apart, each with its land on it: pines on the forest, stacks of bricks on the clay pits, sheep on the pasture, sheaves on the fields, rock on the hills and mounds on the dunes, all kept clear of the token in the middle. Tokens are discs; harbours are jetties out to a round landing, cream for a 3:1 and in the resource's colour for a 2:1. The pieces stand up and throw shadows: a road is a beam along its edge, a post a house, a factory a long shed with a saw-tooth roof and a chimney, all in the seats' colours, and a piece just built drops on with a little bounce. The blockade is a dark ship with oxblood sails, turned broadside to you, and sails in an arc to the hex it's moved to. Two ivory dice lie on the table in the frame's free corner, bottom right; every roll throws them in tumbling, bouncing and settling on the numbers rolled (a check reads which face is up), so the line above the island no longer shows the dice in 3D. The camera is fitted to the land and its harbours, not the frame or the dice: the island fills the whole width of the box (the frame and the table run off its edges), or its height where the box is too short for that, the camera's height (46 to 72 degrees) and whether the island sits flat side or point toward you chosen to make it largest; the dice lie in whichever corner of the view is furthest from the land. Two fingers pinch to zoom (to 3.2 times) and move the island, the mouse wheel zooms where it points, one finger moves it once zoomed, and a double tap puts it back; a tap that didn't move is still a tap on a ringed place, and a drag or a pinch never is. What must read sharply and every place a tap may go are an SVG over the canvas, placed through the camera's own projection: the tokens' numbers and pips (6 and 8 in oxblood, dimmed under the blockade), the harbours' rates, the last move's ring and the ringed corners, edges and hexes with the same data-v, data-e and data-h as the flat island, so the page takes a tap the same way in either. Colours come from the page (the land from factor.css, the seats from style.css) with each theme's light by day and by night, and it follows the theme as it changes. It draws only while something moves (a roll, a piece dropping, the ship sailing), and asks the device for its low-power graphics. The menu's "Flat island, to save battery" (`flat` in `factor:opts`) puts back the island as it was, the SVG of `factor-draw.js`, with the dice in the line; a device that can't draw in 3D gets the flat island on its own. The 3D drawing isn't covered by tests; it was looked at in software WebGL at 390 by 600, 664 and 844, at 820 by 1180 and at 1180 by 820, in all four themes by day and by night, the dice checked to settle on the roll over several rolls, and the switch to flat and back clicked through. Not yet seen on a phone's own GPU.

Saved state: the game as `factor:game` (seats.js), the menu's choices as `factor:opts` (points to win, players, computer level, the narrator's voice, flat or 3D) and your record as `factor:record`.

`tests/factor.mjs` checks the island over 300 seeds (nineteen hexes, 54 corners, 72 edges, the harbours, 6 and 8 never touching), the setup (the snake, the second post's payout), the roll (payout, a factory's double, the blocked hex, the bank's shortage), the seven (who discards, the blockade, the steal), building (costs, the distance rule, the piece limits), the longest road (cut by a rival, a tie, passing on), the cards (not the turn bought, one a turn, the largest fleet), trading (every rate, offers answered seat by seat), the win on your own turn, the state through the database at every move, and whole games of every level at three and four seats, every move legal, resources conserved and the same seed the same game. Not yet: a course (Learn), and a result on the games screen.
