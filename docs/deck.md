# Deck
The pile, reviewed: what you didn't know, brought back until you do. Every knowledge game records an item when you show you don't know it: Punt when you answer wrong, pass, or stake 20% or less; Quote when the truth is outside your market or the market is wider than three ranges; Chart when a pin is over 500 km off (unless it's within 600 km inside the right country, as Deck counts it) or you took a clue; Crates when a clue's crate is got wrong (through its own learn deck); Chess's puzzle rush when you miss a puzzle; Nexus when you leave out a link or pick one that isn't there, and Nexus when you ask what a thing is (a "?", one card per thing: which description fits it). Items move through six piles with growing gaps, Leitner's boxes on Pimsleur's clock: ultra-short (due at once), short (10 minutes), medium (12 hours), long (a week), longer (a month), longest (three months); right in the longest and it's learned. Chess puzzles skip the short-term piles: a tactic seen ten minutes or twelve hours ago is still in mind, so they go ultra-short, then a week, then three months, then learned. (Until October 2026 the week was the last pile; what had been learned by then came back once, a month after it was learned, so nothing skips the long checks.) Right moves an item up a pile, in Deck or in the game that banked it (a confident right answer in Punt, a tight quote, a clean pin, a solved puzzle); wrong drops it to the first; right in the long pile and it's learned. Deck shows the piles and what's due per game, and a review asks each due item back in its own form: Punt's questions as options (drawings included), Crates' clues as which-country-or-commodity, Chart's places as which-country in the first pile and then on the map of their region (right within a share of the map's width that narrows up the piles, about 500, 310 and 190 km on Asia's map, or within 600 km anywhere inside the right country, since a lot of the countries are vast; a map whose region doesn't reach a place, as Asia's doesn't reach Norilsk, widens to take it in), Chart's countries on the map, anywhere inside the outline, Quote's numbers as a number judged in the question's own range, Chess's puzzles on the board. Up to 40 a review, the most revised first: the highest pile first (what's lasted longest is what a lapse would cost most), the longest overdue first within a pile, so moving a new miss up from the ultra-short pile waits until every longer check is done. The games' learning modes deal what's due in the same order.

Learning mode, one setting for every game (set in Choose since 4 November 2026, docs/learning-modes.md; once Deck's, Punt's, Quote's, Chart's, Nonogram's and Crossword's menus; 24 October 2026, Korbi: "Learning mode should always get three options: no repeats, learn, revision, and mixed… particularly crosswords but others too"), a choice of four. **Revision** (the default, and what "on" always was) deals what's due, the longest-term pile first, since that is what a lapse would cost most. **Learn** deals the shortest-term pile first, due or not yet, so a new miss is drilled before it fades. **Mixed** takes one of each in turn, the shortest-term then the longest-term due, nothing twice. **No repeats** deals nothing from the piles (what "off" was) and keeps every set fresh. Whatever the mode, a random set keeps off what that game dealt lately: Chart and Quote draw from the questions not dealt among the last 1,500 before any that were, Crossword weighs an entry dealt lately at a fiftieth (so a bank that has all been dealt still fills a grid, and what the mode puts in is never held back), Nonogram and Punt already kept their own record of pictures and questions seen. The last 1,500 keys per game are kept (`pile:dealt`), so a pool of fewer comes round again in turn. A shared link's set and the day's are the same for everyone and don't use the history. Where the old switch was saved, on reads as Revision and off as No repeats. Punt leads each block of a bank level with what the mode picks (up to half the block, spread through it) and fills the rest with questions you haven't seen before any you have; Quote and Chart lead their sets the same way. Crates keeps its own learn mode, which feeds the pile (not yet moved onto these four); Deck's review is always of what's due. `pile.js` is the store and scheduler (`mode`, `picks`, `dealt`, `freshPicks`); `tests/pile.mjs` checks promotion, demotion, gaps, the four modes' orders and the old switch.

No clicks needed. A typed card (Quote's numbers) has its field ready to type in, and the field stays focused from one typed card to the next (on a phone the keyboard stays up): type, Enter to answer, then either Enter to move on or simply type the next answer, which starts the next card with it. On a card with options, 1 to 9 pick one and Enter moves on.

Parley's cards say their word as they appear, in the course's own language, with a loudspeaker beside it to hear it again. The voice is the phone's own (`voice.js`, shared with Parley). Browsers let a page speak only after a tap, and Review is that tap, so even the first card is heard; the first touch anywhere also primes the voice with a silent word.

No card can be learned by its position: a card's options come in a new order every deal ("all of the above" and its kind stay last), and a Punt card whose options come from Crates' bank gets fresh wrong answers each time (other countries or commodities of the kind; other clues the answer doesn't carry). A Chart place is a which-country card only in the first pile; from the second pile on it's plotted: tap where it is on the map of its region, right within a share of the map's width that narrows as the card climbs (a 25th, a 40th, a 60th: on Asia's map about 500, 310 and 190 km), with the distance and the true spot shown after.

Language words come in a block per language (Chinese together, French together), where that language's first due word would have come, and are shuffled within the block rather than kept in chapter order; a chapter is Parley's to drill.

The map cards move: drag with a finger or the mouse, pinch, scroll the wheel or press + and − to zoom around the pointer; a tap that barely moved places the pin. The tolerance is set by the first view, so zooming in to aim doesn't tighten it, and the reveal redraws as you move the map.

The pile keeps each question as it was when it went in. Deck refreshes Punt's (October 2026) from their banks when it opens, so a question rewritten since (a giveaway fixed, its maths typeset, its diagram redrawn) comes back rewritten; its place in the pile and when it's due are untouched (pile.refresh), and a question gone from its bank keeps what it stored.

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), each screen's halves stand side by side: what's due and the piles on the left, the lists and Review beside them; a review's question and picture on the left, its answers beside them.

**Scope** (31 October 2026, Korbi: "the "random" banner should be in/our controllable from settings. Add "deck" to this banner as third option. In "deck" the user should be able to select what topics/games to cover/not cover"). On the games screen Deck's is the first of the banner's three buttons (below, and README, Random mode; each a third of one row, 31 October 2026, Korbi: "move the deck thing in the banner in the same row as the random one. the "x cards" icon really doesn't need to be there, it's already mentioned in the first button. so the banner should only be one row: deck, random, and one button to configure both, each taking up 1/3 of the row"): a plate, Deck with 13 due beneath it, which opens Deck on a review at once (`deck.html#review`; on Deck's own page it starts one where you stand, and does nothing while a review is under way), and off when nothing in the scope is due. The count of cards Deck covers is no longer a button of its own: the third button, Choose, opens one sheet for Random and Deck, a switch at its head between the two (it opens on the side you last looked at), and Deck's side has the games (Punt, Quote, Chart, Crates, Chess, Slate, Parley, Wine (Brut's cards, from before it was retired), Nonogram, Isomer, Lode, Crossword) and the topics, each a tick box with All and None, everything ticked at first. A topic is a subject of the knowledge base, the ones Deck's coverage list shows (Countries, Paintings, Law, People…); only those the piles already hold something about are offered, plus any already left out, so none is stuck out unseen. Deck's own menu has the same sheet ("Choose games and topics") and its overview a line saying what's left out, with Choose beside it.

A card is covered when its game is ticked and, if it is about things the knowledge base knows, at least one of them has a topic that is ticked: a card about the Mona Lisa and Leonardo, a Painting and a Painter, is still asked while only one of those topics is covered, and goes when both are out. A card about nothing in the base (a word, a puzzle, a number) follows its game alone. Both narrow, so a card must pass both. What's covered is what's counted and dealt: the plate's count, the overview's "due now" and Review's count and cards, and the toast's "still due" all come from the same rule (`deck-scope.js`, pure, tested in `tests/deck-scope.mjs`), and the learning modes of the other games are untouched (a game's own dealer still draws what's due for it). The overview's piles and lists still show the whole pile: the scope picks what's reviewed, it doesn't hide what you hold.

The choice is this device's own (`suite:deck`, the games and topics taken out, which never syncs: the phone and the iPad may review different things), kept as what's taken out, so a game or topic that arrives later is covered, as everything is at first; topics are kept by label, which survives a rebuild of the base where a number would not. The knowledge base's index (`kb-index.js`, 118 KB) is not loaded by every page for this: the line fetches it only when a topic is out or the sheet lists them, showing the sextant until it's there; Deck's own page already has it. The line is not drawn in a partner's watching frame or an offline party, as Random's isn't.

A scope changed in the middle of a review (1 November 2026, Korbi: "when the user changes settings to deselect a game or topic that they are currently being tested on, revise the stack they're revising to exclude those") revises the stack being reviewed, once the sheet closes rather than at every tick, so ticking a game out and back in leaves its cards where they were. The cards already answered stay, since they counted. Every card still to come that the scope no longer covers leaves, by the rule above (`revised` in `deck-scope.js`: a card about two topics stays while one is in). So does the card on show if you haven't answered it, the next one taking its place; once answered it stays until you move on. A toast says how many cards left, and "x of y" and the Finish button follow the shorter stack. If nothing is left the review ends with "Nothing left to review in what Deck covers." The stack is not topped up with other due cards, and ticking something back in doesn't add its cards; the next review deals what is covered then.

A review of one topic (4 November 2026, Korbi said yes to the Ledger's "Review this"): `deck.html#review&topic=<label>` (or `#topic=<label>`, `asked` in `deck-scope.js`) reviews what is due about that subject from every game, whatever the device's scope, this once: nothing is kept in `suite:deck`, and the sheet's changes leave that review's cards alone. A card about nothing in the base is not in it; with nothing due in the topic, Deck says so and stays on the overview.
