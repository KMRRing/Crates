# Cull
Shade cells so that no number is left twice in a row or a column, no two shaded cells touch side by side, and the cells left unshaded stay joined in one piece: Nikoli's rules, unchanged (October 2026). Every puzzle has exactly one answer, reached by reasoning, never by a guess. Tap a cell to shade it, again to keep it (a ring in the game's plum, the circle a solver pencils round a number that stays), again to clear it; hold a cell, or right-click it, to keep it at once. Only the shading counts: the puzzle is solved the moment exactly its answer is shaded, so keeping is for your own notes. Undo and Redo step through your marks. A rule you break shows at once, with a line under the board saying which: two shaded cells side by side in oxblood, a number kept twice in a line, the cells your shading has cut off from the rest tinted; all from the rules alone, never from the answer. A hint (30 seconds) first puts a wrong mark right, and otherwise makes the next step a person would take and says why ("two 4s side by side: one of them stays, so the other 4s in that line are shaded"), the cells it settled in gilt and the cells that say so dashed.

The board is the twist, chosen in the menu: Square, 5×5 to 12×12, numbers 1 to the side; or Hex, a hexagon of hexagons 5, 7, 9 or 11 across (19 to 91 cells), numbers 1 to its width, every cell in three lines (a row and two diagonals) with six neighbours. Hex earns its place as hex Minesweeper does: the same rules on a lattice that changes how they play, three directions to scan for a number's rivals and a connection rule that hardly ever bites. Left out: letters for numbers (the same puzzle in other symbols, nothing new to reason about), and boards past 12 (a cell of a 12×12 is already 26 pixels on a phone). Nothing comes from the knowledge base: Cull is numbers, and has no content to choose.

Making a puzzle (`hitori-engine.js`). Shade a board at random as far as it will go (every cell, in random order, shaded if it touches no shaded cell and leaves the rest joined, so no cell could be shaded on top: a maximal shading); number the kept cells with no number twice in a line (the cell with fewest choices first); give each shaded cell the number of a kept cell in one of its lines, since a shaded number with no rival left unshaded needn't be shaded; then mend it to one answer: the counting solver finds a second, and a cell shaded in ours and kept in that one takes the number of a cell kept in both, so the other answer has a number twice; again until one is left. The counting solver searches with every forced consequence followed (a shaded cell's neighbours kept, a kept number's rivals shaded, a cell that would cut the board kept) and counts the answers in which every shaded cell has a rival left unshaded; that and the maximal shading together prove the answer unique among every shading there is, since any other would either have a needless shade or a cell more shaded than ours. `tests/hitori.mjs` checks it against a plain search of every shading on small boards. Counting takes a fraction of a millisecond.

Difficulty is the hardest step a solver needs that reasons as a person does, the simplest step first, never how many numbers there are. Easy: what a mark beside a cell forces, a kept number's rivals, a pair side by side (one stays, so the line's other copies go), a sandwich (a cell between two of the same number is kept), a number with no rival left. Medium: the connection rule too (a cell that joins two parts of the board is kept; one that, kept, would wall itself in with its own rivals is shaded). Hard: a trial, a cell supposed one way and its consequences followed a round at a time until a rule breaks, within two rounds. Expert: a trial three rounds deep or more, how deep the search must go. The generator steers towards the level asked: a shaded cell's number changed to another rival's, kept when the puzzle stays unique and grades nearer, the first to reach the level taken, else the nearest, and the line says so ("No Expert puzzle came of this size this time: this one is Hard"). Measured: every square size reaches every level; about eight in ten 12×12 and hex 9 Experts come out Expert. On a hex board the connection rule was never once needed in 120 puzzles steered towards it (a kept cell has six neighbours, so the kept cells are hardly ever at risk of being cut apart), so hex has no Medium; on the widest hex a supposition always broke within two rounds, so it has no Expert either. The menu offers only the levels a board has. Puzzles are made in a worker (`hitori-worker.js`), with a spare of each kind lately played kept, so New puzzle is instant; in Chromium with the processor slowed four times, as a phone's stand-in, today's 8×8 Medium takes 20 ms, a 10×10 or 12×12 Hard a quarter to a third of a second, an 8×8 Expert a fifth, a 12×12 or hex 9 Expert a second and a half (seven at worst, when Expert doesn't come and every try is used), and the rete shows only for a kind with no spare yet. The same seed makes the same puzzle on every device: only the seed's randomness and a fixed number of tries, never a clock.

Together, one table on `duel.js`, two ways. The split board, a pair's default: one board for the two of you, each seeing the numbers on your own half only (the first to sit down the top half and the left of a middle row, the other the rest), the other half blank paper. Shading and keeping are shared and show on both phones at once, each ring in its keeper's colour; Point lights a cell on both phones in your colour for a few seconds, so "what's this one?" needs no coordinates. Your rows are whole, so you can work your half, but every column takes both of you: alone, a half settles between 6% and 53% of a board, never all of it (in 80 halves measured, not even with trials), and the deal checks each board's halves with the plain rules (a few milliseconds; with trials it took a tenth of a second and more, and never found one the plain rules missed). A chessboard split was tried first and dropped: alone, a player could settle almost nothing, so one of you only read numbers out to the other. One time for the team, from the start to the solve (plus hints), the same on both phones (the first phone to see it solved writes the time, and both take it from the table), recorded as the pair's team result. The race: everyone the same puzzle on their own phone from the same countdown, the others' progress showing as how much of the answer's shading they have right, the lowest time plus hints winning; a party plays it, up to six. The menu's Together part chooses the next match's way and its board, size and level; the level in the menu deals a new match at that level.

No computer rival (3 November 2026, Korbi: "'against the computer' makes little sense"; "probably drop from all").

Score: your time plus 30 seconds a hint, lower better. Bests by board, size and the level a puzzle came to, in `hitori:times` (not `hitori:best`, which the games screen reads as highest-is-best). Today's puzzle is an 8×8 Medium made from the date (`today()`), the same for everyone; its first solve is today's time on the games screen (`noteDayTime`, `hitori:today`), counted the moment the shading is the answer, and the tile opens today's while it's still to play, asking before leaving a puzzle you've started. A link (Copy a link to this puzzle) carries the seed, board, size and level. The run in play is `hitori:run` (its numbers and answer, your marks, undo and redo, the clock, hints, the hint showing), saved at every mark and every five seconds, so a reload or a partner watching sees exactly what's on the screen (the rival's progress follows from the clock); `hitori:prefs` is the board, size, level and how a match for two is played, `hitori:spares` the spare puzzles, `hitori:duel` a race's own run (`duel.js`). The clock and the rival stand while the menu, the rules or the games screen is open, and while the page is away.

Held sideways, the board takes the left with all the height, the time beside it at the top, the line and the tools low. By night the board's tiles are lifted towards the lettering and the shading is near black, so a shaded cell reads as a hole in the board (on the dial's own tiles the two were too close to tell at a glance).

The rules (October 2026, as in every classic) are a sheet of their own, from Rules in the header or The rules in the menu, in short plain prose: the aim, Nikoli's three rules, the two boards, a worked example (a pair side by side, and a cell between two of the same number), how to play here (the taps, a hold to keep, what's marked, the hint), the levels, the time and today's puzzle. It replaces the paragraph of rules the menu folded into How it works, so the menu keeps to choices. It scrolls inside itself under its pinned head, opens at its top each time, and the clock and the rival stand while it's open.

`tests/hitori.mjs` checks the boards and their lines, the rules, the counting solver against a plain search of every shading (56 small puzzles, and a needless shade the maximal check catches), 99 puzzles across every board, size and level offered (one answer each, solved by reasoning to it at its label), a seed's puzzle the same every time and today's the same for a day, each level needing its hardest step and no harder, every step of the solver true of the answer, marks, rule breaks, hints that alone solve a puzzle, the split (halves within a cell of each other, neither finishing alone, both together settling all of it), and a run and the split board's table through JSON and the database's round trip. Two phones in one browser on the stand-in database played a split board through (the lobby, the countdown, a mark and a pointed cell crossing over, the solve with one time on both, the pair's team record) and a race (the result on both, the head-to-head record).

Not done: a split board for more than two, hex boards past 11 across.

Co-op (3 November 2026, `docs/coop.md`, `hitori-coop.js`): a paired partner who watches a solo puzzle can ask to join it, and both then edit one board with every number shown to both: not the split board above, which is dealt as its own match on `duel.js` and never shared this way (a table's run is not `hitori:run`). Kept cells are shared notes. No Point (the split board's), and a shared solve earns no best, today's time or Random round; the host's `hitori:run` follows the shared board.
