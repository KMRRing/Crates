# Columbiad

An idle-section aim-and-launch game: a drag back like a slingshot sets the angle and power of a launch from a pad on a big Earth, and the ring is behind a big Moon, so the shot has to be bent round its pull. Twelve chapters, each opening with one twist (the Moon, behind it, round and home, a world that moves, the Moon as the pad, a station, giants and rings, a sun, a belt, limits, other targets, set pieces) and ending on a set piece, 60 levels and more, instant retries, no fail state. Chapters 1 to 3 are the first twelve levels.

## Traps

- **The same on every device.** `columbiad-engine.js` keeps its integrator to + − * / and sqrt: Math.sin, cos, pow, exp and atan2 are not bit-identical between V8 and JavaScriptCore, and a shot that hits on one phone must hit on the other. The Moon's rail is a table built by half-angle recurrence (`turnsOf` removes whole turns first, so it returns exactly), not a sine. The winning shots in `columbiad-proofs.js` are stored with a margin, and levels are robust enough that a last-bit difference cannot decide one.
- **The step is a function of the state only**, never of the frame time (`dt` from the distance to the nearest body and the speed), or a slow phone would fly a different shot.
- **A hit is the first entry into the ring on the continuous path** (segment against circle at every step, in the target's own frame so a ring on the Moon works), not a sample that lands inside.
- **The Earth's turn is never rounded.** It was drawn at a spin rounded to 0.4 degree and one new picture every third of a second, so it hopped 3 px at a time: the spin is `SPIN` times the time that has run, the map is read at that exact angle (`columbiad-sphere.js`, bilinear, a mip pyramid and several reads along a pixel's patch near the limb), and the page draws every second frame (30 a second) while it turns. `tests/columbiad-globe.mjs` fails on a hop, a seam or limb flicker.
- **One Sun for everything drawn.** A coast is an anti-aliased ramp, never a threshold, or it stair-steps when the picture is stretched to a tablet; no black outlines.
- **A moving level has no running clock.** Every shot starts from world time 0 and the player sets the launch countdown (`aim.t`, tenths of a second, to 10 s unless the level says `delayMax`): the aim view shows the world at that time, the flight starts there, and when it ends the world is at that time again. So nothing depends on how long the page was open, and the same angle, power and countdown are the same shot. The last settings are kept per level (`columbiad:aims3`) and the previous shot's path stays as a faint ghost. A level with nothing that moves has no countdown at all (`isMoving`).
- **The daily is the day's level** (`dailyLevel(day)`, from the fourth level on, integer arithmetic only), its scatter seeded by the day, its run in `columbiad:drun` and its stars by the day in `columbiad:daily`, so the camp's level, stars and aims are never touched by it.
- **The world is stylised on purpose** (`columbiad-levels.js`): the Earth 4.2 across its radius, the Moon 1.35, the Moon's pull a fifth of the Earth's (honest masses, 81 to 1, give a Moon too weak to steer by). A level's `dial` is a straight line from its slowest to its fastest speed; the old stretched dial is for the honest world the engine tests use.
- **Levels are made with the finder, and proved.** `node tools/columbiad-find.mjs write 5-1` (or `chapter 5`) searches each level's aim (angle, power and countdown) and stores its best shots and their window (`columbiad-proofs.js`: degrees, power units and, where it moves, seconds; the window's size is the difficulty). `tests/columbiad.mjs` replays every stored shot and fails if one stops winning. A level that stands still must have a body across the straight line to its ring (or, for a free return, a gate): a straight aim (plus or minus 2.5 degrees, any power) never wins. A level that moves has no one straight line, so its stored shot must be more than 3 degrees off the bearing of the ring at the launch time and a straight shot (any power, six launch times) may win at most one time in ten; a level marked `open` is allowed the straight way (none is now). **A level with a Moon must use it:** with every Moon's pull zeroed (`withoutPull` in `columbiad-bots.js`) no stored shot may still win, and the finder drops any shot that does. A ring on a wide loop round a Moon is not enough (a lob reaches its near side without the pull): it rides the Moon, loops close round it, or answers only while it is over its top (`win`). `columbiad-bench.mjs` is the older grid sweep of the first twelve.
- **Old saved progress is not read**: the keys carry a 3 (`columbiad:camp3`, `run3`, `aims3`, `drun3`), since the level ids were reused for different levels.
- **The preview costs 26 flights**: it is computed when the aim changes, never per frame, and is short on purpose (`cone.len`), shorter in later levels. It is a spray of dots, not a line, and blurred well beyond the real scatter (`previewSpread`: a shot itself is not that scattered): a line of any kind reads as the answer.
- **New rules are in the engine, drawn in the view** (levels are data): `from` (the launch body) and `inherit` (a shell leaves a moving body with its velocity), `hazards` (lethal discs, bands with gaps for a ring plane; a flight ends `burned`), `air` (drag), a ring's `win` (the world-time window it may be entered in), `thrift` (stars for the least power). Bodies are drawn by `kind`: the moon, red and ice ones are the Moon's craters tinted, the giant, sun, rock, comet and station are gradients (still pictures, so nothing in them can jitter).
