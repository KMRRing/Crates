# Company
Hidden loyalties round a table (October 2026; called Quest until 3 November 2026): the mechanics of The Resistance and Avalon, with roles and a setting of its own, for five to ten, against the computer or with friends each on their own phone (Invite players: `seats.js` runs it, the computer taking the seats nobody has). A Hanseatic trading house, the Company, sends five voyages along the League's route; most at the table are its loyal hands and a few are Vitalians, in the pay of the pirates, who alone know who they are. Files: `quest.html`, `quest.js`, `quest.css`, rules in `quest-rules.js`, the computer players in `quest-bot.js`, `tests/quest.mjs`. Ids, filenames, tests and the `quest:` saved keys keep the old name so saved games and rooms read as before.

## Rules, in short
Five voyages; three delivered win it for the Company, three lost for the Vitalians (two at five and six, three from seven to nine, four at ten). The Alderman names a crew of `SIZES[players][voyage]`, everyone votes openly, a majority sends it, else the seal passes; five refused in a row and the Vitalians win. Each in the crew delivers or spoils, unseen; loyal hands only deliver; one spoiled share loses the cargo, two on the fourth voyage from seven players up. Only the count is told.
Roles: the Lookout (loyal) knows the Vitalians but the Stowaway, and the Mutineer comes with it; the Pilot (loyal) knows the Lookout and the Wrecker, not which is which; the Stowaway is hidden from the Lookout; the Smuggler knows no one and is known by none. The Pilot and the Stowaway come only with the Lookout; a table has room for as many Vitalians' roles as it has Vitalians (the last chosen go first). The narrator never says a role.
The mutiny: after three cargoes delivered, the Vitalians confer, each names whom they think is the Lookout, and one strikes. Who: the Mutineer if a person plays it, else the first Vitalian round the table from the Mutineer that a person plays, else the computer's Mutineer, going with the name most gave. Which seats people play is read from the moves (the computer's carry `bot`; `s.people`).
Talk: accuse or trust any player at any time, or take it back; no more accused than there are Vitalians, nor trusted than the others who could be loyal. A word is not a move (`talk()`, put on the table as a change of its own, never through the runner). The computer players talk from the device that runs the table, in the lulls, `questChat`.

## Traps
- The computer players reason from their role and the play, never from roles they cannot see: every seating of the Vitalians (at most 210) weighed by how well it explains the votes, the cargoes and the words. Traps found tuning it: counting every hand's word buried a loyal player, so with no Lookout only the first few voices count; one threshold for all made a table move as one, hence each player's temper; Vitalians told to refuse every clean crew gave themselves away, and reading a leader's own place on the crew as guilt let them win nearly all.
- The Lookout, as a computer, votes and leads as a plain hand would, steering by what it knows only some of the time, or the strike finds it.
- The centre's `.qs-now` has a fixed height so the route above never moves as the step changes: keep its lines short (four at most).
- `s.say` is the narrator's line as the state saved it: a game saved before 3 November shows one line in the old words, then the new.
- Votes and cards are given before your turn and sent when it comes; the page looks at the table afresh before the runner moves the next computer player (docs/seats.md).
- The look has no pictures: a portolan chart in gradients (`.qs-ring::before` rhumb lines, `::after` the compass circle), wax seals as radial gradients, SVG without filters (an SVG filter on a large picture came out blurry in Safari).
