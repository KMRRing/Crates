# Blend
The blender's job. An order comes in, a product with its specification and a sales price: summer or winter E10 (EN 228 class A or F), Super Plus 98 E5, winter E85, summer or winter B7 (EN 590 class B or F), arctic class 1, paraffinic diesel (EN 15940), Jet A-1 with SAF, VLSFO 0.5% (ISO 8217 RMG 380), ECA marine gasoil 0.1%. You have a tank farm of components with real properties and prices (Eurobob, reformate, alkylate, isomerate, FCC naphtha, light straight-run, butane, ethanol, ETBE, toluene; straight-run gas oil, hydrocracker diesel, LCO, kerosene, arctic and summer HVO, RME, PME, UCOME, heavy gas oil, GTL, diesel slops; jet kerosenes and HEFA; residues, marine gasoil, cutter stock) and set each in steps of 5% until the blend adds up to 100 and every line of the specification passes, at the best margin you can. Renewable content earns the compliance credit (UCOME double). Par is the exact best blend on the 5% grid, found by trying every one. A spec line turns green or red as you go; tap it for what the property is and which components move it; tap a component for its note and full properties. Hint names the best 5% move: the one that fixes most of the shortfall, or, once you pass, the one that earns most. Certify when every line passes; the order is done within €5 of par.

The maths is the practice: most properties blend linearly by volume, octane by blending values (ethanol's RON of 120), vapour pressure by blending RVP (ethanol's 130 kPa at low blends), and flash point, cold flow (CFPP, freeze point) and viscosity by index, so the component that is worst in that property dominates: 5% of slops at a flash point of 24 takes straight-run diesel from 66 to 45 °C, and 20% kerosene takes it to 54; lubricity saturates with about 2% FAME. The orders teach the constraints that bite: the vapour-pressure ceiling on summer E10 against ethanol, the floor in winter and butane's 358 kPa, aromatics and olefins caps against reformate and FCC naphtha, the 5% ethanol limit on Super 98, LCO against cetane and polyaromatics, kerosene against cetane, viscosity and flash point in winter diesel, the density window against HVO, palm ester's +10 °C CFPP, a slops cargo's flash point, the arctic class, EN 15940's aromatics ban, the aromatics floor that caps HEFA in jet, sweetening residue to 0.5% at distillate prices. Figures are textbook-typical. `blend-engine.js`; `tests/blend.mjs` checks every order has a passing blend, the blending maths, and that hints lead to one.

The page fits the screen: the order in two lines (its story behind About), a compact margin card with the verdict beside Hint and Certify, the specification as chips in the spec's order (red off spec; tap one for the limit, this blend's value and why), and the tank farm taking the rest and scrolling inside itself, each tank's figures on one line (tap its name for the rest).

A partner watching (Rooms) sees the blend as you set it. Blend has saved under blend2: since its rebuild (its stars under blend:), and watching copied only blend:, so a watcher saw the tank farm as the order opens; both go across now (October 2026).

Held sideways (README, Bigger screens), the tank farm, where you blend, takes the left, and the order, the margin with its buttons and the specification stand beside it.

Together (October 2026): the same order for both of you, from the same starting blend, each blending on their own phone (README, Head to head), from the partner sheet; the header's order deals a new match on it. Par shows as ever; the hint doesn't. Certify is your answer, asked once and final: the highest margin wins, then the quicker, so it's a race to par as much as a hunt for it. The others show where the cost line was, blending or certified at their margin. Your own blends, bests and stars stay as they were. A party doesn't offer it (October 2026): a long, quiet game makes a poor round at a table of friends.

**Parcels, a second mode** (23 October 2026; `blend-parcels.js` the rules, `blend-parcels-ui.js` the page; the menu's Mode switches between Orders and Parcels and the choice is kept). Korbi, "Blend's second mode is a go": fuels with incomplete certificates put into tanks. The orders' game is blending to a known spec from components whose figures are all known; this one is the day a trader actually has, parcels on the dock with a certificate of analysis (COA, the seller's lab report) that gives some of the figures a product is specified on and leaves others "not tested" (NT), and a few tanks to fill. You load whole parcels (a tap on A, B or C; – leaves it on the dock, sold at 5% under cost) and when you close the tanks the receiving lab tests what is in each: a tank that meets its specification sells at the product's price plus the credit on its renewable content; one that doesn't is downgraded to a blendstock and sold at 15% under what its parcels cost, no credit (the share is of cost, not of price, so that dumping cheap parcels off spec doesn't pay). The day's profit is that, less the dock's haircut and the tests bought.

What you see of an untested figure is a range: what such a parcel typically shows, give or take a spread per property (`SPREAD`: cetane 7, flash point 12 °C, CFPP 6 °C, RVP 6 kPa…). The selected tank's specification is drawn as the range its parcels leave each line in, from the blend of their low ends to the blend of their high ends (which bounds it, because every kind of blending in `blend-engine.js` only rises with any component's value; `blendPropsOf` is the engine's blend of components given as property sets), each line green when it passes whatever the untested figures are, amber when it could fail and red when it fails whatever they are. A tank's card says the chance the whole tank passes (the untested figures drawn 120 times from their ranges, the same draws every time, so the number doesn't flicker), and the line under says what to do: take something out, or test a parcel, or load something safer. A test (the inspector's, €4,000–5,000 by desk) makes one figure of one parcel the true one, from the parcel's own certificate dialog (tap its name). So a cheap parcel (LCO, slops, heavy gas oil, palm ester, butane) is worth some risk and a test sometimes pays for itself, but testing everything doesn't.

Five desks, each with its own tanks and parcels from a seed (a fresh day deals another): first cargoes (one summer B7, six parcels), summer and winter (a summer B7 at CFPP 0 °C and a winter one at −20 °C), the petrol yard (summer and winter E10, butane and an olefins or aromatics figure left out), three tanks, and a blind shipment (two or three figures of every parcel untested). The deal is drawn again, up to 24 times, until the day is worth playing: a perfect-information profit of €60k or more, and a hand that trusts the certificates' typical figures giving up at least €12k of it. Par is perfect information, the best loading if every figure were known, which no one has; three stars is 60% of par, two 30%, any profit one. In a simulation over the deals (`tests/blend-parcels.mjs`) loading by the typical figures falls well short of par on every desk, reading the chances beats both that and loading only what is certain, and what knowing every figure is worth is more than a test costs but less than testing every figure.

Kept in `blend2:parcels` (the desk, the day in hand with its seed, the loading, the tests bought and the result, so a partner watching sees it, and each desk's best and stars) and `blend2:mode`. The stars count with the orders' on the games screen. A match (Together) is always the orders; the parcels have no match.
