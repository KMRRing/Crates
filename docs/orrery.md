# Orrery

The fifth idle game (October 2026; README, Idle), built first as a turn-based match-3, rebuilt the same month as a real-time game in 3D, again the same week with set speeds and tipping hoops, on 22 October on a beat, and later that day with a plane for each colour, hoops that drift, four buttons that tip every hoop into a colour's plane at once, and then the colours for the hoop chosen alone and no hoop standing still, two speeds each way (a place on every beat, or two) and a line struck however its hoops are going: `orrery.html`, `orrery.js` and `orrery.css`, its rules in `orrery-engine.js`, its drawing in `orrery-3d.js` (flat, in `orrery-flat.js`, where there's no WebGL) and its sounds in `orrery-sound.js`. Four brass hoops hang on a pivot axle round a sun, like an armillary sphere: a straight rod from twelve o'clock through the sun to six, on which each hoop turns and tips. Twelve places on each hoop hold a planet, strung on its wire like a bead, in four enamels (yellow, green, blue and red), each colour with a mark of its own (Saturn's ring, bands, a crescent, a polar cap) so they're told apart without their colour. Every hoop is always going, a place on every beat or two, either way, resting between, so between beats every planet is exactly on one of the twelve places round the sun (the long marks on the horizon ring). Three or four planets of one colour, one to a hoop, in one direction from the sun, are a line however their hoops are going: at twelve or six o'clock any colour at any tips, anywhere else only on hoops tipped to that colour's own plane. The next beat strikes it, before it steps the hoops on: the planets clear and score 100 for three and 300 for four, times the combo (a quarter more for each line struck within two beats of the last, to three times). A press of a colour tips the hoop chosen into its plane, or every hoop with none chosen, and every so often each hoop tips a step out of it by itself, at a pace of its own, until its colour is pressed again. Every few beats a new planet lands in a free place: every 1.8 beats at first, quicker and quicker after that (below), so the quicker the tempo the quicker they come; it is foretold 1.5 seconds ahead (a faint planet and a ring of its colour where it will land, on its hoop, so it travels with it). A planet that has nowhere to land ends the game.

The beat. A metronome the player sets, at 1, 2, 4, 6, 12 or 20 beats a minute (Korbi, 23 October 2026: "20 should be the max speed for orrery", "Min speed should be 1, then 2, then 4, then 6, then 12"): the quickest a beat every three seconds, the slowest one a minute. The tempo is the speed of the whole game, because everything the game does that isn't a picture is counted in beats: planets land and hoops drift a number of beats apart, a combo lasts two beats, and a line waits for the next. So "the faster it goes, the faster planets spawn" (his words): at 20 a minute a game goes at 12 a minute's pace and a little over half again, and at 1 a minute twelve times slower, which is a game for a very long afternoon (a careful game of about 674 seconds at 12 is about 2 hours at 1). A first game is at 12; after that a new game starts at the tempo last chosen (`orrery:tempo`, an index into the list, reset to 12's when the list changed), and the game in play keeps its own. The tempo is under the orrery between a − and a +, with a metronome whose arm swings to reach its side on each beat (in the second before it, where a beat is longer than that); − and + are dimmed at the ends. A change takes effect on the next beat, which falls a beat of the new tempo after the last one or at once if that has gone by; what is waiting (the planet foretold, each hoop's drift) is stretched or squeezed to the new beat, so the game is the same game at any tempo and only its clock changes. The tempo takes the row's whole width. (Beside it there was a bar of the beats coming, a row for each hoop with a chevron where it steps and each hoop's next drift at its end; Korbi found it very unclear on 23 October 2026 and it was taken away. The speed is shown by the line and the buttons; the drift is not marked on the orrery (see "Numbers taken off", below).) The beat is heard too: a soft wooden tick each beat, and an escapement's click (the lock and its drop) when hoops step on it.

The hoops step. Every hoop is always going, set to one of four by the four buttons under the orrery: two slots a beat and one slot a beat anticlockwise, then one and two clockwise (a slot is a place on a hoop; Korbi's word, 23 October 2026: "two slots per beat, and one slot per beat (into either directions)", below). There is no still, nor a rhythm slower than every beat (Korbi, 22 and 23 October 2026, below). A button draws its setting as a curl over the top the way it sends the hoop: one chevron on it for a slot a beat, two for two. Clockwise is as the hoop is seen on the screen: the right-hand buttons send it clockwise from wherever the camera is, in front of the orrery, behind it or between, and however the hoop is tipped (`looks` in `orrery.js` asks the view which way a hoop's own turn looks, from its twelve and three o'clock round the sun), and the bar of beats, the curls beside the hoops' numbers and the line say it the same way. Until 22 October 2026 the buttons were straight chevrons, left and right, and right turned a hoop the engine's own way round, which from the front is anticlockwise: Korbi found right going anticlockwise and asked for the curl and for right to be clockwise. A swipe was always the finger's way round and stays so. A step is one place, 30 degrees, drawn as one tick; the fastest hoop is round in six beats, the slowest in twelve. (From 22 to 23 October 2026 the fastest took two places on every beat, Korbi's "moves two rotations every tick", read as two places every beat; from 23 to the evening of that day the hoops were slower, a place every second, fourth or eighth beat; and they are back to the pace they began at, now with a tempo as slow as one beat a minute to slow the game.) A hoop keeps its setting until it's set again. Set anew, it steps on the next beat, so the hand gets an answer at once, and then on every beat after it. Set to the setting it has, a hoop carries on as it was. A new game deals every hoop at a slot a beat and all the same way, stepping on every beat: anticlockwise seen from above, as the planets go round the sun. The whole orrery turns together, and while it does no step changes which planets are together, so nothing lines up by itself (as nothing did when the hoops started still) until a hoop is set going at a speed of its own. There is no coast and no easing of speeds any more: a step is drawn as a short tick, a quarter of a second, quick off the place, a hair past the next and back onto it, like a wheel let go by an escapement, and it stops exactly on a place.

The colours. Under the four speeds are four buttons, one for each colour, larger, and there whether or not a hoop is chosen: with a hoop chosen a press of one tips that hoop alone into the colour's plane, and with none chosen every hoop at once (Korbi's requests, 22 October 2026, below). Each is drawn as its colour's planet, mark and all, on a hoop seen edge on at its plane's tilt (as it's seen: mirrored with the camera round behind, where a hoop's three o'clock side is on the left), with the hoops' numbers, 1 to 4, under it, lit for the hoops in its plane now. It stands pressed while what a press of it is for lies in its plane: the hoop chosen, or with none chosen all four. So choosing a hoop shows at once that the colours are for it (the button of its plane, if it's in one, stands pressed, and the buttons' words say "tip hoop 2 into it"), and with none chosen a drift that takes a hoop out of a plane all four were in shows at once (its number dims, and the button is no longer pressed). A tip is at once by the rules and drawn turning in a fifth of a second, so a press reads as immediate. A press costs nothing but the press, and pressing the colour the hoop chosen (or every hoop) is in already does nothing. A hoop is tipped on its own by choosing it and pressing a colour; the seven tip buttons that did it before the colours are gone, and a hoop leaves a plane only by a press of another colour or by its drift (through level and 30 degrees either way, which are no colour's). Earlier the same afternoon the tips were a row of seven buttons for the hoop chosen, beside its spin, each with a dot of the colour whose plane it was; before that, the same seven buttons as the spin's, turned to tipping by a second tap on the hoop or a Spin and Tilt switch. The line over the buttons says the hoop chosen's speed and tilt: "Hoop 2 · ↻ 2 slots a beat · +45°, in red's plane" (the speed's words there, the same on the button's label); with none chosen it says "Tap a hoop to choose it, or a colour to tip all four", which is what a colour does then. Both fit a phone's width in every theme.

Lines, exact, and the colours' planes. Each planet has a direction from the sun in three dimensions. Every hoop crosses the axle at twelve o'clock and at six, however it is tipped, so planets at twelve (or at six) are in one direction whatever their hoops' tips: those are the gates, where any colour lines up at any tips. Hoops tipped alike lie in one plane, so planets at one place round them are in one direction too; but away from the gates a line counts only in its own colour's plane, a tip of its own: yellow's at −45 degrees, green's at −15, blue's at 15 and red's at 45. Four hoops tipped to red's plane line up red anywhere round, and every colour still at the gates; blue planets on them make no line off the axle, however well they're placed, until those hoops are tipped to blue's. With places and tips both whole, it's exact: the same place on hoops at the colour's tip, or at the same gate (twelve and six are opposite sides of the sun, so two at twelve and one at six are no line). A line is made however its hoops are going: what counts is where the planets are once a beat's steps have landed, or after a tip, a drift or a landing between beats (Korbi's choice, 22 October 2026, below, when he took away the setting still that lines needed until then). So a line is made by the hoops' speeds and planes together: planets of a colour brought to one place round on one beat, at a gate, or in the colour's plane with their hoops tipped to it. A line is looked for whenever something changes (a beat's steps, a tip, a drift, a planet landing; a hoop set anew changes nothing until it steps), and once standing it's ringed in its enamel and beamed from the sun, brighter as the beat nears. The first beat at least three tenths of a second after it stood strikes it, before that beat's steps, so it's struck where it stood though its hoops step on in that beat (its flare and its score rise from there). Struck on the next beat rather than the one that made it, a line is seen made, for a beat, and a fourth can join it in that beat: a fourth hoop with a planet of the colour at the line's place, pressed into the plane (the colour pressed for that hoop alone), or a planet landing there; otherwise a four is four hoops brought to one place on one beat. One made less than three tenths of a second before a beat (by a press, say) waits for the next, and by then its hoops' steps have taken it apart, unless they go round alike and carry it with them. A line broken before its beat (the hoops pressed into another colour's plane, or a hoop of it drifting out) isn't struck; setting a hoop of it anew breaks nothing, since the beat strikes before that hoop steps. Why lines once needed hoops standing still, and why they don't now: below, under Why it was rebuilt.

The planes are fixed, the same in every game, rather than dealt from the seed: a player learns them once, like the hours on a dial (red at the right-hand end of the tips, yellow at the left, blue and green either side of level), and the hand learns where their buttons are; dealt, they'd have to be read off the dots again every game, while the deal and the drift give each game a shape of its own already. They are every other tip, so one drift always takes a hoop out of its plane; level, and 30 degrees either way, are no colour's (a hoop drifts through them, and lines up there only at the gates). Left to right they go in the enamels' own order, the order the suite always gives them in. A new game deals each hoop into a different colour's plane, in some order from the seed, so all four planes are on the orrery from the first moment and at first only the gates line up three; no line stands at the start, as before, nor three planets of a colour at one place round, since the orrery turns together at first and would carry those to a gate with nobody's hand on it.

The drift. Every so often each hoop tips a step, 15 degrees, by itself: one way until it reaches 45 degrees and then back the other, a slow pendulum (from +15: +30, +45, +30, +15, level, −15 and on), each hoop at its own steady pace, every 6, 7, 8 or 10 beats (30, 35, 40 or 50 seconds at 12 a minute), the four paces dealt to the hoops from the seed with which way each goes first, so no two drift together for long. The pace is counted in beats, as the planets' landing is (since 23 October 2026; before, both were on the clock, so that a slow tempo wouldn't spare the drift as well as give the time to aim; with the tempo now the speed of the whole game, a slow tempo slows the drift with the rest, and the hand has the same number of beats to work in). A press winds no hoop's drift: each drifts on its own steady clock whatever is pressed, so a press holds each hoop in the plane only until its own next drift, which its countdown shows. Winding them on a press (every hoop's clock, so a fresh press would hold all four for at least the shortest pace, or only the clocks of the hoops a press tips, as a tip of the hand did before the four buttons) was played out first, while hoops could stand still, and left the drift touching a careful player hardly at all: it presses before nearly every line in a plane, so every press wound the clocks again, and at 45 a minute it met about 10 drifts a game and had to press a drifted hoop back into a line it was making less than once a game, where winding none about 63 came under it and it pressed one back about 14 times a game. With every hoop going it's the same, if milder: winding none, about 88 drifts come under the careful player and it presses a drifted hoop back about 8 times a game (winding the clocks of the hoops a press is for, 47 and 4); and the drift costs it more than a third of its score (28,925 at 45 a minute, against 46,250 without the drift and 36,175 winding those clocks), and the quick player as much (64,625, against 100,625 and 125,100). Each hoop drifting at its own pace whatever is done is also what Korbi asked for first: "each ring at its own pace, slowly, but consistently". A drift is a tip like any other by the rules: one that takes a hoop of a standing line out of its plane breaks it, one into a colour's plane can make one, and a line at a gate stands whatever its hoops drift to. It's foretold, so it's fair: each hoop's pace is steady, so a player can work out when it tips; once it has, the colour's button shows it. It's heard (a spring giving a notch, softer than a tip of the hand) and seen (the hoop turns to its tip as a tip turns, flashing in the enamel of the plane it tips into, if it's one). It stands still with the game (the menu, the tab, the end) and is kept with it, so a game opened again drifts on as it would have.

Drift after the steps, and something to aim at (23 October 2026, Korbi: "the random drift tilts should happen after the belts move, not right before. Also there should be some mechanism to ensure that the planets spawn in such a way that there's typically 1-3 available options for triples to aim for. Right now especially at the start there's nothing to do because it's all different planets on different belts"). A drift now happens inside a beat, after the hoops have stepped: the beat strikes the lines standing, the hoops step, then every hoop that is due drifts, and lines are looked for once with both done. A hoop whose time falls between two beats waits for the next (the beats are where the game changes; before, a drift fell at any moment, often just before a beat and took a line out from under a player who had lined it up to be struck). By the rules it is at once, and it is drawn a tick later (`TICK`, the length of a step), so the eye sees the hoops step and then the tilt, with its sound and flash after them. A drift can no longer save or break a line the same beat strikes, since it comes after the strike; one that tips a hoop out of the plane a step has just made a line in breaks it, and one into the plane makes one, struck on the next beat. The pace is unchanged, in beats.

Something to aim at: a line takes one planet to a hoop, so a colour can be lined up only while it has planets on three hoops or more (`aims` counts those colours). The deal was random, and about one game in three began with none: every colour's planets sat two to a hoop or fewer, which is the "nothing to do" Korbi found. Now `newGame` deals again (up to eighty times, from the seed, so a game is still the same from its seed) until one or two colours are on three hoops. And each planet foretold is steered a little (`aimed`): with nothing to aim at, it is the colour already on most hoops (two, for a third) set down on a hoop it isn't on; with three colours to aim at already (`AIMS.most`), it is never one that makes a fourth, so there is still a colour to choose and it is the one worth the press; otherwise as it came, the scarcer colours likelier. Steered at foretelling, so a line struck in the moment before it lands can leave it less useful; it is "typically" one to three, not always. Played with no hand and nothing cleared, before: a start with none in about a third of games, and by thirty planets all four colours aimable half the time; now none at the start, and one to three throughout (three, mostly, since the board fills). The balance figures of the harness below are from before and have not been taken again.

A colour's share of a hoop (27 October 2026, Korbi: "user is sometimes softlocked because the same planets appear on the same rings and are impossible to 'wear off' and occupy the ring so others can't spawn ... make it so that there's a cap on how many of the same planet each ring can hold and make it less probable that a planet spawns on a ring that already has one of that planet on it"). A line takes one planet from each hoop it runs through, so a hoop crowded with one colour is worn down only a planet a line of that colour, each line needing the colour on two other hoops as well, and all the while its places are kept from the other colours: games ended with a hoop holding as many as nine of one colour and room nowhere else. Now no landing takes a hoop past three of a colour (`CAP`, a hoop's even share: twelve places, four colours), so a full orrery holds exactly three of each colour on every hoop, and the next planet's hoop and colour are drawn together (`newcomer`): every hoop with room and every colour it can still take, weighed by the room on the hoop, by the colour's scarcity in the orrery (as before), and by a half (`ALIKE`) for each planet of that colour on the hoop already, so a colour mostly lands where it isn't, which is where it can make a line. The steering above works within the cap. A planet foretold while the orrery was full, landing in room a line has made only on a hoop already holding three of its colour (the line took one of that colour from each of the others), comes as a colour that hoop can take. Over the first thirty planets of games played with no hand (120 games), a planet now lands beside 0.97 of its colour on its hoop on average (1.25 before) and beside two or more 23% of the time (35%), and no hoop ends a game with more than three of a colour (nine, before).

Games last longer for it, since they end only when the orrery is truly full: at 12 a minute the careful player below lasts about 930 seconds (703 to 1,501; 649 just before) and scores about 21,250 (7,025), with about 85 lines (49 at the gates and 36 in planes, 30 of them fours), pressing a colour about 73 times a game (38 of them putting a hoop back) under about 98 drifts; the quick one lasts about 1,315 seconds (761) and scores about 57,375. Touching nothing fills the orrery in about 275 seconds with 3 lines; every hoop at a speed of its own and red pressed every ten seconds, 380 seconds and 13 lines (1,275 points); buttons at random, 390 seconds and 14 lines (1,425). The planes are worth more than they were: never pressing a colour, the careful player lasts about 820 seconds and scores about 16,025, a quarter less than pressing (they were about even). The pace has not been changed, so a careful game at 12 a minute is now about fifteen minutes, not the ten to twelve it was measured for; quickening `PACE` would bring it back.

Controls. Tap a hoop (its wire, or a planet on it: the nearest within about 20 pixels) to choose it; it glows, and the four speeds and the four colours are for it; tap it again, or anywhere that isn't a hoop or a button, to let it go, and with none chosen the speeds and the colours are for every hoop (the speeds since 23 October 2026, Korbi: "When no belt is selected the user should be able to select the direction/speed buttons of orrery as well, in which case it should apply that direction/speed to all belts"; each hoop is set the way the button looks from where the camera is, as it is for one, and a button stands pressed when all four hoops are set that way). A tap that lets a hoop go is any tap off the hoops and the buttons: the sky (a drag there turns the view instead), the strip, the line, the page round them. The slivers between the buttons of a row and between the rows count as the buttons, where a finger meant one, and it's read from the browser's own tap, which gives a near miss to the button beside it, so a colour pressed a hair low is still for the hoop chosen. The header is all buttons (the game's name, which opens the games screen, and Menu), and like the menu and the games screen they keep the choice. **Numbers taken off** (24 October 2026; Korbi: "Remove the little 1 234 from orrery (the ones showing drift and angle), user should figure out themselves"). The hoops were numbered one to four at their knuckles where they cross the axle at six o'clock, each number on a disc of the colour's enamel while its hoop lay in that colour's plane (the angle) and with a fuse of brass burning down round it in the last seconds before the hoop drifted (the drift). Both are gone, with the numbers: reading a hoop's tip from the hoop itself, and when it will tip from its pace, is the game. The same small numbers were on the colour buttons too (`1 2 3 4` under each, lit for the hoops in that plane now, so a drift showed there as a number moving from one button to another); they and the hoop numbers in the buttons' spoken labels are gone as well, since without numbers on the orrery they would name nothing. What is left: a colour's button still stands pressed while the hoop chosen (or all four) lies in its plane, and the line under the orrery, for the hoop chosen, still says how it goes round and how it is tipped (`This hoop · …`, where it was `Hoop 3`). (Each also had its setting drawn beside it, as on its button, so that how the hoops go against each other read at once; Korbi found those marks reduced clarity and they were taken away on 23 October 2026.) A swipe is the hand's way of setting a hoop going: along a hoop it sets the hoop going the way the finger went, a slot a beat, or two for a quick one (under 4.5 radians a second, or more: a sixth of a turn drawn in more than about an eighth of a second, or quicker), and chooses it. Swipes were kept, tried with timed pointer events: one gesture chooses a hoop and sets it going, which needn't be precise (the beat makes every step exact). The swipe across a hoop that tipped it went with the Spin and Tilt switch (with no mode to say which was meant, a swipe across a hoop and one along it at its three or nine o'clock are the same stroke on the screen), and a hoop is tipped on its own now only by choosing it and pressing a colour. A drag on the sky away from the hoops turns the camera round the orrery and from low to nearly overhead (kept as `orrery:view`; the menu's Reset the view lets the room choose the height again). The clock starts at the first hoop chosen or button pressed (the tempo can be set before), and stops with the menu, the tab and the end of the game, a step, a tip or a drift being drawn standing still with it; a game opened again waits for a touch, under a plaque that gives the rules for the first games, then only how to go on. The strip has the score, the combo while it lasts, the room left (free places) and your best, kept as `orrery:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the hoop chosen is kept as `orrery:sel`, so a partner watching sees it. The game in play is kept (`orrery:run`) every five seconds while it runs, at each change of tempo and whenever a line is struck, and opens again where it was. Sideways, on a tablet, the tempo, the line (with room for two lines), the four speeds and the four colours are in the side column, low, 21rem wide (widened from 17 for nine buttons, and kept for four, which have the more room each); on a phone they're under the orrery, the tempo.

The orrery in 3D (`orrery-3d.js`, on the vendored three.js that Topple also uses; it has no perspective camera, so one is made from the orthographic one): brass hoops (thin lathed tubes, wider apart than the five were, so each reads on its own) with a small brass bead at each of their twelve places, on an axle with a knuckle where each hoop crosses it, the planets on the wires and a little larger on each hoop outward; the sun, a glowing core in a halo that beats a little with the metronome, on a column from a foot of brass inlaid with navy; a horizon ring, a band of navy under brass with a scale of degrees, a tick every five degrees and a long one at each of the twelve places, round it all, with a soft shadow beneath; and standing on it at three o'clock and at nine, two brass quadrants in the plane square to the axle, where a hoop's tip lifts its side (the three o'clock side for a tip the positive way, the nine o'clock side for the other), with a bead at every tip and a jewel of a colour's enamel at the tip of its plane, red's at 45 and blue's at 15 degrees on the right, yellow's and green's on the left, so a hoop in a colour's plane points its high side at that colour's jewel. The horizon ring itself is level, and every plane crosses it only at twelve and six, where all of them do, so it can't mark a plane: the quadrants stand on it for that. A hoop's step is drawn as its tick and its tip as a turn, both worked out from the game's own time (`turnOf` and `tiltOf` in the engine), so they stand still with the game. The camera is fitted to the outline of the hoops at their furthest tips, the horizon, the quadrants and the foot. The hoop chosen glows; a hoop drifting flashes as it tips. A line standing has a ring round each of its planets in its enamel, drawn over everything, and a beam from the sun to each (laid on the page by day, since light added to a light page is lost; added as light by night); struck, its planets swell, rise and go in a flare with sparks where the line stood (its hoops step on in that same beat, so the strike is drawn at the line's place round, not where its planets' places have gone), the sun kicks, and the score rises from there with the combo beside it. The planet foretold has a ring turned to the camera. There are no model or texture files, so it's there offline. Each theme has its brass, its enamel and its light by day and by night. Where there's no WebGL the page draws the same game flat (`orrery-flat.js`, the same calls, from straight above: a hoop is an ellipse, as narrow as it is tipped, the half of it that's tipped down dashed, since from above a tip either way looks the same otherwise, and drawn in its colour's enamel while it lies in that colour's plane; a line standing ringed and beamed). What it says over the orrery (the plaque and the scores) is an SVG in the screen's pixels, through the same projection.

Sounds are made as they play, in Web Audio (`orrery-sound.js`), with no files: the beat's tick, the escapement's click of hoops stepping (brighter for more at once), a tink where a planet lands (higher on the outer hoops, on a pentatonic scale), a click when a hoop is chosen, a button's tick that climbs with its speed, a soft two-note sweep for a press of a colour, a spring giving a notch for a drift (softer, gliding the way the hoop goes), a small turn of a key for a change of tempo, a bell struck up a chord when the beat strikes a line (a note for each planet, so four is a longer chord than three, and a combo starts it higher), and two falling notes at the end. The menu's Sound switch (`orrery:sound`) turns them off; they stand still with the menu. Not heard by anyone yet.

Why it was rebuilt (Thursday 22 October 2026, Korbi): "Orrery is cool but a bit too difficult for the player to control. It feels like getting lucky is a more consistent strategy than actually trying to align them." The diagnosis: a hoop was steered by its speed, not its place; a hold coasted one, two and a half or five degrees by the speed it held from, where a line needed the planets within about four degrees; the slow speed was precise but took half a minute for a quarter turn, and the five hoops drifted into about seven lines a game with no hand on them, so aiming cost effort and often missed while waiting paid steadily. His answer: "I think orrery maybe the steps should be discretized. There's a number of beats per second, but the fastest is one beat per seconds. The user has a way to increase and decrease the speed of beats per second. The user sets whether the rings move at all, or every, every second, or every third beat. Maybe also remove one ring from the orbit because it makes it harder to control and visualize". Readings chosen where that left room, to be checked by playing: the tempos and the first step, above; the hoops start still, so the orrery does only what you make it do, which is the point of the complaint (the metronome, the bar of beats and the landings keep it alive to look at); the start deals ten planets for the four hoops (three, three, two and two of the colours, none in line), the hoops tipped −30, −15, 15 and 30 degrees in some order, all different, so at first only the gates line up and tipping hoops alike opens the other ten places (since the planes, each in a different colour's plane); swipes kept, above; planets land on the clock, as asked, so a quicker tempo gives more steps between landings. And one rule changed from the plan that came with it, which counted lines the moment the beat's steps landed, on hoops going round too.
