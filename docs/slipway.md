# Slipway
A puzzle in 3D (`slipway.html`, `.js`, `.css`; the rules in `slipway-puzzle.js` and the levels in `slipway-levels.js`, neither with a page; the workshop in `slipway-carve.js`; the bench drawn by `slipway-3d.js`). A part of a ship's cog lies as a ghost in a block of timber; free it with a few tools, each used from the right side, in the right order, and within a budget. Seven levels, each with a par (the fewest tool uses that solve it), stars by tools used, a hint that names the next tool, and undo.

## Rulings
- 19 October 2026, Korbi: "a carving, sawing, wood-block modification job, so the user does most themselves; realistically there should be a ghost outline; focus on this as a pure carving game at first."
- 23 October 2026, Korbi: "Slipway assembly yes." That was the Yard (parts fitted together, tested by a gale). It was removed on 3 November 2026 and the assembly is not built.

## Verdict (3 November 2026)
Marginal, and not yet a tile. The levels are sound (each is proven solvable at its par, and shown unsolvable from the wrong side or in the wrong order) but there are seven of them, perhaps ten minutes of play, and the one real idea (a cut is the plane through your eye and your line, so look edge-on at a face first) is learned on level 1 and only reused after that. Nothing brings you back once all are three stars. A different change would fix it, not a harder puzzle: a level generator (a part from three to five flat faces and a notch, par proven by `search()`, one a day) would give it a supply. Without that, I would cut it.

## Traps
- A saw is the plane through the eye and the two ends of the drawn line, so the view it is drawn from is the puzzle. The camera is 233 cells away (near-orthographic), so a face seen edge-on is a line. The named views are exact (`VIEWS`; orbiting snaps within 3°). The Corner view is edge-on to the 45° faces by coincidence: `tests/slipway-puzzle.mjs` draws every level's saws from it and checks they do not land. The camera never goes below the bench (elevation 0 to 89.9°).
- The plane takes the top three layers of waste under its sole, never past the part's line, and refuses on a knot. The layer of cells half a cell from the line is left by the plane (it takes only beyond 0.5) and not counted by the grade (waste is beyond 0.7), so a notch D layers deep is D-1 to both. The sole's facing is the facing most of a stroke's points agree on (`strokeFacing`), not the first touch: the surface-net mesh tilts a pocket's floor near its wall, and a stroke begun there took 21 cells of 56 and left the mast step unfinished.
- The drill needs a face within 12° of square to its line of sight, so a slope is never drilled and a hole must be bored before its slope is cut. Magnets lay a saw on the part's flat face (6°, and 1.5 cells inside or 2.5 outside its edge) and the bit on a hole's axis (2.2 cells); without them no finger draws it.
- A knot must be on the timber's surface, because the shader paints it only where a surface passes through its sphere, and the sphere it paints is the one the plane stops on. A knot inside the block stops the plane and cannot be seen.
- Nothing may be solvable by a shorter way than its par: leave-one-out and the exhaustive `search()` over the level's declared faces, `strokes` and holes prove it. A new level needs a `solution` that plays through `apply()`, and `strokes` if it has a plane.
- `vendor/three.js` is a cut-down bundle: no `LineLoop` (the saw's guide is `LineSegments`) and no orthographic camera.
- Saved: `slipway:carve` (the level in hand, its cells run-length coded; version 2), `slipway:parts` (`{ id: { s: stars, u: tools used } }`), `slipway:best` (the stars together, worked out from `slipway:parts` at start-up), `slipway:opts`. Saves of the earlier carving game (a score in the tens of thousands, old part ids) are ignored or dropped, never read as stars, and `slipway:fit` and `slipway:fitted` are removed.
