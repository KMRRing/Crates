# Settle
The eighth idle game (October 2026; README, Idle): `settle.html`, `settle.js` and `settle.css`, its rules in `settle-engine.js`. A tank farm of six tanks, eight parcels high, under a header pipe, and four grades from the heaviest to the lightest: water (999 kg/m³ at 15 °C), FAME (fatty acid methyl ester, biodiesel, 883), diesel (835) and naphtha (700), each in an enamel and lettered W, F, D, N so it's read without its colour. Two parcels are offered at a time under the farm, each with its density. Drag one onto a tank (or tap it, then the tank): a parcel sinks below everything lighter and settles on what's as heavy or heavier, lifting the lighter ones a level, so a tank always stands in order, heaviest at the foot. While it's held over a tank the farm is shown as the pump would leave it (where it settles, what it lifts, the lines that would ship out ringed, the line above the parcels saying what it would score or the spill it would cause), and letting go pumps it. Parcels of a grade at one level, in neighbouring tanks, ship out once there are enough of them for the tanker at the jetty (three of its grade, four of any other; below); what floated above them drops a level, which can line up more, a second wave. A pump scores ten times the square of all it ships, its waves together (three 90, six 360). You choose only the tank: density chooses the level, so the thinking is in what a parcel lifts and into line with what. Every few pumps a delivery comes in of its own (it never makes a line, and never fills a tank past eight): one every three pumps at first, every two after 25, every pump after 60, two at a time after 120 and three after 200; the line above the parcels counts down to the next. A ninth parcel in a tank spills its lightest over the top, a life lost, unless the pump ships some of that tank out first; the third spill ends the game.

Slops and the draw-off (October 2026). FAME standing directly on water sours, as biodiesel on a wet tank bottom does: after five pumps with the two touching (every pump counts, whichever tank it went into), the FAME parcel touching the water turns to slops (S, a speckled mud of FAME's colour and water's), an emulsion of 950 kg/m³ that settles between them and never lines up. A mark where water and FAME meet counts down the pumps left, red when the next pump sours it, and the line under the farm names the tank nearest souring; once slops stand between the water and the FAME they no longer touch, so a tank sours once until it's drained. The clock stops when they part (the water or the FAME shipped out). Every ten pumps a draw-off is ready, the third card beside the parcels (it counts down until then, and waits once ready): dragged onto a tank like a parcel, it drains the water and slops from that tank's foot, the two heaviest, and what stood on them drops into their place, where it can line up with the neighbouring tanks and ship. Held over a tank it shows the farm drained and the lines that would ship, and the line says what it drains and scores; a tank with nothing at its foot can't take it. It counts as a pump (the deliveries and the souring move on), the parcels offered stay as they were, and the next draw-off is ten pumps away. A game saved before slops and draw-offs opens with both clocks at their start.

The tankers (October 2026). A tanker at the jetty, drawn over the header pipe with its grade's cargo on deck, takes one grade: three of it in a line ship, and any other grade needs four. Every ten pumps the next comes in, always for another grade, and waits beside it ("next", its grade, "in 7": the pumps until it comes); as it berths, the lines of three of its grade standing ready ship, scored as a pump's are, ten times the square of all it takes. A line of three that doesn't ship yet is dotted in brass, standing ready for its grade's tanker or a fourth, and a parcel held where it would make one says so. Water goes by barge (tank bottoms taken away to be treated), the rest by tanker. The line under the farm explains the tanker for three pumps the first time it's seen, in a first game after the first pump (`settle:jetty` counts them); a game saved before the tankers opens with one in and the next ten pumps away.

The score and your best are in the strip, the best kept as `settle:best` the moment it's beaten, so the games screen has it; the game in play is kept (`settle:run`) and opens again where it was. Held sideways the farm is on the left with all the height, the score, the line and the two parcels beside it; on a phone on its side the score runs along one line and the line under it takes two rows, as upright, since a taller column rode up over the score.

Why the deliveries grow to three at a time: tanks that always stand in order line up easily (the foot of most tanks is the same heavy grade), and a player taking the pump that ships most and keeps the tanks low lasted 3,000 pumps with a delivery every pump. Three at a time after 200 ends that player's game soon after.

Why the tankers (reviewed October 2026): with three of any grade shipping, lines formed by themselves, since every tank's foot is water and every tank stands in order. A habit of taking whatever ships, else filling the emptiest tank, lasted about 251 pumps against a careful player's 287 and scored four-fifths as much (24,000 against 31,000), and only one of its spills in ten came before pump 200: the deliveries' pace ended a game, not the play. With the tankers that habit lasts about 194 pumps, its first spill at about 169, and scores about 18,000; a player who lays pairs (two of a grade side by side at one level, a line one parcel short) lasts about 252 and scores about 30,000, and one who also readies lines of three for the next tanker lasts about 265 and scores about 35,000, nearly twice the habit (80 games each). The grade that ships by threes changes every ten pumps and the next is known, so the tanks worth building towards change too. Four in a line for every grade, tanks standing on footings of different heights and one grade held back from shipping were tried and did less: four in a line paid only a player planning two pumps ahead.

Why these numbers: played out by simple players (`moves` lists the twelve pumps, two parcels into six tanks, and `draws` the tanks a ready draw-off could drain), one pumping anywhere spills three times in about 27 pumps, and one always filling the emptiest tank in about 63; one taking the pump that ships most and keeps the tanks low and level lasts about 216 (135 to 321) and scores about 21,000. Before the tankers, that player lasted about 230 (138 to 295) and scored about 25,000; slops alone cut it to about 213; the draw-off as well brings it back to about 249 (135 to 415, 40 games), scoring about 23,000, with some 18 slops made and 21 draw-offs spent a game, so the two roughly balance and the game asks more: when to spend the draw-off, and where FAME may stand. A draw-off every eight pumps let it last about 295, every twelve about 252; souring after four pumps, 277 with a draw-off every ten (it drains more slops than it lets form), after three, 244. Every random thing (the parcels dealt and offered, each delivery's grade and tank) comes from the game's seed and the number of the draw, so a game opened again goes on as it would have.

`tests/settle.mjs` checks the deal from a seed (every tank in order, no line standing), settling (below everything lighter, on what's as heavy or heavier), lines (three at a level in neighbouring tanks, two not enough, a tank without that level breaking it, any level counting), shipping (what floated above dropping into place, a second wave seen in play), the points, a pump (the game pumped from left as it was, the parcel used replaced, twelve pumps), a spill past eight and a pump that ships its own tank down first, the deliveries (never a line, never past eight, every tank in order; more often and more at once), slops (FAME on water a pump nearer souring each time, the fifth turning it; never in a line; settling under FAME and on water; keeping water and FAME apart; the clock stopping when water ships from under it), the draw-off (draining water and slops, what's above dropping and shipping; counting as a pump, the next ten away; only a tank with something at its foot, only when ready; waiting once ready; a game saved before it given its clocks), the tankers (one in from the start and the next for another grade; three of its grade in a line, four of any other, three of any with none given; a pump shipping with the tanker it finds, then the next coming in and shipping the three of its grade standing ready, scored; the clock between; one every ten pumps, never for the grade before, every grade in its turn; a game saved before them given its first), the end at the third spill, a game saved and opened again, and whole games: pumping anywhere spilling three times in under 60 pumps, pumping (and drawing off) with thought lasting half as long again or more, and laying pairs scoring a quarter more than the habit of shipping when it can, else filling the emptiest tank.

Levels (4 November 2026; Korbi's plan: "Settle: levels and a 3D tank farm. Settle is already decent. Add levels that turn it into a 3D tank farm (tanks, header pipe and tankers in 3D, the 2D view kept in Settings for battery saving, as for Factor), without losing what makes the current game work."). Sixteen levels in `settle-levels.js`, each the same game with parts switched off (`rules`: deliveries, souring, tanker, draw-off; a state with no `rules` is the endless game, all on) and an end: ship `goal` parcels within `limit` pumps, stars by pumps against `par`. Traps: `par` and `plan` are the best a search found, not a proof (tests/settle-levels.mjs replays every plan and holds the win at par); a level is won the moment enough has shipped, so nothing is delivered or soured after that pump; with the tanker off, three of any grade ship; a level's run is `settle:lrun`, the endless one `settle:run`, `settle:mode` says which opens, `settle:levels` holds each level's stars and `settle:stars` (the games screen's number) their total.

The 3D farm (4 November 2026): Menu, View: Flat or 3D (`suite:settle-view`, this device's own, flat the default for the battery; a watching frame never writes it). `settle-3d.js` only draws what settle.js plays (same engine, same keys); its pure parts (layout, camera fit, which tank a finger is over) are `settle-3d-geo.js`. Traps: the vendored three.js has no perspective camera, Euler, plane geometry or textures (the camera is an orthographic one given a perspective matrix, as in Factor and Bridges; the letters are blocks of small boxes baked into each grade's parcel); a tank is picked by the upright it stands on in the picture, never by raycasting; it draws only while something moves (no idle loop); a tap on a tank while a parcel is picked pumps at once, so a double tap pumps and then brings the view home; without WebGL, or after the context is lost, the flat farm comes back with a notice.

The Farm (4 November 2026; Korbi: "3D settle was really meant as in, why not make it a 3x3 board where the user gets a pickup if they manage a 2x2 if the barge is there or 3x2/3x3 otherwise"). A second board beside the Row, chosen in Menu, Board (`settle:kind`; the Row, its levels and its keys are untouched; Korbi decides later whether the Farm replaces it). Nine tanks in a 3x3, each still eight parcels settling by density; a pickup is one grade filling a rectangle of neighbouring tanks at one level: a 2x2 for the vessel at the jetty (the tanker, the barge for water), a 3x2, 2x3 or 3x3 for any grade; a line of three, an L or a diagonal never. Rules in `settle-farm-engine.js` on the shared `settle-rules.js` (the Row's `settle-engine.js` is the same factory with its line rule), page in `settle.js` (`useEngine`), keys `settle:frun` and `settle:fbest` (the games screen's `settle:best` stays the Row's). No levels. Tank numbers run 1 to 9 from the back left; row 0 is the back.

Why these numbers: only the start (21 parcels, 14 for six tanks scaled up) and the deliveries differ from the Row (a delivery every 3 pumps, 2 after 30, every pump after 70, two at a time after 130, three after 190): a rectangle is dearer than a line (four or six parcels against three or four), so the Row's pace ended a thoughtless player too soon and the habit player (whatever ships most, else the emptiest tank) too. With the simple players (`tests/settle-farm.mjs`): pumping anywhere spills three times in about 34 pumps; the habit lasts about 118 pumps and scores about 9,000; a careful one keeping the tanks low about 150; one laying pairs (two of a grade side by side or behind each other at one level, a rectangle part made) about 280 and 45,000, five times the habit (the Row's pair-builder lasts about 250 and is only twice the habit: rectangles pay by the square of more parcels, so the gap is wider). A game lasts as long as the Row's. Scoring, waves, souring, the draw-off and the tankers are the Row's. Deliveries are only kept from making a rectangle that ships, so a 2x2 for a vessel not yet in can stand ready (dotted).

The Farm's 3D (traps): the tanks are cages (rings and bars), not glass, because a full-height opaque tank hides the row behind it; the rows stand on terraces stepping up to the back (`settle-3d-geo.js` `farmLayout`) at 58 degrees so the foot of the back tanks shows, but the same level is then not one height across rows, so the rectangle that would ship glows as a sleeve over each parcel drawn through whatever stands in front (`halo`, no depth test); letters are also on the top of each drum (the front is foreshortened from above); the headers run behind the tanks so the tops show; no roofs. A tank is picked by where it stands in the picture and, where uprights overlap, the nearer one wins, nearness measured along the ground (the terraces make the sight-line depth nearly equal between rows). The flat Farm is nine cells, each tank's eight levels as bands.
